# The Grace Roll

> On the Table, nobody asks if a thing can be done. They ask what it will cost, and who will be made to pay it.
>> — Rim Road saying

The rules of *The Long Grace* are short at their heart. One kind of roll settles nearly everything. Around it are wrapped the systems that make this world itself: what it costs to call on the god in your blood, how the god grows back, how the mind wears through, how a whole village can go mad together, how the body starves and festers and is broken, and how the dead hold you to your word.

This chapter gives every one of those systems, and for every major rule it gives at least one **example of play**: a moment from a game, told the way it happens at the table, with the dice showing. The examples follow one company of Guests across the Table. They are:

- **Ressa Weir**, a Brinehollow Netwatch officer who has begun to doubt the job (played by Maya; you met her in *Making a Guest*). Cut 2, Regrowth 1. Hand 3, Gut 2, Lung 4, Eye 2, Tongue 1, Caul 3. Flesh 12, Guard 17.
- **Halloway Brisk**, a Fatlands renderer with a lapsed Union card and a jar she will not open (played by Dev). Cut 2, Regrowth 2. Hand 4, Gut 4, Lung 2, Eye 2, Tongue 2, Caul 2; Blades 4, Feast 3. Flesh 15, Guard 16.
- **Tolly Fane**, a Vigil courier with lids she uses sparingly and rouse enough for nine days (played by Sunniva). Cut 2, Regrowth 3. Hand 2, Gut 2, Lung 3, Eye 4, Tongue 2, Caul 3; Stealth 4, Search 3. Flesh 10, Guard 15.
- **Abate Kell**, an Unfed Tablebreaker who carries a folded place card with his own name on it (played by Ade). Cut 0, Want 3. Hand 3, Gut 3, Lung 3, Eye 2, Tongue 1, Caul 4; Blades 4, Resolve 3. Flesh 14, Guard 17.

The Carver in every example is called the Carver. She is not always kind.

## When to Roll

Roll only when three things are true: the outcome is uncertain, failure would be interesting, and something is at stake. A Seamer stitching a cut in a quiet shop does not roll. A Seamer stitching a cut while the patient screams and the Kept in the next room are pounding on the wall to be let in, does.

When nothing is at stake, the Carver simply says what happens. When the outcome is impossible, nobody rolls: you cannot argue a Hush into giving back its silence.

### The Three Questions

Before calling for a roll, the Carver asks herself three things, quickly, the way a cook tastes the pot:

- **Could this go either way?** If a Guest with Blades 4 wants to joint a pig, it goes one way. If she wants to joint it in the dark while it is still screaming, it might not.
- **Would failing be worth playing?** If the only result of failure is "nothing happens, try again," do not roll. Let them succeed, or let it take longer and cost something. Rolls should open doors, even the wrong ones.
- **What does it cost?** Everything on the Table costs something: time, lacks, Hunger, Fray, trust, a finger. If the Carver cannot say what failure costs, the roll is not ready yet. Say it out loud before the dice move: *if this goes badly, the Netwatch hears you.* Players make better choices when they can see the price.

### Saying Yes, Saying "At a Price"

Most of what Guests do in play should simply happen. They walk the Rim Road, they buy bread, they argue with an innkeeper about the room. When a player describes something competent and reasonable for their character, the Carver says yes. When a player describes something hard but plausible, and the Carver does not want the fun of rolling for it, she may offer a **price** instead: *you can climb the piling without a roll, but you'll lose your bail-bucket in the dark, or you'll take an hour, or you'll have to Partake.* The player may take the price or roll the dice. This is not a rule so much as a habit, and it is the habit that keeps play moving.

[box: Example of Play: To Roll or Not]
The company arrives at a Fatlands inn at dusk. Dev says, "Halloway checks the stew before anyone eats it. She's a renderer. She'll know what's in it."
The Carver thinks. Halloway's Calling Talent, *Know the Cut*, already tells her what meat used to be by taste. There is no uncertainty about whether she can taste the stew. "You taste it. Pork, mostly. A little mutton. And something that isn't either, a soft fatty meat you know from wakes." There is no roll for that part. But Halloway's talent says telling *whether it was a person* is a Hard (14) Eye + Feast roll, and telling *which person* needs a Helping. "If you want to know more than that, roll."
Dev rolls: 2d10 shows 6 and 3, plus Eye 2 and Feast 3, for 14. Exactly Hard. "It was a person. Not old. You can't tell who." No Helping, so no name. The innkeeper's wife is watching Halloway's face across the room with a terrible patience.
[/box]

## The Roll

To attempt something uncertain, a Guest rolls **2d10** and adds one **Attribute** and one **Skill**. The Carver says which pair fits; players may suggest another and argue for it. The total is compared to a **Difficulty**.

**Total = 2d10 + Attribute + Skill (+ modifiers) (+ Teeth, if Partaking)**

| Difficulty | Number | Example |
| Routine | 10 | Climb a knotted rope; find an inn on the Rim Road; calm a frightened child. |
| Hard | 14 | Climb a wet pier-piling at night; pick a Seamer's lock; lie to a Waker. |
| Grim | 18 | Stitch a severed hand back on in a moving cart; out-argue a Clausewright. |
| Dire | 22 | Climb a cliff of the Hanging Abbey in a gale; lie to a Tenanted judge. |
| Impossible | 26 | Talk a Sayer out of a sentence; outrun the Black Noon. |

If the total equals or beats the Difficulty, the attempt succeeds. Otherwise it fails. A failed roll does not have to mean nothing happens: it may mean you succeed at a cost, too late, or with a complication, at the Carver's judgment. Failure on the Table should always move the story somewhere worse, never to a dead stop.

### Choosing the Pair

The same task can be rolled with different pairs, and the pair says how it is done. Breaking down a bricked door in Kest is **Gut + Labor** if you put your shoulder to it, **Eye + Craft** if you find where the mortar is weakest, **Hand + Filch** if you would rather pick the lock on the cellar hatch beside it, and **Tongue + Persuade** if you would rather the family opened it for you. A player who describes their approach has already chosen their pair. The Carver should allow any pair that fits the description and set the Difficulty by the approach: shouldering a bricked door is Grim; finding the weak course of mortar might be only Hard, but takes ten minutes, and the thing on the other side has heard you knocking.

[pull] What can you do? Very well. Now: how are you going to do it, and who is going to hear? | — Netwatch sergeant's catechism

### What the Numbers Mean

A character with an Attribute of 2 and a Skill of 2 adds 4 to their roll, and the 2d10 average 11. Such a person succeeds at Routine tasks nine times in ten, at Hard tasks about two times in three, at Grim tasks a little more than one time in four, and at Dire tasks almost never, unless the dice double up or the god helps. A master with 4 and 4 adds 8: Hard tasks become reliable, Grim ones better than even, Dire ones possible. This is deliberate. On the Table, the ordinary person is good at ordinary things and helpless before the extraordinary, which is why the extraordinary keeps winning, and why so many people Partake.

| Bonus (Attribute + Skill) | Routine 10 | Hard 14 | Grim 18 | Dire 22 |
| 2 | 79% | 45% | 15% | 1% |
| 4 | 90% | 64% | 28% | 6% |
| 6 | 97% | 79% | 45% | 15% |
| 8 | 99% | 90% | 64% | 28% |
| 10 | 99% | 97% | 79% | 45% |

These figures are approximate and include the Grace on double 10s and the Lack on double 1s. Add a Tooth (average 3.5) and every row climbs by nearly two lines. That is the temptation.

### Helpings

For every full 4 points by which a total exceeds the Difficulty, the roll earns one **Helping**. A Helping is an extra: more information, more speed, a better price, a quieter entry, a cleaner cut. In combat, each Helping adds +2 damage. A player can usually spend Helpings as they like, within reason; the Carver may offer a menu.

*Example: Ressa (Eye 2, Search 3) searches a drowned house for the ledger of the Called. Difficulty Hard 14. She rolls 7 and 9: total 21. That beats 14 by 7, one Helping. She finds the ledger, and with her Helping she also notices that the last entry is written in her brother's hand.*

A margin of exactly 4 is one Helping; 8 is two; 12 is three. Helpings are counted from the Difficulty the roll actually faced, including any modifiers. In an opposed roll (below), they are counted from the loser's total.

| Kind of task | What a Helping can buy |
| Searching, investigating | A second clue; the clue's meaning; time saved; nobody saw you looking |
| Persuading, lying, haggling | A better price (10% each); a favour later; they believe it more than you meant |
| Stitching, healing | +1 Flesh restored; a cleaner scar; no fever; the patient stops screaming |
| Sneaking, climbing, running | Half the time; a better position; you bring someone with you |
| Crafting, mending | It lasts longer; it is beautiful; it hides something |
| Rites, prayers | One more mourner steadied; the god does not notice; the dead are quieter |
| Combat | +2 damage; or a weapon quality's Helping effect (Hooking, Stunning, Entangling) |

### Grace and Lack

When the two d10 show the same number, the roll is an **omen**.

- If the roll **succeeds**, it is a **Grace**. Something goes better than it had any right to: the strongest effect the situation allows, a Helping on top of whatever was earned, an enemy disarmed, a door that was going to be locked found open, a second piece of information. In combat, a Grace attack also inflicts a **Mangling** (see Combat).
- If the roll **fails**, it is a **Lack**. The worst plausible complication happens: the weapon breaks, the lie is not only seen through but believed to be something worse, the patient's wound opens further, the god notices.
- **Double 1s ("Licked Clean")** always fail, whatever the total, and always with a Lack.
- **Double 10s ("Second Helping")** always succeed, whatever the Difficulty (short of the impossible), and always with a Grace.

Teeth (see Partaking) never count toward doubles. Only the two d10 can make an omen. Adversaries' rolls make omens exactly as Guests' rolls do.

[box: Lacks Worth Having]
A Lack should never be the Carver punishing the player. It should be the world being itself. Good Lacks on the Table: your Tooth bites (gain 2 Hunger); somebody you love hears what you just said; the thing you did leaves a mark that a Cutwright will be able to read; the noise wakes something in the cellar; your knife slips, and now there's blood on the bread; the rope you tied comes undone, in Cradlewrack, as everything does; you say "I promise" without meaning to, in Oathen.
[/box]

[box: Graces Worth Having]
A Grace should feel like the world, for one breath, being kind, and it should be a little uncanny, because on the Table kindness usually is. Good Graces: the man you are lying to wants to believe you, and does, and will defend your lie to others; the wound you are stitching closes cleaner than it should, and the patient remembers your face for the rest of their life; the lock was never locked; the Kept woman you are interrogating laughs, and tells you the thing you came for, and something else besides; the fly-front parts around you as though you were expected; the bread at the inn is safe, and you know it is safe, and you eat until you are almost full.
[/box]

### Modifiers

Circumstances add or subtract from the total, usually by 2 (sometimes by 4 for something overwhelming). Good tools, time, and help add; darkness, injury, fear, cold and Wants subtract. Do not stack more than three modifiers. If the situation is that lopsided, change the Difficulty instead.

- **Help:** another character who could plausibly assist gives +1 (or +2 with the *Second Chair* Knack). Only one helper counts, unless the task is physical and large (lifting a cart, pulling a Called man out of the surf).
- **Tools:** the right tool is assumed; a fine one gives +1 or +2; no tool at all may make a task impossible or Grim.
- **Time:** taking ten times as long gives +2. Rushing gives −2.
- **Injury, exhaustion, hunger:** −2 per condition, as specified in their rules.

Track-based penalties (Fray, Starving, Bloodied, Weariness in the Vigil, Pangs in the Fast, a Kept character's Rot) are **conditions** and apply in addition to the three-modifier limit; the limit is for the Carver's circumstantial judgments, not for the state of a Guest's body and mind. A Guest who is Rattled, Starving 2 and Bloodied simply has a very bad day. The Carver may still decide that the pile has become absurd and that the task is impossible until they rest. That is usually the right decision, and the right scene.

### Opposed Rolls

When two characters act against each other (a lie against a suspicion, a chase, an arm-wrestle, a haggle), both roll. The higher total wins; on a tie, the defender (or whoever is keeping the situation as it is) wins. Helpings in an opposed roll are counted by how much the winner beat the loser by.

A Threat-rated character rolls 2d10 + Threat in an opposed roll. When a Guest acts against a quick-lined adversary who is not actively resisting (sneaking past a sleeping guard; lying to a clerk who has no reason to doubt), the Carver may set a flat Difficulty of 10 + Threat instead of rolling for them. Both approaches give the same average; the opposed roll is wilder.

[box: Example of Play: An Opposed Roll]
Tolly Fane has to get a sealed satchel past a Waker at the Vigil's west gate without the Waker finding the brine-cut rouse sewn into its lining. Sunniva describes Tolly yawning, very slightly, at exactly the wrong moment, so that the Waker's attention goes to her face and not her bag. "That's Deceive," says the Carver. "Tongue + Deceive against his search." Tolly has Tongue 2, Deceive 3. The Waker is Threat 3.
Sunniva rolls 8 and 5 for 18. The Carver rolls 4 and 4 for 11: a pair, and a loss, so a Lack for the Waker. Tolly wins by 7, one Helping. "He sees you yawn," says the Carver, "and he's on you with the needle before you can close your mouth, a quick bright jab in the meat of your forearm. You're awake. You're very awake. He's so pleased with himself for catching a yawner that he waves you through without touching the bag, and he writes your name in his book as a Good Citizen, Cooperative When Corrected." The Lack makes the Waker's failure worse for him. The Helping, Sunniva decides, means the Waker will remember her kindly. The Carver writes that down. Kindness from a Waker is a thing that can be called in.
[/box]

### Long Tasks

For efforts that take hours or days (restitching a grandmother, deciphering a seating plan, crossing the Uncovered), the Carver sets a **number of Helpings needed**, typically 3 to 8, and a time per roll. The Guests roll until they reach the total or run out of time, and a Lack along the way costs something real.

For a long task, a success that earns no Helpings still counts as one step of progress; each Helping counts as one more. A failure counts nothing and spends the time. A Lack loses one step of progress already made, and costs something besides. A Grace counts double. Several Guests can work on the same long task, each rolling once per period, if the task allows it (three Seamers can work on a grandmother at once; only one Cutwright can read a page).

| Long task | Helpings | Time per roll | Roll |
| Restitch a Kept grandmother for spring | 4 | 1 hour | Hand + Stitching, Hard 14 |
| Copy a Cutwright ledger before the clerk returns | 5 | 10 minutes | Eye + Reckoning, Hard 14 |
| Draft a forty-page marriage vow in Oathen | 8 | 1 day | Tongue + Clause, Hard 14 |
| Cross a day of the Uncovered without losing the line | 3 | 4 hours | Lung + Wayfaring, Grim 18 |
| Brick in a room in Cradlewrack before the pang | 4 | 15 minutes | Gut + Labor, Hard 14 |
| Talk a village out of a Reaping lottery | 6 | 1 evening | Tongue + Persuade, Grim 18 |

[box: Example of Play: A Long Task]
In a house in the Sinks of Lastgate, the company has promised a Kept family that they will restitch great-grandmother Orla Strake before the spring feast, in exchange for a name. Halloway has Stitching 1 and Hand 4; the family's own Threader, a nervous boy of thirteen, will help her (+1). The Carver sets four Helpings at Hard (14), one hour per roll, and says the feast begins in five hours.
Hour one: Dev rolls 9 and 7, plus 4, plus 1, plus 1 from the boy: 22. Beats 14 by 8. Success plus two Helpings: three steps. The left arm goes back on, neat as a sleeve, and Orla Strake says, through her stitched jaw, "Oh, that's better, love, that's lovely."
Hour two: 3 and 3. A pair, and 3 + 3 + 6 = 12, a failure: a Lack. "The thread pulls through," says the Carver. "Not the new thread. The old thread, in her neck, the guild thread from two hundred years ago. It parts like wet paper and her head goes over sideways on the cord of her spine and hangs there, looking at you, and she is still saying *lovely*." One step lost: they are back to two. Everybody at the table checks their Fray. (The Carver calls for a Dread 1 check; Halloway's Butcher's Calm would have ignored it, but she does not have that Knack.)
Hour three: 14 on the nose. One step: three. Hour four: 17, one step: four. Done, with an hour to spare. Orla Strake sits at the head of the feast with her head stitched on a little crooked, which the family says gives her a thoughtful look. The name she gives them is the right one.
[/box]

### Threat

Minor non-player characters do not need full statistics. They have a **Threat** rating from 0 to 5 that they add to all their rolls instead of Attribute + Skill (attack rolls are listed separately). A Rim Road innkeeper is Threat 1. A Netwatch lieutenant is Threat 3. Lady Corrow Vane is not a Threat-rated character.

| Threat | Who | Example |
| 0 | Helpless or untrained | A child; a Seated guest asked to stand; a Counted sleeper |
| 1 | Ordinary folk | A Rim Road innkeeper; a Fatlands farmhand; a Finisher pilgrim |
| 2 | Trained | A Netwatch hookman; a Cull-bailiff; a Forsworn tough |
| 3 | Professional | A Waker; a Reckoner; a Company Warden; a Bench clerk at Clause |
| 4 | Expert | A Netwatch lieutenant's best; a Second Table huntsman captain |
| 5 | Best in the land at their work | Jem Crackle in a kitchen; Jude Crake in a cell |

A Threat-rated character may be much better at one thing than their Threat suggests. The quick line may say so (*Bench Clerk, Threat 3, Clause 4*), in which case they add that number for that kind of roll instead.

[pull] A man with a hook and a wage is Threat 2. A man with a hook and a grudge is Threat 2 and the Carver rolls twice. | — from a Carver's margin

## Partaking: The Tooth

Anyone with a Cut of 1 or higher carries a god. Before any roll, a Guest may **Partake**: call on the god in their blood to lend them its strength, its senses, its terrible certainty. When you Partake:

- Roll **one Tooth** (a d6, in a different color from your d10s) and add it to the total. A character of Cut 3 or higher may roll **two Teeth** if they wish. Teeth are rolled with the 2d10. They do not count for doubles.
- Gain **1 Hunger** for Partaking.
- Gain **1 additional Hunger** for every Tooth that shows a 1. The god bit back.
- Describe how the god shows itself. Each land's chapter describes its **Tooth**: a Sallowreach Guest's hands go cold and their fingers seem one joint too long; a Fatlander's mouth floods with saliva; a Brinehollower's ears pop and their voice drops an octave; a Vigil-born's eyes stop blinking; a Cradlewracker feels something turn over inside them; an Oathener hears their own heartbeat repeating their last words.

Partaking is powerful. A Tooth adds 3.5 on average, the difference between a Hard roll and a Grim one. It is also how the end comes. Every Guest who Partakes is choosing, roll by roll, to bring their god closer to the surface.

The Unfed and Blanks cannot Partake. The Unfed have Want instead.

### Partaking, Clarified

- **Declare before you roll.** The god does not come when you see the dice have failed; it comes when you ask. A player who has already rolled may not Partake on that roll.
- **Partaking costs 1 Hunger for the act**, whether you roll one Tooth or two. Rolling two Teeth (Cut 3+) costs no more for the asking, but doubles the chance of a bite.
- **A Lack while Partaking** costs 2 more Hunger in addition (see Hunger). The god was there for your failure, and it was interested.
- **A bite is a manifestation.** When a Tooth shows a 1, the god bites back with more than Hunger: each land's Tooth entry says what the body does. A Sallowreacher's hand clenches and stays clenched; a Fatlander must eat something within reach; a Brinehollower coughs up cold water and something small and living; a Vigilant gains Weariness; a Cradlewracker doubles over in a contraction; an Oathener is taken at their last word.
- **Witnesses notice.** Partaking is visible to anyone who knows what to look for, and on the Table everyone knows. A Cutwright watching can make the *Read the Line* roll at +2. A Reckoner can sense it without a roll. In polite company in most lands, Partaking openly is as rude as unbuttoning your trousers at the dinner table, and in the Fast it is a confession.

[pull] I only did it the once, and it was to save a child. Then I did it twice, and it was to save a child. Then I stopped counting, and there were always children. | — Sabeth Thorne, attributed

[box: Example of Play: The Tooth]
On the Mile at Lowmark, a Called girl of sixteen is walking west along the pier toward its unrailed end, and Ressa Weir has one chance to get the rope around her before she steps off into forty feet of air above the dry seabed. The Carver says it is Hand + Shooting to throw the noose clean over her shoulders at Grim (18), because the wind is up and the girl is moving.
Ressa has Hand 3 and Shooting 0. Without help she needs 15 or more on 2d10, which she has a little more than one chance in ten of rolling. Maya says, "I Partake."
She rolls 2d10 and a red d6 together: 8, 4, and the Tooth shows 5. Total 8 + 4 + 3 + 0 + 5 = 20. Success, no Helping. Ressa gains 1 Hunger (to 4). "Your ears pop," says the Carver, "and you hear the sea in your own chest, very close, the long slow pull of it going out, and your arm moves the way the tide moves, without hurry, and the loop drops over the girl's shoulders like it was always going to. She doesn't fight. She just stops, and looks back along the rope at you, and her face is the face they all have. You know the one." The Carver does not call for a Dread check. Ressa has seen that face three hundred times. That, Maya decides, is the problem.
[/box]

### Tooth Odds

A single Tooth adds 1 to 6. It shows a 1 one time in six. A Guest who Partakes six times in a session should expect to gain about seven Hunger, and to be bitten once. A Cut 3 Guest rolling two Teeth adds 2 to 12 (average 7), gains 1 Hunger for the act, and is bitten on about one roll in three. Players who learn these odds use the Tooth more carefully. Players who forget them reach the Course by the end of a story. Both are good play.

## Hunger

**Hunger** is a track from 0 to 10. It represents how awake and active the god in your blood is right now: the rise of appetite, compulsion, and wrongness in the body. Hunger rises when you:

- Partake (+1, and +1 per Tooth showing 1).
- Suffer a Lack while Partaking (+2).
- Eat godtainted food or handle relics with Taint (see the Rim Market).
- Spend time in a place where your god is strong, such as a Hush, the Trench or the Dilation (+1 per hour or more).
- Give in to a Want (+1).
- Refuse your Sop for too long (see below; +1 per day after the third).

When Hunger reaches **10**, the god takes a step forward. **Regrowth rises by 1** and Hunger empties to 0. Describe the change. Something in the body is different now, and it does not change back.

Hunger above 10 does not carry over: a Guest at Hunger 9 who gains 3 rises one step of Regrowth and empties to 0, not to 2. The god takes its step and sits back, satisfied, for now. Let that moment breathe at the table. It is the most important thing that will happen to that character in the session.

### How Hunger Feels

Hunger is not a number to the Guest; it is a body. The Carver should describe it as it climbs, in the land's idiom:

| Hunger | How it feels (any land) |
| 0–2 | Quiet. The god is a weight in the blood, like a sleeper in the next room. |
| 3–5 | Restless. Cravings, a tell in the hands or the mouth, dreams that are not yours. |
| 6–8 | Close. The god's wants arrive in your thoughts sounding like your own. Others notice. |
| 9 | At the door. Every sense is the god's. Food, doors, water, sleep, words: everything is a lure. |

At Hunger 6 or more, the Carver may describe the land's Wants arriving more often and more temptingly, though the Want rolls themselves do not change unless a land's rules say so.

### The Sop

Each land's god can be quieted, for a while, by giving it a little of what it wants. This is the **Sop**: closing something in Sallowreach, eating in the Fatlands, bailing or submerging in Brinehollow, the dangerous blink in the Vigil, opening a door in Cradlewrack, keeping or making a vow in Oathen. Each land's chapter defines its Sop precisely.

Performing the Sop takes a few minutes to an hour. It removes **1d6 Hunger**. But the Sop is the god's own habit. Each time a Guest Sops in a single day after the first, they must make a **Caul + Resolve** roll (Routine 10, Hard 14 for the third time, and so on), or the next Sop becomes a Want they cannot easily stop.

A Guest who goes **three days without Sopping** begins to gain 1 Hunger per day as the god grows restless.

| Land | The Sop, in short | Its sting |
| Sallowreach | Close something meant to stay open, that matters to someone | The Want to Close grows a step harder for the day |
| The Fatlands | Clean the Plate: eat until it hurts, every scrap | Human flesh Sops 1d6+2; *The Second Plate* grows harder |
| Brinehollow | Wade: stand in brine past the knees through a turn of the tide, facing west | The next Calling roll is harder |
| The Vigil | The Lull: eyes closed for a count of ten, a nudger's hand in yours | An immediate blink check at Hard |
| Cradlewrack | Let It Out: open something meant to stay shut | The next closed thing aches at you |
| Oathen | Swear: an unconditional oath of Weight 2+, before a witness | The oath binds as normal |
| The Fast | None. The Unfed have no god to quiet. | Their hunger is only hunger |

*Example: Halloway Brisk is at Hunger 7 after a hard night. At the inn she orders the stew she knows is partly a person, and eats it all, every scrap, while Abate Kell watches with his arms folded. Dev rolls 1d6: 5. Hunger falls to 2. Because the meal was human flesh, the Fatlands Sop removes 1d6+2: the Carver lets Dev add the 2, and Hunger falls to 0. "You feel wonderful," says the Carver. "You feel heavy. You feel like your mother's kitchen." For the rest of the day, Halloway's Want of The Second Plate is one step harder to resist. Abate does not speak to her until morning, and in the morning he says "No, thank you" three times to the bread she offers him, and does not take it on the fourth.*

[pull] The Sop is the god's leash. Mind whose hand is on the other end. | — Purgation tract, nailed to a waystation door

## Regrowth

**Regrowth** is a track from 0 to 12. It measures how far the god has come back in you. It rarely falls. The four stages, and the **Brink** before the last, are common to every land, though each land's chapter describes what they look and feel like there:

| Regrowth | Stage | What Happens |
| 0 | — | The god is asleep. Blanks and the Unfed are here forever. |
| 1–3 | The Taste | Small wrongnesses. Dreams, cravings, a tell in the body. Gain the land's Taste Gift. |
| 4–6 | The Appetite | Compulsions. The body wants what the god wants. Gain the Appetite Gift and the first Want. |
| 7–9 | The Course | Flesh changes. You are visibly becoming something else. Gain the Course Gift and the second Want. Dread 1 to those who see you unclothed. |
| 10–11 | The Brink | The god is nearly here. Gain the Brink Gift. All Wants are Hard (14) to resist. Dread 2 to those who see your changes. |
| 12 | The Seating | The god is, in some real portion, present again, wearing you or making a place of you. The character is lost. |

**Gifts** are the god's power leaking through. They are real, useful and terrible, and they are listed in each land's chapter. A Guest has every Gift from every stage they have reached.

**Wants** are compulsions. Each Want has a **trigger** (an open door, a closed eye, food left on a plate). When a Want is triggered, the Guest must either act on it or roll **Caul + Resolve** against Routine (10) in the Appetite, Hard (14) in the Course and Brink. Acting on a Want removes nothing and adds 1 Hunger. Resisting it successfully costs nothing; failing means acting on it anyway, or suffering 2 Fray if the player and Carver agree the Guest stood firm by sheer horror.

Where a land's chapter gives its own difficulties for a Want (Sallowreach's rising ladder, the Fatlands' Grim for meat), the land's numbers take precedence. The core numbers above are the floor: no Want at the Brink is ever easier than Hard.

[box: Example of Play: A Want]
Tolly Fane is Regrowth 3, still in the Taste, but she has been awake on the road for a long time and the Carver has been counting. At a farmhouse in Cradlewrack the company finds a dead-tired shepherd's wife asleep sitting up at her kitchen table, which in Cradlewrack is merely sad. Tolly is from the Vigil. Her chapter gives the Want *To watch*, triggered by a sleeping or drowsing person, though Tolly is not yet in the Appetite and should not have it.
The Carver does not call for the Want. She does something worse: she describes it. "You find yourself standing in the doorway. You don't remember deciding to. You're counting her breaths. Eleven. Twelve. You know she won't wake. You know that's not true here, that people sleep here and get up again, but your whole body is waiting for her not to." Nothing mechanical has happened. Sunniva writes *Regrowth 4 soon* in the margin of her sheet and draws a small, shut eye.
[/box]

### Can Regrowth Be Reversed?

Almost never. A Purger's Grace can roll it back by one, once per stage. The relics of some lands can delay it. Orrum's tables can stop it entirely, in the way that death stops a fever. There are rumours of a dozen cures on the Rim Road, and every land is certain that one of them works. None of them do. The Carver may allow a single, terrible, story-driven exception in a campaign. It should cost more than the character wants to pay.

| d10 | Cures sold on the Rim Road, and what they actually do |
| 1 | A Sallowreach Closer's glove, worn to bed. You wake with your own hands gone grey to the knuckle. Nothing else. |
| 2 | Fasting with the Thin. Removes 1 Hunger a day, costs 1 Flesh a day, and the Board's bailiffs want a word. |
| 3 | Brine drunk at dawn from a Tenanted man's bucket. Taint 2 Regrowth (Dromm). Worse than nothing. |
| 4 | A Cutwright's bloodletting "to thin the line." 2 Flesh, a scar, and your Cut written in a ledger you will never see. |
| 5 | A Sayer's word, sealed in wax, spoken over you. It works, for exactly as long as the wax holds. Then everything comes at once. |
| 6 | A month in the Fast, eating only carried bread. Hunger stays quiet. The table that appears on the last night has your name. |
| 7 | Sleeping in the Vigil, under guard, for one minute. The god does not care. The Tuckers keep the fee. |
| 8 | Marrying a Blank. The Blank does not know the reason. When they find out, the Carver may award a Derangement to one of you. |
| 9 | Being opened by Dr. Halloran Quease, who wants only to look. You gain a new kind of pain, catalogued as No. 401. |
| 10 | The Purgation's full rite: emetics, bleeding, the basin. Bring It Up, on a Grace, takes one point. It is the only one on the list that ever has. |

[pull] Every land is certain that the cure is next door. Every land is next door to someone. | — Rim Road saying, continued

### The Seating of a Guest

At Regrowth 12, a Guest is **Seated**. Their god is, for a moment and in some portion, back in the world. What happens depends on the land: a Sallowreach Guest becomes a Hush; a Fatlander becomes a warm hill with, perhaps, a mouth; a Brinehollower's Tenant takes the whole house; a Vigil-born lies down and becomes a sleeper who moves; a Cradlewracker bears what they were always going to bear, or becomes the door; an Oathener becomes a Sayer, whose every word is so.

The player and the Carver play out the Seating together as a final scene. It should be awful and it should be beautiful. Afterward the character is an NPC, a place, or a power, and the player makes a new Guest. A Seated Guest's former companions should meet them again.

#### Playing the Last Scene

A Seating is announced, not sprung. When a Guest reaches Regrowth 11 the Carver should say so plainly, out of character: *the next time your Hunger fills, you are Seated.* From then on, the player knows every Partake is a step toward the door. Some players stop. Some players choose the moment.

When the Seating comes, the Carver hands the scene to the player. The player describes the last few minutes in the character's voice, and may do three things, each of which the Carver must honour:

- **A last act.** One thing the Guest does with the full power of their god, without a roll: the Hush that swallows the Cullmaster's estate, the hill that rises under the Reaping field and holds it still, the Tenant that walks out of the house of a family it will never harm. It must be within the god's nature.
- **A last word.** One sentence the Guest says to someone who loves them. The Carver will bring it back, word for word, at the worst and best possible moment later in the campaign.
- **A last line.** One thing the Seated character will *not* do, even as a god's portion, as long as the campaign lasts. *She will never let the silence reach her sister's village.* *He will never root on a road.* This is the What You Will Not Do of the Seated, and it is the only thing of the person left in the god.

Then the companions make their Dread check. A Seating is Dread 5.

[box: Seating Play Example: The Hill at Hessop's Furrow]
Halloway Brisk has been Regrowth 11 for three sessions. Dev knows it. In the fourth session, in the Fatlands, a Cullmaster's dusk shift is walking eight debtors into a Reaping field, and one of them is a child of eleven with her hands tied, and the wheat has begun to lean. Halloway has Hunger 8. Dev says, "I Partake. Both Teeth? No. One. One's enough." The Tooth shows a 1. The god bites back. Hunger 10.
The Carver puts down her pen. "Tell me."
Dev talks for four minutes. Halloway walks into the field. She takes off her boots, which she has never done on Fatlands soil in her life, because her mother told her not to, because of what happened to her mother. She stands among the debtors and the leaning wheat and she roots. "Her last act," Dev says, "is that the field stops. It doesn't take anyone. It can't. She's holding it. All of it. Every ear." The Carver agrees: that is within Ummer's nature, which is to hold and to feed.
Her last word goes to Abate Kell, who is holding her arm and trying to pull her up out of the ground while her feet go down into it like warm wax. It is: "Lack keep away." The grace before eating, said to a man who has never eaten with her.
Her last line: *she will never Reap.* Not a debtor, not a child, not a Cullmaster.
The companions roll Dread 5, Impossible (26). Abate Kell has Caul 4, Resolve 3, and spends Want for **Steady** (+2): he rolls 6 and 9 for 24, and fails, and takes 5 Fray. Ressa fails worse. The child with the tied hands is the only one who does not seem afraid. She sits down beside the new warm mound and puts her hand on it, and it is warm, and something under the soil is breathing very slowly.
The hill at Hessop's Furrow is on the Carver's map now. The Cullmasters cannot work that field. In the next campaign, the hill has a mouth.
[/box]

[box: Seating Play Example: The Sayer at the Ledger]
Not every Seating is a sacrifice. A player running a Clausewright from Tacit has reached the Brink, and the Carver warns her. She says nothing in character for two sessions, writing every line of dialogue in conditionals on scraps of paper and handing them to the other players to read aloud. In the third session her character is captured by the Bench, who know exactly what she is about to become, and who have a sentence drafted for her to speak.
She Partakes to resist the bit. The Tooth fills her Hunger. She is Seated in a cell in the Gilded Cells, a Sayer, with a gold bit already in her mouth.
Her last act is to bite through it. The Carver allows it. Her last word is to the Guest who was her Surety: "You are released." It is so, and every oath he carries for anyone falls from his body like snow. Her last line: *she will never speak a sentence the Bench drafted.* She becomes an NPC: a Sayer who will not be used. The Bench now spends a great deal of time and money trying to find wording that she did not draft, but would say anyway. The Carver has a whole campaign in that.
[/box]

## Possession: The God at the Wheel

At the Brink, the god does not wait for the Seating to drive. It reaches for the reins.

From **Regrowth 10** onward, a Guest may be **Ridden**: for a short while, the god in the blood acts through the body, and the person watches, or does not. Possession is not the Seating. The Guest comes back. But each time, a little less of them does.

### When the God Reaches

The Carver may call for a **Wheel check** whenever one of these happens to a Guest at the Brink:

- A Tooth bites back (shows a 1).
- Their Hunger reaches 8 or more.
- They fail to resist a Want with a Lack.
- They stand in a place of their god's power (a Hush, the Trench, the Dormitory, the Dilation, the Gilded Cells, a Laden hill's top).
- They Break.

The Wheel check is **Caul + Resolve** against **Grim (18)** at Regrowth 10, **Dire (22)** at Regrowth 11. The Guest may not Partake on a Wheel check; the god does not help you resist itself. A companion who is holding them, speaking their name, or reminding them of Who Waits at Home gives +2 (instead of the usual +1). An Oath on the Dead that bears directly on the moment grants its +2 Strength as normal.

- **Success:** the Guest holds the wheel. They gain 1 Fray.
- **Failure:** the god drives for a number of **rounds** (in a fight) or **minutes** (otherwise) equal to a Tooth rolled now. The Carver plays the character; the player may suggest, but does not decide. When it ends, the Guest gains 2 Fray and one mark of **Ridden**.
- **Lack:** as failure, but measured in **hours**. The Guest suffers Lost Time for it, and the Carver narrates what was done only as it is discovered.
- **Grace:** the Guest holds the wheel, gains no Fray, and for the rest of the scene knows what the god wants, clearly, in words. This is not always a comfort.

### What the God Does

A Riding god acts with all the Guest's Gifts, at the Guest's Attribute and Skill values, and may Partake freely through them (each Partake adds Hunger to the Guest, and the god does not mind if that Hunger fills). It acts according to its nature, not out of malice. It is not interested in the Guest's friends, except as things in a room.

| Land | When the god takes the wheel |
| Sallowreach | Ossel closes. Every open thing in reach: eyes, doors, mouths, wounds, conversations, candles, lives. It goes from one to the next with great tenderness. |
| The Fatlands | Ummer feeds, and is fed. It eats whatever is nearest, then roots on the nearest soil; or it feeds others, gently and without stopping, past all refusal. |
| Brinehollow | Dromm walks west. The body goes to the nearest water, of any size, and kneels at it, and begins to speak to it in the second register. |
| The Vigil | Iss lies down, and lays down. The body looks for something soft, and for anyone near it who is tired, and hums. |
| Cradlewrack | Vey opens. Doors, locks, stitches, bricked rooms, graves. If nothing is shut nearby, it opens the Guest's own oldest scar, and listens through it. |
| Oathen | Tolm witnesses. The body speaks the truth about everyone present, aloud, in plain sentences, and each sentence binds. |
| The Fast | Orrum has no portion in the Unfed. An Unfed Guest is never Ridden. But a Returned, an Unfed who has eaten, is Ridden by a god that does not know them, and that is worse. |

### Ridden

Each mark of Ridden stays. They do not count toward Fray. At **3 marks**, the god may take the wheel once per session without a Wheel check, at a moment of the Carver's choosing, for a single Tooth of minutes. At **5 marks**, the Guest's own name begins to feel like a costume; those who love them make a Dread 2 check on meeting them after an absence, because something in the face is wrong. A Purger's Bring It Up, on a Grace, may remove a mark of Ridden instead of a point of Regrowth. Nothing else does.

[pull] He came back from it the way you come back from a long walk. Tired, and pleased, and with somebody else's mud on his boots. | — Sennet Gull, of a neighbour

### Possession by Others

The Wheel rules also serve when something outside the Guest tries to take it: a Tenant in Brinehollow negotiating for an arm, a Seeming made solid in the Vigil, a Sayer's word, an Assembler's Part grafted on. The Carver sets the Difficulty by the strength of the thing (Hard for a fragment, Grim for a Tenant, Dire for anything Seated). Blanks and the Unfed gain their usual protections: +2 for a Blank, and the Unfed may spend Want for **Nothing to Hold** or **Clean Through**.

[box: Example of Play: Ridden]
Ressa Weir, three campaigns' seasons later, is Regrowth 10. In the Wreck Orchard on the Uncovered, at night, her Tooth bites. The Carver calls for a Wheel check at Grim (18). Maya rolls Caul 3 + Resolve 3 + 2 (Abate is gripping her arm and saying her name) and 2d10 showing 5 and 1: total 14. A failure.
The Carver rolls a Tooth: 4. Four minutes. "You stand up," she tells Maya, privately, as Ressa. "You walk to the nearest wreck. There's a puddle in its hold, rainwater and salt, a foot across. You kneel. You put your face very close to it, and you begin to talk to it, and it's not your voice. It's lower. It's patient. It is telling the water about Ama." Ama is Ressa's sister-in-law. She is Who Waits at Home.
Abate, at the table, says, "What's she saying about Ama?" The Carver says, "You can't make it out. Only the name, over and over, and something that sounds like a list." When it ends, Ressa gains 2 Fray and a mark of Ridden. She remembers kneeling. She does not remember what she said. Neither does anyone else. The Carver does, and has written it down.
[/box]

## Fray and Dread

The Table breaks minds as surely as bodies. **Fray** is a track from 0 to 10 measuring how far a Guest's composure has worn through.

### Dread Checks

When a Guest encounters horror, the Carver calls for a **Dread check**: roll **Caul + Resolve** against a Difficulty set by the horror's **Dread rating**:

| Dread | Difficulty | Examples |
| 1 | Routine (10) | A rotting body. A Kept man restitched at the dinner table. Toothed wheat. Being Called by a stranger's voice. |
| 2 | Hard (14) | Watching a murder. A Kept woman coming apart in your hands. The Reaping taking someone you have just met. A Tenanted stranger looking at you. |
| 3 | Grim (18) | A loved one Called, Tenanted, Reaped or Breaking. A ritual killing. Eating a person, knowingly. A face you know on a place card. |
| 4 | Dire (22) | A person becoming a place. A Hush swallowing a village. The god in your blood speaking. Kin-slaying by your own hand. |
| 5 | Impossible (26) | A Seating. A god's face. The Host turning to look at you. Knowing that what you did changed nothing. |

- **Success:** gain 1 Fray (or none, on a Grace). You hold.
- **Failure:** gain Fray equal to the Dread rating. On a Lack, add 2 more, and the Guest freezes, flees, screams, vomits or laughs, at the Carver's choice, for a moment that matters.

A Guest only checks once against the same source in a scene, and the Carver may rule that a horror seen too often stops counting (a Seamer does not check for corpses). Guests may Partake on Dread checks. The god does not mind if you are frightened, and sometimes it is the god that makes you brave.

[pull] Fear is the body being honest about the room it is in. | — Dr. Halloran Quease, *A Catalogue of Pains*, entry 3

*Example: In the Lofts of Lastgate, an aisle of shelved Set-Aside turn their wrapped heads toward Tolly Fane and whisper* company. *Dread 3, Grim (18). Tolly has Caul 3, Resolve 2. Sunniva Partakes: 2d10 show 7 and 2, the Tooth 6, total 20. She holds, and gains 1 Fray and 1 Hunger. "Your pupils go wide," says the Carver, "and for a heartbeat you see the whole aisle from very high up, every bundle, every label, the way a sleeper would see it. It's peaceful. It's the most peaceful thing you've ever seen. That's why you aren't afraid." Sunniva marks the Hunger and looks unhappy about the peace.*

### Fray Effects

- **Fray 0–4:** steady.
- **Fray 5–7:** **Rattled.** −1 to all Eye and Caul rolls. Sleep is thin, appetite strange, and you jump at doors.
- **Fray 8–9:** **Fraying.** −2 to all rolls except to flee, hide or fight. You see things at the edge of the lamp. You are short with the people you love.
- **Fray 10:** **Break.**

### Breaking

When Fray reaches 10, the Guest **Breaks**. For the rest of the scene they are not in control of what they do: the player describes the Break with the Carver (running into the night, attacking the nearest person, curling under a table, laughing until they choke, saying every secret they have). Afterward, the Guest gains a **Derangement** and Fray drops to 3.

Roll on the tables below or choose one that fits what broke them. Derangements are permanent unless treated. Each has a **trigger** and an **effect**; when triggered, roll **Caul + Resolve** (Hard 14) to master it for the scene.

**Which table?** Roll on **Table One: The Common Madnesses** for a Guest's first Break, or when the horror that broke them was Dread 3 or less. Roll on **Table Two: The Deep Madnesses** for a second or later Break, or when the horror was Dread 4 or 5, or when the Guest Broke at the Brink. The Carver and player may always choose instead of rolling; the best Derangement is the one that grows out of what happened.

[box: Example of Play: A Break]
Abate Kell has been at Fray 7 since the hill at Hessop's Furrow. In the next session, at a crossroads in the Fatlands, he finds one of Orrum's tables laid in the ditch, warm, with eight chairs and a card at every place. He takes his axe to it, which is his trade. The Carver tells him that the card at the head of the table, when it falls into the mud, says *Halloway Brisk*, in a fair round hand, and that there is a second card under it, still in its envelope, with his own name.
Dread 3, Grim (18). Ade rolls 2 and 2, a Lack, total 4 + 7 = 11. Fail: +3 Fray, +2 for the Lack. Fray 12, which is a Break.
Ade and the Carver agree on the Break together. Abate does not run. He sits down in the mud beside the smashed table, with his back to it, with his back carefully to every chair, and says the Decline, *no thank you, no thank you, no thank you*, for the rest of the scene, faster and faster, and will not let anyone touch him. Ressa sits beside him until it gets dark. Because his second Break comes from a Dread 3 horror, the Carver could use Table One, but this is Abate's second Break, so they roll on Table Two: 14, **The Ledger**. That fits. From now on he keeps an account.
[/box]

#### The Deep Madnesses Described

**1. The Rot Within.** The Dead Man's deeper cousin. You are not only dead; you are *finished*, and everyone can smell it, and they are too polite to say. You refuse food (what would a corpse do with it?), you will not wash (the water would only loosen things), and you apologise to everyone who comes near you for the smell. In Sallowreach you may petition for your own Setting Aside. When triggered, you act as a corpse would, for the scene: −2 to every roll requiring will to live. You gain 1 Starving level each day you are triggered at a meal.

**2. The Changeling House.** The Impostors, spread through a household. Not one loved one but *all* of them have been replaced, the whole house, the whole street; only you remember the real ones, and you are being watched to see if you notice. When triggered you cannot accept food, care or comfort from anyone you knew before your Break. Who Waits at Home cannot remove your Fray. You may try to find "the real ones," and the Carver may decide where you look.

**3. The Borrowed Skin.** Not My Hand, grown to the whole body. You are wearing someone: the god, a dead sibling, the person you killed. The body is a coat, and it does not fit, and it is beginning to smell of its owner. When triggered you pick and pull at the seams of it (1 damage, no Armor) and suffer −2 to all Hand and Lung rolls until the scene ends. A Seamer who stitches you can make you feel it fits, for a week (Hard 14).

**4. The Other Hours.** Lost Time with a pattern. The hours you lose are always the same hours, and in them you go somewhere and do something, regularly, as if keeping an appointment. You find receipts in your own hand, a key you do not own, a lock of hair. The Carver keeps a secret ledger of the other hours; each session, roll a d6, and on a 1 something you did in them arrives to collect.

**5. The Second Guest.** There are two of you. The other one has a name (the player and Carver choose it), opinions, a voice that comes out of your mouth, and one Want of its own. It is not the god; it may be worse, because it is entirely human. When triggered, roll Caul + Resolve (Hard 14) or the other takes the scene, with your skills and none of your loyalties. It will tell your companions things you never told them. Some of them will be true.

**6. The Bright Fever.** Mania, and catching. You are luminous, tireless, certain, funny, generous, beloved; you give away your lacks, you start three projects, you make speeches at waystations, and people follow you. When triggered you gain +2 to Persuade and −2 to everything requiring caution, and you cannot rest. Everyone who spends a scene with you while triggered makes a Dread 1 check; those who fail take the Fever for a day. The crash, when the scene ends, costs you 2 Fray.

**7. The Cold Plate.** The loss of empathy, all at once, like a tooth pulled. Other people's pain is interesting to you, the way a lock is interesting. When triggered you may prolong it, and you want to; resisting costs Caul + Resolve (Hard 14). You no longer take Dread from harm done to others, and you gain +2 to Intimidate. You may not gain Fray reduction from rites, from Who Waits at Home, or from any comfort. The only cure that has ever worked is someone loving you anyway, for a year. It is said that Jack Tenterhook has this, and that he is waiting.

**8. The Fold.** Your mind has been captured, or offered itself. Some doctrine, some congregation, some voice (the Bedded, the Deepening, the Second Helping, the Finishers, the Purgation, a Celebrant of your own company) has the answer, and you have stopped asking. When triggered you must defend the doctrine and its leader. You begin play with Hold 3 to that group (see *Cult Control*). It does not have to be a cult. It only has to be certain.

**9. The Surgeon's Itch.** Bodies are puzzles and they are all slightly wrong. You want to open them and put them right. When triggered by a wound you must tend it, even an enemy's, even in a fight; when triggered by a closed body, a sleeping one, a dead one, you must resist (Hard 14) taking out your knife to look. You gain +1 Stitching. Seamers have a word for this, and they do not employ those who have it.

**10. The Gravewalker.** The dead are better company. You go to them: to the burial grounds of the lands that still bury, to the Lofts, to the Sump's edge, to the wake-tables of the Fatlands after the guests have gone. You sit with the dead, talk to them, sleep among them, and the Carver may decide that one night a sexton or a Loft-Warden finds you somewhere you should not be, in a state that should not be described, and the town decides what it thinks you did. Dread 2 for companions who find you. When triggered, resist or go to the nearest dead.

**11. The Chorus.** The dead talk to you, all of them, and not only the ones you knew: everyone who ever died within a mile, in the order they died, giving their names. In Sallowreach this is merely true. Elsewhere, when triggered, you cannot hear living voices over them for the scene (−4 to hearing-based Search and to understanding speech). Sometimes, very rarely, one of them tells you something you could not have known.

**12. The Unborn.** You have not been born yet. Everything is a rehearsal, a dream in the womb, and the real beginning is coming. You do not fear death because it cannot touch what has not begun. When triggered you gain +2 to Dread checks and suffer −2 to every roll that requires caring about consequences. In Cradlewrack the Attendance takes a professional interest in you. You will try to find the place where you are to be born.

**13. The Throne.** You are chosen. Your god, or the Table, or the dead, have marked you to rule, or to save, or to end. When triggered you will not take orders, accept advice, or admit fault, and you gain +2 to Intimidate and Tongue rolls to command. You may begin to believe your Gifts are greater than they are. The Carver may let you, once, and let you find out.

**14. The Ledger.** You keep accounts. Every kindness done to you is a debt; every debt must be repaid exactly, no more, no less, at once; every loss must be balanced by a loss. When triggered by an exchange you must settle it, and you cannot accept gifts at all without a Caul + Resolve (Hard 14) roll. You remember every debt anyone owes you. You begin to take payment from those who do not settle.

**15. Under the Skin.** Something is living under your skin: grubs, wheat-teeth, the sea, a Part looking for its body. You feel it move. When triggered you must scratch and dig at it (1 damage, ignores Armor; a Lack opens a Ripping wound) and suffer −2 to Hand rolls for the scene. On the Table it is sometimes true, and a Seamer who looks may find something. That is the cruelty of it: you can never be sure it is madness.

**16. The Swallowed Name.** You have lost your name. You know it is on your sheet, you know people call you by it, but it is not yours; it belongs to someone dead, or to the god, and to say it aloud would be a lie. In Oathen this is dangerous. When triggered you cannot give your name, sign it, or swear by it, and oaths sworn by you count one Weight lower (to a minimum of 1) because the god is not sure who swore them.

**17. The Guest Who Came Home.** The one you lost has come back. Not as a Kept or a Tenant: alive, as a stranger, wearing a stranger's face, and only you can see it. You will follow them, protect them, give them everything you have, and grieve all over again when they do not know you. When triggered you cannot act against the stranger, and you gain 1 Fray each time they refuse you.

**18. The Mirror at Table.** Your reflection is late. It moves a moment after you do, or does not move, or is eating. When triggered you must cover or break the reflecting surface, or watch it and take 1 Fray. Over time, the reflection seems to be getting closer to the glass. In the Vigil you may find others who see their reflections laying tables.

**19. The Weight of Hands.** Your hands want to do the worst thing they can. Holding a knife, you imagine the throat of the person beside you; holding a child, you imagine the drop. You will never do it, and you cannot stop imagining it, and you are sure that one day you will. When triggered you must put down whatever you hold, or roll Caul + Resolve (Hard 14) to keep it, and you gain 1 Fray either way. This madness is a torment of the good. It never makes anyone act. Tell the player so.

**20. The Hollow Room.** Despair without the Pull. Nothing calls you; nothing is there to call. Food tastes of nothing, faces are furniture, the god in your blood is the only thing in you that wants anything. When triggered you cannot spend Portions, gain no Fray reduction from rest, and may not take the Last Meal bonus. You can still be reached by someone who sits with you. The Carver should make that worth doing.

[pull] She didn't go mad. She went somewhere very quiet and very reasonable and then she sent back word that we were all mad, and she was polite about it. | — Wreckwife, to the Netwatch

### Mastering and Treating Derangements

When a Derangement triggers, the Guest rolls **Caul + Resolve** (Hard 14) to master it for the scene. A Guest may Partake on this roll. A Celebrant, Seamer or friend who has spent at least one downtime period tending the Guest gives +2. A Guest who masters the same Derangement three sessions running may, with a deed the Carver accepts, lower its difficulty to Routine (10) for good. They are not cured. They are only used to it.

**Derangements** can be treated with months of care, a Celebrant or Seamer who understands the mind, and 10 Portions. Some never go. Some of them, on the Table, are not wrong.

### Contagion

Madness on the Table is catching. It always has been: the Seeming of the Vigil is only the most organized form of it. When a Guest or NPC **Breaks** in the presence of others, every witness makes a **Dread 2** check. Anyone who fails and is at Fray 5 or more also takes the same Derangement, in a shared form: they see what the Broken one saw, believe what they believe, count the same count. The Carver should make this feel like a cold draft moving through a room.

As the gods regrow, contagion grows stronger. In a place where a Seating has occurred, or near a Brink-stage noble, witnesses check at Dread 3.

A shared Derangement is weaker than the original: its mastering roll is Routine (10), and it fades after a week unless it triggers in that week, in which case it is permanent. Two people who share a Derangement trigger each other: when one is triggered, the other must roll too. This is how households go mad together.

### Despair

Of all the Derangements, **The Pull** is the most dangerous, and the Table is full of people who have answered it: the Finishers singing their way into the Hushes, the Called walking into the surf, the sleepless of the Vigil lying down on purpose, the starving of the Fatlands crossing into the Fast to find a table where they can finally feel full.

When a Guest with the Pull is triggered and fails to master it, they move toward the god's place and invitation. They can be stopped by their companions (a roll, a grip, a rope, a word, a reason). If they reach it and nothing stops them, the player and the Carver decide together what happens, out loud, with the table's consent. This book never describes such an ending as a method or a release. It describes it as what it is on the Table: a loss that lands on everyone who loved them, and a little more of the god in the world. A Guest's companions who witness it gain Fray as a Dread 4 horror, and the Carver should give the grief room.

[box: Holding On]
Stopping someone who is answering the Pull is never a single roll. It is a scene. The companion must reach them (a chase, if need be), hold them (Gut + Brawl or Gut + Labor, opposed by the Pulled one's Lung + Athletics), and then give them a reason: a Tongue + Persuade roll against Hard (14), with +2 if the reason is Who Waits at Home, the Last Meal, or an Oath on the Dead the Pulled one has not yet kept. Succeed, and the Pull lets go for the night. Fail, and they must be held until dawn, and both gain 1 Fray for every hour of it. Nobody who sits up through that night with someone they love is the same afterward. Give them Portions for it.
[/box]

### Recovering from Fray

- **A night of real rest**, safe, warm and fed (and awake, in the Vigil): remove 1 Fray.
- **A rite** led by a Celebrant or performed sincerely: remove 2 Fray.
- **A vice**: drink, rouse, poppy, brine tonic, gambling, sex (off-page), violence. Remove 1d6 Fray, but roll Gut + Endure (Hard 14) or develop a habit. Habits work like Wants.
- **Time with Who Waits at Home**: remove all Fray, once per story, if they are still themselves.
- **Portions**: 2 Portions removes 3 Fray between sessions.

## Madness in the Community

A person Breaks in a night. A village Breaks over a season, and nobody in it can say when it began. As the gods regrow, whole communities are wearing through: the Netwatch villages where the morning count goes unanswered on three houses in a row, the Fatlands hamlets that start drawing lots for the dusk shift among themselves, the streets of the Slope where a yawn goes through a tenement like fire. This section lets the Carver track a community's collective Fray and play out what happens when it fails.

### The Pall

A community (a village, a street, a waystation, a ship's crew on the Uncovered, a regiment) has a **Pall** from 0 to 20. It is the community's Fray: how much horror it has absorbed and not healed. It also has a **Steadiness** from 0 to 5, like Threat, measuring what holds it together.

| Steadiness | What holds them |
| 0 | Nothing: a refugee camp, a Breakdowns tenement, a crew stranded on the Uncovered |
| 1 | A little: a market, a well, a shared trade, a waystation's routine |
| 2 | A village with a working faith, a Celebrant, a midwife, and the morning count |
| 3 | A close village with old rites, kin on every street, and a reason to stay |
| 4 | A fortress of habit: the Hanging Abbey, the Attendance's lying-in houses |
| 5 | Almost nothing on the Table. Wanting, on its best day. |

**The Pall rises** when the community suffers:

| Event | Pall |
| A Break witnessed in public | +1 |
| A death by murder, Reaping, Calling, or the Counted bed | +1 |
| A public execution or ritual killing | +2 |
| A loss to the Pull: someone who answered it | +2 |
| A preacher of despair or ecstasy working the streets for a week | +1 |
| A Seating within a day's walk | +3 |
| A week of famine (half rations for most) | +1 |
| The community discovers it has been lied to by those it trusted | +2 |

**The Pall falls** when the community heals: a rite of mourning for the named dead (−1d3), a feast (−1, if the food is safe), a rescue the village can see (−2), a murderer exposed and dealt with justly (−2), a cult's leader driven out (−2), the Guests sitting up with the grieving for a week (−1), a season with no losses (−1).

### The Turn

Once each week of game time (or once per session, if the Guests are passing through), the Carver rolls for the community: **2d10 + Steadiness** against **10 + the Pall**.

- **Success:** the community holds. The Pall falls by 1.
- **Failure:** the Pall rises by 1, and the Carver chooses or rolls an event from the community's current band.
- **Lack:** the Pall rises by 2, and the worst event in the band happens.
- **Grace:** the Pall falls by 2, and the Carver describes an act of ordinary decency that the Guests witness: a widow feeding her neighbour's children, a Netwatch man who sits down on the pier beside a Called girl and simply talks to her until the tide turns.

### The Bands of the Pall

| Pall | Band | What the community is like |
| 0–4 | Whole | Grief is grieved. Dread checks in town are as normal. |
| 5–9 | Uneasy | Rumour, locked doors, quarrels. Contagion checks in town are made at Fray 3 or more instead of 5. |
| 10–14 | Feverish | Shared Seemings, witch-hunts, scapegoats. Contagion at Dread 3. Cults gain +2 to Hold. |
| 15–19 | Broken | Clusters of loss. The Pull goes through the streets. Each Turn, one event, even on a success. |
| 20 | Lost | The community belongs to the god. It empties, or Seats, or simply stops. |

| d10 | Uneasy events | Feverish events |
| 1 | Doors barred in daylight; neighbours stop calling names through walls | A shared Seeming: the whole street saw a tall figure laying a table in the square |
| 2 | A family moves its Kept or its old ones into the cellar, and will not say why | A scapegoat is chosen: a Blank, a foreigner, a Guest |
| 3 | A child's counting-game changes its words | A preacher gathers thirty in a barn, and they come out singing |
| 4 | Someone reports a neighbour to the Wakers, the Netwatch, the Board | A house is burned with a family in it, "for their own good" |
| 5 | The Celebrant stops holding rites; he says he does not know who is listening | Everyone in a street shares a Derangement for a week |
| 6 | A well is declared tainted, and argued over | The village begins drawing lots for something, and will not say what |
| 7 | Bread is hoarded | A mob forms at dusk with no leader and no object |
| 8 | A child goes missing and is found, and will not say where | The Guests' lodging is marked with a sign in chalk |
| 9 | Two old friends come to blows over nothing | A public confession, and a punishment invented on the spot |
| 10 | The Guests are asked to leave, politely | The village elder announces that the god has spoken to him |

### When a Community Breaks

At **Broken**, the community is losing people to the god faster than it can mourn them. This is where the Table's worst tragedies happen: the walks into the sea at Lowmark in 118 A.G., the Finishers' great pilgrimage of 590, the Slope tenement that lay down in a single afternoon. Run these as tragedy. The camera stays on the people left behind. The Carver never describes how anyone is lost, only the shape of the absence.

| d10 | Broken events |
| 1 | The morning count goes unanswered on four houses. Their doors are open, their beds made. Their bail-buckets sit neatly on the steps, rinsed. |
| 2 | A family of five answers the Pull together, holding hands, singing a nursery song. One child let go of the hand, and came back, and has not spoken since. |
| 3 | The village Celebrant leads a procession toward the god's place. Half the village follows. The other half barricades the road, and some of them are holding their own children back by the coat. |
| 4 | The Seamer, the midwife, the healer, the one whose job was to keep people alive, is gone. A note in a careful hand asks someone to feed the cat. |
| 5 | The graves, wake-tables, Lofts or niches are opened in the night, and the village's dead are taken out and sat at a long table in the square, dressed for dinner. Nobody will say who did it. Everybody's hands are dirty. |
| 6 | A mother insists all her children are impostors. Three neighbours agree with her about their own. The Guests are asked to take the children away. |
| 7 | Someone the Guests have spoken to, liked, perhaps helped, is gone in the night. They left the Guests a gift: something small, and kind, and terrible. |
| 8 | The surviving elders hold a council and vote, solemnly, by the old law, to offer the god what it wants. The vote is not unanimous. The losers are not allowed to leave. |
| 9 | A rumour runs that the Guests can stop it. A crowd gathers at their door, begging. Each one has a name to give them. |
| 10 | Nothing happens. For a week, nothing at all. People are polite. Then a Cutwright arrives with a ledger, and the Guests learn the Pall has spread to the next village down the road. |

Each Broken event adds the listed horror's Dread check for the Guests, usually Dread 3, and a Dread 4 if someone they know is among the lost.

### Lost

At Pall 20, the community is the god's. The Carver decides what that means in the land: in Sallowreach, a new Hush opens where the village was, and the village is very quiet; in the Fatlands, the fields close over it in a single Carving and the houses are found next spring in the middle of a new wood of bleeding trees; in Brinehollow, the village walks west on one night, and on that night the sea, far off, rises an inch; in the Vigil, a street of beds; in Cradlewrack, a crowning opens under the square; in Oathen, a gorge goes silent forever; in the Fast, a table, very long, with everyone at it, smiling. A Lost community cannot be saved. Only its survivors, if any, can be.

[box: Example of Play: The Pall at Gannet Reach]
Gannet Reach is a Netwatch village of ninety souls on the Brinehollow shore, Steadiness 2 (a chapel, a midwife, the morning count). The company arrives at Pall 6: three Called this season and a public Break at the fish market. Uneasy.
Week one: the Carver rolls 2d10 + 2 against 16: total 13. Failure. Pall 7. She rolls an Uneasy event: 4, *someone reports a neighbour.* An old man denounces his daughter-in-law to the Netwatch as a Tenanted, because she has been kind to him lately. Ressa is Netwatch. She is asked to take the daughter-in-law away.
Ressa refuses. She sits with the family for an evening, and Halloway cooks for them. The Carver gives them −1 Pall for the feast: back to 6.
Week two: the Deepening arrives, Mother Limpet's lanterns on the shingle at night, singing. +1 for the preacher. The Turn: 2d10 + 2 = 18 against 17. Success, −1. Pall 6. The Carver notes that Limpet's people have Hold 1 on four families.
Week three: the Turn comes up 1 and 1. Licked Clean. A Lack. Pall rises by 2 to 8, and the worst Uneasy event happens: the Guests are asked to leave, politely, by the same old man, who weeps as he asks. If they go, Gannet Reach will be Feverish by Tablenight. If they stay, they are going to have to go down to the shingle and talk to Mother Limpet.
[/box]

[pull] You don't hear a village go. You hear it stop calling names through the wall, one house at a time, until it's quiet enough to hear the sea. | — Lieutenant Sable Reef, Netwatch

## Cult Control and Gaslighting

The Table is full of people who will tell you what is happening to you. The Bedded say the room is home. The Second Helping says being eaten is being loved. The Deepening walks you into the sea with a lantern and a hymn. The Finishers, the Barren Order, the Purgation, the Second Table, a kind old man at a waystation who has a theory: all of them want your mind, and most of them want it kindly. These rules cover how a mind is taken, slowly, by people.

### Hold

A group or person working on a character builds **Hold** over them, from 0 to 5. Hold is tracked separately for each group. It is built through **methods**, and each method is a scene:

- **The Warm Table.** Food, shelter, praise, belonging. Most effective on the hungry and the lonely.
- **The Confession.** The target is drawn to tell their secrets; the group keeps them.
- **The Severing.** The target is kept from Who Waits at Home, from companions, from anyone who knew them before.
- **The Long Night.** Exhaustion, fasting, ritual, repetition, until thought goes soft.
- **The Answer.** The doctrine explains the target's suffering. It always does.
- **The Debt.** A gift, a rescue, a cure that half works. Now they owe.

At the end of each such scene, the recruiter rolls **Tongue + Persuade** (or Threat) opposed by the target's **Caul + Resolve**. If the recruiter wins, Hold rises by 1 (by 2 on a Grace). If the target wins with a Grace, Hold falls by 1. The same method cannot raise Hold twice in a row; a skilled recruiter varies them.

| Circumstance | Modifier to the recruiter |
| Target is Rattled (Fray 5+) / Fraying (8+) | +1 / +2 |
| Target is Starving, Hollow or worse, or Weary 5+ | +2 |
| Target is cut off from Who Waits at Home and companions | +2 |
| Target has The Devotion, The Gaslight or The Fold | +2 |
| Community Pall is Feverish or worse | +2 |
| The doctrine matches the target's own god or grief | +2 |
| Target has *No Thank You* (Knack) | −2 |

| Hold | What it means | To act against the group |
| 1 | Sympathy. The target defends them in argument. | No roll |
| 2 | Belonging. The target feels warmer among them than anywhere. | Routine (10) |
| 3 | Obedience in small things. The target reports on their friends. | Hard (14) |
| 4 | Obedience in large things. The target will cut off kin and harm outsiders. | Grim (18) |
| 5 | Captured. The target is theirs. | The character becomes an NPC |

A Guest at **Hold 5** is captured: the player hands the character to the Carver until they are brought back, and may make a temporary Guest in the meantime. The captured character gains **The Fold** if they did not have it.

### Bringing Someone Back

Hold falls when the target sees the group plainly: a leader caught in a lie (−1), a promise of the doctrine broken in front of them (−1), the death or harm of someone the group sent to the god (−2), Who Waits at Home reaching them in person (−1, and +2 to the target's next roll). An Oath on the Dead that the group is making the target break gives the target +2 on every roll against them.

Bringing back a captured Guest is a long task: Hold × 2 Helpings needed, one roll per day of the companions' Tongue + Persuade (or Rites, or Stitching, if it is done by care rather than argument) against the group's Hold-difficulty from the table. Every day of it costs the captured Guest 1 Fray, because coming back is tearing something out. A Lack sends the target back to the group for a night, and the group knows who came for them.

[pull] Nobody ever joined us. They came to supper, that's all, and then they came again. | — Brother Glut of the Second Helping, to a Board inquiry

### Gaslighting: Doubt

Sometimes the attack is not a doctrine but a memory. *You never saw that. You were asleep. You always say that. Nobody's door was open. Your brother walked in; we all saw him; you weren't there.* On the Table, where the Vigil records hallucinations as news, where blinks steal seconds and Lost Time steals hours, gaslighting is easy and terribly effective.

When someone tells a character that their memory of something is false, the liar rolls **Tongue + Deceive** opposed by the target's **Eye + Reckoning** (the same roll the Vigil uses to trust a memory). If the liar wins, the target gains 1 **Doubt** about that memory. At **3 Doubt**, the target accepts the false version and gains 1 Fray. A character with **The Gaslight** Derangement suffers −4 on these rolls, as that Derangement says, and starts at 1 Doubt on any memory they are contradicted about.

Evidence breaks Doubt: a ledger, a witness who has no reason to lie, the body itself. A companion who confirms the memory gives +2. The Vigilant at Weariness 5 or more, any character who has had Lost Time or a blink in the scene in question, and any character Ridden in it, suffer −2, because they know that they might be wrong.

[box: Carver's Advice: Gaslighting Guests, Not Players]
These rules are for doubting a *character's* memory, not the player's. Never tell a player that something they heard at the table did not happen. Instead, tell them openly: *the innkeeper says the door was shut all night; you have one Doubt.* Let the player decide how the Guest feels. The horror comes from watching your own character start to give in, not from being lied to by your friend across the table. Guests may gaslight each other only with both players' consent, agreed out of character, before the scene.
[/box]

[box: Example of Play: The Bedded]
Tolly Fane, Regrowth 4, Weariness 6, has been lodging for three nights in a house on the Slope where Sister Vesper Null's Bedded hold their quiet meetings. The Carver has run three scenes: a warm room where the Bedded let Tolly sit on a floor that does not slope (the Warm Table); an evening when a girl Tolly's age told her, gently, that her mother in the Dormitory is happy now (the Answer); a night when Tolly blinked, and a Bedded elder said, kindly, that she had been gone for almost a minute and that she had smiled (the Long Night).
Each time, the recruiter (Threat 3, +2 Weariness, +1 Rattled, +2 for a doctrine that matches her god) rolled against Tolly's Caul 3 + Resolve 2. Tolly lost twice. Hold 2. Then the elder told her she had not blinked at all the night before: she had slept for a full count of ten, and come back, which nobody does. Sunniva rolled Eye + Reckoning, at −2 for the blink: she lost. One Doubt.
Ressa comes to the house the next morning and says: "You were asleep for one breath and Abate pinched you. I was there." That is a companion confirming the memory; +2 on the next roll, and the Carver lets the Doubt stand at 1. But Sunniva says, quietly, that Tolly wants to go back to the meeting that night. Just to see. The Carver writes *Hold 2, Doubt 1*, and says nothing at all.
[/box]

## Investigation

Much of play on the Table is finding out: who took the hand, where the Called go, what the Cullmasters' list is for, why the Hushes follow the Hand-lines. These rules make investigation fast, fair and unpleasant.

### Clues Are Not Rolled For

The clue that keeps the story moving is never behind a roll. If a Guest looks in the right place with a sensible approach, they find it. Rolls decide **how much more** they find, **how fast**, and **what it costs**. A failed search does not hide the clue; it means the clue is found late, or loudly, or by the wrong person first, or that finding it costs Fray, Hunger, or a night. The Carver should plan each mystery as a chain of **leads**, each pointing to at least two others, so that no single failure stops the investigation.

### The Senses of the Table

Every skill can investigate. Each finds different things.

| Skill | Finds |
| Eye + Search | Physical traces: marks, objects, the door that was opened from the inside |
| Hand + Stitching | What a body says: how it died, what was done to it, what it ate, how long ago |
| Eye + Lore | History, doctrine, whose crest, which bloodline, what the old carving shows |
| Eye + Reckoning | Ledgers, tallies, clocks, the arithmetic that someone wanted hidden |
| Caul + Instinct | People: who is lying, who is afraid, who already knew |
| Caul + Godsign | Godsign traces: a closing, an opening, a Tenant's passing, a binding |
| Eye + Feast | Taste and smell: what meat, which poison, whose kitchen |
| Tongue + Clause | Documents: what the contract really says, the Exit buried in an oath |

Each Helping on an investigation roll buys one of: **another clue**, **the meaning of a clue**, **time** (half as long), or **discretion** (nobody knows you found it).

### The Bill

Every investigation has a **Bill**: a clock, from 4 to 8 boxes, that fills as the Guests stumble. A box fills on every failed investigation roll, on every day that passes, and on every Lack (two boxes). When the Bill is paid in full, the thing being investigated happens: the next blink-murder, the dusk shift goes out, the Second Table dines, the witness is Set Aside. The Guests can see the Bill. The Carver should draw it.

### Each Land Its Witness

Every land offers investigators a way of knowing that would be impossible anywhere else, and each comes at a price.

- **Sallowreach.** Ask the victim. The dead are Kept, and can talk, if their jaw is on. They may lie, and they may not know who did it, and a Kept murder victim is Dread 2 to interview.
- **The Fatlands.** Taste it. A Fatlander with *Taste the Name* learns the eater's secrets from a mouthful of flesh, at the cost of Hunger and the horror of witnesses.
- **Brinehollow.** The indoor tide returns what it takes. Search a cellar at the ebb, and the sea may give back a ring, a tooth, a letter. Ask a Tenanted judge, who will answer truthfully and in two voices.
- **The Vigil.** The Seeming is evidence. If a street saw it, it is on the record, and the Seemer's ledgers can be read. Whether it happened is a separate question.
- **Cradlewrack.** The Tally records every birth of every kind. If something was born, it is written down, and the Tallyman knows the arithmetic does not close.
- **Oathen.** Have the suspect repeat their promise aloud, and watch. Judge Thane Urrow needs no other evidence. Neither will you.
- **The Fast.** Read the place cards. The Host knows every name, and spells them correctly.

[box: Example of Play: The Missing Hands of Clench Row]
In the Sinks of Lastgate, four Kept on Clench Row have woken without their hands. The Carver sets a Bill of 6: when it fills, Marrow Jack sells the hands to someone who is collecting a particular set. Her leads: the stumps (Stitching), the victims' testimony (Instinct), a drop of cedar oil on the sill (Search), and a fence's ledger in the Lower Pilings (Reckoning).
Halloway examines the stumps: Hand 4 + Stitching 1, Hard (14). She rolls 15. Success: they were taken with a fine saw, not a cleaver, by someone who knew where the wrist bones part. Ressa interviews the first victim, an old Kept woman named Hessa Coombe, Instinct against Routine (10) because she wants to talk: 19, two Helpings. Ressa learns that Hessa can feel her missing hand, right now, being washed, gently, in warm water; and (the second Helping) that the water smells of cedar and salt. The Carver lets the Helpings buy *meaning*: the cedar is Lastgate perfume, which marks the buyer as rich; the salt is Brinehollow brine, which the rich of the Vigil drink in their rouse.
Tolly searches the sill: 11, failure. One box of the Bill fills. She finds the drop of cedar oil anyway (it is the clue that moves the story), but she finds it late, after a neighbour has seen her and gone to tell someone. Four boxes left, and somebody in the Lower Pilings now knows the company's faces.
[/box]

[pull] In Lastgate the murdered testify at their own inquests. It has not raised the conviction rate. They are as bad at noticing things as the rest of us. | — Justice Ever Lathe, through his clerk

## Chases

When someone runs and someone follows, use a **chase**. A chase is fast, a series of opposed rolls around a track of **Lead**.

### The Lead

The chase begins at a **Lead of 2** (a close chase: the hunter saw them go) or **3** (a head start). Each round, the quarry and the hunter each roll **Lung + Athletics** on foot, **Lung + Wayfaring** mounted, on a cart, or across hard country, or **Lung + Stealth** if the quarry is trying to vanish rather than outrun. Whoever wins moves the Lead by 1 in their favour, plus 1 per Helping.

- **Lead 0:** caught. The hunter is close enough to grab, strike or tackle. A fight begins, or the quarry surrenders.
- **Lead 6:** lost. The quarry is gone, hidden, or out of reach; the hunter must find a new trail, which is an investigation.

A group chasing or fleeing rolls with its slowest member, unless it leaves them behind. That choice is the most important one in most chases, and it should be put to the players out loud.

### Breath

A body can run hard for a number of rounds equal to **Gut + Endure**. After that, each round requires a **Gut + Endure** roll at Hard (14) or the runner suffers −2 to chase rolls, cumulative. Bloodied, Starving and Weary characters run out of breath at half the count. The Kept, the Tenanted and the Seated never tire. That should be terrifying.

### Complications

Each round, the side that lost the previous roll may describe a **complication**, or the Carver rolls on the table below. A complication is a single roll for whoever reaches it: success keeps the pace, failure costs 1 Lead (and a Lack costs 2 and a fall, a wound, or a dropped thing). The Carver should always describe the place.

| d10 | Chase complications |
| 1 | A crowd: a market, a procession, a wake. Tongue + Intimidate to clear it, or Lung + Athletics to thread it. |
| 2 | A climb: a wet piling, a cliff stair, a slope of loose red clay. Lung + Athletics, Hard (14). |
| 3 | A door, barred. Gut + Labor to break it (Grim 18), or Hand + Filch to open it (Hard 14). In Cradlewrack it is already open. |
| 4 | A drop: a cut-away pier, a crowning, a sunken storey. Lung + Athletics, Hard (14), or take 1d6 and lose 2 Lead. |
| 5 | A hiding place: the quarry may switch to Stealth, opposed by the hunter's Eye + Search. |
| 6 | Something in the way that should not be: a Kept beggar, a laid table, a Counted sleepwalker. Dread check, Dread 1–2. |
| 7 | Mud, fen, warm soil that gives underfoot. Lung + Wayfaring, Hard (14). A Fatlander standing still may root. |
| 8 | The hue and cry: bystanders take a side. The side with the better reputation here gains 1 Lead. |
| 9 | A narrow way: a plank, a ledge, a single-file canyon path. Only one can pass at a time; the quarry may block it behind them. |
| 10 | The land itself: a Black Noon, a pressure day, a pang, a Yawn, an echo, the smell of bread on the wind. The Carver decides who it helps. |

[box: Example of Play: A Chase on the Mile]
A Rim Road Company clerk the company needs to question bolts from them on the Mile at Lowmark at dusk. Lead 2. The clerk is Threat 1; Tolly (Lung 3, Athletics 2) gives chase, because she is fastest.
Round one: Tolly 15, clerk 9. Tolly wins by 6, one Helping: the Lead falls by 2, from 2 to 0. Caught. Sunniva wants him held, so the Carver calls for a grapple, Hand + Brawl against the clerk's Guard of 11; Tolly rolls 10 and misses by one. She gets a fistful of his coat, and he tears out of it and keeps running. The Carver puts the Lead back to 1 and hands Sunniva the coat. There is a ledger in the inside pocket.
Round two: the clerk lost, so the Carver rolls a complication: 6, something in the way. A procession of the Deepening is walking down the pier toward the end of the Mile with lanterns, two hundred strong, singing, and the clerk is running straight into it. Dread 2. Tolly holds. The clerk does not stop at the procession. He joins it. He takes a lantern from the nearest hand and walks with them, and he is weeping, and he looks back at Tolly once over his shoulder with an expression of enormous relief.
Tolly can follow him into the procession, and the Carver tells her plainly that this is now a different scene: the procession has the Pull. Sunniva has the ledger. She lets him go, and the Carver lets that cost her 2 Fray, and the table is quiet for a while.

## Health, Hunger, and the Body

### Ordinary Hunger and Starvation

Every person needs about one day's bread a day: one lack's worth. Each day a character goes without food, they gain one level of **Starving**: −1 to all rolls per level, cumulative. At Starving 3, they cannot recover Flesh. At Starving 5, they lose 1 Flesh per day. A day of decent food removes one level. In the Fatlands, ordinary food never satisfies (see that chapter); in the Fast, nothing grows, and the Starving rules are the weather. Inside the Fast, use the **Pangs** track from that chapter in place of Starving levels; a character who leaves the Fast converts Pangs to Starving levels at half, rounding up.

Half rations (a Fast "Pantry measure," a sailor's biscuit and water) neither add nor remove a level. A character may go a number of days equal to **Gut** on half rations before they begin to gain a level every second day.

| Starving | What the body does |
| 1 | The stomach talks. Thoughts go to food every few minutes. Short temper. |
| 2 | Cold hands and feet, though the day is warm. Dizziness on standing. Dreams of meals. |
| 3 | The body begins to eat itself. Wounds will not close. The breath smells sweet and wrong. |
| 4 | Hair comes away in the comb. Gums bleed. The mind goes sharp and strange; you recite recipes you were never taught. |
| 5 | The ankles swell. Sores open where you lie. The heart thuds slow and heavy. You lose 1 Flesh a day. |
| 6+ | You stop feeling hungry. That is the worst sign. Each dawn, Gut + Endure (Grim 18) or suffer Ruin. |

**Refeeding.** A character at Starving 4 or more who eats a full meal must roll **Gut + Endure** (Hard 14) or lose 1d6 Flesh to cramps, sweats and the heart's stutter. The Unfed know this as refeeding sickness, and so does every Crumb-Runner who has watched a rescued child die of the first good supper.

**What will be eaten.** Starving people eat leather, glue, bark, tallow candles, the wallpaper paste, the dog. At Starving 4, a character who has a person's body within reach and nothing else must roll **Caul + Resolve** (Hard 14) to leave it uneaten; a Fatlander with *The Long Pig* rolls at Grim (18). Eating a person knowingly is Dread 3, as the Dread table says, and if it crosses What You Will Not Do, the Guest takes 5 Fray as well. On the Table this is not a rare horror. It is how half the lands remember the Lack.

[box: The Arithmetic of the Lifeboat]
The Uncovered is full of salvage crews who were stranded between tides, and the Admiralty records are full of what they did. If a company of Guests is starving together and there is not enough, the Carver should make them decide who eats, out loud, each day. Track rations openly. Every day that someone takes less so that another takes more, give the giver a Portion at the session's end. The Table remembers who fed whom. So do the players.

### Cold, Exhaustion and Sleeplessness

Each night without shelter in Lack, or without sleep, imposes **−1 to all rolls** (cumulative, up to −4) until the character rests. The Vigil has its own rules for sleeplessness, which are worse.

### Poison

A poison has a **Potency** (the Difficulty to resist), an **Onset**, an **Effect**, and a **Course** (how many times the victim must resist before it is done). When poisoned, the victim rolls **Gut + Endure** against Potency at Onset and again at each step of the Course; each failure applies the Effect. A Seamer may give +2 by care (Hand + Stitching, Routine 10, the right purge or poultice). An *Iron Stomach* gives +2; a Fatlander's *Iron Belly* lets them eat mild poisons as food. Detecting a poison by taste or smell is **Eye + Feast**, at the poison's Potency. The Table's poisons are as particular as its gods:

| Poison | Potency | Onset / Course | Effect per failure |
| Widow's Supper (Fatlands, in gravy) | Hard 14 | an hour / 3 | 2 damage; the victim keeps eating |
| Grey Kiss (Sallowreach, from a Closer's glove) | Grim 18 | at once / 1 | the limb touched goes cold and dead for a day |
| Drowned Milk (Brinehollow, bottled bail-water gone bad) | Hard 14 | a night / 3 | lungs fill: −2 Lung, then drowning |
| Long Sleep (Vigil, poppy and linen-water) | Grim 18 | minutes / 2 | +3 Weariness; in the Vigil, often murder |
| Second Water (Cradlewrack, red spring-water) | Hard 14 | a day / 4 | false labor, −2 to all, then 1d6 damage |
| Hushwort (Oathen, chewed) | Routine 10 | minutes / 1 | the tongue goes numb; no speech, no oaths |
| Hearthsmoke (anywhere, rumoured from the Fast) | Dire 22 | an evening / 1 | the victim smells bread and walks toward it |

[pull] Mind the gravy. It is always the gravy. | — Jem Crackle's only published advice

### Disease

Disease moves slower than poison and further. A disease has a **Virulence** (the Difficulty to resist catching it, and to throw it off), an **Interval** (how often the sick roll), and **Stages**. On exposure, roll **Gut + Endure** against Virulence; failure means infection. Then, once per Interval, the sick roll again: two successes in a row throw it off; each failure advances a Stage; the last Stage is death, outside Sallowreach. A Seamer's care each Interval gives +2.

| Disease | Virulence | Interval | Stages |
| The Sweats (Rim Road inns) | Hard 14 | day | fever (−1) / delirium (−2, Seeing) / Ruin |
| Weep-Fever (Sallowreach) | Hard 14 | week | rash / weeping sores / never ends, never kills |
| Gut-Worm (Fatlands, field-meat) | Routine 10 | week | hunger / wasting despite eating / the worm is longer than the gut |
| Brine-Lung (Brinehollow, foreigners) | Hard 14 | day | cough / bailing like a native / the Calling, at a stranger's tide |
| Rouse-Heart (Vigil, years of dose) | Grim 18 | season | flutter / stutter / Ruin at the next second dose |
| Childbed Fever (Cradlewrack, any bearer) | Grim 18 | day | heat / sepsis of the wound / Ruin; the wound will not close |
| Mouthrot (Oathen, under the bit) | Routine 10 | week | sores / teeth lost / jaw (as the Mangling) |
| The Bloody Flux (anywhere starving) | Hard 14 | day | cramps (−1) / blood (−2, lose 1 Flesh) / Ruin |

When a disease spreads through a community, add +1 Pall for each week of it.

### Rot and Festering

Wounds left unstitched for a day may fester: roll Gut + Endure (Hard 14) or lose 1 Flesh per day and suffer −2 to all rolls until treated. In Sallowreach, festering never kills, and never stops.

A festering wound smells first: sweetish, then like cheese, then like the Sump. It goes red, then hot, then purple-black at the edges, and the red climbs the limb in streaks toward the heart. A festering wound treated within three days can be cleaned (Hand + Stitching, Hard 14; on a Lack, the Seamer opens something they should not have). After three days, the limb must come off: a Grim (18) Hand + Stitching roll, a Dread 2 check for everyone in the room, and the patient suffers Ruin as though they had taken a *Severed Limb* Mangling, but lives. A Seamer who takes the limb off cleanly may offer it to a buyer. Many do. In Lastgate, Nan Pickering pays by the pound.

### Healing

- **First aid** (Hand + Stitching, Routine 10, a few minutes, once per wound) restores 2 Flesh, +1 per Helping.
- **Rest**: a full day of rest restores Gut in Flesh; a full day under a Seamer's care restores double.
- **Injuries** from the Mangling table heal according to their own entries, and some never heal.

## Torture

Torture is in this book because it is on the Table: in the Jar Room's interrogations, in the Cullmasters' back offices, in Jack Tenterhook's former career, in the cells of every Purgation raid that took a high-Cut prisoner and wanted the names. These rules describe what it costs and what it gets. They never describe how. The Carver should keep it off-page, or to a single sentence of aftermath, unless the whole table has agreed otherwise.

### What It Gets

Each session of torture (an hour, a night) the torturer rolls **Tongue + Intimidate** opposed by the victim's **Caul + Resolve**. The victim suffers 1d6 damage, ignoring Armor, whatever the result, and on a Lack by either side suffers an Injury from the Mangling table. If the torturer wins, the victim answers one question per Helping, plus one.

The answers are not necessarily true. The victim says what will make it stop. The Carver decides, privately, whether each answer is the truth, a lie, or the victim's honest belief about what the torturer wants to hear. A torturer may roll **Caul + Instinct** against the victim's Caul + Resolve to tell which, and even then, a person who has been broken may not know themselves. This is why the Bench of Clauses, which has a god for a lie-detector, does not torture, and why every other land does it anyway.

A Blank, a Kept, and a Sayer each suffer differently. The Kept feel everything and cannot be killed by it, which makes them the Table's favourite victims and its least reliable witnesses. A Sayer's answer becomes true. Do not torture a Sayer.

### What It Costs

The torturer makes a **Dread 2** check each session they continue. And every session, whatever the roll, the torturer marks one **Stain**. Stain never goes away.

| Stain | What it does to the torturer |
| 1 | Dreams. The victim's voice in the dark, saying ordinary things. |
| 2 | The Dread checks for torture stop being needed. This is not relief. Those who know you sense something: −2 to Persuade with friends. |
| 3 | Who Waits at Home knows, without being told. They can no longer remove your Fray. |
| 4 | You gain **The Cold Plate** (Table Two, number 7) if you did not have it. |
| 5 | You are good at it. The Carver may make you an NPC: a person others hire. |

*Example: Halloway Brisk has the Cullmaster Varn Sweetbread's assistant tied to a chair in a slaughterhouse in Sated, and the dusk shift goes out in two hours, and there is a child on the list. Dev says she is going to make him talk. The Carver says, out of character, "Okay. One sentence of it, then we cut. What does Halloway do with her hands?" Dev thinks, and says: "She takes off her apron, folds it, puts it on the block. She rolls up her sleeves. That's all you see." Tongue 2 + Intimidate 2 + 2d10 (14) = 18, against the assistant's Threat 2 + 2d10 (9) = 11. One Helping: two answers. The assistant takes 4 damage, which happens off-page. He tells her which field, and he tells her the child's name, which she already knew. Both true. Halloway makes her Dread 2 check (14 on the nose: 1 Fray) and marks Stain 1. That night she dreams of the assistant telling her, very calmly, about his mother's garden. She will dream it for the rest of her life.*

[pull] The difference between my old work and my new work is the tipping. | — Jack Tenterhook, wake-valet. He is lying. It shows on him.

## Downtime and Ruin

Between stories, time passes: a week, a season, the long dead months of Lack. Downtime is where wounds close, debts come due, and lives quietly come apart.

### Downtime Actions

Each **downtime period** (usually a week to a month) each Guest chooses one action, two if the period is a season or more:

- **Mend.** Rest under care. Heal all Flesh, and advance every healing Injury one step. Remove 1 Fray.
- **Work.** Ply your Calling. Earn (Standing × 5) lacks for a week, or (Standing × 20) for a season, on a Routine (10) roll of the Calling's best pair; each Helping adds 10%.
- **Tend.** Go home to Who Waits at Home. Remove all Fray once per story, as the Fray rules say, if they are still themselves; the Carver tells you how they are.
- **Treat.** Undergo treatment for a Derangement: one period of the months of care the rules require. Three periods and 10 Portions treat one Derangement.
- **Restitch.** For the Kept, or for those keeping them: Seamer upkeep, as the Sallowreach chapter gives.
- **Pursue.** Advance a long task: research, a ledger, a forty-page vow. One roll per week.
- **Petition.** Seek Standing: a deed the Calling recognises, and the Portions to pay for it.
- **Carouse.** A vice, as the Fray rules give: 1d6 Fray gone, a Gut + Endure roll against a habit, and lacks spent at the comfortable or lavish rate.
- **Keep Faith.** Tend an Oath on the Dead: visit the grave, the niche, the shelf, the place they were taken. +2 to the next roll made in the oath's name.

### The Cost of Living

Every downtime period costs the cost of living for its length, at the Guest's chosen rate: destitute 1, modest 3, comfortable 8, lavish 25+ lacks a day. A Guest who cannot pay drops a rate. A Guest who cannot pay the destitute rate begins to slide into **Ruin**.

### Ruin of a Life

**Ruin** in combat is the collapse of a body. Ruin in downtime is the collapse of a life, and it is measured the same way, in steps that do not come back easily:

| Ruin | What has happened |
| 1 | Debt. You owe someone (the Company, a Surety, a fence) and they know where you sleep. |
| 2 | Disgrace. Your Calling's people have heard. −1 Standing. |
| 3 | Destitution. You sleep in doorways or the Lofts' porch; Starving rules apply on any bad week. |
| 4 | Outlawry. A warrant, a bounty, a Forsworn mark, a Board debt sold to the Cullmasters. |
| 5 | The list. Your name is on someone's list: the dusk shift, the Dormitory overflow, the Blank trade, a Reckoner's knife, the Second Table's menu. |

A Guest gains a step of Ruin for a downtime period they cannot pay for, for a public crime, for a broken contract or oath, or for the deeds of their enemies. They lose a step by paying down debts (Ruin × 50 lacks), by a great service to a power that can lift them, or by fleeing to another land and starting again at Ruin 1, which is what the Rim Road is for.

### While You Were Away

At the end of each downtime period, the Carver rolls a d20 for one Guest's Who Waits at Home.

| 1–6 | They are well. They wrote. The letter is short and kind and asks when you are coming home. |
| 7–8 | They are ill. Not badly, yet. They did not want to worry you. |
| 9 | They have a new friend, very attentive, who belongs to one of the Table's congregations. |
| 10 | They are in debt, because of you: someone came asking, and they paid. |
