# Making a Guest

Every player except the Carver makes one character, called a **Guest**. A Guest is a person of the Table: born in one of its seven lands, descended from a bloodline that ate some part of a god or refused to, trained in a trade, owing debts and loyalties, and carrying, in all but the rarest cases, a god that is slowly growing back inside them.

Character creation takes about an hour the first time. It works best done together, at the table, so that the Guests can be bound to each other before play begins. A group of strangers who meet at an inn is the oldest beginning there is, and on the Table it is also the most honest: the Rim Road is where everyone ends up.

Groups in a hurry can take one of the seven ready-made Guests at the end of this chapter, one from each land, and be playing in ten minutes. Groups with time to spare can roll on the **Lifepath** tables after Step Nine and let the dice tell them what their Guest ate, buried, betrayed and swore. Neither is required. Both are recommended at least once.

[pull] We ask the land first because the land doesn't change. Names change. Trades change. The god you were born carrying is the one you'll be buried carrying, if anyone bothers to bury you. | — a Company intake clerk, explaining the ledger's order

## The Steps

1. **Concept.** Decide, roughly, who you want to play.
2. **Land.** Choose where you were born. This sets your signature attribute, your god, your Gifts and your Wants. Choose or roll a name and a face.
3. **Cut.** Choose your bloodline's station: Blank, Scrapling, Common, Prime or High. This sets how much god is in you, where you start on the Regrowth, and what you own.
4. **Attributes.** Distribute points among Hand, Gut, Lung, Eye, Tongue and Caul.
5. **Calling.** Choose your trade. It gives you skills, a Calling talent, Standing, and starting gear.
6. **Skills.** Distribute skill points.
7. **Knacks.** Choose two Knacks: small, specific advantages.
8. **Derived traits.** Calculate Flesh, Guard, Hunger, Regrowth and Fray, and note your Gifts, Wants and Sop.
9. **The Four Questions.** Answer the Last Meal, the Oath on the Dead, Who Waits at Home, and What You Will Not Do. If you like, roll the Lifepath first and let it answer for you.
10. **Gear and money.** Spend your lacks at the Rim Market.

## Step One: Concept

Start with a sentence. *A Seamer from Lastgate who has stitched her own mother back together thirty times and is beginning to resent it. A Fatlands drover who walks herds of things that are not cattle and has stopped naming them. A Clausewright's apprentice who said one careless word as a child and has spent twenty years keeping it.* The best concepts carry a wound and a want, and a reason to be on the road.

Discuss concepts as a group. Guests should have a reason to travel together, or at least a reason not to kill each other. Good shared premises include: a Rim Road Company caravan crew; a Cutwrights' College survey team; a band of pilgrims or refugees; a Purgation cell; the household staff of a dying noble; a Tablebreaker company; strangers stranded at the same inn when the snow comes down and turns out to be fat.

## Step Two: Land

Choose the land of your birth. Each land is described at length in Part One; its chapter ends with a section called **Playing a Lander**, which you will need now. The land gives:

- **+1 to a signature attribute** (applied after you distribute points, and the only way to start at 5).
- **Your god.** If your Cut is 1 or higher, the god your ancestors ate is in your blood, and the land's rules for the Regrowth apply to you wherever you go.
- **Gifts:** powers you gain as the god grows back.
- **Wants:** the compulsions that come with them.
- **The Sop:** the act that quiets your god, at a price.
- **The Tooth:** how your god shows itself when you call on it.

### Exiles and the Rim-Born

A character born on the Rim Road, in a waystation or a caravan, still has a land: the land of the bloodline they descend from. The god goes where the blood goes. A Brinehollower born in a Sallowreach inn still bails at dawn. If your parents were of two lands, choose one; the other god is too weak in you to count, though the Carver may give you a single Taste of it as a quirk.

[pull] Half-and-halves are the saddest people on the Road. They get one god's hunger and the other god's manners, and neither land will bury them. | — a waystation keeper at Saltmile

## Step Three: Cut

What part of the god your ancestors were given decides your station, your wealth, and how much of a god you are carrying. Choose one.

| Cut | Station | Starting Regrowth | Teeth | Starting Wealth |
| 0 — Blank | Born without godsign. Hunted. | 0 (cannot rise) | None | 10 lacks |
| 1 — Scrapling | The poor; descended from the bone-lickers. | 0 | One | 15 lacks |
| 2 — Common | Tradesfolk, yeomen, clerks. | 1 | One | 40 lacks |
| 3 — Prime | Gentry, guildmasters, minor Cuts. | 2 | Two | 120 lacks |
| 4 — High | Nobility. The god is close. | 4 (the Appetite) | Two | 400 lacks and a retainer |

**Cut 5** exists, but only among the oldest houses, and their members are usually too far gone to walk the Rim Road. It is not available to Guests.

A Scrapling at Regrowth 0 still carries the god: they can Partake, and their first Hunger to fill will take them into the Taste. They simply have not felt it yet. Most Scraplings say they never will. Most of them are wrong by the time they are forty.

### Blanks (Cut 0)

A Blank is a child born with no godsign at all in a land where everyone else has some. Blanks cannot Partake and their Regrowth can never rise, unless they eat godflesh (see the Fast chapter for what happens then). They gain **+2 to any roll made to resist a god's influence** (a Hush, a Calling, an oath's Breaking, a Seated presence), and they never gain Hunger. They are also the most valuable people on the Table. Every land wants them: as saints, as specimens, as surgical stock. A Blank Guest begins play with a **Bounty**: somebody, somewhere, has posted a price for them. Agree with the Carver who.

### The Unfed

A Guest born in the Fast is Unfed, not Blank. They carry no god because their ancestors refused, not because of a fluke of birth. Their Cut is 0; they gain +1 to any attribute instead of a signature, and use the **Want**, **Pangs** and **Invitation** rules in the Fast chapter. They begin with Want 3 and the Unfed kit described there (ration-cloth, water flask, wax tin, household axe, three days' Pantry measures, a coat that is not warm enough) and, in place of a Cut's starting wealth, **2d6 lacks**: the Fast has no money worth counting. They are not hunted quite as hard as Blanks, but only because there are so many more of them.

### High Cuts

Playing a noble means starting closer to the end. A High Cut Guest begins at Regrowth 4, in the Appetite, with the land's Taste and Appetite Gifts already in hand and its first Want already active. They are rich, connected, feared and despised: Scraplings spit at them, Reckoners have their names on lists, and the Second Table sends them invitations. A High Cut Guest also gains **+1 Standing** in their Calling and a **retainer**: a loyal servant (Threat 1, Flesh 9) who will die for them, and probably will.

[pull] My lady's god is closer to the surface than mine. I can see it when she laughs. It laughs half a breath later. | — a retainer of the Hethe household, Lastgate

## Step Four: Attributes

Every attribute starts at 1. Distribute **9 points** among them. No attribute may be raised above 4 at this stage. Then add your land's +1. An adult of the Table averages 2 in everything; a 3 is notable, a 4 is remarkable, a 5 is the best in a city.

[box: The Six Attributes]
- **HAND:** dexterity, violence, delicacy, craft. Picking a lock, gutting a pig, stitching a lip, cutting a throat.
- **GUT:** strength, toughness, endurance, appetite. Carrying a corpse, holding down poison, taking a beating, eating what must be eaten.
- **LUNG:** speed, agility, stamina, stealth, breath. Running, climbing, hiding, swimming, outlasting.
- **EYE:** perception, reason, memory, learning. Noticing the extra joint, reading the ledger, remembering the face.
- **TONGUE:** persuasion, deceit, command, oaths. Talking, lying, bargaining, swearing, and knowing when to say nothing.
- **CAUL:** will, instinct, faith, the uncanny. Resisting your god, enduring horror, praying, feeling the thing in the next room before you see it. Named for the membrane some are born in.
[/box]

Each land's people favour the attribute their god favours, and the Table has jokes about all of them. A Sallowreacher can stitch anything but a conversation. A Fatlander can carry an ox and will want to eat it after. A Brinehollower can outrun you and out-hold-their-breath you and will not tell you where they are going. A Vigilant sees everything and remembers it, which is the problem. A Cradlewracker knows what is behind the door before they knock. An Oathener can talk you into anything, which is why they never do.
