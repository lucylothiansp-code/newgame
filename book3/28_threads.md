# Threads Between the Lands

> Every land is certain that the cure is next door.
>> — Rim Road saying

> The road goes round. So does everything on it.
>> — painted over the door of the Company inn at Saltlick Cross

The lands of the Table are not separate stories. They share a road, a coin, a grace and a doom, and the crises in each are beginning to touch. This chapter takes the open storylines that cross borders and lays them out as campaign threads: who is involved in each land, how the thread escalates if nobody interferes, what the Guests can see and do about it, what it will cost them, and how it might end. Most of the endings are bad. That is not a failure of imagination; it is the setting. A thread resolved well is a thread that ends one way rather than a worse way.

Each thread is written in stages. Advance a thread one stage whenever the Guests spend a season elsewhere, or whenever the GM wishes the world to feel the weight of the clock. Threads touch: the Sayer's Sentence feeds the Deadwatch, the Blank Trade feeds the Second Table, the Low Water feeds Eight Chairs, and almost everything, sooner or later, feeds Eight Chairs.

Every thread opens with an **in-world document** that hints at it before anything is explained, offers **Scenes the Guests Might Witness** to stage in full, and closes with a **What It Costs** box: the price, in Fray, Hunger, coin and conscience, of touching it at all. Fifteen threads follow: the twelve of the first edition, deepened with what *Seven Tables* has since uncovered, and three new ones that the year 642 is about to pull tight.

[box: Running Threads]
Pick two or three threads to run in the background of a campaign and let the others exist only as rumors. Keep a simple track for each, Stage 1 to Stage 4, on a card the players never see. When a thread reaches Stage 4 it resolves, with or without the Guests, and the world changes. Guests who intervene can hold a thread at its current stage for a season, push it back one stage at great cost, or steer which ending it reaches. They cannot make it not have happened.
A useful habit: whenever a thread advances off-screen, put one physical sign of it in front of the Guests within the next session. A place card on an inn table. A candle that keens when the wind drops. The world should be seen to move before it is explained.
[/box]

## The Deadwatch

[pull] The dead cannot be Counted. Think on it, Castellan. It is the only sum in the city that comes out clean. | — Sabine Larch, to Envoy Dray

**Summary.** The Vigil cannot sleep and is running out of everything: beds, rouse, oil and time. Its Warden-Prime has sent Envoy Castellan Dray to Lastgate to buy the secret of Keeping from Regent Ansel Grue: if the Vigil's citizens could be killed and kept upright, a city of the dead would never need to sleep. The flaw is geography. The Kept cannot leave Sallowreach, so the Vigil would have to move there. Dray has not written this down, because he knows Ilvane Stroud will say yes; and Stroud has already decided, without telling him, to leave the sixty thousand Counted behind on the Lid, unwatched. A dispatch from the clerk Bell Lisle, now on the Rim Road, will make them both tell the truth.

[fiction]
*To C. Dray, Envoy, care of the Company post-house, Lastgate. Fourth letter of Carving. Burn after.*
Castellan. A Counted sleeper cannot be relieved of the risk she poses except by being relieved of her bed; a citizen who dies awake in the fen poses no risk at all. I have struck forty names from the general rows this month and can strike no faster without being noticed. You could strike a hundred thousand and be thanked on the Stair. I do not ask you to love the arithmetic. I ask you to show me where in it the murder is. Tell the Regent the Lessening pays in rouse, promptly.
*S.L.*
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *The Vigil:* Envoy Castellan Dray; Warden-Prime Ilvane Stroud; Dr. Aldous Crane, whose dose curve gives the Vigil eleven years; Benedikt Lour, already drawing plans for the whole population; Sabine Larch, First of the Lessening, who writes to Dray every week because the dead cannot be Counted; Bell Lisle's dispatch, somewhere between Lampwick Halt and Fogmouth.
- *Sallowreach:* Regent Ansel Grue, tempted by a hundred thousand new taxable dead; Lady Corrow Vane, who would have to Close nobody and keep everyone; the Heirs, who see a new population of the never-dying arriving to sit on top of them forever; Tam Ruddock, who already knows how to move people across the Drop and will sign on with Dray in 642 for a fee that would let him stop.
- *The Rim:* the Company, which would carry the migration and price it; Fogmouth, where the line is painted.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Dray negotiates in Lastgate. Small trials: Vigil volunteers are brought north, killed in Sallowreach, and kept. They do not sleep. It works. The Lessening quietly funds the volunteers' passage. |
| 2 | Bell Lisle's dispatch arrives. Dray and Stroud confess by letter, and agree. The Watch votes in secret to relocate. Caravans of the Slope's poor begin moving west under Company contract. |
| 3 | The first mass Killing at Fogmouth: thousands of Vigil folk die north of the Drop and stand back up. The Heirs riot. The Mortuary Court demands a toll per head. Tam Ruddock buys a bigger notebook. |
| 4 | Lastgate's lower levels fill with sleepless dead foreigners. The Vigil's lamps go out one district at a time as the city empties into the fen. The Counted lie on the Lid with nobody counting them. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The Trial at Fogmouth.** A Slope laundress named Ottie Fane, thirty, lidded, nine days without a full sleep, kneels on the north bank where the line is painted on the planks in Company red. She has asked to be drowned rather than cut, because she has heard the Kept feel their wounds. Two Fen Guard hold her under the black water. It takes longer than anyone wants. Her heels drum the boards; her fingers find Dray's boot and close on it. Then the drumming stops, and the Guard lift her out, and the water runs out of her nose and her open mouth in a long brown sheet, and her chest does not move, and she sits up. She coughs up a palmful of fen-weed. She looks at Dray, and her eyes are bloodshot and wide and perfectly clear. *I'm still tired,* she says. *I thought it would stop.* It never does. That is a Dread 3 check.
- **The Lower Levels.** A Guest sent to inspect the first Vigil lodgings in Lastgate's flooded under-streets finds a long cellar where two hundred dead foreigners stand in rows because there is nowhere to lie down, all awake, all rotting at the speed of the fen, the soft ones at the ankles going green and slack where the water laps. Nobody talks. They count, under their breath, in the Vigil way. Their eyes follow the lamp.

**What the Guests Can Do.** Escort or sabotage the trial caravans. Expose to the Vigil's poor what Keeping actually feels like (Kept bodies rot and feel it for centuries). Uncover the Court's hidden reason for agreeing: the Court believes new dead without Hand-line blood may slow the Hushes. Steal Dray's commission, which the Warden-Prime would rather die than see read aloud. Intercept, delay or deliver Bell Lisle's dispatch; whoever carries it decides when the city's two oldest friends stop lying.

**Endings.**
- *The Dead City:* the Vigil moves north and becomes a district of Lastgate, a hundred thousand rotting, wakeful foreigners. The Dormitory is abandoned with its Counted inside, unguarded, and the lamps go out over sixty thousand beds.
- *The Broken Deal:* the Heirs drag Dray to a Hush; the Vigil, betrayed, closes the Rim at Lampwick Halt; Sallowreach loses its rouse and oil, and Lastgate's candle-makers turn to the only fuel left.
- *The Worst Arithmetic:* a Vigil sleeper is brought north, unconscious, and killed in Sallowreach. They do not wake and do not die. They lie there, Kept and Counted at once, and the Dormitory's number goes up by one.

[box: What It Costs — The Deadwatch]
Witnessing a trial Killing is Dread 3; witnessing a mass Killing at Fogmouth is Dread 4. A Guest who helps a volunteer die gains the volunteer's gratitude, which is permanent and sleepless. Sabotaging a caravan earns the Lessening's attention: within a season, the Guest's name (or a loved one's) appears on a list for the general rows. Siding with the Heirs closes the Vigil to the party; siding with Dray closes Lastgate's upper levels.
[/box]

## The Host on the Road

[pull] Lack keep away. And if it won't, set it a place. | — said by Rim innkeepers since the tables began

**Summary.** For six centuries Orrum kept to the Fast. Now its tables have been found laid at crossroads in three other lands, and the seemers of the Vigil, the scavengers of Brinehollow and the pilgrims of the Fatlands have all seen the same tall figure laying a table. That figure is Welcome Ardent, the Host's steward, walking a mile ahead of his god around the Rim. On fourteen walls in fourteen capitals, Envoy Prudence Lowell has made a small chalk stroke where she stood to eat facing the plaster, like a table seen edge on. In three of those capitals, someone has drawn eight short marks beneath it.

[fiction]
*A place card, cream board, gilt edge, found at the crossroads of the Drove Road and the Rim, Plenty 641. The hand is beautiful and slightly unsteady.*
**Goodwife Hanna Spurling, of Stile Farm**
*Seated at the third place. The soup is barley with marrowbone; the bread was baked this morning, and there is butter. Please do not wait for the others. You have been hungry such a long time. You were always so polite about it.*
*(On the reverse, in the same hand, smaller:)* I am sorry. I am so sorry. Do not sit. It is very good. Do not sit.
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *The Fast:* Welcome Ardent, polite and exhausted; Spare Tolland of the Hostwatch, mapping the warm ground; Decline Marsh and the Tablebreakers; Envoy Prudence Lowell and her chalk marks.
- *The Vigil:* Mirela Sconce, who recorded eleven districts hallucinating a table being laid in the street.
- *The Fatlands:* Osanna Blythe and her three hundred pilgrims, hungry every second of their lives, looking for a table where they will finally feel full.
- *Brinehollow:* salvagers on the Uncovered who have found tables laid on the dry seabed with the plates still wet.
- *The Rim:* the wardens at Saltlick Cross; every innkeeper who has seen a tall shape stoop to look in at the window.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Tables appear at Rim crossroads, one a season. Tablebreakers cross the Hem to smash them. Place cards bear the names of people who live nearby. |
| 2 | Welcome Ardent is seen in person at a Rim inn. He asks, politely, whether anyone would take over his duties. The first non-Unfed sit down: a Fatlands family, a Vigil nudger, a Kept man a yard north of the Drop at Fogmouth, who now cannot leave the table or the fen. |
| 3 | Orrum itself is seen on the Rim at night, stooping to look in at windows. Whole villages along the Fatlands border are found Seated. Osanna Blythe's pilgrims find their table. |
| 4 | Tables are laid in every capital on the same night, with a place card for every head of state. In each, one chair is left without a card. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The Spurling Table.** Hanna Spurling sat. So did her husband and two grown sons, at the places laid for them. The Guests find them nine days later: the table is still warm to the touch, the bread still steams when torn, and the four Spurlings are eating. Their cheeks have fallen in to the shape of the teeth beneath. Their wrists are rope. Their nails have grown long and curled against the spoons, and their lips are split and bleeding into the soup, and they are smiling, and they say *you must try the butter*. They do not want to be lifted. They scream, thin and high, if they are.
- **The Face at the Window.** In a Company inn after midnight, the shutters on the road side darken one by one, left to right, as something very tall walks slowly past outside and stoops at each. At the last window it stops. The Guests hear it breathe once, like a bellows in an empty forge. In the morning the frost on the sill has melted in the shape of two great hands laid flat.

**What the Guests Can Do.** Track Ardent and talk to him (he is the only one of Orrum's guests who can still get up, and he will answer questions honestly if he is not invited to sit). Smash tables, at the risk of finding their own names. Protect or stop the pilgrims. Discover that the cardless chair at each table faces away from the others.

**Endings.**
- *The Long Table Grows:* the Seated spread along the Rim, a second endless table around the whole continent, happy, wasting, praising the food. Trade stops because the road is full of diners.
- *The Relief:* a Guest accepts Ardent's position. Ardent finally sits down and eats, and dies in moments, weeping with gratitude. The Guest now walks a mile ahead of the god forever, and knows exactly whom the tables are for.
- *The Unanswered Door:* the tables stop, all at once. Orrum stands still at Hallowboard facing the eighth side. The Hostwatch reports that it has never looked so frightened.

[box: What It Costs — The Host on the Road]
Coming upon a Seated family is Dread 3. Smashing a table is a Hard Caul + Resolve roll; failure means the smasher eats one mouthful first, without meaning to, and gains 2 Hunger and the Want *Sit Down* (resist Hard 14 whenever offered food by a stranger) until the next Tablenight. A Guest who finds their own name on a card makes a Dread 4 check. Speaking with Ardent costs nothing but sleep: the night after, the Guest dreams of laying a table for someone who is late. Taking his position is the end of that Guest's story as a player character.
[/box]

## Brine in the Rouse

[pull] Dry Jonah's Tonic — Sea-Air in a Bottle — For the Chest, the Nerves, the Long Night. Do Not Exceed Three Drops. | — label, Lowmark bottling, sold on the Slope at four lacks

**Summary.** Captain Jonah Skerry, called Dry Jonah, bottles what Brinehollowers cough up at bailing and sells it inland as tonic. It sells best in the Vigil, where the Slope dealer Rook Quarter-Bell cuts it into rouse because it is cheap and it works. The sleepless are now feeling the Calling, four hundred miles from any coast. Customers stay awake. They have also started walking west. On the coast, the Assay clerk Brenna Kelp has proved that the sea falls by nine hundred and twelve thousand barrels for every Tenanted soul returned from it, a figure the clerks call *the Kelp*. If a Vigil walker comes back Tenanted, the constant will have to count her. The ocean, it seems, does not mind which land its people come from.

[fiction]
*From the private daybook of Dr. Aldous Crane, Apothecary-General. Not for the Watch.*
Batch 41, Slope-street rouse, cut approx. one part in nine with coastal tonic (Skerry's). Subject held awake 31 hours on a standard dose; control, 30. One hour. One hour is eleven thousand waking hours across the city per night. One hour is a year off the curve. Side effects: dry cough, salt crust at the lips by the second day, a tendency to stand at west-facing windows. Subject 6 asked me, very politely, what the water was like where I came from. I have not reported this batch. I have ordered forty barrels.
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *Brinehollow:* Dry Jonah, who has not bailed in years and is due to be Called before Tablenight; Tolly Brack, who knows by the color of bailing water who will be Called next; Brenna Kelp and her constant; the Netwatch, who are going to see Called strangers arrive from the east.
- *The Vigil:* Rook Quarter-Bell; Dr. Aldous Crane, desperate for anything that stretches the dose; the Wakers; Inspector Hale Grimmer, whose blink-murders begin to cluster on the western edge of the city; Senna Vole, on her forty-first dive into the room.
- *The Rim:* Lampwick Halt and Lowmark Spur, the two ends of the walk.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | The Slope's addicts stand at west windows. Dr. Crane notices that brine-cut rouse holds an hour longer and does not report it. |
| 2 | Vigil citizens begin walking west across the plateau in their sleep-that-is-not-sleep, eyes open, straight lines. The Wakers net them at the city edge. Dry Jonah is Called from the deck of the *Bess*. |
| 3 | Crane formulates brine into the official rouse. The whole Vigil now carries a little of the sea. Lines of walkers cross the Rim heading for a coast they have never seen. |
| 4 | The first Vigil walkers reach the Uncovered and keep going, out toward the Trench. Some come back Tenanted: calm, courteous, rested. They are the first people in the Vigil in three centuries who sleep and wake again. The sea, by the Kelp, falls a little faster. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The West Window.** A Slope tenement, fourth floor. Eleven people stand at a single west window, shoulder to shoulder, faces to the glass. Their breath has fogged it and then salted it, a white crust the shape of eleven mouths. One of them, a boy of seventeen, is coughing quietly and steadily; what comes up is grey, foaming and smells of a harbor at low tide, and in it are threads of something green and slick that moves a little on the floorboards before it stops. None of them turns around. One of them is humming a tune the Guests will hear again, if they ever stand on the cliff at Lowmark at Low Water.
- **The Net.** A line of walkers crosses the plateau at dawn, spaced exactly apart, eyes open and dry. The Wakers string a net across the road. The walkers walk into it and keep walking, and the net creaks, and their faces press through the mesh until the cord cuts into the cheeks, and they do not stop pushing, and they do not cry out, and they bleed.

**What the Guests Can Do.** Trace the supply chain from bailing buckets in Lowmark to dealers on the Slope. Kill or bargain with Dry Jonah before the sea takes him. Shepherd walkers home or follow them to see where they go. Present the Watch with the choice: brine buys the Vigil years of wakefulness at the cost of the sea pulling on every citizen.

**Endings.**
- *The Walking City:* the Vigil adopts brine-rouse openly and loses a tenth of its people a year to the west. The Watch calls it emigration.
- *The Tenanted Wardens:* Tenanted Vigil-folk, rested and calm, are found to be immune to the Dormitory's dream. The Watch begins appointing them to office, as Brinehollow did with its judges. Nobody asks whose eyes are looking out.
- *The Meeting of Waters:* the Trench, the draining sea and the long dim room in the Vigil's dream turn out to be closer than anyone thought. Senna Vole, on her forty-first dive, hears water.

[box: What It Costs — Brine in the Rouse]
A Guest who takes brine-cut rouse gains a single extra watch awake per dose but rolls Caul + Resolve (Hard 14) each dawn after: failure means a Called walk of an hour westward before waking, Lung + Athletics to stop safely. Three failures and the Guest hears the Calling permanently, as a Brinehollower does, wherever they stand. Netting walkers is Dread 2. Killing Dry Jonah makes an enemy of every coastal bottler and buys nothing: the tonic trade has six other captains. Telling the Vigil's poor the truth starts a riot at the Bitterhouse in which the Wakers' long needles are used freely.
[/box]

## The First to Eat

[pull] What grows in an Unfed body is not the god the body ate. | — Odo Venn, private notes, never sent to Quire

**Summary.** Tobias Wether, a runaway raised on six centuries of refusal, crossed into the Fatlands and ate. The Reckoner called Scour spent a year undercover in the Fatlands, had to eat to keep his cover, and came home with godsign. Two Unfed bodies now carry a god that was never theirs, and the meat inside them seems to be looking for something. Tobias does not know that the drover Sal Trotter, who took him in, named him after the son she culled for a debt; or that the lead steer of her herd, branded with that son's plot-number, hums her song a beat ahead of her. Wont Bellamy has guessed what Scour is, and has written to the Second Table that he may soon have "a dish of rare interest."

[fiction]
*College tasting-slip, Odo Venn's hand, folded into a hymnal at Sated. The official copy, filed at Quire, reads "Subject W.: Ummer-line, trace, unremarkable."*
Subject W. Blood drawn from the thumb, tasted at room heat. First: Ummer, faint, the usual gravy and grass. Under it: nothing. Not absence, as with a Blank. A space. A room with the door left open. I held it on the tongue for the count of twelve and something on the far side of the space noticed me holding it. I have rinsed eleven times. I will not record this.
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *The Fast:* Scour, hiding his godsign under his sleeves and volunteering for every raid; Reckoner-Captain Scant Hollis; the Abstinent, for whom this is the worst possible news; Meek Garrity, whose research says the Unfed never refused at all; Wont Bellamy, with his letter.
- *The Fatlands:* Tobias Wether; Sal Trotter and her humming steer; Corder Lank of the Thin, who is very interested in a man who came from emptiness into hunger; Brother Glut of the Second Helping, who sees a convert.
- *Cross-border:* Odo Venn of the College, who wants to taste them; the Second Table, who want to know whether an Unfed stomach makes room for a god.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Tobias shows his first godsign. It is not purely Ummer's: he dreams of a long dim room and closes doors behind him. Scour's signs are similar. |
| 2 | The College learns of them. Unfed blood with godsign is, by the ledgers, impossible. Odo Venn tastes Tobias and goes pale and does not report the flavor. |
| 3 | Scour's comrades find out. The Abstinent strike his name from the Pantry. He flees to the Rim, where the Second Table is waiting with an invitation. |
| 4 | Tobias and Scour, kept apart, begin to show identical signs at the same hour. Neither carries any one god. Both are getting hungrier for the same thing, and when they meet, they set a table. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The Doors.** Tobias, lodged at an inn, cannot pass through a door without closing it behind him, then opening it, then closing it again from the other side, three times, his lips moving. If anyone stops him he goes grey and sweats and his stomach makes a long, low, hollow sound, like wind across the mouth of a jar. The door he did not get to close is found in the morning with the paint scored by fingernails on the inside, low down, where a crouching person would reach.
- **Under the Sleeves.** Scour, in a Reckoner bunkhouse, unwinds his wrappings by a candle when he thinks he is alone. The godsign on his forearms is the marbling of good beef, fat laced through red, but in it, if a Guest looks closely, the fat has drawn itself into lines: a long rectangle, and eight small squares around it.

**What the Guests Can Do.** Protect them, hide them, study them or kill them. Bring Tobias back into the Fast and see what happens to the Lack around him. Learn, from Venn's private notes, that what grows in an Unfed body is not the god the body ate.

**Endings.**
- *The Empty Vessel:* the Second Table buys Scour and serves him, as a test. The diners all sleep badly afterward, and dream of a room where someone is laying a table.
- *The Lack Breaks:* Tobias returns to the Fast and plants. The dead soil sprouts where he walks. The Abstinent call it a miracle and then realize what it means: the heartland can be fed if its people eat. Wont Bellamy wins the next vote.
- *Two Places Laid:* in Lack 642, Tobias and Scour vanish from their guarded rooms on the same night. Two Orrum tables are found the next morning at the Rim, with their names on the cards, and the chairs pushed in.

[box: What It Costs — The First to Eat]
Hiding either man makes the Guests enemies of the Reckoners (who end lines) or of the Second Table (who end dinners), and usually both. Tasting their blood, as Venn did, needs Caul + Godsign (Grim 18) to learn anything and imposes a Dread 3 check whatever the result; on a Lack the taster gains the Derangement *Open Doors* (cannot bear a door left shut behind them). Killing them is murder of the gentlest people in the chapter, and their tables are laid anyway.
[/box]

## The Blank Trade

[pull] Price per pound, Unfed, dressed: forty lacks. Price per pound, Blank, living, any land: name your figure. | — chalked inside a poacher's lean-to, Fast border

**Summary.** Wren Hollowell in the Fatlands, Cassian Wry in Oathen and the entire population of the Fast are worth more in parts than whole. Blank blood, marrow and organs are believed to slow the Regrowth. Dr. Mercy Thrane of the Fast is following the buyers, and the trail leads to the Second Table. She carries a plank from a hidden surgery in Sated with *A.T.* scratched into it and believes it was a patient's. It was her brother's. Abide Thrane has lived for nine years in Master Quillon Barr's brine-tanks, and has started to put on weight; and Barr has read Mercy's book, admired it, and sent her a pair of spectacles.

[fiction]
*Docket, Rim Road Company freight, Wanting Post to Sated. Found pasted inside a barrel lid.*
Consignment 6612. Contents: PICKLED GOODS (BRINE), 4 barrels, sealed. Shipper: A. Kettering, sexton, by proxy. Receiver: K. Dragoman, for the account of a gentleman. Weight declared: 9 stone 4. Weight at Sated scales: 9 stone 4. *Company does not open sealed freight.* Clerk's note in pencil, faint: *one of them knocked.*
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *The Fast:* Dr. Mercy Thrane; Marshal Keep Aldane, holding the border against poachers; Abide Kettering, the sexton who sells the omitted dead at Brindle Cross; Withy Gaunt, who once sold a baby there so that he would live; every Unfed child sent to fetch water at dusk.
- *The Fatlands:* Kale Dragoman, poacher, plump and kind and smelling of woodsmoke, who is that baby grown and does not know it; Wren Hollowell, with a bounty on her weight that does not specify alive; Lord Chastain Loin, who wants a Blank set in aspic for the Second Table's cellar; the Board of Plenty.
- *Oathen:* Cassian Wry, the crown's Blank spy, kept in luxury and never let out of sight; the Bench of Clauses, which has received an offer.
- *Cross-border:* Master Quillon Barr and Lady Seraphine Dalgety of the Second Table; the Rim Road Company, whose wagons carry sealed brine-barrels it does not open.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Unfed disappearances double along the eastern border. Thrane finds a surgery in Sated with an Unfed man's name scratched inside a cupboard. |
| 2 | Wren Hollowell is taken. A Fatlands Gut-line matriarch is seen at the Weighing ten stone lighter and, for the first time in thirty years, not eating. |
| 3 | Oathen's Bench agrees, in a forty-page conditional, to lease Cassian Wry's kidney to a foreign buyer. The surgery is scheduled at Treaty Stair. |
| 4 | Thrane traces the trail to Barr's surgery, and finds Abide, nine years a donor, fat and smiling, and the eighth course on a menu pinned to the wall, still blank. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The Tanks.** Barr's cellar is warm and smells of vinegar, cloves and the inside of a cheek. Six copper tanks, lidded, each with a glass port at face height. In the third, Abide Thrane floats in green brine to the chin, his scalp shaved and seamed, plump, pink, the skin of his belly puckered with nine years of neat little scars like the stitching on a quilt. He opens his eyes at the lamp and smiles, and mouths a word through the glass. It is his sister's name.

**Summary.** Cray Lobbett found a stone table on the seabed of the Uncovered, older than the Gorging, with eight chairs, seven pushed in; the Passenger who rides Orrin Wake says Cray has three visits left before he sits. Hallowboard, at the center of the world, has eight sides. Grandfather Peat, a bog body hauled from the Sump, is the last witness to the Night of Seven Tables and says the god was smiling. Meek Garrity has found the seating plan for Hallowboard, and Sober Osmond a line in the Empty Chair rite about *the one who comes late*. In the Vigil, Aveline Morne has spoken in Bed One: *There is someone at the door who has not been fed. Set another place.* Someone is going to put these together. This is the thread that leads to the Secrets chapter, and it should be the spine of any long campaign.

[fiction]
*Transcript, interview with the bog body called Grandfather Peat, Lastgate, by a hired translator of the Sump dialect. Paid by an unnamed College buyer.*

A. At the bank. We were the bank. We stood.
Q. How many tables were there?
A. Seven tables. Eight sides to the hill. We laid the seven.
Q. And the god was smiling?
A. Ours was. Ours had seen him come.
Q. Seen who come?
A. (*long silence; the translator notes the jaw working with no sound*) Write down that I was smiling too. Write down that we were all so glad to be eating that nobody looked behind.

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

Every major discovery is a Dread check, rising: the seabed table Dread 3, Peat's full account Dread 3, the matching rubbings Dread 4, the word itself Dread 5. Silencing Garrity makes the party murderers of a gentle old man for the sake of a Refusal that never happened. The GM should never let this thread be cheap.

| 1 | A Bonded Hand steals from the Company and does not break. The wardens hang him anyway, quietly. The Company begins re-swearing contracts. |
| 2 | Quill becomes ill. Her body shows marks that match no oath she has broken. The Company doubles her guard and readies its second notary. |
| 3 | A treaty fails: two lands discover that a border sworn in Oathen two centuries ago no longer binds either side. War is spoken of, carefully. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The Thief Who Did Not Break.** A Bonded Hand named Teague Orr pockets a platter at Wanting Post, and waits, white, for his fingers to go. They do not. He laughs. He cannot stop laughing; he walks out into the yard laughing and the wardens take him there, and the Guests watch the quiet hanging behind the stables, done with a wagon-chain, Company-fashion, because a man who can break his bond and live is worse for business than a thief.
- **Ona's Default.** The Surety's chamber in Tacit, if Stage 4 arrives. Ona Fell at her desk, and the oath she stands for coming due all at once, in her body. The Guests hear her joints go first, a sound like knuckles cracked in a quiet church, one after another, wrists, elbows, shoulders, then the long bones, and she does not fall, because she is holding the edge of the desk, and she keeps holding it.

**What the Guests Can Do.** Protect Quill. Investigate why the Kept Rain was late; Yusra Thole knows and cannot lie, so she will not speak, but her Tally can be read. Find the Company's second notary, and lay Vael's three drafts side by side before anyone swears them. Carry the Company's frantic re-swearing across the Rim. Discover that Odalys Penhallow is preparing for the Company to survive the lands, and what she means to feed the wagons.

**Endings.**
- *Unbound:* the Rim dissolves into seven tolls and seven armies. Every land must now get what it needs from neighbors it finds obscene, without a neutral road in between, and it gets it by force.
- *The New Witness:* the Company finds something else to swear by. It is listening, and it is not Tolm, and it is very glad to be asked.
- *The Last Wagons:* Penhallow's sealed wagons set out, carrying the Spokes and the archive, around and around the Rim, and the Company becomes a procession with no destination, lit at night, stopping at no inn.

[box: What It Costs — The Slipping Seal]
Reading Yusra's Tally and understanding it is Eye + Reckoning (Grim 18) and a Dread 3 check; from then on the Guest knows the god is growing, and every Oathener they meet can ask them about it. Untying the knot without killing anyone requires all three of Quill, Ona and the Company to be re-sworn in one sitting before the Bench: a Dire Tongue + Clause roll by whoever drafts it, with each Lack on that roll costing one of the three their life. Anyone a Guest saves here is in their debt by oath, which, in Oathen, is not a figure of speech.
[/box]

## The Stolen Ledgers

[pull] Every line ends. The College only writes down where. | — motto over the door of the Master Concordance, Quire

**Summary.** Silas Fenwright, a renegade Lineman of the Cutwrights' College, walked out of Quire with three quires copied from the Master Concordance: the full lines of the highest houses of three lands, and the College's graph of every Regrowth bending upward at the same moment after 600 A.G. Everyone wants them. Whoever holds the ledgers knows where each god is regrowing fastest, and who is next. In the north, Provost Carrion Sedge has already learned what it is to hold a dangerous ledger: his own map of the Hushes has a pinhole in it, and the pinhole is the Hall of Hands, and he has dated the next great Hush to Lack 642, give or take a season.

[fiction]
*Page offered for sale at the Groaning Board, Plenty 641. The buyer was found before dawn with the page folded into his mouth.*
House Dalgety, Eye-line. Cut 5 in the direct line since 402. Mean Regrowth of living members: 7.4. Rate since 600: doubled. Projected Seating of the head of house: within the decade. Projected rate after 641: *see graph, sheet iv*. (Annotation, Lune's hand:) Every curve on sheet iv bends at the same place. Seven gods do not agree on a date unless something has given them one.
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *The College:* Archprovost Hesper Lune; Odo Venn; Provost Carrion Sedge.
- *Cross-border:* the Purgation, who want a massacre list; the Second Table, who want a menu; the Reckoners, especially Deny Fallon, who wants a target roll.
- *Every land:* noble houses who would pay anything to have their own line removed, and their rivals' added.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Fenwright offers a single page for sale at the Groaning Board. The buyer is murdered before dawn. |
| 2 | A Lancing in the Fatlands follows the ledger's exact order of houses. A Reckoner raid in the Vigil follows the same page. |
| 3 | The College's graph is copied and nailed to toll-houses on the Rim. The Bait doctrine takes it as proof. Riots in three capitals. |
| 4 | The third quire, the one Fenwright has not shown anyone, is opened. It is a list of every Blank born on the Table in two centuries, with their present whereabouts. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The Lancing in Order.** A Gut-line hall in the Fatlands, Purgation lancers at the door with the page in a leather sleeve. The seventh name on the page is a girl of nine. The lancer looks at the page, and at her, and at the page. The Guests are in the room.

- *The Ledgers Burn:* the Guests destroy the quires; the Master Concordance at Quire burns soon after, in a fire that no one admits setting. Nobody now knows where the gods are, which helps no one.
- *Your Own Line:* the ledger shows that a Guest is closer to a Seating than they knew, by far. Every faction that holds a copy knows it too.

[box: What It Costs — The Stolen Ledgers]
Holding even a page makes the holder a target of four factions; the GM rolls a d6 each week the party carries one, and on a 1 someone comes for it. Reading one's own line changes nothing in the blood and everything in the mind: Dread 4 if it ends in a Seating within the decade. Burning the quires is the only ending that saves the Blanks on the third list, and it blinds everyone, including those who might have used the graph to save a land. Returning them to Quire is a 30-day journey with every road watched.
[/box]

## The Low Water

[pull] The sea says dawn's at the half-bell. Your chests say it's now. I ring for the chests. | — Finn Undertow, Tide-Bellman of Lowmark

**Summary.** *A new thread.* Every Brinehollower fills with the sea's water at dawn and dusk, and for ten years that tide in the chest has drifted ahead of the tide on the Uncovered. Finn Undertow, who rings the Headland bell for the chests and not for the sea, has done the sum: the drift closes at the Low Water of Carving 642, when every Brinehollower will fill at the same moment. He does not know the drift follows the slow breathing of Ondine Vasht, the Dowager Below, in the cellars under Fathomhouse. That same night Ilse Marl's chart of the Tenanted's singing completes, and Dagna Hull's *Follow* makes its trial descent toward the Trench with Commodore Vell and his knives aboard. Meanwhile Reeve Calder Stokes carries to Tacit the figure Brenna Kelp proved by buckets, *the Kelp*: the sea falls nine hundred and twelve thousand barrels for every soul returned to it. By the Company's tolls, which had the figure first, Brinehollow has eight years and a season, and the Company will sell the price. The Drowned Bench has read Brenna's copy. Justice Maren Oake wrote in its margin: *The figure is correct. The constant will change.*

[fiction]
*Chalked on the inside of the Headland bell-house door, Lowmark. Each morning the old figure is rubbed out with a wet thumb and a new one written.*
377. Chests ahead of the sea by 11 min. Mile Nine filled at the half-bell, all of it, one breath. Dowager's house dark. 376. 12 min. A child on Mile Two filled in her sleep and did not wake for the bell; her mother says she was smiling. 375. 12 min. Rang early. Admiralty clerk again. Told him the sea is not the one that drowns in bed.
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *Brinehollow:* Finn Undertow and his count; Ondine Vasht, the Dowager Below, breathing; Ilse Marl and her chart; Dagna Hull, building a ship to come back, and Hollis Brine, sabotaging it with her own hammer because he heard Nerys Colm's invitation and knows there is no coming back; Commodore Lysander Vell; Brenna Kelp and the Kelp; Justice Maren Oake; Father Ebb, holding a broken chain closed in his fist.
- *The Rim:* Reeve Calder Stokes and his satchel; Auditor-General Lysander Coyle, who had the constant in 633.
- *Cross-border:* the Second Table, for whom the *Follow*'s hold is a carving party; the Vigil's brine-walkers, who will reach the coast at the season's end; Cray Lobbett and the table on the mud, toward which, and away from which, Ilse's chart runs.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Finn rings early and is reprimanded. Calder Stokes sets out for Tacit. Vell tries to buy Ilse's pages and Brenna's working. Whole Miles begin to fill on the same breath. |
| 2 | The Number Twice: Brenna's proof and Calder's meet, or are made to. The Admiralty kills to keep one of them quiet. The figure is posted on a tide-post on Mile Fourteen and torn down by morning. |
| 3 | The Company prices the coast in Tacit and sells the price. The Deepening prepares its largest procession. The *Follow* is floated; its air-bladder valve is fitted with a hand-lock on the inside. |
| 4 | The Low Water of 642. Every chest on the Table fills at once, and breathes in time with the Dowager Below. The ring on the Uncovered walks. The *Follow* goes down. The constant changes. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The Filling.** In a crowded Lowmark tavern on a drift night, sixty people choke at the same instant, without warning, mid-word and mid-swallow. They go purple together, bent double together, and sixty mouths spill the same cold grey water onto the boards together, a sound like a slop-bucket emptied down a stair, and then they straighten and look at one another, and nobody speaks, and at the far end of the room a man who did not finish coughing in time is lying on his face in the wet. Anyone not of the coast in that room is suddenly alone.
- **The Hold.** Below the *Follow*'s deck, behind a bulkhead Dagna never drew, Vell's hold: hooks, salt-tubs, a rack of eight grey knives, and lead-lined boxes the size of a man's torso, with drain-holes. It smells of cedar and fresh tar and, under that, a butcher's back room. Chalked on the bulkhead, in a neat hand, *First course*.

**What the Guests Can Do.** Bring Finn's count to the Admiralty, which does not want it, or to the Dowager Below, who may. Put Brenna and Calder in one cellar, the most dangerous meeting in Brinehollow, or keep them apart. Expose the *Follow*'s hold, which makes Dagna strike Vell; expose Hollis, which ruins her; or do nothing, and all three go down together. Read Maren Oake's marginal note and ask the Drowned Bench what the constant will change to.

**Endings.**
- *The Coast in One Breath:* the drift closes and does not open again. Brinehollow fills and empties as one body, forever, to the Dowager's breathing, and when she is still, everyone is still. The Admiralty calls it discipline.
- *The First Course Delivered:* the *Follow* reaches the bottom of the Trench, and Vell's knives find what is reassembling there. The air-bladder rises without anyone aboard. Months later, Second Table diners in four capitals report a dish that tastes of being held.
- *The Constant Changes:* at the Low Water, the ring walks inland, and the sea follows it, not as a wave but as a long, slow, calm return, through the people: every Tenanted soul on the coast begins, gently, to weep salt water, and does not stop, and the Uncovered goes dark and wet under the moon for the first time in six hundred years.

[box: What It Costs — The Low Water]
A non-Brinehollower in a room when the chests fill makes a Dread 2 check; a Brinehollower Guest on the night of Low Water makes a Grim Caul + Resolve roll or walks with the ring, and must be physically restrained (Gut + Labor, Hard 14, each minute for ten minutes). Bringing Brenna and Calder together marks every Guest present for the Admiralty's quiet men and for the Company's auditors. Sabotaging the *Follow* drowns its crew; saving its crew delivers its hold. Anyone who reads the Kelp and understands it takes a Dread 3 check and can never again watch a Tenanted bride walk up the steps without doing the sum.
[/box]

## Three, One, Three

[pull] There is someone at the door who has not been fed. Set another place. | — the lips of Aveline Morne, Bed One, as transcribed by Matron Cecily Dorm

**Summary.** *A new thread.* In Carving 640, Queen Dowager Sibyl Aubrey of Cradlewrack, forty-three years pregnant with a King who has ruled from her womb since 601, felt him kick three times, then once, then three, and again, until dawn. Nobody had asked him anything. It has happened every night since. Absalom Gage, the bricklayer of Kest, feels the same pattern in his walls: the knock the highlands give a new brick, three, then the answer, then three again. Old Hinge hears it from the far side of the Sill, answered from the Queen's chamber. In the Vigil, Corwin Half-Bell, the only man to turn around in the long room and come back, will not stand with his back to a door. In Oathen, between the beats of the new heart under the Gullet, there is a sound like a chair being drawn up to a table. The knocking comes from one place, and is heard in all of them.

[fiction]
*A clapping game of the Kest hills, played by girls on the steps of the pang-houses. Collected by an Attendance midwife, Carving 641, who notes that she had never heard it before that year and that every parish knows it.*
*Knock three, knock one, knock three again,*
*Who's on the step in the wind and the rain?*
*Knock three for the bread and one for the cup,*
*Three for the chair, and nobody get up.*
*Nobody answer and nobody tell,*
*Nobody open, and all will be well.*
*(At the last word the girls slap the step three times, then once, then three, and the one who is slowest must stand facing the door until the next pang.)*
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *Cradlewrack:* Queen Dowager Sibyl Aubrey and the King Within, whose kick is law; Lord Chamberlain Eamon Seele, who reads him questions; Absalom Gage; Old Hinge at the Sill; Una Knocker, whose longest-bricked debtor has begun, at night, to tap back.
- *The Vigil:* Corwin Half-Bell, who cannot bear an open door; Sister Vesper Null, who came back from the room on purpose, while the real Vesper stands at the back of it listening to the same knock; Aveline Morne in Bed One; Matron Cecily Dorm and her pencil.
- *Oathen:* Yusra Thole, whose goatskin of Gullet sand beats once a minute against the ear.
- *The Fast:* every Unfed household, which closes the door from the outside, opens it, and closes it from the inside, and has done so for six hundred years without anyone remembering why.
- *The Rim:* every innkeeper on the road, who has begun to hear a knock at the third hour of the night on the door that faces the middle of the continent.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | The King kicks the knock nightly. Gage's new walls knock back. The clapping game spreads through the Kest hills in a single season. |
| 2 | The knock is heard in the Vigil: doors in the Dormitory's oldest rows, struck from the room side. Corwin will not enter the building. Rim innkeepers begin nailing their middle-facing doors shut. |
| 3 | On a single night the knock is heard in every land at once, at the same hour. In the Fast, a hundred doors are found open in the morning, opened from the outside. Aveline speaks her sentence again, louder. |
| 4 | Someone answers. The knock stops, everywhere, for a full night. In the morning the King has turned in the womb, and lies facing north, and Old Hinge reports that the far side of the Sill is quiet, the way a room is quiet after a guest has come in. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The Throne Room.** The Queen Dowager, ninety, propped on a litter in a room of red silk, her belly vast and hard and blue-veined under a linen shift, the skin so stretched that it shines in the lamplight like a skinned grape. The Chamberlain reads a question. Nothing. Then, unprompted, the belly moves: a heel, or a fist, against the drum of her, from inside, three times. The courtiers do not breathe. Once. The Queen's mouth opens without sound. Three. Somewhere below the floor, in the old walls, a brick answers.
- **The Oldest Rows.** In the Dormitory at the third bell, on a night of Stage 2, a Guest sitting watch hears it: a knock, three, one, three, on the inside of a door at the end of a row where there is no door, only a whitewashed wall. Every sleeper in the row has turned their face toward the sound. Their eyes are closed. Their lips are wet.

**What the Guests Can Do.** Trace the knock from the Queen's womb to Gage's walls to the Sill to the Dormitory, and draw it on a map, and see that the lines meet. Ask the King, through the Chamberlain, where the knocking comes from; the answer, given by kick, is that it comes from the room with the table. Stop the clapping game. Bring Corwin and Vesper to Bed One at the same bell, where the rows will turn toward them and the knock may be heard from this side. Never, ever knock back.

**Endings.**
- *Nobody Answer:* the Guests persuade every land to keep its doors shut and its mouths shut. The knock goes on, nightly, everywhere, forever, and a generation grows up that has never slept through a night without it. The Fast's custom spreads to every land and nobody remembers why.
- *The King Delivered:* the King is born at last, in the Throne Room, forty-four years late, a man-sized infant with an old man's eyes, and his first act is to knock, three, one, three, on the floor of the world, from this side. Something on the other side is very pleased.
- *Someone Answers:* the knock stops. Read the Secrets chapter, and decide who opened the door.

[box: What It Costs — Three, One, Three]
Sleeping a week within the Aubrey palace gives a Guest the knocking dream; each further week is a Dread 2 check. Hearing the knock in the Dormitory's oldest rows is Dread 3. A Guest who knocks back gains the Derangement *The Late Guest* (they set an extra place at every meal, and become distressed and then violent if it is cleared) and the GM notes that the knock is one door nearer to them. Telling anyone in Cradlewrack that the King is answering, rather than speaking, is treason under the Royal Assent.
[/box]

## The Lead Coffin

[pull] You asked to be of use, dear boy. I have found you a use. | — Lord Absalom Hethe, letter to the west wing of Glovehall

**Summary.** *A new thread.* The Second Table has a problem: a god that is regrowing is a god that moves, and the Gorging worked because the Providers held still. Lord Absalom Hethe, Hand of the Court and a Trencherman since 625, has solved it. Osric Vane, Lady Corrow Vane's gentler brother, at the Brink and confined in Glovehall's west wing since his bare hand finished a Fatlands factor in 628, can close anything: a candle, a fly, a conversation, a Kept man through a door. He cannot refuse a request framed as a duty, and he longs to be useful. A Vigil clockmaker has drawn him a coffin, lead-lined, its breathing-holes meshed with lead, to hold in what he does until the lid is opened before a god on a table. Osric leaves Glovehall in Grace 642, declared to the Company as wine.

[fiction]
*Company freight docket, Fogmouth, Grace 642, draft. The clerk's pencil note is in another hand.*
Consignment: WINE, CLARET, one cask, coffin-form, lead-lined, Hethe cellars, for the account of a gentleman, to be carried east by the Rim to a destination to be given at Wanting Post. Weight: 14 stone. Handling: KEEP UPRIGHT. DO NOT OPEN. DO NOT SPEAK TO. *(Pencil:)* Flies dead for twenty paces round the dray. Carter's lamp went out twice. Cask asked me very kindly whether I was well.
[/fiction]

**The Players.**
- *Sallowreach:* Lord Absalom Hethe; Osric Vane, and his lead mittens, and the Fatlander Pell Ambry, whose face he sees in every face; Lady Corrow Vane, who keeps her brother in the west wing because she loves him and because she is afraid; the Mortuary Court, whose Hushes are fed, in part, by what Osric's presence does to the fen; Edda Thrush, the Hushwalker, who wants to see if Dunmere has moved.
- *The Rim:* the Company, which carries what is declared; the carters, who will not take the dray twice.
- *The Fast:* the Hostwatch, who would see the coffin come over the Hem; Orrum, who has been seen, in the Second Table thread, standing still as if waiting; Wont Bellamy, who will meet the dray at Wanting Post.
- *Cross-border:* Lord Absalom Thrale, Grand Carver; Commodore Vell and Lord Casimir Morwen, each of whom would like the coffin to come to their land's table first.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Hethe visits the west wing with letters and a request framed as duty. Osric agrees, and weeps, and thanks him. The coffin is built in a Lastgate cooper's yard; the cooper's hands go numb and stay numb. |
| 2 | Grace 642: the coffin leaves Glovehall. The small Hush in the Glovehall orchard stops growing. Fogmouth watches the dray go and the flies drop around it. |
| 3 | The dray moves east along the Rim. What leaks through the lead is enough: wherever it stops, fires gutter out, voices stop mid-word, and the sick die quietly in the night. Inns begin to refuse it. Sallowreach's Hushes, meanwhile, slow. |
| 4 | The coffin reaches its table. The lid is opened, in the presence of a god. The god is still. |

#### Scenes the Guests Might Witness

- **The West Wing.** A black double-barred door with a hatch. The sound goes first: footsteps soften, the flies stop, the lamp leans away. Then the voice, warm and educated: *Hello? Is someone there? I promise to keep my eyes shut.* Through the hatch, a gaunt man of fifty-four in a silk blindfold and lead mittens, whose bare wrists, where the mittens end, are grey and smooth as a church floor. He asks the Guests, very kindly, whether Lord Hethe's errand is a good one. He so wants it to be.
- **The Stopped Inn.** The Guests overnight at a Company inn where the cask is stabled. Nobody at dinner can finish a sentence. The hearth goes out three times and cannot be coaxed. The landlord's old dog lies down by the stable door and does not get up. Upstairs, a drover who was mending from a cough is found at dawn finished, with his eyes open and his face smoothed of every line, grey and polished as a church step, and his daughter is screaming at the stable for someone to take the cask away.

**What the Guests Can Do.** Visit Osric and tell him the truth about Hethe's use for him, which will break his heart and possibly the door. Hijack the dray; nobody can bring themselves to open the cask, and nobody should. Learn from the Glovehall gardeners what the Hushes do while Osric is gone, and decide whether a slowed fen is worth a held god. Follow the coffin to see whose table it reaches.

**Endings.**
- *The God Held Still:* the lid opens at Hallowboard on Tablenight, and Orrum, which has been running around the Rim laying tables for a year, stops, and lies down, and the Second Table takes up its knives. See the Second Table thread, and the Secrets chapter.
- *Of Use:* the Guests bring Osric home, or to somewhere better than home, and find him a duty worth his life: an hour a day at the Ferrying Post, mittens off, finishing by consent the Kept who petitioned for a Closing and could never pay. He does it gladly, for years, and the Mortuary Court, which sells Closings at four thousand lacks, never forgives the Guests.
- *The Lid Lifted Early:* someone opens the cask on the road. Osric, blind, ungloved, terrified, takes off his blindfold to see who has come. Everything within a quarter-mile finishes: people, horses, fires, sounds, the road itself, which is found afterward smooth and grey as his wrists. Osric is found standing in the middle of it, alone, apologizing.

[box: What It Costs — The Lead Coffin]
Standing in the same room as Osric is Dread 3; seeing him unmittened is Dread 4, and his bare touch finishes anything. Each night spent near the dray, a Guest at Ruin must pass Gut + Endure (Grim 18) or finish quietly before dawn. Stopping it makes enemies of Hethe and the Second Table; telling Osric the truth gives Lady Corrow Vane cause to close whoever told him. Delivering him anywhere at all is choosing what he is for, which nobody has ever let him do.
[/box]

## Where the Threads Meet

No thread in this chapter is complete on its own. The Deadwatch draws on the Lessening's lists, and the Lessening's lists feed the Undying Flame. The Low Water carries the Second Table's knives to the Trench, and the Lead Coffin carries their stillness to the Host. Brine in the Rouse fills the Vigil with the sea, and the sea's constant is being sold in Tacit while Tacit's god grows three days a year. The Missing Hand and Three, One, Three are the same labor heard from two ends. And all of them, followed far enough, lead to a long dim room, a table, and a chair that is not pushed in.

The GM need not tell the players this. They will notice. Let them notice in their own time, one knock, one place card, one empty chair at a time, and let the noticing cost them.

[bigquote] Seven tables, and seven peoples at them, and every one of us so glad to be eating that nobody looked behind. | — Grandfather Peat, through a translator, Lastgate, 641
