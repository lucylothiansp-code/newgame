# Blood on the Cloth

> There is no clean way to open a person. There are only quick ways and slow ways, and the slow ways are cheaper.
>> — Jem Crackle, the Cook's Knife, to an apprentice

[fiction]
The Warden came at Halloway with a boathook, the long kind the Netwatch use for pulling the Called out of the surf, and she knew from the way he held it that he had pulled more people out of things than he had ever put into them. That was his mistake. He wanted to hook her, drag her, take her alive. She only wanted him to stop.
She let the hook go past her ear. It caught her coat at the shoulder and tore it, and she stepped inside the length of the pole, where it was no use to anyone, and put the cleaver into the meat where his neck met his collarbone. His breath came out of him in a long, surprised whistle, and something warm and copper-smelling ran over her knuckles and into her sleeve.
He sat down. He kept looking at her. He was trying to say something about his wife. She worked the blade free, the way you do, a little rock and a twist, and that was worse, because then the blood had somewhere to go.
She had butchered four thousand animals in her life. Her hands knew this. That was the part she would not be able to forget: how well her hands knew it.
[/fiction]

Violence on the Table is fast, ugly and expensive. Fights are short. People who are hurt stay hurt. Guests who solve every problem with a blade will be dead, maimed, or something worse inside a few sessions, and they will deserve the stories told about them.

This chapter gives the rules for fighting: the round, the attack, the wound, the Mangling, and the long list of things people do to each other with hooks and cleavers in the dark. It ends with three fights played through from first blow to last, with the same company of Guests who walk through *The Grace Roll*: Ressa Weir, Halloway Brisk, Tolly Fane and Abate Kell.

[pull] Every fight on the Table ends the same way: somebody has to clean the floor. Ask yourself, before you start, whether it will be you. | — Netwatch drill-sergeant

## The Round

Combat is played in **rounds** of about three seconds. Each round, every combatant gets:

- **One action:** an attack, a dash, a grapple, a first-aid attempt, a shouted plea, a Sop, a Partake-and-act, anything that takes real effort.
- **One move:** a few steps, drawing a weapon, standing up, opening a door (in Cradlewrack it may already be open).

A character can give up their action for a second move. Talking a little is free.

### Initiative

At the start of a fight, everyone rolls **1d10 + Lung + Eye**. Act from highest to lowest. Ties go to the Guests. Those taken by surprise do not act in the first round. Initiative is rolled once for the whole fight, unless something dramatic resets it (a Hush opening, a pang, the lights going out in the Vigil).

Threat-rated adversaries roll **1d10 + Threat**. A mob or a swarm acts once, on a single initiative. The Carver may roll once for a whole group of identical foes and have them act together; it is faster, and it makes them feel like a pack.

### Surprise and Ambush

A fight that begins with one side unready begins with an **ambush**.

- **Setting it.** The ambushers roll **Lung + Stealth** (or Threat); use the *lowest* roll among them, because one cough gives everyone away. The ambushed roll **Eye + Search** (or Threat); use the *highest*, because one sharp eye is enough. If the ambushers win, they get a **surprise round**: one full round before the ambushed act at all. If they win with a Helping or more, every attack in that round counts the target as **unaware**.
- **Unaware targets.** An unaware target's Guard is 10, flat: no Lung, no skill. Attacks from hiding on a Silent weapon gain +2, as that quality says. The Reckoner's talent *In Their Beds* adds +4 damage against an unaware target. Sleeping targets are always unaware, and in the Vigil, a sleeping target is a crime scene.
- **Sensing it.** A character who loses the ambush roll may still roll **Caul + Instinct** at Hard (14). On a success they act in the surprise round, last. A *Light Sleeper* cannot be surprised while asleep or resting, as the Knack says, and the Carver should honour it with glee.
- **A failed ambush.** If the ambushed win, nobody is surprised; roll initiative as normal, and the ambushers are probably standing somewhere foolish.

[pull] The Reckoners say a Godeater dies in his bed or not at all. It isn't a boast. It's a method, and the method is patience. | — Dr. Mercy Thrane, notes

## Attacking

To attack, roll **2d10 + Hand + Blades** (for edged weapons), **Brawl** (for fists, clubs, teeth, grapples) or **Shooting** (for anything thrown or loosed), against the target's **Guard**.

- **Melee Guard** = 10 + Lung + the best of Athletics, Blades or Brawl.
- **Ranged Guard** = 10 + Lung + Athletics. Add +2 for partial cover, +4 for heavy cover. Firing into melee without hitting friends needs a Helping, or a friend is hit on a Lack.

If the attack total meets or beats the Guard, it hits.

### Damage

**Damage = weapon damage + 2 per Helping − the target's Armor.**

A hit that is reduced to 0 or less by Armor does no Flesh damage, but on a Grace it still inflicts a Mangling. Damage is subtracted from **Flesh**.

| Weapon | Damage |
| Fist, kick, bite | 1 |
| Knife, awl, needle, sling | 2 |
| Cleaver, hatchet, club, sickle, shortbow | 3 |
| Sword, axe, spear, boathook, harpoon, crossbow | 4 |
| Poleaxe, maul, greatcleaver, Tablebreaker's axe | 5 |
| Siege weapons, falling masonry, a Laden's weight | 6+ |

Weapon qualities (Hooking, Ripping, Heavy, Reach, Silent, Brutal, and so on) are listed in the Rim Market.

[box: Carver's Advice: Saying What the Blade Did]
Numbers kill people; words make it matter. After every hit, say one true thing about the wound before the next roll: where it went, what it sounded like, what it smelled like, what the hit person did with their face. Keep it to one sentence for ordinary damage. Save the paragraphs for Manglings and Grievous wounds, and for the first blood a Guest ever draws. If the table winces, you have said enough. If someone says the Grace, stop at once and describe nothing more than the number.
[/box]

### Grace and Lack in Combat

- **A Grace on an attack** inflicts a **Mangling** in addition to its damage (roll on the table below). Grace attacks are where people lose eyes and fingers.
- **A Lack on an attack** means the weapon sticks, breaks, flies from your hand, or hits someone you did not mean to hit; or you overextend and the target gets a free attack against you.
- **A Lack on a defense** (when you are rolling to resist a grapple, or the Carver calls for a roll to dodge something unusual) means you fall, drop something, or take a Mangling yourself.

### Other Actions

- **Grapple:** Hand + Brawl vs Guard. On a hit, the target is held: they can only try to break free (opposed Gut + Labor or Lung + Athletics) or attack with a small weapon at −2. A holder can choke (1 damage per round, ignoring Armor), drag, pin or bite.
- **Disarm:** attack at −2; on a hit, no damage, the target drops their weapon.
- **Shove or trip:** Gut + Labor or Hand + Brawl vs Guard; the target falls or is pushed back. Standing up takes a move.
- **Aim:** spend an action aiming to gain +2 on your next ranged attack.
- **Full Defense:** spend your action defending: +4 Guard until your next turn.
- **Flee:** move away. Anyone engaged with you gets a free attack unless you win an opposed Lung + Athletics roll.
- **Partake:** you can Partake on any combat roll. Many Gifts are combat abilities.

### More Maneuvers

The Table fights dirty, in kitchens and on piers and in the aisles of the Lofts. These maneuvers cover most of what people try. None of them stack with each other in the same action.

- **Charge.** Move and attack in one action, if you have a run of at least five paces: +2 to the attack, and −2 to your Guard until your next turn. A Reach weapon set against you strikes first.
- **All Out.** Throw yourself into the blow: +2 to attack and +1 damage, −4 Guard until your next turn. Renderers call it *going through the joint*.
- **Two Weapons.** With a Light weapon in the off hand, make one extra attack at −4 against the same or an adjacent target. A Lack on either attack is a Lack on both.
- **Feint.** Tongue + Deceive opposed by the target's Caul + Instinct (or Threat). Win, and your next attack against them this round or next counts them as unaware (Guard 10). Lose, and they know your tricks: −2 to feint them again this fight.
- **Bind.** Lock the enemy's weapon with yours, or with a hook, or with your own forearm: Hand + Blades vs Guard. On a hit, neither of you may attack with those weapons until one breaks the bind (opposed Gut + Labor). Others may attack the bound one at +2.
- **Brace.** Set a Reach weapon against a charge or a rush. The first enemy to close this round takes your attack before theirs, at +2.
- **Hold the Line.** Two or more fighters shoulder to shoulder in a doorway, on a pier, in a canyon cut: each gains +2 Guard while the other stands. Mobs can only reach a held line with as many attackers as there are bodies across it (see *Fighting Many*).
- **Cover.** Ready an action: name a target and a trigger (*if he moves for the lamp*). When the trigger happens, you act first.
- **Cast Into.** Shove a held or prone enemy into something worse: a fire, a fen, the Sump, a crowning, a laid table, off the end of the Mile. Gut + Labor vs Guard (Guard 10 if held). The Carver decides what the worse thing does; it is usually more than a weapon would.
- **The Dead as Shield.** Hold a body, or a Kept, between you and the blows: +2 Guard, and every hit that misses you by 2 or less hits the body. If the body is someone the enemy knew, they make a Dread check before they swing. If it is someone you knew, you make one when it is hit.
- **Rally.** Shout, slap, needle or bully a companion back into the fight: Tongue + Intimidate (or Rites) vs Routine (10). Remove 1 Fray from them, or let them shake off a freeze from a Lack on a Dread check. Once per companion per fight.
- **Yield.** Drop your weapon and beg. Roll Tongue + Persuade against Hard (14), or Grim (18) if you have killed one of theirs. Success means the enemy must make a choice, and most people on the Table, faced with a begging human being, do not strike. Failure means they will. The Carver should let NPCs Yield, often.
- **Drag.** Pull a fallen ally out of reach: your move and your action, Gut + Labor vs Routine (10), or Hard (14) under attack. Enemies engaged with you get a free attack.
- **Kill the Light.** Put out a lamp, a lantern, a candle in reach: an action, no roll unless someone protects it. See *Fighting in the Dark*.

### Called Shots

A fighter may aim for a particular part of the body. A Called Shot takes a penalty to the attack. If it hits, it deals damage as normal, and if it scores **at least one Helping**, the attacker may choose the matching Mangling result instead of the extra damage from that Helping. A Grace on a Called Shot inflicts the chosen Mangling *and* rolls on the table for another.

| Target | Penalty | With a Helping, choose |
| Hand or weapon arm | −2 | Knuckles; or the weapon drops |
| Leg or knee | −2 | Hamstrung; or the target falls |
| Face or mouth | −2 | Teeth; Jaw (with two Helpings) |
| Head | −4 | Skull |
| Eye | −4 | Eye |
| Throat | −4 | Throat (with two Helpings); otherwise target cannot speak or shout for 1d6 rounds |
| Belly | −2 | Gut-Stuck; Opened (with two Helpings) |
| A Kept's joint | −2 | Severed Hand or Severed Limb, for dismemberment |

Armor still counts, and the Carver may rule that a helm or a gorget protects the part entirely. Called Shots against an unaware target take no penalty. That is what assassins are for.

[pull] Aim for the hand that holds the hook. A man without his hook is only a man on a pier, and most of them remember they have mothers. | — Lieutenant Sable Reef, to recruits

### Fighting in the Dark

The Table is lit by tallow and oil, and a great many of its fights happen after the lamp goes over.

- **Dim light** (a single candle, moonlight on the Plate, fog-light in Sallowreach): −2 to attacks and to Search.
- **Darkness** (the lamp is out, a cellar, a new moon licked clean): −4 to attacks and Search, and everyone's Guard counts as though they had no skill bonus (10 + Lung only), because nobody can see a blow coming.
- **Finding a foe.** In darkness, a character must know where an enemy is before attacking: Caul + Instinct or Eye + Search (with the darkness penalty) against the foe's Lung + Stealth, as an opposed roll. On a loss they swing at where the enemy was. On a Lack, they hit a friend.
- **The lamp-bearer.** Whoever holds the light is seen by everyone and sees little beyond its circle. Attacks against a lamp-bearer from outside the light ignore the darkness penalty. Attacks from the lamp-bearer into the dark suffer it in full.
- **What sees in the dark.** A Brinehollower in the Course sees in total darkness. The Kept do not need to see to grasp, and they smell the living (−2 to Stealth against them for anyone not wearing vinegar or cedar). The Counted, the Seated and things born in Cradlewrack's lower wards behave as though it were noon. A Guest who Partakes may ask the Carver whether their Tooth gives them any sense in the dark; the answer should be in their land's idiom, and it should never be free.
- **In the Vigil.** There is no darkness, by law. When the lamps go out in the Vigil, it is because someone wanted them out, and every Vigilant within sight makes a Dread 1 check, because the dark is the closest thing in their lives to the room.

[pull] Put out the light, and every man in the room becomes the size of his fear. | — Lamplighters' Guild saying

### Fear in the Fight

A fight against horrors calls for a Dread check when the horror is first seen, not once per round. Seeing an ally Mangled is Dread 2. Killing someone for the first time is Dread 2 (Dread 3 for someone you knew, Dread 4 for kin).

A Guest who freezes, flees, screams or vomits on a Lack loses their next action, and the Carver should describe it. A Guest who reaches Fray 10 mid-fight Breaks at once, and their Break plays out during the fight; the contagion rules apply to everyone who sees it, friend and foe.

### Morale

Most people on the Table do not want to die, and most of them are hungry, tired and paid badly. Threat-rated adversaries check **morale** when: their side has lost half its number; their leader falls, flees or Yields; they suffer a Mangling or see one; or a horror of Dread 2 or more appears on the field. The Carver rolls **2d10 + Threat** against Hard (14). Failure means they break: they flee, Yield, or hold back and do nothing useful. A Lack means they turn on their own leader, or on whoever brought them here.

Some never check: the Kept, the Called, the Seated, the faithful in their rite, the starving at a table, anything Ridden by a god, and Reckoners within reach of a high Cut. Some always check at Grim (18): conscripts, debtors on the dusk shift, a mob without a preacher.

## Flesh, Ruin and Death

**Flesh** = 8 + Gut + Endure. A Guest at half Flesh or less is **Bloodied**: −1 to all rolls.

When Flesh reaches **0**, the character suffers **Ruin**: they take an **Injury** (roll on the Mangling table) and collapse. They can crawl, whisper, and do nothing else useful. They are **Dying**.

A Dying character who takes any further damage is **dead**. A Dying character who is not treated within (Gut) minutes must roll **Gut + Endure** (Hard 14) every minute; failure means death. A successful first-aid roll stabilizes them at 1 Flesh.

In **Sallowreach**, nothing dies. A character who would die there becomes **Kept**: see that chapter. They keep their Injuries. They keep everything.

Damage below 0 does not carry over; Flesh stops at 0, and the next point of damage is the end. A Seamer's *Reseat* (the Seamer Calling) may spare a Ruined character the Injury, but not the collapse. Ruin heals: a Ruined character who is stabilized and rests recovers Flesh as normal, and is up and walking, badly, at a quarter of their Flesh.

[box: Damage on the Table, by the Numbers]
An ordinary Guest has 10 to 14 Flesh. A knife does 2 to 6, a sword 4 to 8, a crossbow bolt at close range 4 to 10. Two or three solid blows will drop anyone. A Grace can end a fight at once. Armor matters enormously: boiled leather turns a knife into a bruise. The cheapest way to survive a fight is not to be in it. Carvers should let NPCs surrender, flee, bargain and beg, and they should do so often. On the Table, everybody is somebody's kin.
[/box]

## The Mangling Table

Roll **1d20** when a Grace attack lands, or when a character suffers Ruin. Add +2 if the weapon is **Brutal** or the damage dealt was 8 or more. Every Mangling is also a **Dread 2** horror for those who see it happen to someone they care about.

| 1–2 | **Laid Open** | A long shallow cut that unzips the skin and shows the yellow fat beneath. Bleeds freely: 1 damage per round until bound. Scar. |
| 3 | **Knuckles** | Fingers broken or split to the bone. −2 to Hand rolls until healed (2 weeks). On a Lack later, one finger is lost. |
| 4 | **Teeth** | A blow to the mouth: teeth spat out in pieces with the blood, lips torn. −2 to Tongue rolls for a week; permanent gaps. |
| 5 | **Rib-Crack** | You hear it before you feel it: a green-stick crack, then the grinding. −2 to Lung rolls; every deep breath is a knife (4 weeks). |

| 7 | **The Ear** | Torn half off, hanging by a flap of gristle. −2 to hearing-based Search. Stitched back crooked, or lost. |
| 8 | **Gut-Stuck** | A wound in the belly that lets out the smell of the bowel. Fever in 1d3 days unless treated (Grim 18 Stitching); −2 to all rolls while it festers. |
| 9 | **Eye** | The eye is punctured, crushed, or hangs on its cord. Lost. −2 to Search and Shooting thereafter (−1 after a month of adjustment). Dread 2 to see. |
| 10 | **Skull** | The scalp splits and the bone beneath dents like an egg. Stunned for 1d6 rounds; roll Gut + Endure (Hard 14) or lose memory of the last day. |

## The Grievous Table

Some blows are worse than a Grace. A **Grievous** blow is any of the following:

- A Grace on an attack that also scores **two or more Helpings**.
- A Grace with a **Heavy** or **Brutal** weapon against a **held, prone or unaware** target.
- The killing stroke of an **execution** that goes wrong (see *Executions*).
- Being crushed by a Laden's weight, falling from the end of the Mile, a pang bringing down a house, a Tablebreaker's axe in a frenzy, or anything else the Carver judges to be more than a body can be asked to bear.

On a Grievous blow, roll **1d20** on this table **instead of** the Mangling table. Grievous wounds are Dread 3 for anyone who cares about the victim, and Dread 2 for everyone else who sees them. Most are permanent. Several are fatal outside Sallowreach, and inside it, they are simply what you will be from now on.

## Special Situations

### Fighting the Kept

The Kept of Sallowreach cannot be killed. Reducing one to 0 Flesh drops it, and it goes on moving: crawling, biting, grasping with whatever is still attached. To stop a Kept enemy you must **dismember** it: each Mangling result of Severed Hand, Severed Limb or Butchered removes the use of that part. Burning helps; it does not finish. Hand-line touch and the Hushes are the only true ends.

This is the **Unending** ability given in *Things That Eat and Are Eaten*: a Kept at 0 Flesh is Undone for 1d6 rounds, then recovers 1d6 Flesh and resumes, unless it has been dismembered, sunk, Closed, or carried across the border. The two rules are one rule. Undone, it crawls and grasps at −4; recovered, it fights as before with whatever it has left.

#### Kept Enemies, Clarified

- **What a Kept feels.** Everything. A Kept does not check morale, because it cannot die and knows it, but it feels every blow, and it may scream, beg or curse the whole time. A Kept that begs is Dread 2 to keep fighting.
- **Taking them apart.** A Called Shot at a Kept's joint (−2) with a Helping severs it (Severed Hand at the wrist, Severed Limb at the elbow or knee). A Kept with no working limbs is out of the fight; a Kept with its head off is out of the fight but not out of the conversation.
- **What the parts do.** Severed parts of a Kept go on. Roll a d6 for each: 1–2 it lies twitching; 3–4 it crawls toward the nearest warm body (Threat 0, Flesh 3, Attack +2, damage 1, Grapple 10); 5 it goes back to its owner and tries to reattach; 6 it goes somewhere else, and the Carver writes down where.
- **Fire.** A burning Kept takes 2 damage a round, does not stop, and spreads the fire to whatever it grasps. Fires in Sallowreach smoulder for decades. A Kept that has burned is Rot +2 and Dread 2 to see.
- **Vinegar and cedar.** The Kept find the living by smell. A character doused in fen-vinegar or cedar perfume is −2 to be found by a Kept in the dark, and a character with *Vinegar Kiss* is ignored by Kept until they act.
- **Kept Guests.** A Kept player character follows the same rules, with Rot as the Sallowreach chapter gives it. They are terrifying allies in a fight, and the Carver should make them pay for it in Rot.

[box: Why You Do Not Fight in the Sinks]
The Kept outnumber the breathing nine to one in Sallowreach, and every one of them feels every blow and cannot die of it. A brawl in a Lastgate tavern does not end; it goes on, in a smaller and sadder way, for decades, in the Lofts and the Jar Room and the back rooms of part-thieves. Captain Dace Morrow, whose army cannot lose a man, has a standing order that his soldiers are never to fight the Kept at all, only to restrain them. His soldiers are Kept. They know.

### Fighting Many

Mobs, the Forsworn of the Breakdowns, a Reaping crowd, the Finishers walking in: treat a mob as one character with Flesh equal to 5 × its number, Threat equal to 1 + one per 5 members (max 5), and damage equal to its best weapon + 1 per 5 members. Each 5 Flesh lost drops one member. Mobs break and run when they lose half their number, unless they are faithful, starving or Called.

**Clarifications.** A mob attacks with **Threat + 3** and has **Guard 10 + Threat**, Armor equal to what most of its members wear (usually 0). It acts on one initiative and makes one attack a round against each target in its reach. The mobs already written up in *Things That Eat and Are Eaten* (the Forsworn Mob, the Called Procession, the Two-Legged Cattle) use their own quick lines, which already account for all this; the formula here is for the mobs the Carver makes up on the night.

- **Width.** A mob can only bring as many members to bear on a held line as there is room. In a doorway or on a pier, a mob attacks with Threat 1 + one per 5 members who can actually reach, however many are behind. This is why the Netwatch fight on the Mile and the Breakdowns' slum-lanes are a yard wide.
- **Fire and fear.** Fire, a crossbow volley, a horror of Dread 2+ or the fall of a preacher forces an immediate morale check, even before half are down.
- **Crush.** A character caught in a moving mob without a weapon out, or knocked down in one, takes the mob's damage each round, ignoring Armor, until they win an opposed Gut + Athletics roll to get out or up.
- **The Pall.** A mob is a community at its worst. A mob that forms in a town adds +1 to the town's Pall; a mob that kills adds +2. A mob that Guests disperse without bloodshed (by Yield, by Rally, by a Celebrant's preaching) lowers the Pall by 1.

### Fighting Gods

You cannot. A Seated god has no Flesh to take. The rules for what you can do instead are in *Things That Eat and Are Eaten*.

### Executions, Torture and Murder

Not every killing is a fight. When a Guest kills a helpless person, no roll is needed for the act. The roll is afterward: a Dread check at Dread 2 (Dread 3 for someone who begged; Dread 4 for kin). Torture extracts information on an opposed roll of the torturer's Tongue + Intimidate against the victim's Caul + Resolve, but the Carver decides how true the answers are, and the torturer makes a Dread 2 check each session they continue. Jack Tenterhook of the Vigil says the difference between his work as a wake-valet and his old job is the tipping. He is lying, and it shows on him.

The full rules for torture, including the **Stain** it leaves on the torturer, are in *The Grace Roll*. Kin-slaying by your own hand is Dread 4 whatever the circumstances, and on the Table, where everyone is somebody's kin, the Carver should ask before each killing whose cousin this was.

### Ritual Killing

Some killings are meant to buy something. The Second Helping offers the eaten to the god of feeding. The Cullmasters walk the dusk shift into the fields believing the Reaping can be steered. The Deepening walks the willing into the sea with lanterns. The Purgation burns high-Cut houses with the families inside to starve the god of its vessels. The Second Table rehearses. In a hundred villages, in the worst years, the elders draw lots.

A **ritual killing** is a killing performed as a rite: with words, with a place, with a method fixed by doctrine. It is led by one celebrant, who rolls **Tongue + Rites** against Hard (14), or Grim (18) if the victim resists or begs. Every participant and witness makes a **Dread 3** check, as the Dread table gives, and those who take part willingly each mark one **Stain**, as the torture rules give. A ritual killing adds +2 to the Pall of any community that knows of it, whether or not it approved.

Then the celebrant rolls on the table below to see what the rite has bought. The Carver may choose instead, but should be honest with herself about which result the world would give.

[pull] We gave it my sister and the sea still went out. We gave it Hask's boy and the sea still went out. Mother Limpet says the sea is not going out. She says it is going home, and we are the road. | — Wreckwife, Lowmark

| d10 | What the rite buys |
| 1–5 | **Nothing.** The god was not listening, or was, and did not care. The field still Reaps. The sea still falls. The interval still shortens. The celebrant will say it worked. |
| 6 | **Nothing yet.** A sign that something was heard: the wheat leans, the tide stirs, a door opens. The congregation will want another. |
| 7 | **A sop.** Every participant with a Cut loses 1d6 Hunger, as if they had performed their Sop, and gains the god's Want of that land for a day. |
| 8 | **The wrong thing.** The god takes the offering, and something else besides: a participant's Regrowth rises by 1, or the next Reaping takes the celebrant's own child. |
| 9 | **A delay.** The thing feared holds off for one Turn of the Pall, one pang, one Reaping, one night. Then it comes, as it was always going to. |
| 10 | **Attention.** Something answers: a god's attention, Dread 4, falls on the celebrant. The Carver decides what it wants from them now. |

This table is the Table's answer to sacrifice. The Long Grace contains no rite that saves a village, no offering that turns back a god, and no victim whose death was worth it. The demand is real; the giving is real; the grief is real; the bargain is not. Run every ritual killing knowing that, and let the Guests find it out.

Pell Hogget's tally for Sallet Bottom that year, copied in two hundred villages, shows the Reaping took nine. It took nine the year before. Crust Leggat's name is not among them. That is the whole of what the company won, and the Carver should make sure the players understand that it was everything.
