# The Table and Its Gods

These are the first nine plates of the set, and the ones every other plate answers to. The land plates show the Table as its peoples live in it now: the fens, the plains, the drying coast, the lit city, the clay, the canyons, the empty middle. The god plates show what those peoples ate to get there. Every god in this chapter was a body. It had a temperature, a smell, a weight that pressed the ground flat, a way of breathing that was the weather of its country. The painter's first job is to make the viewer believe that, because the whole horror of *The Long Grace* rests on it: these were not ideas that died. They were neighbours, and they were carved.

Each plate keeps the shared frame: the thin gold double-line border, the diamond corners, the land's sigil in the notch at top centre, the title in widely spaced light capitals and the italic subtitle in the lower centre band. Keep that band dark and quiet in every composition. The god is never in it. The god is always just above it, or to one side of it, looking at it, the way a dish looks at whoever is sitting down.

Plates two to seven each pick one of two moments: the night of the eating, from inside the crowd, or the god's Regrowth now, as it pulls itself back together out of its people. Plate eight shows the one god still walking. Plate nine shows a gap.

Painters should read **The Story in the Frame** first. It holds the god's whole body as its people knew it, and none of it is metaphor.

## THE LONG GRACE
[plate: world]
[pull] In the Long Grace, my grandmother kept bees. | — overheard at Fogmouth; the speaker's grandmother is still living
**Plate:** The Long Grace · *The Table, six hundred and forty-one years after the meal* · The Table · bone-white on pure black
#### The Shot
An impossible height, but not a map. The camera stands on the last broken shoulder of the salt wastes beyond the eastern rim, far above the canyons, on a 24mm lens tilted slightly down, so the whole continent lies before us under night the way a plate lies before someone who has just sat down. The Table is round, nine hundred miles across, and from here it is seen as an ellipse foreshortened into the 3:1 frame, its far edge curving off into the black at both sides. The left third holds Brinehollow and Cradlewrack: the western coast a bone-pale crust where the sea has pulled back, the Uncovered glittering like spilled sugar. The right third holds the near ground: the lip of Oathen's canyons cracking away under the camera like the rim of a dropped dish, ink-black fissures running toward us. The centre third is the Fast: a disc of perfect, empty black, the hole the ring of lands is built around. The Rim Road circles it as a thin unbroken line of tiny lamp-points, the inns lit for the night, a necklace laid around nothing. Fog lies over Sallowreach at the top of the frame as a grey film; the Vigil is a smudge of soot-light at upper right. Exactly at the centre of the black disc, very small, is one point of light that is not a lamp. The Plate, the moon, hangs full and low at upper left, enormous, its face scored with faint marks like knife-scratches on pewter. The lower centre band, where the title sits, is the dark southern Fast and the Hem of the Rim Road: calm, black, unlit.
#### Light and Colour
Two lights only. The full Plate rakes the continent from upper left, cold, bone-white, catching every ridge and salt-flat and leaving the hollows absolute black. The Rim Road lamps are pinpoints, the same bone-white, no warmth allowed, so the ring reads as stitches rather than comfort. The single point at Hallowboard is the only exception and it must be ambiguous: almost white, perhaps a breath of warmth, small enough that a viewer is not sure they saw it. No other colour. No sky gradient; the black above the horizon is the same black as the Fast.
#### Texture and Flesh
Paint the land as a carcass that has healed badly. The canyons in the foreground are dry-brushed scar tissue, the stone flaking in layers like old fat on a cold joint. Ridges radiate outward from the middle the way ribs radiate from a spine, faint, never stated, only felt in the dry-brush direction of the strokes. The salt of the Uncovered is scumbled thick and cracked, like the glaze on a dish left in an oven too long. The fog over the fens has the texture of a cataract. The black of the Fast is not flat: it is laid in many thin glazes so it has depth, wet and endless, like the inside of a mouth seen from far away. Ink spatter drifts across the upper sky like crumbs, which is what the Table calls stars.
#### The Story in the Frame
This is the Table now, in 641 A.G., seen as nobody on it ever sees it: whole, at once, from the outside, on the night before something. Six peoples live on this rim of land around an empty middle, each in the country where it ate its god, each carrying that god in its blood as a set of Cuts. They trade with one another along the Rim Road, which runs two thousand four hundred miles around the Hem and never crosses in. They say *Lack keep away* before eating with strangers, and none of them knows who the grace is addressed to. They are all, at this moment, sick with the same thing at the same speed, and each government is hiding its numbers.

The image must feel like a table laid and waiting. The continent is round because a plate is round. The Rim Road is the gold rim of the china. The lands are the courses set around it, and the black disc of the Fast is the bare place at the middle where the dish will go. Everything on the Table has been eaten once already; the viewer should feel that it is about to be served again.

What happened just before this moment is six hundred and forty-one years of digestion. What happens next is the end of the Grace: the College's graphs bent upward in every land after 600, all at once, which is impossible if the Regrowths are separate things. The gods are getting up. The point of light at Hallowboard is the Host, awake, laying a table. Nothing else in the frame is moving, but the painter should make it feel as if the whole continent has just, very slightly, been turned toward someone.
#### Hidden Details
- The Rim Road lamps are not evenly spaced. Counted, the clusters at the great inns form seven groups; there is an eighth gap on the ring between the Fast-side of Sallowreach and the Hem near Wanting, where no inn has ever stood. Players who count chairs will find the count never closes.
- The scratches on the face of the Plate, read at a slant, are a knife and fork laid side by side, the way a diner signals that they have not finished.
- The faint ribs in the land converge not on Hallowboard but a hair to one side of it: on the eighth side of the stone table.
- At the far western edge, out past the Uncovered on the drained seabed, there is one tiny rectangular shadow: Cray Lobbett's table, with a single long shadow cast from one of its chairs.
- A line of very small pale marks crosses the western sea toward the coast like footprints on wet sand. It runs in, not out.
- The fog over Sallowreach has a gap in it shaped like a pair of open hands.
#### Painter's Prompt
A vast cinematic 3:1 night panorama from an impossible high cliff at the edge of a salt wasteland, looking down across a whole round continent laid out like a plate: cracked canyon rim in the black foreground, a pale coast crusted with salt at left, grey fog over fens at top, a thin glittering ring of tiny inn lamps circling a perfectly black empty heartland at the centre, one minute ambiguous light at its exact middle; an enormous low full moon at upper left scored with faint knife-scratches; strictly bone-white on pure black, near-monochrome, dark oil painting with dry-brush and ink-wash texture, land rendered like healed scar tissue and faint radiating ribs, ink spatter for stars, huge negative space, the lower centre left dark and calm for a title, quiet dread, a world waiting to be served.
**Negative:** bright colours, blue sky, sunrise, cartoon, fantasy map labels, compass rose, legible text, modern objects, satellites, city lights grid, clean digital gloss, lens flare, saturated green, cheerful mood, visible gods, figures in foreground

## OSSEL, THE CLOSING HAND
[plate: ossel]
[pull] The Hand did not stop us. It only reached over our heads, now and then, to close the eyes of the ones we were too slow for. | — the Corse telling, as kept in the Lofts
**Plate:** Ossel, the Closing Hand · *God of death and endings* · Sallowreach · bruise-green and fly-black on tea-stain sepia
#### The Shot
Eye level of a standing fen-woman, slightly below, on a 35mm lens, the camera among the crowd and shouldering through it, so the first plane is the backs of the starving: ragged hoods, bone-knives held low, sharp shoulder-blades under wet cloth, out of focus and almost black. The left third is Ossel's length running away from us into the fog along the reed-bank below what will be Lastgate: a grey shape like a fallen ash tree as long as a road, half sunk in black water, its far end lost. The centre third, upper half, is the moment of the plate: one of the great hands lifted off the peat, slow as a heron, reaching over the heads of the crowd, a fingertip the size of a loaf descending toward something small and wrapped that a mother holds at the back of the throng. The fingers are too long and have too many joints, nine and ten each. In the midground below the hand, on the bank, an old woman sits in a reed hurdle with her mouth working: Agna Corse, eating the last joint of the smallest finger. The right third is the fire and, beyond it, across black water, the empty bank on the far side: reeds, fog, and a place where the fog bends around nothing, very tall, sitting. One small boy at the extreme right edge of the crowd is turned away from the god, staring at that bank. The lower centre band is the black water and the crowd's backs: dark, still, unreadable.
#### Light and Colour
The fire at right is the key light, low and sepia, throwing the crowd into silhouette and catching the underside of the lifted hand from below so its palm glows like old tallow. Cold fog-light from above flattens everything else to tea-stain brown and grey. The accent is bruise-green, sparing: in the cut flesh where the finger-joint came away, in the lips of the families who have eaten from the hands, in a faint sheen on the black water. Fly-black is the darkest value: the crowd, the far reeds, the hollow inside the fog on the empty bank.
#### Texture and Flesh
Ossel's skin is grey the way fog is grey, finely ridged like a fingernail, dry-looking but beaded with condensation. Where the knives have gone in, the flesh beneath is cold and pale and wet, the texture of a cellar mushroom, and the paint should look chilled: no warmth in the reds at all, only a bruised lilac-green going to grey at the edges. Knuckle joints lie open on the peat, small and white, many to a finger, each cracked clean like the hinge of a box. The fen-folk's knives are ground from cattle shoulder-blades and still show the bone grain. Their lips are grey by the light. Breath does not smoke from the mouths of the ones who have eaten most. The peat is black and glistening, studded with tiny fired-clay votive hands, palms up. Flies hang in the fog in a fine drift, motionless, as if they too were waiting.
#### The Story in the Frame
Ossel lay in the northern fens for as long as anyone remembered, long and grey and still as a fallen ash, half in the black water and half on the bank. It had a body, but the fen never really looked at the body; it looked at the hands. They were enormous, each wide enough to cup a cottage, and they lay open on the peat, palms up, and they were the only part of the god that ever moved. The fingers had nine and ten joints apiece. The face, which few paintings show, was a long grey oval low to the water, lidded, patient, more like the idea of a face than a face. It smelled of cellars and turned earth and the inside of a closed box. When someone died, their kin carried them on a reed hurdle and laid them within reach, and talked to them of eel-traps and courting until the hand came, and closed their eyes and mouth and folded their arms. Then the body went into the Sweetwaters and was clean in a season. People who lived beside Ossel were calm about everything.

This plate is the first night of the eating, in the ninth year of the Lack. The fen-folk stood around their god for hours with knives and could not begin. While they stood, children at the back of the crowd died of hunger, and each time the hand lifted over everyone's heads and closed the child's eyes with one fingertip. Show only the lifted hand and the small wrapped shape it reaches for; never the child. It did that eleven times before ninety-year-old Agna Corse asked her grandson to cut her the last joint of the smallest finger, because she would not last till morning and would like to have eaten something. She is eating it now, raw, with her gums. She is, by some accounts, still upright in a Lastgate loft.

What happens next: the fen eats slowly, ashamed, all night, and the families who take the hands become the Hand-lines. The boy at the edge of the frame runs into the fen before dawn and the water takes him; he is Grandfather Peat. He says the god was smiling. In the painting, Ossel's low grey face, half turned toward the empty bank, should be doing something at the corners of its mouth that a viewer cannot quite name.
#### Hidden Details
- The left thumb's last joint lies on the peat untouched, already beginning to curl closed, as if holding something very small. It is the one piece nobody would take.
- Twelve tiny clay votive hands lie in the peat near the mother; one has two thumbs. Twelve children, eleven closings: the Tasker twins went in one breath, under one fingertip.
- On the far bank, the reeds are bent outward in a ring around an empty space, as if something large were sitting there and the reeds were giving it room.
- Agna Corse's grandson's knife hand is already grey to the wrist. He will be sunk in the Sump for five hundred years.
- One huge fingernail on the right hand has been half pared away: the slate-sized nail later kept in the Hall of Closings.
- The boy staring at the far bank is the only figure in the crowd whose breath smokes.
#### Painter's Prompt
Dark painterly 3:1 night scene in a fog-drowned fen, shot from inside a crowd of starving peasants seen from behind as black silhouettes with bone knives; an immense grey god lies half sunk in black water like a fallen ash tree, its colossal open hands on the peat, fingers with too many joints; one hand lifts slowly over the crowd, a loaf-sized fingertip descending toward a small wrapped bundle a mother holds; an ancient toothless woman eats a pale severed finger-joint in the foreground; across a low fire, an empty reed-bank where the fog bends around something unseen and tall; tea-stain sepia and grey with bruise-green accent in cut flesh and grey lips, fly-black shadows, oil and ink texture, cold chilled flesh rendered like mushroom and cellar stone, lower centre left dark for title.
**Negative:** bright colours, warm red blood, cartoon, visible child faces, gore on children, heroic poses, armour, text artefacts, modern objects, clean digital gloss, sunny sky, saturated green grass, glowing magic effects, monster teeth on the god

## UMMER, THE LADEN
[plate: ummer]
[pull] We ate the god and the god watched us do it, kindly, the way a mother watches. That is the part I cannot forgive it for. | — Grace-Keeper Amity Rowle of Dimble, 590 A.G.
**Plate:** Ummer, the Laden · *God of harvest and plenty* · Fatlands · raw meat red and tallow yellow
#### The Shot
Low and close, almost lying in the grain, a 28mm lens at knee height on the great threshing floor at Brawn Tilth, the place the south now calls the Trencher. The god fills the frame from edge to edge like a fallen barn. The left third is its head, resting on the floor-stones, enormous and soft, one huge clouded eye open toward the camera and the mouth carved by nature into the smile every corn-dolly copies. In the near left foreground a woman sits in the hollow under its jaw with a baby, chewing. The centre third is the flank, opened: people with their faces in its side, arms buried to the shoulder, ribs prised apart and meat handed back over heads into the dark. The belly has been opened and grain is pouring out in a hot wave, knee-deep, spilling toward the camera across the stones, and in the grain are small white things like teeth. The right third holds the hind quarters and, behind them, the black fields of the plains under a starless sky. In the midground right a drover sits in the grain eating while his wife, behind him, stitches his split belly closed with a harness needle. In the far background, barely visible against the dark field, there is one figure standing upright and still, close to the god's rump, with its hand resting on the Laden's back. The lower centre band is the spill of grain and grease in deep shadow: textured, calm, dark.
#### Light and Colour
Torches stuck in the grain, many of them, low and smoky, throw a greasy amber light upward across the flank. The accents are both land colours and they should feel edible: tallow yellow in the fat layer and the grain, raw meat red in the open flesh and the gravy running between the floor-stones. Everything else is near-monochrome umber and black. The god's eye catches one cold point of light that is not torchlight. The sky is black, and the smoke from the torches hangs in it like steam over a pie.
#### Texture and Flesh
Ummer's hide is earth and bark and pelt at once: a furrowed crust like a loaf's, matted with thistle-down, with wheat-stubble growing out of it in places like the hair on a knuckle. Under the crust is a hand's depth of yellow fat, glossy, warm, beaded, and under the fat is dense dark meat bleeding gravy that steams in the cold. Paint the opened flank thickly, impasto, so the fat looks like it would give under a thumb. The grain spilling out is hot, gold, clotted with grease; the small white teeth in it are rendered exactly like husks until you look. Faces of the eaters are slick to the eyebrows. The drover's wound is a dark line with grain running from it into his lap, the waxed thread pulling it shut. Grease shines on every floor-stone like a wet road.
#### The Story in the Frame
Ummer was the one god that walked. It was a vast, slow, broad-backed shape like a laden haywain made of earth and bark and pelt, the size of a barn, head always down, ambling across the southern plains, and the fields bowed toward it as it passed and rose up heavier behind it. Its legs were thick as granary posts, its feet broad as carts and soft-soled, and its footprints never failed a seed. It shed a warm, faintly sweet down in Carving that the women spun into swaddling. Its face was long and gentle, an ox's face crossed with a loaf's, with clouded kind eyes and a wide mouth that always seemed to be smiling. It smelled the way an oven smells when you open it. Children rode on its back the length of the parish at Harvest Home. It was the most loved of the gods and the most taken for granted.

This plate is the last evening of the ninth Carving, when Ummer came in off the stubble and lay down on the threshing floor like an ox that has decided. There was no ceremony and no first cut. The starving went toward the smell on hands and knees and bit it. The Gut-line families were nearest the belly and opened it, and the floor was knee-deep in grain. Kell Sowerby is eating until his belly splits, and his wife is stitching him while he eats; he will live to ninety and never once feel full. The woman under the jaw is chewing meat and giving it to her baby with her tongue; that baby's descendants sit on the Board of Plenty.

The god's eye is open. It has been open the whole time. It is watching them the way a mother watches. Paint the eye so the viewer cannot look away from it, and cannot find any reproach in it. By dawn there will be nothing but grease and grain and people asleep in it with their mouths still moving, and around noon they will notice that the hunger has not stopped. It never has. Now, in 641, Ummer is regrowing in the Laden, and the Fatlands say the land will one day stand up and walk.
#### Hidden Details
- The figure standing behind the god's hindquarters, hand on its back, appears in every surviving Fatlands procession frieze. Nobody has ever named it. Its face is turned not toward the feast but toward the empty fields.
- Among the white teeth in the grain, one is clearly a human milk-tooth. The Fatlands wheat that grows teeth before ears started here.
- A corn-dolly lies trampled in the grease, its red-thread mouth stitched wider than any made before the Gorging.
- The harness needle in the wife's hand is the heirloom the Sowerby grandchildren still show at weddings.
- One forefoot is folded under the body; the other is pressed into the clay just outside the floor, making the last footprint, the one that never dries.
- The god's mouth is not quite closed. Between its lips, in the shadow, a little grain is still being offered outward, as if it were trying to feed them too.
#### Painter's Prompt
Dark oil-painted 3:1 low-angle scene on a torchlit stone threshing floor at night: an immense gentle barn-sized god made of earth, bark and pelt lies on its side, its kind clouded eye open and watching, its wide mouth smiling, while a starving crowd tears into its opened flank with bare hands and faces; a hand-thick layer of glossy yellow fat over dark bleeding meat, a hot wave of grain pouring from the opened belly across the stones, small white teeth hidden among the husks; a drover eats while his wife stitches his split belly with a harness needle; a still figure stands far behind the god, hand on its back; near-monochrome umber with raw meat red and tallow yellow accents, thick impasto fat, smoky torchlight, grease shine, lower centre left dark for title.
**Negative:** bright colours, clean butchery, cartoon, cute animal, gore on children, baby in danger, modern farm equipment, text artefacts, clean digital gloss, daylight, blue sky, heroic composition, glowing magic, sexualised figures

## DROMM, THE FATHOM
[plate: dromm]
[pull] The sea gave back every one of them that night. It has not given back one since. | — Lowmark tide-post inscription, cut 118 A.G.
**Plate:** Dromm, the Fathom · *God of the deep and what is kept under* · Brinehollow · drowned teal and salt-white
#### The Shot
From the water, at the level of a man in a rowing boat, on a 50mm lens with the horizon set high, looking up the shingle at Longslab on the third evening of the hauling. The foreground is surf and rope: ten thousand lines running out of the black water up the beach, taut, singing, hands wrapped in sailcloth gripping them, and a boat's gunwale cutting the bottom left corner. The left third is the shore crowd hauling, bent nearly double, their mouths open, threads of blood running from their ears down their jaws. The centre third, rising out of the shallows and onto the stones, is Dromm: a grey, slick mass like a reef that breathes, the size of a hill, no face anywhere on it, its skin moving in long slow ripples like the water it has left. The hooks are sunk in it by the thousand, and on the barbs, caught in the god's hide, are the drowned it kept: bundles of bone wrapped in weed, white dough-soft bodies swollen out of their clothes, one fresh, his eyes open, his hair moving in the wash as if nodding. The right third is the beach where the drowned have been laid out in long rows on the shingle, covered with sail, and beyond them the Lung-line families have already cut in under the god's ribs and stand chest-deep in a gush of brine pouring from it. The sea in the background is pulling back from under the boats. The lower centre band is the wet foreground surf, dark and even, with the ropes fading into it.
#### Light and Colour
A low sullen dusk from behind the camera, out over the sea, almost gone, giving everything a cold flat sheen. The accent is drowned teal: in the deep water, in the shadows under the god's ripples, in the faces of the long-drowned. Salt-white for the foam, the swollen bodies, the crust drying on the hauling ropes and on the hauler's beards. Lantern-wax gleams faintly on the face of the freshest drowned man, the last warm note in the image, almost gone. The sky above is the colour of a gutted fish's belly.
#### Texture and Flesh
Dromm's skin is the texture of wet slate and deep-sea fish together: slick, grey, mottled with barnacle-crust and pale lice, the ripples across it painted as long liquid strokes. Where the hooks go in, the hide puckers like a lip. The cut under the ribs reveals lungs the size of chapels, folded grey sponge running with cold water, glossy and swollen. The drowned are rendered with tenderness rather than gore: weed knotted in ribs, skin like soaked bread, lips gone pale blue, fingers wrinkled to the bone. The haulers' ears bleed in fine threads, the blood diluted pink by spray. Brine crusts every surface white. The shingle steams faintly where the god's water runs over it. The sea-water pooling around it is thick and slow, like oil.
#### The Story in the Frame
Nobody living ever saw more than Dromm's back: a long grey reef far out past the shelf that broke the surface on the stillest days and breathed. Its whole body, as the coast learned when they dragged it up, was a heaving mass the size of a hill, grey and slick, with no face anyone could find, no eyes, no mouth that opened; it was all pressure and patience, ridged with old barnacles, and its slow breathing sounded like surf turned inside out. It held the drowned. The coast rowed their dead out with lanterns and let them down on weighted ropes over the deep place, and Dromm kept them, along with every secret ever sealed in a bottle and dropped into *the Keep*. Divers who went near it came up with nosebleeds and the feeling of being enormously, patiently watched. Storms broke on its back before they reached the shore.

It would not come up on its own. This plate is the third evening of the hauling, when the coast finally dragged it onto the shingle at Longslab with ten thousand hooks hammered from door-hinges and pot-handles. The sea fought for it: the water thickened until boats could not row, and pressed the ears of the haulers until they bled. On the second day the hooks began bringing up the drowned, caught in the god's hide, and the coast laid them out in rows and later, when hungry enough, did not look at the rows. A woman at the third rope sees her husband come up nine years drowned and not a day changed, and does not let go.

What happens next: the god takes four days to stop moving, and the people do not wait. A girl puts her head inside a lung-lobe to drink, hears the sea from the inside talking, and walks into the water a week later, the first of the Called. On the fourth night the whole coast wakes coughing salt. Now, in 641, the sea is leaving the coast and going into the Tenanted, because Dromm is gathering itself back together, and when the last of the sea has gone in it will rise from the Trench as it was dragged from it.
#### Hidden Details
- The woman at the third rope has a ring tattooed on her breastbone, nine short strokes pointing inward, the old coast mark of Dromm.
- Among the hooks in the god's hide is a green glass bottle sealed with pitch, caught by its neck. Its family seal is legible to anyone who knows the Admiralty's rolls.
- Each breath the god takes shows in the image as the waterline drawn back a little further from under the boats: there are three faint tide-marks on the shingle, each lower than the last.
- One of the drowned in the rows on the beach has turned its head to face the sea.
- The lung-drinker girl is visible at the far right, her hair soaked, looking west with a small, pleased expression.
- Far out on the horizon, where the sea is drawing back, there is a flat dark shape just breaking the surface, square-edged, too regular to be rock.
#### Painter's Prompt
Dark painterly 3:1 seascape at dusk, viewed from a rowing boat in the surf: thousands of taut ropes and iron hooks drag a colossal faceless grey sea-god like a breathing reef up onto a shingle beach, its slick barnacled skin rippling like water; the hooks bring up the long-drowned caught in its hide, weed-wrapped skeletons and pale swollen bodies, one freshly drowned man with open eyes and hair moving in the wash; haulers bent double with blood threading from their ears; rows of covered dead on the stones; at right, people stand chest-deep in brine pouring from a cut beneath its ribs; near-monochrome grey with drowned teal shadows and salt-white foam and crust, wet glossy brushwork, ink spatter spray, lower centre surf kept dark and calm for a title.
**Negative:** bright colours, tropical water, blue sky, cartoon, tentacle monster, kraken, visible eyes or face on the god, heroic sailors, modern ships, text artefacts, clean digital gloss, sunset orange, gore on children, glowing magic

## ISS, THE LIDDED
[plate: iss]
[pull] It never woke. Write that down and underline it. Then ask yourself why you need it underlined. | — margin note in the Ludmere telling, College copy, unknown hand
**Plate:** Iss, the Lidded · *God of sleep* · the Vigil · lamp amber and smoke
#### The Shot
A long low dormitory in the Height, seen down its length from one end at bed height on a 40mm lens, with a strong perspective that runs the rows into darkness. This is the Regrowth plate, now, in 641. The foreground left and right are the first beds: two of the Counted, lying utterly still under linen, their faces turned up, needling-marks on the soles of their bare feet, a lamp hung on a hook between them. The rows go back and back, sixty thousand implied, a corridor of pale shapes under a ceiling lost in lamp-smoke. The centre third is the double exposure that makes the plate: the smoke above the beds thickens into the shape of the god as Ludmere knew it, a curled hound as long as a valley, head on its paws, lying along the whole length of the ward above the sleepers, translucent, made of the smoke from ten thousand lamps. Its great lid, at the centre top of the frame, is shut, and along its lower lid a row of tiny figures lie asleep like children along a bolster. The lid is trembling. Just visible, the lashes have begun to part by a hair. The right third shows a Vigil warden in a smoke-stained coat with a long needle and a lamp, frozen, looking up. Far at the vanishing point of the ward, beyond the last beds, there is a table, and someone is sitting at it with their back to us. The lower centre band is the dark floor between the beds: bare boards, smoke pooled low.
#### Light and Colour
Hundreds of hanging lamps, all amber, all slightly guttering, set in two long lines that converge into smoke. They are the only light source and they are not warm in feeling: they are the light of a city that has not slept in three centuries. Lamp amber is the accent, burning in pinpoints and haloes. Smoke takes all the rest: grey, brown, soft. The ghost of Iss is lit from beneath by the lamps, so its belly glows dirty gold and its upper body fades into black.
#### Texture and Flesh
The Counted are three centuries asleep and dust does not settle on them. Their skin is waxen and smooth, eyelids glossed as if wet, faces untroubled. The linen is perfectly clean, which is wrong in a room this smoky. Their feet show rows of tiny healed needle-scars. The ghost-god is painted in scumbled smoke that coheres into fur only at the edges: the thick nap of a hound, the soft pads of its folded paws, the lid-scales of its closed eye like overlapping moth-wings, thin and warm. The warden's face is the counterpoint: wrecked by waking, eyes red-rimmed and lidless-looking, skin grey and creased, rouse-stain at the corners of his mouth, lamp-soot in every pore. The air itself should look thick enough to chew.
#### The Story in the Frame
Iss lay curled in the high valley above old Ludmere like a hound by a fire, as long as the valley, with its head on its paws and its great lids shut.
