# The Hunger, Pall, Tablenight and Market Decks

> Shuffle it, love. It's all been dealt before. You're only finding out the order.
>> — a card-sharp at the Full Measure, Gristmoot, to a Brinehollow pilot who asked what he was playing

[sigil: rim]

Four decks follow. Each one replaces a moment at the table where the Carver would otherwise reach for a d10 table and read out a line: the moment a Guest's god takes a step forward, the moment a village fails its Turn, the long night of Tablenight, and the moment a purse opens at a stall nobody has vouched for. A card is a better instrument for these moments than a die, for one reason. A card can be handed across the table. The player holds it, reads it, keeps it, and it stays on their side of the table for the rest of the campaign, face-up beside the character sheet, where everyone can see what the god did.

[pull] Every land is certain its own luck is honest. Every land has a deck it doesn't let strangers cut. | — Rim Road saying

## Using the Decks

None of these cards replaces a roll. The Grace Roll still decides whether a Guest succeeds, the Turn still decides whether a village holds, Hunger still fills at 10. The cards tell you *what it means*: what the body did, what the village did, what the night brought, what the stall was really selling. Where a card gives a rules effect it is in **bold**, and it uses the numbers of the core rules exactly: Difficulties of Routine 10, Hard 14, Grim 18, Dire 22 and Impossible 26; Dread ratings 1 to 5; Hunger, Regrowth, Fray, Pall, Steadiness, Hold, Want and Pangs as the Rules chapter and the land chapters define them.

- **The Hunger Deck** is seventy cards, ten for each of the seven lands. A Guest draws one each time Hunger fills and Regrowth rises. It is kept.
- **The Pall Deck** is thirty-six cards in three bands of twelve. The Carver draws one each week a community fails its Turn.
- **The Tablenight Deck** is thirty cards. It comes out of the box once a year, at dusk on the longest night of Lack, and goes back in at dawn.
- **The Market Deck** is thirty-six cards. The Carver draws one, in secret, when a Guest buys from a stall nobody can vouch for.

| Deck | Cards | Drawn by | When | Kept? |
| Hunger | 70 | The player | Regrowth rises | Yes, for good |
| Pall | 36 | The Carver | A failed Turn | Until the event ends |
| Tablenight | 30 | Both | Dusk to dawn, Tablenight only | Until dawn |
| Market | 36 | The Carver, secretly | A doubtful purchase | Until it betrays you |

**No cards?** Every deck below is printed in order, so it doubles as a table. For the Hunger Deck roll a d10 down the land's ten cards, rerolling any the Guest already holds. For the Pall Deck roll a d12 down the community's band. For the Tablenight Deck roll a d10 and a d6: on a d6 of 1–2 read the d10 against the first ten cards, 3–4 the second ten, 5–6 the last ten. For the Market Deck roll a d6 and a d6 and read them as tens and units of a number from 11 to 66, counting down the thirty-six cards in order: 11–16 are the first six cards, 21–26 the next six, and so on to 61–66. Cross off what is used. Cards are better. Players remember a thing they were handed.

## Printing the Decks

The card pages are laid out three to a row. Print them on the heaviest card stock your printer will take (250 gsm or more), single-sided, and cut along the gutters. Each card is sized to fit a standard poker sleeve (63 × 88 mm), and sleeving is recommended for the Hunger Deck, which will be handled for years and will, in most groups, end up with gravy on it.

Give each deck its own back so they cannot be mixed in a hurry. The suggested backs, if you draw or print your own, are these:

- **Hunger:** a white plate on black, with a single bite out of the rim.
- **Pall:** a row of roofs against a grey sky, every chimney cold.
- **Tablenight:** a chair, seen from behind, turned to face a wall.
- **Market:** a lack coin, shut-mouth face, with the little crack at the corner of the lips.

Print the Hunger Deck's seven land sections on seven tints of card if you can (fen-green, wheat-gold, slate-blue, lamp-amber, clay-red, canyon-ochre, and for the Fast a clean, pressed white), so that a player can find their land's stack by colour. Keep each Guest's drawn Hunger cards in a small envelope clipped to their sheet between sessions. When a Guest is Seated, give the player the envelope. Some groups bury it. One group known to the authors bakes it into a loaf at the wake session and nobody eats the slice with the card in it, and nobody throws it away either.

[box: Carver's Advice: The Card Is Not a Verdict]
A drawn card is a suggestion with authority, not a law. If a Hunger card describes a change that collides with something already established (a Guest who has lost the hand the card would grey), move the change to the nearest part that makes sense, keep the rules effect, and say so out loud. If a Pall card names a figure the community does not have (a Celebrant in a village with no chapel), give the role to whoever holds it in fact: the midwife, the oldest man, the Netwatch sergeant. If a Market card does not fit what was bought, hold it back and play it on the next purchase that it does fit. The only thing a Carver should never do with a card is soften what it says the god does. The god does not soften.
[/box]

## The Hunger Deck

> It doesn't come back the same way twice. That's how you know it's alive. Dead things repeat.
>> — Dr. Halloran Quease, catalogue entry No. 402

The Rules chapter says it plainly: when Hunger reaches 10, Regrowth rises by 1, Hunger empties, and *something in the body is different now, and it does not change back.* The Hunger Deck is that something. It turns the most important moment a Guest will have in a session into a physical object, dealt from the god's own stack, and it makes every Guest of the same land different from every other. Two Sallowreachers at the Course both have grey hands. Only one of them has no smell, and only the other has Kept following her to market.

### Drawing

- **When Hunger fills and Regrowth rises,** stop play. The player shuffles their land's ten cards, minus any they already hold, and draws one. They read it aloud. The Carver describes it happening, now, at the table, in the scene. Let it breathe.
- **The change is permanent.** The card goes face-up beside the character sheet and stays there. Its rules effect applies from this moment until the Guest is Seated. Nothing on the Table reverses it. (A Purger's Grace that rolls Regrowth back by one does not return the card; the god takes a step back, and leaves its footprint.)
- **A Guest never draws the same card twice.** Ten cards are exactly enough for ten steps, from the first rise of the Taste to the last rise of the Brink. A Scrapling who begins at Regrowth 0 takes their first step into the Taste without a card, showing only the land's Taste tell from its chapter. If a Guest ever reaches for the stack and finds it empty, they are at Regrowth 11, the Carver has already told them so, and the next time Hunger fills there will be no card. There will be the Seating.
- **Stage scales the description, never the rules.** The cards are not stages. The stage says how far the god has come; the card says where it came through *this* time. A Guest at the Taste who draws a Course-looking card has the first, small, deniable version of it (a knuckle, not a hand; a cupful in the lungs, not a cistern), and it will grow at every stage that follows. A Guest at the Brink who draws a Taste-looking card has it as the god's afterthought, total and at once. The rules effect is the same either way.
- **Foreign points.** When Taint from another land's god gives a Guest a point of foreign Regrowth (see the Rim Market), they draw from *that land's* stack instead of their own, and mark the card *foreign*. Two gods are now leaving marks on one body. The Carver may spend a foreign card, once per session, to make it flare for a scene.
- **The Returned** (Unfed who have eaten a god) draw from the stack of the land whose god they ate. Their Regrowth rises by 2 each time Hunger empties: they draw two cards, and the second is always the worse, by the player's own judgment, read aloud.
- **The Unfed** have no Regrowth and draw from the Fast's ten cards on different triggers, given with that stack.

[pull] I kept the cards in a tin. Nine of them. My wife counted them every night while I was asleep, which I never was, and I listened to her count, and when she got to nine she'd stop, and breathe, and put the lid on. | — a Hand-line clerk of the Lofts, Regrowth 10

[box: The Hunger Deck at the Table]
The best moments with this deck are quiet ones. Do not draw at the end of a session in a rush; draw at the moment it happens, mid-fight if need be, and pause the fight. Ask the player where the character feels it first. Ask who sees it. The other players should be invited to say what their Guests notice: it is in the companions' faces that the change becomes real. If the player wants to, let them hide the card, face-down beside the sheet, and play the Guest concealing it, until someone sees. The Carver always knows what it says. So, eventually, does everyone.
[/box]

### Sallowreach: Ossel, the Closing

Ossel comes back as an ending. Its marks are cold, grey, silent, and finished, and the Kept of the fen can smell every one of them. Sallowreach Guests draw these ten.

Anyone who sees it flex makes a Dread 1 check.**]
[card: Rinsed | Hunger · Sallowreach | You stop smelling of anything. Sweat, soap, breath, the warm salt of a palm: gone. Your pillow smells rinsed, like air after snow. [card: The Low Lid | Hunger · Sallowreach | Your left eyelid grows cold and heavy and sits a fingerbreadth lower than the right, as if someone had begun to close it and been interrupted. [card: Ash Nails | Hunger · Sallowreach | Your nails go black from the root, thick and dull as horn, and stop growing. Where they scratch living skin they leave white lines that never redden. [card: The Swallowed Ending | Hunger · Sallowreach | You reach the end of a sentence and the end is gone, and everyone listening has lost it too. [card: The Footstep in the Chest | Hunger · Sallowreach | Your heartbeat stops carrying. [card: Grey to the Wrist | Hunger · Sallowreach | The grey crosses your wrist overnight like a tide-line. The skin above it is smooth, seamless, shining, and the lines of your palms are gone. [card: The Pile on the Pillow | Hunger · Sallowreach | Flies within an arm's length fall out of the air and do not get up. Each dusk, one asks to be closed.**]
[card: What Nobody Calls a Smile | Hunger · Sallowreach | Your face smooths at the edges like a face seen through fog, and at rest your mouth curves very gently upward. You cannot feel it. In the mirror it looks content. **Anyone who watches you sleep makes a Dread 1 check. +1 Intimidate. −1 Persuade when comforting the grieving: they know that look.**]

## The Pall Deck

> You don't hear a village go. You hear it stop calling names through the wall, one house at a time.
>> — Lieutenant Sable Reef, Netwatch

The Pall is a community's Fray: 0 to 20, with a Steadiness of 0 to 5 that holds it together, and a weekly **Turn** of 2d10 + Steadiness against 10 + the Pall. The Rules chapter gives the Carver d10 tables of events for each band. The Pall Deck replaces those tables with thirty-six weekly events, each with a rule that pushes the Pall up or down and a choice that puts the Guests in the middle of it. It is meant for a community the Guests are going to stay in long enough to love, which is to say, long enough to watch it go.

### Drawing

- **Sort the deck into its three bands:** Uneasy (Pall 5–9), Feverish (10–14) and Broken (15–19). Each band is twelve cards. A Whole community (0–4) draws nothing; it grieves what it has to grieve, and holds.
- **Once a week, roll the Turn.** On a **success**, the community holds: the Pall falls by 1 and no card is drawn, except at Broken, where one card is drawn even on a success, as the Rules say.
- **On a failure,** the Pall rises by 1 and the Carver draws one card from the community's current band, then applies it. The card's own Pall change is *in addition* to the 1 for the failed Turn.
- **On a Lack,** the Pall rises by 2. Draw two cards from the band, read both, and play the worse.
- **On a Grace,** draw nothing. The Pall falls by 2, and the Carver describes the decency the Guests witness, as the Rules describe.
- **A drawn card is set face-up in front of the Carver's screen** for the week it covers, so the players can see the shape of what the village is doing. Most cards ask the Guests to choose. Let them argue about it. A card that has run its course goes to the bottom of its band.
- **If the band changes mid-week** because of a card, the next week's draw is from the new band. A community that crosses into Lost at Pall 20 belongs to the god; put the deck away.

[box: Carver's Advice: Tragedy, Not Spectacle]
Several cards in the Broken band describe people answering the Pull. Run them as the Rules chapter asks: the camera stays on the people left behind, never on the act. These cards never say how anyone was lost, and you should not either. Say what is missing: the made beds, the rinsed buckets, the cat that needs feeding, the hand that let go. Give the grief room. Check in with your players before you bring out the Broken band, and after. If anyone at the table needs a card set aside, set it aside, and draw again.
[/box]

[pull] A village has a Fray the same as a man. The difference is that a man can be held down till dawn. | — Sister Wren Abbotsley of the Hanging Abbey, in a letter to the Netwatch

## The Tablenight Deck

> Never answer a knock on a licked-clean night. Never answer it twice.
>> — Rim innkeepers' rule

Tablenight is the longest night of Lack and the anniversary of the Gorging, and every land keeps it differently: the banquet the Kept cannot taste, the fast no Fatlands village has ever finished, the west door unbarred, the lamps put out for a minute, the doors propped wide, the twenty-four silent hours of the canyons, the Unfed standing outside with their backs to every door while the table is laid in every house, and on the Rim Road, at every inn, one extra chair set at the table and turned to face the wall. The Tablenight Deck is thirty cards that exist for this one night. It lives in its own box. It comes out at dusk and goes back at dawn, and between Tablenights nobody touches it.

### The Night in Five Watches

Run Tablenight as a session of its own, or as the long middle of one. Divide the night into five watches: **Dusk**, **First Dark**, **the Gorging Hour** (midnight), **the Small Hours**, and **Dawn's Edge**.

- **At Dusk,** shuffle the deck and deal one card face-down in front of each Guest. This is their **Place**. Nobody looks at it yet. A player may turn their Place face-up at any moment of the night they choose, and must at Dawn's Edge if they have not. The card then happens to that Guest, there and then.
- **At each of the four later watches,** the Carver turns up one card from the top of the deck for the whole table, reads it aloud, and plays it.
- **Land cards** describe one land's custom. If the Guests are not in that land, someone of that land is with them (at the inn, in the company, in the Guests' own number) keeping it, and the card happens to them, and through them to everyone in the room.
- **The night's own rules** hold throughout: on Tablenight a chair pulled out within reach is an Invitation check at Dire 22, the Rim inns' gates are shut from dusk to dawn, and nobody in a house says *come in* to anyone for any reason.
- **At dawn,** every card goes back in the box, face-down, whatever it said. Effects that last say so on the card. Everything else ends with the light, or at least stops.

[box: Carver's Advice: The Longest Night]
This is the session in which the campaign's hints are allowed to stand a little closer to the lamp. The extra chair, the extra plate, the grace with a gap in it, the frost-writing with a line left blank: let the players notice them, and let them ask, and do not answer. Nothing on these cards states what the empty place is for. The innkeepers do not know. The Hostwatch have stopped asking. The deck is built so that the Carver never has to say anything more than what is on the card, and the players will do the rest themselves, at three in the morning, with the lamp low, and they will be close.

## The Market Deck

> If it is cheap, it is false. If it is dear, it is false and you paid for the privilege.
>> — Lowmark Spur proverb

The Rim Market is the Table's great leveller: every land's money, every land's goods, every land's lies on one long road. The Rim Market chapter gives the rules for Availability, Counterfeit Goods, Taint and Relics. The Market Deck is what happens when those rules meet a purse. It holds thirty-six surprises in four suits: **Counterfeit** (twelve cards: the thing is not what it was sold as), **Taint** (eight: the thing is exactly what it was sold as, and something more), **Relic** (eight: the thing knows you), and **Seller** (eight: the problem is the person behind the counter).

### Drawing

- **When to draw.** The Carver draws one Market card, secretly, whenever a Guest buys from a stall nobody can vouch for: any stall on a Lack day, any black market, Cure-Sellers' Lane at Gristmoot, anything behind the stockyards after Last Bell, any relic from any dealer. Draw also when a Haggle roll comes up a Lack, and once for the company on any Moot-day, whether or not they buy, because the market will find them.
- **Spotting it.** Before money changes hands, a suspicious Guest may roll **Eye + Craft** (for goods) or **Eye + Lore** (for relics, coin and godflesh) against the item's Availability difficulty, as the Counterfeit Goods rules say. On a success, the Carver shows the card: the Guest sees the trick and may walk away, and the card goes to the bottom of the deck. A Helping shows the card *and* who is behind it. Seller cards are spotted with **Eye + Instinct** instead.
- **Playing it.** If the card is not spotted, the Carver keeps it face-down behind the screen, and plays it at once or, better, at the worst moment within the same story. Most counterfeits are discovered later. That is the point of them.
- **Not every card fits.** If the drawn card cannot apply to what was bought, hold it and play it on the next purchase it does fit. If the Guests leave the market with a card unplayed, it follows them: the Carver may play it on anything they bought in that town.
- **The honest stall.** One card in the deck is an honest stall. It is the cruellest card in the deck, in its way. Read it carefully.

[box: Whose Bones?]
Many Market cards concern coin. Remember that the lack is salt-cured bone, and that a character who has studied the grain (Grim 18 Eye + Lore, once in their life) knows which coins in their purse were people. Such a Guest makes a Dread 1 check the first time they pay for a meal with a coin they know was a person, and after that, as the Rim Market chapter says, they stop counting. Market cards that put a person's bone in a Guest's purse should remind the player of that, and then let them spend it. Everybody does.
[/box]
