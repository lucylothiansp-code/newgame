# The Lack Deck and the Grace Deck

> Cut the deck, love. No, the other way. You always cut toward yourself on the Table. That way, whatever's on the bottom comes to you, and not to somebody who can't afford it.
>> — a card-wife at the Fourway Inn, Rim Road, to a Company auditor

[fiction]
**Painted on the lid of a card box found in the luggage of a drowned Rim Road factor, Lowmark customs house, 638 A.G. The box held two packs, one black-backed and one gilt, both much handled.**
BLACK IS FOR WHEN IT GOES WRONG. GOLD IS FOR WHEN IT GOES RIGHT. THEY ARE BOTH FOR WHEN IT GOES. Do not play with a man who cuts the gold first. Do not play at all east of Tacit. If a card turns up in this box that you did not put in, it is yours now; read it, then burn it, then count again.

Every Grace Roll that comes up doubles carries an omen. On a success it is a **Grace**, and on a failure it is a **Lack**, and the rules ask the Carver to invent, on the spot, either the worst plausible complication or something better than anyone had a right to. Few Carvers can do that well at midnight, after the fourth Lack in a row, while two players argue about whether a Kept man can be bribed with his own fingers. These decks are for those nights: a stock of ready-made omens, in the Table's voice, to turn over, read aloud and play.

The **Lack Deck** holds 72 cards: 18 that can fall anywhere on the Table, 6 for each of the seven lands, and 12 that only fall in a fight. The **Grace Deck** holds 48: 12 general, 4 for each land, and 8 for the fight. Every card has a title, a few lines of what happens, and a **bold rules line** that says exactly what it does in the numbers. Each card is numbered, so a group without printed cards can roll on the tables at the end of each deck and look the card up in this chapter.

## How to Use the Decks

### Draw on the Pair

When a Grace Roll comes up doubles and fails, it is a Lack: the Carver may turn over the top card of the Lack Deck. When it comes up doubles and succeeds, it is a Grace: the player who rolled may turn over the top card of the Grace Deck. Licked Clean and Second Helping (double 1s and double 10s) draw exactly as any other Lack or Grace. The Carver serves the Lack and reads it aloud, in her own voice, to the whole table. The Guest receives the Grace and reads it aloud to everyone else, because on the Table good fortune is a thing people watch you eat.

The card replaces the improvised complication or boon. It does not replace the rest of the rules. A Lack while Partaking still costs 2 more Hunger. A Grace on an attack still inflicts a Mangling. A Lack on a Dread check still freezes, flees, screams, vomits or laughs. The card is laid on top of all that, like a garnish, and like most garnishes on the Table it may be the part that does the real damage.

*You* on a card means the Guest whose roll it was. Where a card names a stranger, the Carver picks someone fitting from the scene; the card is a seed, not a script. Dread, Fray, Hunger, Want, Pangs and Weariness work exactly as in *The Grace Roll* and the land chapters. Where a card and the core rules disagree, the card wins for that moment only.

### Two Piles: the Board and the Knife

Build each deck as two piles. The **Board** is everything that is not a fight: the general cards, plus the cards of whichever land the scene takes place in. The **Knife** is the combat cards, kept separate and shuffled on their own.

- When a Lack or a Grace comes up on an **attack** or a **defense roll** in a fight (an attack, a grapple, a Bind, a Cast Into, breaking free, the Carver's call to dodge something unusual), draw from the Knife.
- Any other Lack or Grace, including one rolled in the middle of a fight (a Dread check when the Kept come up out of the water, a first-aid roll on a man holding his bowel in with both hands, a Yield), draws from the Board.

The Carver may also draw from the Knife for an adversary's Lack, reading *you* as *he* or *she*. It makes the enemy look like a person having a bad night.

### Land Cards Stay Home

Each land's cards go into the Board only while the scene is in that land. A Brinehollow card does not fall in Tacit; the canyons do not have a tide, whatever they have instead. When the company crosses a border, take the old land's cards out of the deck and the discard alike, and shuffle the new land's cards in. On the Rim Road itself, between lands, play the general cards alone: the Road belongs to the Company, and the Company insists that nothing strange happens on it.

The land is where the scene is, not where the Guest was born. Where a land card names a native rule (a Calling roll, Weariness, a Weight of oath, an Invitation check), it applies to foreigners as written: a Vigilant in Brinehollow makes the Calling roll like anyone, and learns why the locals sleep roped. The Fast's cards fall on Godeaters and Unfed alike. The Host does not ask what you have eaten before it lays you a place.

### The Plate, and the Reshuffle

Drawn cards go face up on a discard pile called **the Plate**, where everyone can see what has already been served. A card on the Plate cannot come up again until the reshuffle. Reshuffle the Board and the Knife together, Plate and all, when any of these happens:

- **The pile runs out.** Shuffle the Plate back in and carry on.
- **A new session begins.** The Table starts every night hungry.
- **A Guest's Regrowth rises.** The god has taken a step forward, and it stirs the pot. Reshuffle at once, before the next roll.
- **Tablenight**, in the fiction. On the longest night of Lack every omen is possible again, and some are likelier.
- **The company crosses a border** (swap the land cards, as above, and shuffle everything).

A card that says it stays with a Guest (a place card, a bed number, a Part in the pack, a debt) is taken off the Plate and given to that player, face up, in front of them. It returns to the deck when its business is finished.

### Optional, Always

The decks are optional, wholly and at every moment. A Carver who has a better Lack in her mouth when the dice fall should say it and leave the deck alone. Some groups draw only for Lacks, or only in fights, or only when nobody can think of anything; some, the worst kind, draw every time and read the card in a funny voice. All of these are correct.

### The Carver's Veto

The Carver may veto any card she draws, for any reason, without explanation. Put the vetoed card on the bottom of its pile, not on the Plate, and draw again. If the second card is vetoed too, stop drawing and make the Lack or Grace up in the ordinary way. Never draw a third card. A deck that can be shopped from is not an omen; it is a menu.

A card that touches anything a player has said they will not play is vetoed without a word, as soon as it is turned over, before it is read. That is not a judgment call and does not count against the two draws.

The players have one refusal of their own, and only for the gold. **A Guest may refuse a Grace card** after hearing it, before anything on it happens. The card goes to the Plate and the roll is an ordinary Grace, made up by the Carver. An Unfed Guest who refuses gains **1 Want**, as for declining any offer of value. Nobody may refuse a Lack.

## Printing and Cutting

The cards in this chapter are laid out three to a row, at the width of an ordinary playing card. To make a deck:

- **Copy the pages at full size.** Do not shrink to fit. The card faces in this book are sized for the common poker card, about 63 by 88 millimetres (2.5 by 3.5 inches). Print on the heaviest card your printer will take; 250 to 300 grams is ideal. Plain paper will do if you sleeve it.
- **Cut along the borders** with a steel rule and a sharp knife on a cutting mat, or a guillotine if you have one. Round the corners with a corner-punch or a nail file. The card-wives of the Rim round theirs with their teeth, which is not recommended.
- **Back them.** Draw or print a single card back and copy it in a sheet of nine: for the Lack Deck an empty plate in gold on black, for the Grace Deck an empty plate in black on gold. Glue a sheet of backs behind each page of faces before cutting, so the edges line up. If you do not want to glue, slide each face into a card sleeve in front of an old playing card: red-backed cards for the Lack Deck, blue for the Grace.
- **Mark the piles.** Each land card carries its land's name and number on the face; it helps to put a dot of colour on the corner as well, so land cards can be pulled quickly at a border. Combat cards are marked *Knife*. Keep each land's cards in a small envelope inside the box, with the land's sigil drawn on the flap.
- **Count them** before every session, as the box lid says. There should be 72 black and 48 gold. If there are more, someone at your table has been writing their own, which is the best thing that can happen to a deck and should be encouraged. Number the new ones from 73 and 49 upward, and keep them out of the dice tables.

[box: Carver's Advice: Pacing the Black]
Bad dice can turn up four Lacks in an hour, and four cards in an hour is a pile-up, not a story. After the first card in a scene, consider making further Lacks yourself. One card per scene is a seasoning, two is a meal, three is the Fatlands. The Grace Deck needs no such rationing. Players remember every one, and talk about the ominous ones on the way home.
[/box]

### Rolling the Lack

For groups without printed cards, roll for the Lack instead and read the card of that number in the lists above. Seventy-two is a d6 and a d12 together, read like a clock: the **d6 picks a band of twelve cards**, and the **d12 picks the card within it**. The card's number is *(d6 − 1) × 12 + d12*: a 3 on the d6 and a 5 on the d12 is card 29, *Origin Stamped Twice*. (With a d8 and a d10 on hand, the arithmetic is worse; use the d6 and d12.)

| d6 | Cards | What falls there |
| 1 | 01–12 | General |
| 2 | 13–24 | General (13–18); Sallowreach (19–24) |
| 3 | 25–36 | The Fatlands (25–30); Brinehollow (31–36) |
| 4 | 37–48 | The Vigil (37–42); Cradlewrack (43–48) |
| 5 | 49–60 | Oathen (49–54); the Fast (55–60) |
| 6 | 61–72 | The Knife (combat only) |

The full d72 obeys the same rules as the deck: if the number falls on a card that is not in play (another land's card, or a Knife card outside a fight), roll again. Faster, roll only what is in play:

- **In a fight, on an attack or defense:** roll a d12 and read cards 61 to 72.
- **Anywhere else, in a land:** roll a d6 and a d12. On a d6 of 1–3, read the general card numbered the same as the d12 (01–12). On 4–6, a d12 of 1–6 reads general cards 13–18 in order, and a d12 of 7–12 reads the land's six cards in order (7 is its first card, 12 its last).
- **On the Rim Road, between lands:** roll a d6 and a d12 as above, and on 4–6 with a d12 of 7–12, roll again.

A rolled card that is already on the Plate this session (keep a tally on scrap paper) is rolled again, as the deck would have it.

## The Grace Deck

Forty-eight gold cards. They are beautiful, and every one of them is a little wrong, because on the Table kindness usually is. Twelve fall anywhere; four belong to each land; eight are for the Knife. A Guest may refuse any of them, as the rules above allow, and the Unfed are paid in Want for doing it.

### Rolling the Grace

Forty-eight is a d4 and a d12, read the same way as the Lack: the **d4 picks a band of twelve**, the **d12 the card within it**, and the card's number is *(d4 − 1) × 12 + d12*. A 2 on the d4 and a 9 on the d12 is card 21, *Slack Water*. With no d4, roll a d6 and reroll 5s and 6s; or roll a d6 and a d8 and read *(d6 − 1) × 8 + d8*, which gives the same 48 by a different road.

| d4 | Cards | What falls there |
| 1 | 01–12 | General |
| 2 | 13–24 | Sallowreach (13–16); the Fatlands (17–20); Brinehollow (21–24) |
| 3 | 25–36 | The Vigil (25–28); Cradlewrack (29–32); Oathen (33–36) |
