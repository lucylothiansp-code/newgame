# Before You Sit Down

> We were hungry. Say it plainly. We were hungry, and they were there.
>> — first line of every history written on the Table

[fiction]
My grandmother told it to me the way her grandmother told it to her, with the shutters closed and a crust of bread between us that neither of us was allowed to touch until she had finished.
"There were eight of them," she said, "and they lived with us the way the weather lives with us. You did not pray to them. You did not need to. You woke up and they were there, as big as the hill behind the house, and they were kind, and they were warm, and they fed us."
"Then they stopped," I said, because I knew the story.
"Then everything stopped. Nine years. The seed lay in the furrow like teeth in a dead mouth. The nets came up holding only water. The cows went dry, and then the women did." She looked at the crust. "And on the ninth winter we went up the hill with knives."
"Did they fight?"
"No," she said. "That is the part nobody can stand. They lay down. They lay down like a dog lies down for the hand it knows, and they let us."
She pushed the crust toward me. I said the grace, the way you must: *Lack keep away.* I ate it. It tasted like bread. It tasted like bread for eleven more years, and then one morning it tasted like something that had been waiting for me.
[/fiction]

## The World in One Breath

Six hundred and forty-one years ago, at the end of a famine that lasted nine years, the peoples of the world killed their gods and ate them. Six peoples did. One refused.

It saved them. It also did not stay down. A god cannot be digested, only delayed. In every land, the god that was eaten is growing back: through the bloodlines of the people who swallowed it, through the soil their dead were buried in, through the weather over their heads. Each land is now a culture living inside the slow, wrong resurrection of its own meal.

The poets of the Table have a name for the age that followed. They call it **the Long Grace**: the pause between the blessing and the first bite, the breath a family holds before it is permitted to eat. Six centuries of borrowed time, while the meal sits in the belly of the world and remembers what it was. Everyone alive is descended from someone who ate. The monster is in the blood, and it is waking up. The Grace is ending.

This book is a complete tabletop roleplaying game set in that world. It contains everything you need to play: the history and lands of the Table, the rules for making and ruining characters, a market to buy and sell in, a gallery of the things that hunt and are hunted, guidance for the person who runs the game, and an adventure to begin with.

## What Kind of Game Is This?

*The Long Grace* is a horror roleplaying game. One player, the **Carver** (the game master, or GM: at a feast on the Table, the Carver is the one who decides who gets which portion), describes the world, plays everyone in it who is not a player character, and adjudicates the rules. Every other player creates and plays one **Guest**: a single person of the Table, with a land, a bloodline, a trade, debts, people they love, and a god in their veins that wants things.

Play is a conversation. The Carver describes a situation; the players say what their characters do; when the outcome is uncertain and matters, dice are rolled. Over a session or a long campaign, the characters investigate, travel the Rim Road, bargain, fight, flee, and make terrible choices, and the story that results belongs to everyone at the table.

It is not a game of heroes. There is no chosen one, no sword that kills a god, no cure that works. The characters are people trying to survive inside a catastrophe that is also their inheritance, and the most important rules in this book measure how their bodies change (**Hunger** and **Regrowth**), how their minds fray (**Fray** and **Derangements**), and what they are willing to do to each other to stay whole for one more day. Characters will be maimed. Characters will go mad. Characters will become something that is not a person anymore. Some of the best sessions you will play are the ones in which that happens.

### What You Need

- This book, or at least its rules chapters and the character sheet at the back.
- Two ten-sided dice (d10) per player, ideally of two different colors. Several six-sided dice (d6) in a third color: these are **Teeth**, rolled when a character calls on the god inside them.
- Pencils, erasers, copies of the character sheet, and scrap paper for maps, ledgers, and the names of the dead.
- Three to six people, one of whom has read the rules and at least the chapter on the land where the game begins.
- A few hours, and a willingness to sit with discomfort.

## Content and Consent

This is a game about eating gods and the gods eating back. It is, deliberately, one of the most disturbing settings ever printed for a tabletop game. Before anyone at your table makes a character, everyone should know what is in it.

[box: Content Warning]
*The Long Grace* contains, described on the page and in sensory detail: graphic body horror and gore (flesh, bone, rot, unwanted transformation); murder, including ritual killing, executions and kin-slaying; torture; cannibalism, both desperate and sacramental; grave desecration and the misuse of the dead; blasphemy and corrupted religion; human sacrifice; self-harm and suicide, presented as tragedy and never as instruction; contagious madness, psychosis, hallucination, paranoia, delusions of being dead or of loved ones being impostors, dissociation and lost time; cult control and gaslighting; starvation; possession; and a cosmology without rescue or redemption.
The setting also touches, thematically and never explicitly, on cursed and incestuous bloodlines, sexual taboo among adults, and desecration of the dead. These elements are always implied, off-page, and adult. Children exist on the Table and suffer its horrors; this book never sexualizes them and never lingers on graphic violence done to them.
[/box]

Horror is a collaboration. The point of all this darkness is not to wound the people at the table but to let them feel, safely and together, the weight of a world that cannot be saved. That only works if everyone trusts that the game will stop or turn when it needs to. Use the following tools. They are not optional extras; they are part of the rules.

### Lines and Veils

Before the first session, the group discusses **Lines** (content that will not appear in the game at all) and **Veils** (content that may exist in the fiction but happens off-screen: the camera cuts away, the door closes, the scene resumes afterward). Write both lists down and keep them visible. Anyone may add to them at any time without explanation. The Carver is responsible for honoring them, including by rewriting the material in this book. Every land and adversary in these pages can be run with any of its themes veiled.

### Saying the Grace

Every meal on the Table begins with the words *Lack keep away*. At your table, the same words, or a tap on a card marked with a crossed fork, mean: *stop this, now.* Anyone may say the Grace at any moment, about anything, with no reason given and no discussion required. The Carver immediately removes or alters whatever was happening, and play continues. Nobody asks why. Saying the Grace is never an insult and never an inconvenience; it is the oldest courtesy in the world.

### Checking In

Long campaigns drift. Every few sessions, take five minutes out of character: what has landed well, what was too much, what people want more of. Horror that has stopped being fun for one person at the table has stopped being fun for the table.

### On Suicide and Despair

The Long Grace contains the suicides of characters, in the setting's history and sometimes in play: the Finishers walking into the Hushes, the Called walking into the sea, the sleepless lying down on purpose. This book treats every one of these as a tragedy with a cost that falls on the living. It never describes how; it describes what it leaves behind. If you or someone at your table is struggling outside the game, stop the game. Real people matter more than any story, and help from people who are trained to give it is worth reaching for.

## How to Use This Book

The book is divided into four parts.

- **Part One: The Table** describes the world. *The Long Grace* chapter covers the history, the Providers, the Lack, the Gorging, the Regrowth, the Cuts, the Rim Road and the powers that cross borders. Seven chapters follow, one for each land: Sallowreach, the Fatlands, Brinehollow, the Vigil, Cradlewrack, Oathen, and the Fast. Each chapter covers its land's meal, its rules of horror, its history, factions, people, and how to play someone born there. *Threads Between the Lands* ties them together.
- **Part Two: The Rules** explains how to make a character, how dice are rolled, how the god in the blood is measured and spent, how the mind breaks, and how violence is done.
- **Part Three: The Market and the Things That Eat** contains the full equipment and shopping system, the currencies of the Table, relics made from godflesh, and a bestiary of horrors, human adversaries and Seated gods.
- **Part Four: Setting the Table** is for the Carver alone: how to run horror, how to build campaigns, the secrets of the world, and the introductory adventure, *The Ninth Chair*.

At the back you will find the character sheet and an index.

[box: Words of the Table]
- **The Table:** the known world, a single round continent with a dead heartland.
- **The Long Grace:** the age since the Gorging; the borrowed time before the gods finish growing back.
- **The Gorging:** the night six peoples killed and ate their gods. Year 0 A.G. (After Gorging). It is now 641 A.G.
- **Providers:** the eight gods who lived bodily among the peoples.
- **The Lack:** the nine-year famine before the Gorging. Also: winter; also, the bone coin.
- **Regrowth:** the slow return of an eaten god through the people who ate it. Its marks are *godsign*.
- **Cut:** a bloodline, named for the part of the god its ancestors ate. The higher the Cut, the stronger the god in the blood.
- **Scraplings:** descendants of those who got the scraps; the least changed people alive.
- **Blanks:** those rare people born with no godsign at all. Hunted everywhere.
- **The Unfed:** the people of the Fast, who refused to eat.
- **Tablenight:** the anniversary of the Gorging, the longest night of winter.
- **The Rim Road:** the ring highway around the Fast, along which all trade moves.
- **Carver and Guests:** in this book, the game master (also called the GM) and the players.
[/box]
