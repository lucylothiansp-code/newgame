# Brinehollow

The Count, as kept by Wenna Truss of Ropery Row, Lowmark, Mile Eleven. Written on the back of a chandler's bill and found folded inside her bail-bucket.
I call through the wall at first bell, same as every morning since I could stand. My mother taught me. You put your mouth to the plank where it's worn smooth, where the knot looks like an ear, and you call the names on the other side. Haddie. Bren. Little Os. Old Cobb. And they call back, one at a time, with the cough still in their voices, because nobody has bailed yet at first bell. That is how you know they are home. The wet in the throat. The answer.
This morning I called Haddie and Haddie answered. I called Bren and Bren answered. I called Little Os and he answered in that piping way, still half asleep, and I heard Haddie hush him. I called Old Cobb.
I want to set down exactly what happened next, because they will ask me at the Netwatch house and I will not be able to say it with my mouth.
Nothing answered. Not silence like an empty room. Silence like a held breath. The whole house on the other side of the plank went still, the way a pool goes still when something big turns over under it. And then Haddie began to make a sound I have heard eleven times in my life and never once from her.
I went round. The door was on the latch. Cobb's bed is the one by the west window, because he likes the light, because he has always liked the light, the old fool, and we all said it, we all said you shouldn't sleep facing that way at your age. The rope was still tied to the bedpost. Good tarred hemp from the Ropewalk, three turns and a hitch, done by Bren the night before. It was tied to the bedpost and it went across the blanket and over the edge of the bed and it stopped. The end was cut clean. Not frayed. Not chewed. Cut, the way you would cut it if you had a sharp knife and a steady hand and all the time in the world.
There was no knife in the room.
The floor was wet from the bed to the door, and from the door down the steps, and across the boards of the Row, and on to the pier rail, and over it, and on into the dark of the Uncovered where there is no water any more for nineteen miles. The wet went west. It does not dry fast on the Mile. It never has.
Haddie kept saying, I tied it, I tied it, I checked it, as if that was the thing that mattered, and Bren kept saying, I tied it, Mam, it was me, and Little Os stood in the doorway holding Cobb's bail-bucket in both arms, the old copper one with the dent, and he said, Grandda forgot his bucket.
He did. He will not need it now.
I want to say also that the bed was warm. The pillow still had the shape of him. On the pillow, where his mouth would have been, there was a little crust of white salt, and in the salt there was a print, like someone had kissed it. I do not know what that means. I did not tell Haddie.
They will put his name on the post. They will cut his rope into nine pieces and give one to each of us and one to the wall. We will set his bowl on Tablenight with the seawater in it and we will leave the west door unbarred, and every one of us will lie awake hoping he does not come in.
That is the worst of it, and I will write it because nobody says it. You pray for them to come home and you pray they don't. Because if Cobb comes walking up out of the dark one morning, heavy and gentle and dripping, with his own face and his own laugh and the name of every grandchild on his lips, Haddie will take him in. She will feed him. She will rope him to the same bed. And every morning I will put my mouth to the plank and call his name, and he will answer, in a voice just a little lower than it should be, and I will never again know who I am talking to.
Haddie. Bren. Little Os.
Tomorrow there will be three names.

## The Meal

The western coast ate Dromm, the Fathom, god of the deep sea, of pressure, and of what is kept under. Every land's Gorging has its peculiar horror. Brinehollow's is that the god had to be brought to the table, and that it took four days to stop moving once it got there.

Dromm did not live on land. It lived beneath the whole grey reach of the western water, a presence more than a shape, and the coast knew it the way a body knows its own blood pressure: as a weight, a steadiness, the thing that kept the bottom of the sea where it belonged. Before the Lack, the fishers said that Dromm held the drowned down so they would not frighten their families by floating, and held the storms down so the boats came home, and held the cold water under the warm so the herring rose. It was a god of keeping things where they were put. Its priests were divers who went down on weighted ropes and came back with bleeding ears and with answers.

In the ninth winter there were no herring. There were no answers either. The divers went down and found Dromm lying in the shallows off a long, flat beach called Longslab, closer to shore than any god had ever come, as though it had surfaced to wait. It was so large that nobody living could agree on its shape. Accounts written within a generation describe a back like a reef of black slate, plates of it, crusted with things that were not barnacles; a flank that rose and fell, very slowly, with a sound like surf heard through a wall; and eyes, or something like eyes, that stood open under the water, wider than doorways, the color of the sea a mile down.

The hauling took every vessel on the coast and every hand that could hold rope. Ten thousand hooks, by the count the Admiralty still teaches: shark hooks, gaff hooks, anchors beaten into barbs, meat hooks taken off the walls of empty smokehouses. They set them in the god from boats and from the shallows and from the beach itself, in long lines of starving men and women and children who leaned back on the hemp and sang the hauling songs, and the god came in a yard at a time. It did not fight. That is recorded here as everywhere. It came in the way a drowned man comes in, heavy and unresisting, with the water pouring out of it.

That was the first horror. The water did not stop. It ran out of Dromm in sheets, in torrents, from every gap between the plates, from the eyes, from places that opened along its sides like gills that had never been needed, and it ran for four days. It was colder than any water on the coast. It smelled of iron and of something older than salt. It came out under such pressure that it knocked haulers flat and blinded them, and the people at the front of the lines drowned standing up on dry shingle, their lungs filled from the spray. The beach under the god became a lake, and then a marsh, and it has never dried since.

On the fourth day, the god stopped moving. The water still ran, but more slowly, and the vast flank no longer rose. The butchering began at dusk, by lamplight and by bonfires of wreck-wood, and it was done with whale-spades, axes, and the long knives of the flensing yards. Survivors' accounts agree on the smell: like opening a tide pool that had been sealed for a thousand years. They agree on the sound: that when the first spade went in, every person on the beach felt their ears pop at once, and some of them never heard properly again. They agree that the flesh was white and dense and very cold, and that it was the best thing any of them had ever tasted, and that it tasted like being held.

The families who reached the lungs first were the boldest and the strongest and the nearest, and they did not wait for fire. Dromm's lungs were caverns, chambers that a family could walk into, spongy and silver-grey and still full of the deep. The founders of the Lung-lines went inside the god with their knives and their bare hands and ate the lungs raw, from within, in the dark, with the water rising around their knees. The chronicles of the Vasht family record, with what was meant as pride, that their founder Ondric Vasht ate until he could not breathe and went on eating anyway, and was carried out of the god's chest at dawn half-drowned and laughing and coughing up salt water, and that he coughed up salt water every dawn and dusk from that day until he died. He was the first to bail.

The bones were never fully cleared. The Longslab middens are still there, under the marsh, and the god's skull was too large to move and too hard to break. It was left where it lay, and the sea covered it, and then the sea began, five centuries later, to leave. It is not on the Uncovered now. It was not taken. Nobody knows where it went.

[box: The Missing Line]
Every family of the coast keeps some fragment of the Longslab hauling songs, and every version has the same gap: a verse that has been struck out, sometimes scored through so hard the pen went through the page. In the oldest carved record of the Beaching, a frieze on the Admiralty's Sounding Hall, the hauling teams are shown in long lines, each line labeled for a village of the coast. There are eight lines of haulers. The eighth has been chiseled flat. The figures on its rope are gone, but the rope is still carved, and it runs, like all the others, into the god.
GMs should let players find this and wonder. Brinehollow does not know what the eighth rope means and does not want to.
[/box]

Dromm was the god of holding things down. It is now held in them, and what it holds down in its new house is the house itself.

## The Rule of the Land

The sea is inside the people. Everything in Brinehollow follows from that sentence, and the country has had six hundred and forty-one years to learn how far it goes.

### The Tide in the Body

Every Brinehollower has a tide. Twice a day, near dawn and near dusk, the lungs fill with salt water from nowhere. It is not inhaled. It rises from inside, cold, through the bronchial tree, like water coming up through sand when you dig on a beach. It begins as a tightness and a taste. Within a few minutes the breath gurgles. Within a quarter hour a person who has not cleared it is drowning in their own chest.

Clearing it is called **bailing**. It is done bent over a bucket, with the body tipped forward and the shoulders worked, in long racking coughs that bring the water up in gouts. A healthy adult bails a pint or two. Lung-lines nobles bail gallons. The water is clear, cold, faintly green, and alive in small ways: fine sand, a thread of weed, now and then a sand-flea that hops on the bucket's rim and is gone. It smells like the sea did when there was a sea at the end of every street. Children are taught to bail before they are taught to read, and a child who cannot yet bail on its own is held face-down over a mother's knee and patted on the back, twice a day, as she sings. Every lullaby on the coast is a bailing song, in the rhythm of a cough.

Bailing is done at dawn and dusk the way other peoples wash their faces, privately and without comment. Bail-water is poured into the gutters, which run west, always west, whatever the slope of the street.

Those who cannot bail — the very old, the sick, the drunk, the injured, the unconscious — must be bailed by others, held head-down and struck between the shoulder blades until the water runs. Neglecting to bail a helpless person is murder in Brinehollow law, and it is the most common murder there is, because it requires only that one stay asleep.

### The Pull and the Called

The tide pulls as well as fills. The water in the body wants to go home, and on certain nights it takes the body with it.

The Called rise in their sleep, or in a waking trance that looks like sleep, and walk west. They do not hurry. They do not respond to their names. They walk to the nearest shore, which in the present age may be miles away across the Uncovered, and then they walk into the sea and keep walking until the water closes over them. Nobody who has watched it says the Called look afraid. The Netwatch's records use a standard phrase, written in the logs thousands of times: **"Subject appeared expected."** As if someone were waiting for them and they did not want to be late.

Calling nights are not random, though no one has fully mapped them. They cluster at the licked-clean moon, on pressure days, in the weeks after a death in the family, and on nights when the indoor tide runs unusually high. They cluster in households. They cluster in grief. A widower whose wife was Called in Lack has, by the Netwatch's reckoning, one chance in four of following her before Grace.

The defence is **anchoring**. People sleep roped, chained, or weighted to the bed. Sweethearts tie each other's knots. In the poorer streets of the Mile a whole family may sleep on one floor, lashed in a line to a single iron ring set into the joists, like a boat's crew in a storm. And still, every year, ropes are found cut. Sometimes there is a knife on the floor that the sleeper has kept hidden for weeks without knowing they were hiding it. Sometimes there is no knife at all. And sometimes, which is the thing nobody says aloud, there is a knife in another hand: a spouse who could not bear the pleading any longer, a child who heard a parent crying to be let go and loved them too much to refuse. A rope cut in the night is the country's great domestic tragedy, and the question of who cut it is the question that breaks families.

### The Tenanted

Most of the Called are never seen again. Some come back.

They come weeks later, usually at dawn, walking up out of the surf heavier than they left: denser, slower, their footprints sinking deep into wet sand. They are calm and courteous. They know the way home. They have the same face, the same scars, the same memories down to the names of childhood dogs and the location of the spare key. They greet their families with real warmth and apologize for the worry. They ask for their bail-bucket, and bail a great deal, and then they ask whether there is any supper.

They are the **Tenanted**, and someone else is looking out through their eyes.

It is not always easy to say how one knows. A Tenanted man still laughs at his brother's jokes, but a beat late, as if the joke had to come down a long way to reach him. A Tenanted mother still sings the old songs to her children, but her voice has a second voice beneath it, lower, that sometimes finishes the line first. The Tenanted do not lie, cannot be bribed, do not lose their temper, and are, by every measure anyone has devised, kinder and fairer than they were before. Their families are often happier. That is the horror the Wreckwives exist to share.

Whether the person who walked into the sea is still in there somewhere, folded small under the weight of the thing that came back with them, is Brinehollow's oldest unanswerable question. It is rude to ask a returned person where they went. It is ruder to ask whether they are still there.

### The Sea Is Moving House

Meanwhile, the ocean itself is leaving. Since 512 A.G. the waterline has dropped every year, slowly at first, then by furlongs, then by miles. Harbors stand dry. Fishing fleets sit on mud. The great port of Lowmark has chased the water nineteen miles out across the dry seabed on a road of piers, and is still chasing. The sea is not evaporating. The Salt Assay has proved this, with buckets and with arithmetic, and has been forbidden to say where it is going instead.

The tide is also in the houses. In every cellar in Brinehollow, however far inland, water rises twice a day and drains away again, and leaves kelp on the stairs. A farmhouse forty miles from the coast will have a wet cellar step, a ring of salt on its walls, and once in a while a crab in the coal-bin. The country's buildings have adapted: cellars are tiled, drains run west, and anything of value is kept above the high-water mark, which is painted on the wall of every cellar in red and repainted every year a little higher.

[box: Running the Calling]
On a Calling night, any Brinehollower (or anyone with brine in their blood; see Dry Jonah and the Vigil's rouse) who sleeps unanchored, or whose anchoring fails, must make a **Caul + Resolve** roll. The difficulty is Routine (10) at Regrowth 0–3, Hard (14) at 4–6, Grim (18) at 7–9 and Dire (22) at the Brink. A licked-clean moon, a recent Called death in the family, or sleeping facing west each add +2 to the difficulty. Success: the character wakes at the door or the window, cold and wet to the knees, with the taste of the sea in their mouth. Failure: they walk. Companions may try to wake or hold them (an opposed GUT + Labor roll against the Called character's LUNG + Athletics; the Called do not feel pain and do not tire).
Treat a PC's Calling as a dramatic scene, never a mechanical loss. Give the other players time and agency to stop it. A character who reaches the sea is gone for 2d6 weeks; the GM decides, with the player, whether they return Tenanted (the character becomes an NPC, or continues at the Brink under the rules for Playing a Brinehollower), or not at all.
Above all, play the Calling as grief. The horror is not the walking. It is the empty bed, the warm pillow, the rope end, and the people at the table who have to keep setting a place.
[/box]

[box: Salt Manners]
It is rude to mention how someone sounds when they breathe. Brinehollowers gurgle, rattle, and whistle in the hours before bailing, and politeness requires that everyone pretend not to hear.
It is rude to ask a returned person where they went.
It is rude to stand between someone and a west-facing window.
It is rude to refuse a bail-bucket offered to an outsider. Hold it, say "Keep you dry," and hand it back.
It is very rude, and in some towns actionable, to tie your own knot loosely.

## The Regrowth in Brinehollow

Dromm is growing back, and what it grows back into is depth. The Regrowth here is a slow drowning that never finishes, a body learning, against every instinct it was born with, to live under a weight it cannot see.

### The Taste

Salt on the lips at waking: a fine white crust at the corners of the mouth, as if the sleeper had spent the night on a beach in the wind. Ears that pop indoors, especially in cellars, especially at the turn of the tide. A Brinehollower at the Taste wakes with the sheets damp in the shape of their body. Their voice sounds faintly wet to strangers. They find themselves telling the time by the tightness in their chest rather than by the bell, and they are always right. Children at the Taste hold their breath in play for longer than any child should, and laugh about it when they come up, and their mothers do not laugh.

### The Appetite

The pull. A person at the Appetite stands at windows that face west and does not know how long they have been there. They put their hands flat on the glass. They sleepwalk to the nearest water, and anything will do: a well, a rain barrel, a horse trough, a washbasin. Families find them kneeling on the floor in the dark with their faces in the basin, not drowning, simply resting there, breathing it, perfectly calm. They crave cold food and salt. They eat raw shellfish from the Uncovered middens, which are poison to anyone else, and are not poisoned. They lose interest in warmth. They stop noticing that they have not bailed, and their families start doing it for them.

The bail-water changes too. Tolly Brack the Bailer Boy reads it: clear for the healthy, faintly green at the Appetite, and then, for those close to Calling, a deep sea-blue with a weight to it, like water pulled from a hundred fathoms. Some households keep the dawn bail-water in a glass jar on the sill so they can check its color by daylight. Most would rather not know.

### The Course

The body adapts to depth. The skin goes grey, then the grey of wet slate, smooth and cool and thickened, with a faint pebbling along the spine and the backs of the hands. The eyes widen. The pupils swell until there is almost no iris, black and wet and reflective, and in the dark they shine green like a cat's. The chest stops needing to rise. A person in the Course can sit for an hour without breathing visibly, and the stillness of their ribs is the thing that unsettles visitors most, more than the eyes.

Inside, the change is worse. Dr. Isolde Reave has opened Course chests on her table and recorded what she found: lungs grown spongy, silver, cavernous, packed with chambers like the inside of a sea-cucumber; ribs thickened and drawn inward, as though by enormous pressure; a heart small, slow, and dense as a stone. The lungs hold water now as their natural state. Air is the intrusion. A person in the Course is uncomfortable in open air, as a land-dweller would be under water: they feel thin, exposed, dizzy, light, as if they might float away. They sleep in a cistern, weighted, with only the face above the surface, or, more and more often, with no part of themselves above it at all.

The voice drops. It gains an undertone, like a second speaker standing just behind the first. Ears bleed on pressure days. Fingernails thicken and yellow. Hair falls out in the bath in long dark ropes that drift on the water like weed.

### The Brink

At the Brink the person has heard the invitation in full. It is no longer a pull. It is a voice that knows their name, that has always known it, that is patient and kind and very, very old. The body at the Brink is heavy beyond its size: floors creak under a slight woman, beds break, horses refuse to carry them. They sink in mud to the knee. They are cold to the touch, so cold that kisses leave a white mark. Their tears are salt enough to sting cuts. Their sweat leaves rime. They hear the sea in every silence, not as memory but as direction, as a compass hears north.

Most of the Called at the Brink go, and most of those who come back come back Tenanted. A Tenanted person stands at the Brink permanently: something sits in them, and something of them may still be there underneath.

### The Seating

The Tenant takes the whole house. What walks around afterward is a piece of Dromm, patient, pressing outward against the skin. The fully Seated do not look very different from the Tenanted, at first. Then they stop bothering with the smaller courtesies: they no longer blink, or answer to their name, or eat. Their skin bows outward between the ribs, as if under pressure from within, and their weight becomes impossible. They are not violent. They are simply present, the way a deep place is present, and the air around them thickens until lamps gutter and ears ring. The Admiralty keeps them in the lowest cisterns of Fathomhouse and records them as **"retired to depth"**. Nobody is sure any longer how many there are, or what they are waiting for.

[box: The Tenant Question]
The bible's central uncertainty in Brinehollow is whether the Tenanted are still the people they were. The GM should decide privately, and is encouraged to decide differently for different Tenanted. Some are entirely Dromm wearing a stolen memory. Some are a person and a piece of god sharing a house, each aware of the other. Some may be the original person, returned and unchanged, with every neighbor convinced otherwise (see Esk Penhallow). Players should never be certain. Seeing a loved one return Tenanted is a Dread 3 horror. Seeing one return and being unable to tell is worse, and should be played across sessions, not resolved in one.

## Land and Weather

A long grey coast of shingle, salt marsh, and cliff, three hundred miles from the Cradlewrack border in the north to the Fatlands marches in the south, under a sky the color of a gutted herring. Beyond the old shoreline lies the **Uncovered**: a newly exposed seabed that stretches further west every year. It is not flat. It is a country of ravines and shelves and drowned hills, of weed forests gone to brown leather, of wrecks standing upright on their keels as though still sailing, and of things with too many ribs.

It rains salt. Not a little salt: rain in Brinehollow tastes like a tear and dries on window glass in white lace. Fog comes in from a sea that is now miles away, rolling across the Uncovered in walls, and it smells of tide pools and rot. Gulls nest inland, on church roofs, in chimneys, and they are thin and angry and pick at the eyes of sleeping dogs.

On **pressure days** the air thickens until ears bleed. These come without warning, perhaps a dozen times a year: a sudden heaviness, a hush, birds dropping out of the air to sit stunned on the ground, windows bowing inward with a creak and sometimes bursting. Candle flames stand flat and blue. Everyone in the street touches their ears and comes away with blood on their fingers, and the old men say "the Fathom's turning over." Children have died on pressure days, crushed in their cots by nothing anyone could see. Callings triple on the night after one.

And there is the **indoor tide**. In every cellar in the country, however far inland, water rises twice a day and drains away again, leaving kelp on the stairs. The tide is cold, clear, and comes up from the floor, not the walls. It brings small gifts: shells, sea-glass, a child's shoe, a ring. It has been known to bring back things the Called were wearing when they went.

### Places of Brinehollow

#### Lowmark

The capital, a nineteen-mile pier-city built out across the dry seabed, section by section, after the sea. Old Lowmark on the original shore, now called the Headland, holds the Admiralty, the Sounding Hall, Fathomhouse, and the tide-post. From it the Mile runs west on tarred pilings sixty feet high: a wooden road lined with houses, chandlers, chapels, taverns and bail-houses, all creaking, all leaning a little westward. Mile One is rich, Mile Seven is Netwatch barracks, Miles Twelve to Sixteen are slum, roped and chained and stinking of tar and bail-water. The Nineteenth ends in a working harbor where a few licensed boats go out and come back with catches nobody likes to describe. Sixty feet below, the old seabed is dark and wet and full of what the city drops. People live down there, too.

#### Longslab

The beach of the Beaching. Permanently wet, though no sea has touched it in a century. Water seeps up through the shingle as if the beach were a sponge someone is always pressing. Nothing grows there, and gulls will not land on it. At the tide-turns the wet ground breathes: a slow sigh across two miles of stones, and a smell of the deep. Pilgrims of the Deepening come to lie face-down on it and listen. The Longslab middens beneath are full of bones too large to be any animal's.

#### The Uncovered

The exposed seabed, salvage country, lawless and strange, with a new mile of it every year. Near the old shore it is cracked white salt littered with six centuries of wreckage, skeletons still in the rigging. Further out it turns to mud flats, leather-weed jungle, and drowned valleys where dead reefs stand up like ruined cathedrals, blue with rot. Things live there that came up as the water fell and did not die properly: cart-sized crabs, slow and soft-shelled; ribbed things that drag themselves on fins across the mud at night; fish that breathe air, badly, with a sound like a sick man snoring. And there are older things, uncovered for the first time since before the Gorging: carved stones, paved roads running west into the deep, and at least one table.

#### The Hanging Abbey

The Anchorites' cliff, forty miles north of Lowmark: four hundred feet of black basalt above a field of dry white boulders that was once the sea. A thousand living monks hang on it, chained by iron collars to rings set in the rock, each on a ledge or in a scooped cell, most open to the salt rain. Food comes down in baskets; waste falls. At dawn and dusk the whole cliff coughs at once, and water runs down the rock in sheets. At night they pray aloud, or scream, and from below it is impossible to tell which.

#### The Trench

The place far out where the water is still deep, and where it seems to be going. Thirty miles west of Lowmark's last pier the Uncovered ends at a cliff edge, and beyond it lies black water that has not dropped at all, an abyss of unknown depth with a current that runs downward. From the edge you can hear it draining: a low, endless sound like a throat swallowing. Salvagers who go to the brink report a warm updraft that smells of breath, and a light, very deep, that comes and goes.

#### Fathomhouse

The palace of the Lung-lines princes on the Headland, a grey stone pile with more of itself below ground than above. Its upper floors are halls of glass tanks, where the princes hold court underwater and courtiers in dry clothes bow to them through eight inches of green brine. Its cellars are flooded and have been extended twice to accommodate the Dowager Below. The lowest cisterns hold the retired-to-depth. Servants work in rubber aprons and never go below the fourth stair.

#### The Prop Yards at Keelrow (new)

Four hundred warships stand on dry ground in long rows across the old Keelrow anchorage, three miles south of the Headland, each upright on a cradle of timber props, masts dressed, fully crewed. Sailors reef and furl sails that catch only wind and stand watches over a horizon of salt flats. Beneath the keels the sailors' families live in tents and sheds, a town of children who have never seen the sea and are proud of their fathers' ships.

#### Saltwick (new)

The largest inland town, thirty miles east of Lowmark on the Rim Road spur, and the country's market for everything the Uncovered yields. Its cellars flood twice a day like every cellar in the country, and the townsfolk have given up and turned them into salt-pans: each house harvests its own indoor tide, dries it on racks, and sells the salt. Saltwick salt is reputedly the best on the Table. Visitors who eat too much of it begin to dream of drowning, and some begin to bail.

#### The Wreck Orchard (new)

Eight miles out on the Uncovered, in a shallow basin, nearly two hundred wrecks of every age stand upright on the mud in loose rows, as if parked by a harbormaster. Nobody can explain why they are upright. Nobody can explain why their hatches are all open, or why their figureheads all face west. Salvagers live in the newest of them. Lantern-light can be seen in the oldest ones at night, which are empty.

#### Gullcry Strand (new)

A long stretch of the old shoreline north of Lowmark, now a beach without a sea, where the Deepening holds its processions. A road of crushed white shell, laid by the faithful over two centuries, runs straight from the strand across the Uncovered toward the far water, nine miles long and marked every hundred paces by a post with a lantern-hook. On procession nights every hook is lit.

#### The Sounding Hall (new)

The courthouse of the Drowned Bench on the Headland: a round stone hall whose floor is a shallow pool, ankle-deep, fed by the indoor tide. The accused stand in the water. The Tenanted judges sit on a stone bench at the far side, utterly still, and their verdicts are delivered in two voices. The frieze of the Beaching runs around the walls. The pool is always cold, and witnesses say that when a lie is spoken in the Hall, the water rises.

#### Lobbett's Drop (new)

The place, somewhere on the far Uncovered, where Cray Lobbett found the stone table with eight chairs. He has told nobody its location. Salvage crews have searched for it for two years, and three of them did not come back. The name is a joke on the Mile. It is not funny out there.

For sixty years Brinehollow was the happiest land on the Table. The herring came back so thick that nets broke, whales beached themselves like gifts, and the Lung-lines, who coughed water twice a day and swam like seals, became admirals and princes. When every newborn of the Brimming arrived gurgling and had to be held head-down until the water ran, it was called a mark of belonging. Inlanders who did not bail were "dry," which is still an insult.

The First Calling ended all that in one night. Four hundred people of Lowmark rose under a licked-clean moon and walked into the harbor in a long, quiet, orderly line, holding hands. The eleven who came back were the first Tenanted, and those who took them in were the first Wreckwives. By 240 the Admiralty had noticed that the Tenanted made perfect judges, and in four centuries no one has successfully appealed a verdict of the Drowned Bench. In 512 the sea failed to return to its old line, and the Admiralty declared a temporary low tide. It has been temporary ever since. In the last ten years the sea has fallen faster than in the previous fifty, the Callings have quadrupled, and the Tenanted on the Bench have gone from nine to ninety. In 639 Brenna Kelp presented the arithmetic that explained it, and the Admiralty made her arithmetic a crime.

## Rule, Law and Politics

Brinehollow is ruled by the **Tidal Admiralty**, a navy that has become a government. Its fleet sits aground on props at Keelrow, fully crewed, drilled daily, awaiting the sea's return. Rank is still given in ships: a district governor is a Captain, a province is a Squadron, the treasury is the Purser's Office, and a Captain may never have been afloat. Laws are issued as Standing Orders and read aloud from the deck of the flagship *Steadfast*, which has not moved since 588.

Above the Admiralty sit the **Lung-lines princes**, the House of Vasht and its cadet branches, who can no longer breathe air for long and hold court from glass tanks in Fathomhouse. They sign the Admiralty's orders with a wet seal, through a hatch. Beneath the Admiralty sits the **Drowned Bench**: the Tenanted judges, whose verdicts are final, and whose loyalties nobody has ever thought to question out loud.

The great political fact is water. A land of sailors and fishers has lost its sea, and the Admiralty's entire policy is to pretend this is temporary. To say "stranded" of the fleet is an offense against the Articles. To publish the Salt Assay's figures is treason. Every faction's position is ultimately a position on the water: whether it is coming back, whether it should, and where it has gone.

Law is naval. Crimes are tried before the Drowned Bench, standing ankle-deep in the Sounding Hall's pool. Common sentences are flogging with a wet rope, fines in lacks, impressment into the Dry Fleet, and **loosing**: the convict is sentenced to sleep unanchored for a set number of nights, under guard, with the Netwatch instructed not to intervene. Loosing is not formally a death sentence. Everyone knows it is one, slower and more uncertain, and that the Bench's Tenanted judges hand it down with what looks like compassion. Neglecting to bail the helpless is murder. Cutting another's rope is the worst murder of all, and its punishment is to be loosed for life.

## Culture and Daily Life

**Food.** Brinehollow eats what the Uncovered gives and what the Rim Road sells. Before the fall it ate fish, and fish are now a luxury hauled nineteen miles down the Mile in wet barrels. The poor eat Uncovered shellfish dug from the drying mud, which are safe for those who bail and poison to anyone dry; salt-cured gull; seaweed bread; and Fatlands meat in brine, which everyone pretends not to examine. Meals are cold and salty. Hot food is considered a little vulgar. The grace before eating with strangers is "Lack keep away, and keep you dry."

**Dress.** Oilcloth, tarred wool, sealskin for the rich. Everyone wears a rope at the waist, coiled, for anchoring at night: the cheap ones are hemp, the fine ones silk-and-wire, and a noble's waist-rope may be braided with silver and the hair of ancestors. The bail-bucket hangs from it. Losing your bucket is like losing your shoes. Course-sufferers wear veils of wet gauze to keep their faces moist.

**Marriage.** Sweethearts exchange bail-buckets. A wedding is the tying of a shared anchoring rope, each partner knotting the other's end before witnesses, and the vow is "I will hold your line." Marriage among the Lung-lines is a matter of keeping the blood deep; marriages between first cousins are ordinary, and the Cutwrights' ledgers for the House of Vasht show older and closer pairings that the family does not discuss and the College does not publish. Remarriage after a Calling is permitted after a year and a day, which leads, often enough, to a household with two spouses when the first comes back Tenanted.

**Death.** Those who die of ordinary causes are laid face-down on the Uncovered, weighted with stones, for the tide to take; the tide no longer comes, so the bodies lie in long rows on the salt flats, and the gulls are fat. The Called receive a **rope funeral**: their cut anchoring rope is divided among the family, one piece each and one for the wall, and the name is cut into the nearest tide-post. There is no body. There is never a body. Families keep the pieces of rope in a box by the bed, and hold them during the count.

**Children.** Taught to bail before they read, roped to a parent's wrist at night until seven. Children's games include holding-breath contests, which parents forbid and which are universal, and "Called," in which one child walks blindfold toward a wall while the others try to drag them back. Children are said to see the Tenanted most clearly: a Tenanted parent's own child often refuses to be held by them, at first, and then forgets why.

**Festivals.** Low Water, at the lowest tide of Carving, when the Tenanted gather on the Uncovered and sing. The Knotting, in early Grace, when every rope in a household is replaced. Tablenight, when families set a bowl of seawater at the table's head and leave the west door unbarred, once a year, for whoever might come home.

**Oaths on the dead.** The most binding oath in Brinehollow is sworn on a piece of a Called person's rope, held in the fist, with the words "By the line that did not hold." To break it is to invite the Called one to come back for you. People believe this literally, and sometimes they are right.

**Proverbs.** "Tie your own knot." "A dry man drowns twice." "Every wall has an ear, and every ear is a neighbor's." "Don't sleep facing your wishes." "The sea gives back, but not change."

## Factions

### The Tidal Admiralty

The state: proud, hidebound, and commanding the largest landlocked navy in the world. The Admiralty believes, as an article of faith and of law, that the sea will return, and that when it does Brinehollow must be ready to sail. It wants order, the illusion of normality, and above all silence about the numbers. Its officers are brave, competent, and drilled to the bone in an art that has no use. Enemies: the Salt Assay's leakers, the Deepening, the Second Table's agents within its own ranks. Allies: the Netwatch, the Rim Road Company, which still pays port tolls on a port with no water. A PC joins by enlistment at any Admiralty house on the Mile.

### The Anchorites

An order who chain themselves permanently to rock, so the Calling can never take them. Their abbey is the Hanging Abbey, a cliff face hung with a thousand living monks. They believe the Calling is a temptation, and that refusing it, night after night, for a whole life, is the only holiness left. They want to endure. Some want more: to chain the whole country. Enemies: the Deepening, whom they call drowners. Allies: Wreckwives, who send them food. A PC joins by spending a month on the cliff in a trial-chain; most who try come down. A few did not want to.

### The Deepening

A cult that treats the Calling as a sacrament. They believe Dromm is not regrowing but welcoming, that the sea is going home and taking its children with it, and that walking in willingly, in procession, with lanterns, is not death but homecoming. They want everyone baptized. Their processions at Gullcry Strand are illegal, regular, and quietly attended by the Netwatch, who are under orders to intervene and seldom do. Enemies: Anchorites, the Admiralty, every family with a member who joined. Allies: none declared; the Tenanted, it is whispered. ### The Netwatch

The Admiralty's night patrol. They hunt the Called along the shore with nets, hooks, and rope, and drag them home. They know every alley that runs west, every gap in the pier rails, every rope-cutter. They are heroes in the households they have saved and bogeymen to the Called themselves, who fight in their trance with terrible, painless strength. Enemies: the Deepening. ### The Wreckwives

A sisterhood, open to all sexes, of those whose spouses came back Tenanted. They share advice on how to live with a stranger who knows your name: what to say, what not to ask, how to tell if anyone is still in there, how to stop yourself loving what came back, or whether to. They keep records of every Tenanted spouse, and their records are better than the Admiralty's. They want the truth, and fear it. ### The Salt Assay

The office that measures the falling sea. Its figures are a state secret. Its assayers stand at posts along the old shore and the new piers with chains and gauges, and in cellars with buckets and scales. Since Brenna Kelp's finding the office has been half-purged, and its surviving clerks know that the numbers they record are evidence of something the Admiralty will kill to hide. They want to be believed, or to be safe; it is getting hard to be both. A PC joins as a clerk, and learns quickly to keep a second ledger.

## Brinehollow Now

The Salt Assay has done the arithmetic the Admiralty forbade: the volume of sea lost each year matches the number of Tenanted. The ocean is moving into the people, a bucket at a time. Every Called person who comes back brings a measure of the deep with them, folded up inside, and the sea is that much lower. The Tenanted now sit on every court in the land. A scavenger has found something on the Uncovered that should not be under any sea. And a shipwright is building a vessel to follow the water down.

The Callings are accelerating with the fall. Last Carving there were three nights on which more than five hundred walked. The Netwatch cannot cover the Mile. Dry Jonah's bottled brine has turned up in the Vigil's rouse, and sleepless strangers four hundred miles inland have started walking west. On the Uncovered, salvagers report a tall figure, seen at a distance at dusk, setting a table on the mud with great care, and walking on.

### Adventure Hooks

- **The Rope-Cutter of Mile Fourteen.** Nine ropes cut in nine nights on the same street, with no knife ever found. The Netwatch suspects a murderer. The families suspect each other. The truth is that a Tenanted grandmother has been walking the street at night, doing what she believes is a kindness, and the Drowned Bench has already ruled it lawful.
- **The Follow's Pilot.** Dagna Hull needs someone to lead the iron vessel's trial descent at the Trench edge, and someone else to find out which of her nine hundred volunteers is sabotaging it, and why the saboteur weeps.
- **Three Copies.** Brenna Kelp has hidden three copies of her finding. The Admiralty, the Second Table, and the Wreckwives all want them. One copy is in the Hanging Abbey with Father Ebb; one is in a bail-bucket that has passed through four sweethearts' hands; and one has been read aloud on a procession night, to the Deepening, who wept with joy.
- **Eight Chairs.** Cray Lobbett will take the PCs to his table, for a price. Getting there is three days on the far Uncovered. Getting back is less certain. On the table, someone has laid a place.

## The People of Brinehollow

#### High Admiral Corvin Sund

Commander of four hundred warships standing on dry ground. Sixty-eight, square, grey-whiskered, in a blue dress coat kept so wet with brine that it never quite dries and smells of a harbor at low water. His voice is the parade-ground bark of a man who sailed as a boy, the last year the fleet floated, and has spent fifty years waiting to sail again. He inspects the ships weekly, has the hulls scraped of barnacles that are not there, and will hang any officer who says the word "stranded." He has hanged four. He wants the sea back, and failing that he wants no one to admit it is gone. He fears the Salt Assay's numbers, and more than that he fears the morning he will stand on the *Steadfast*'s deck and feel the pull himself. **Secret:** he has had the flagship's keel sawn half through in the night, so that it can never float away without him. PCs meet him at an inspection, or in the dock when he wants a leaker found.

#### Harbormistress Kessa Drowle

She builds Lowmark's mile of new pier each year: fifty-two, tar to the elbows, a carpenter's pencil behind each ear, a cough she bails into the sea of sawdust on the work-barges. She speaks in measurements. She has calculated the year the timber runs out and the year the sea drops off the shelf entirely. They are the same year, nine years from now, and she has told no one but the PCs if they buy her enough rum. She wants to finish the Chase with dignity. She fears the twentieth mile: the survey shows the seabed beyond it falls away into the Trench's slope, and the pilings will have nothing to stand on. **Secret:** she has begun ordering pilings longer than any sea could need, and she does not know why. PCs meet her when she hires guards for the timber barges, which the Uncovered's salvagers raid.

#### Justice Maren Oake of the Drowned Bench

Called at thirty, returned after forty days. She is fifty now, broad and grey-skinned, with black eyes and hands that never move during a hearing. The air around her is cold and close and smells of the deep, and those who stand before her feel their ears pop. She speaks in two tones at once, and the lower one sometimes finishes first. She has never given an unjust verdict, and she has sentenced more people to loosing than any judge in history, always gently. She wants justice, in a sense larger than the Admiralty's. **Secret:** her husband Tam has visited her chambers every week for twenty years, and she has never once told him that the woman he married is still in there, folded small, awake, and listening. PCs meet her standing ankle-deep in her court.

#### Father Ebb

Abbot of the Anchorites, chained to the same rock for fifty-one years. #### Tolly Brack, the Bailer Boy

Eleven years old, small, freckled under the salt-grime, with strong thin hands and a voice cracked from shouting "Bail-o!" up the Mile at dawn. He goes door to door bailing those too old or weak to cough for themselves, holding them over his knee and pounding their backs, for a crumb a time. He knows from the color of the water who will be Called next. Blue means soon. He does not tell them. He tells their families, if they pay, and has started telling them for free. He wants his mother, who was Called when he was six; he fears that he is starting to see blue in his own bucket. PCs meet him at dawn, at the door of anyone they are visiting.

#### Sennet Gull, the Wreckwife

Her husband Davey walked into the sea and came back kinder. Thirty-four, sharp-faced, red-knuckled, smelling of the mussel-cannery where she works. She keeps him in the cistern under the house, brings him his dinner, sits on the edge and talks to him about her day while he floats on his back with his black eyes open, listening, and says the right things. The old Davey drank and hit. This one does not. She has started to prefer him. That is what frightens her. **Secret:** she has begun to notice that she can no longer remember the old Davey's voice, and she has wondered, in the dark, whether the thing in the cistern took that too. PCs meet her at a Wreckwives' table, asking for advice she does not want.

#### Dr. Isolde Reave

A lung surgeon of the Headland, forty-five, thin, bespectacled, in a leather apron stiff with brine and old blood. Her rooms smell of carbolic and low tide. She opened a drowned chest at high tide and found the water in it had a current: it moved in the lungs in a slow circle, westward. In the next one she found a shell, alive, of no known species, pink and whorled, which she keeps in a jar and which has grown. She wants to understand. She fears that she does. **Secret:** she has started opening chests that are not yet drowned, with the patients' consent and sometimes without it, and the shells are in the living too. PCs meet her when they need a Course chest explained, or a body procured.

#### Hask Tarn, the Pilot

He guides salvage parties across the Uncovered. Sixty, lean, weathered, with a sounding-lead on a cord around his neck that he swings as he walks. He used to pilot ships over the same ground and navigates by remembered depth: "Six fathom here, sand bottom; mind, it shelves." He is never lost. He will not go near the places where he once lost the bottom, where the lead ran out of line, because they are still there, and they are still bottomless, and he has heard them breathe. He wants one more season's pay. He fears the deep places, and the day his memory of depth fails. **Secret:** he knows the way to the Stone Table; he steered over it in 581, and the lead came up warm. PCs hire him.

#### Nerys Colm, the Half-Called

She walked into the surf up to her chin and then, uniquely, turned around. Twenty-eight now, wild-haired, salt-burned, with one eye that has gone wholly black and one still blue. She lives in a sealed room in the Netwatch barracks, by her own request, with wax in the keyhole. She has heard the invitation in full and can repeat it. Nobody who has listened to her has stayed ashore. She wants someone to tell her what made her turn. She fears that she knows: she heard the eighth voice in the chorus, under the others, and it was hungry. **Secret:** she has written the invitation down, once, in a letter she meant to burn. #### Dagna Hull, the Shipwright

#### Cray Lobbett

A scavenger on the far Uncovered, forty, lean as a dried eel, eyes red from salt wind, hands scarred by weed-knives. He found a stone table on the seabed, older than the Gorging, with eight chairs. #### Old Wet Tom

#### Ilse Marl, Choir-Mistress of the Low Water

Sixty, small, fierce, ink-blotted, wrapped in shawls on the Uncovered at the lowest tide of every month with a lantern, a lap-desk, and frozen fingers. At the lowest tide the Tenanted gather and sing: hundreds of them, in a ring on the mud, eyes closed, mouths open, in a music with no words and a beat like a slow heart. She alone writes it down, in a notation she invented. Laid end to end, forty years of her transcriptions make a nautical chart: soundings, currents, and a course leading west and down. **Secret:** the chart has a destination, and it is not the Trench. PCs meet her at the Low Water, where she asks them to hold her lantern.

#### Finn Undertow, the Bellman

He rings Lowmark's tide bell so the city knows when to bail. Forty-four, one-eyed, roped to the bell tower on the Headland, with forearms like hawsers. The tides in the body no longer match the tides of the sea, and he has to choose which to ring. For years he chose the sea. Now he rings the body, and the city coughs as one when he does. He feels the body's tide in his own chest, and in everyone's, as a single swell. **Secret:** the body tide is drifting too, toward a single moment in which every Brinehollower will fill at once, and he can count the days. PCs meet him if they climb the tower to ask why the bell rang early.

#### Reeve Calder Stokes

An auditor sent by the Rim Road Company to price Brinehollow's losses. Thirty-three, Oathen-trained, pale, precise, ledgers under one arm. He is an outsider with dry lungs. He has been here three months, and this morning he tasted salt. He wants to finish the audit and leave. He fears that he has been asked to price something that has no price. His Company contract, sworn before Jessamy Quill, binds him to deliver the audit in person in Tacit. **Secret:** the audit shows the Admiralty's tolls and Brenna Kelp's numbers point to the same thing, and the Company already knew. PCs meet him hiring them to escort him, which he notes in the ledger as "guides, drying."

#### Ottiline Hawse, the Ropewalker

Mistress of the Ropewalk on Mile Three, a quarter-mile shed where anchoring ropes are spun, laid, and tarred. Fifty, hands black with tar, smelling of hemp and pine. Every rope in Lowmark passes through her shed, and she keeps a ledger of every rope cut. She can tell by the cut: the blade, the angle, the strength of the hand. She knows which ropes were cut by the sleeper, which by the beloved, and which by no hand at all. #### Esk Penhallow, the Returned Who Wasn't

Called at twenty-six, gone eight weeks, came back. He swears he is not Tenanted. He says he remembers the sea: cold, the weight, a voice asking, and then a sandbar and a long walk home, starved. His wife will not let him in. His mother has rung the Wreckwives. His children scream. He is a little heavier, a little slower, he cannot explain why his voice has dropped, and he does not believe it has. #### Abel Drainer, the Wringer

Leader of the Purgation's Brinehollow cell, a gaunt, cheerful former fishmonger with a mangle in his cellar. #### Cutwright Ansby Wrack

The Cutwrights' College's genealogist in Lowmark, thirty-nine, soft-spoken, with ink-stained cuffs and a cough she pretends is a cold. #### Gannet Shale, the Table-Seer

A salvager girl of nineteen, quick and sunburned, who walked the far Uncovered alone for a week and saw, at dusk, a tall stooping figure laying a table on the mud. White cloth. Candles. A chair pulled out. It looked at her and waited, and she ran. She went back the next morning. The table was gone. The candles' wax was still on the mud, and the drips went west. She has told the story in every tavern from Saltwick to Mile Nineteen, and has been laughed at in all of them except one, where an old man in a Fast traveler's coat bought her a drink and asked, very carefully, whether she had been thanked. ## Playing a Brinehollower

You were born gasping. Your mother held you over her knee and beat the sea out of you, and you have bailed twice a day ever since. You sleep roped. You call names through the wall. Somewhere west of you, the water knows your name.

**Attribute bonus:** +1 LUNG. Brinehollowers hold their breath longer, swim better, and move more quietly than any people on the Table.

**Bailing.** A Brinehollower who misses a bailing suffers −2 to all rolls until they bail, and after a further hour begins to drown (1 Flesh per ten minutes). Another character can bail a helpless one with a Routine HAND + Stitching roll.

**The Sop: Wading.** Stand in salt water, the sea or any basin of brine, past the knees, through one full turn of the body's tide, facing west, listening. Remove 1d6 Hunger. The god is pleased, and the next Calling roll you make is at +2 difficulty.

**The Tooth.** When a Brinehollower Partakes, the ears pop, the chest fills, and the voice drops into its second register. Witnesses hear the sea in the room. On a Tooth showing 1, the character coughs up a gout of cold water, sand, and something small and living, and loses their next action to bailing.

### Gifts

**The Taste (Regrowth 1–3).** Choose one. *Deep Breath:* hold your breath for ten minutes per point of Caul; you cannot drown in that time and ignore smoke, gas and choking. *Salt-Sense:* by tasting the air you know of a pressure day an hour before it comes, and by tasting someone's bail-water, their Regrowth stage. Cost: either Gift used in front of dry folk marks you as deep-blooded.

**The Appetite (Regrowth 4–6).** Choose one. *Tide-Sight:* Partake to know the direction and distance of the nearest body of salt water, and of every Called and Tenanted person within a mile; −2 to resist The West for the rest of the night. *Pressure:* Partake when grappling or striking to add 2 damage and make the target's ears bleed; on a Helping, the target is stunned for a round. Cost: each use leaves you with salt rime on the skin, and dogs will not come near you for a day.

**The Course (Regrowth 7–9).** Gain *Fathom-Body:* Armor +1 from thickened skin; see in total darkness; breathe water as easily as air; ignore cold. Cost: in open air for more than a day without immersion, −1 to all rolls; you must sleep submerged or gain 1 Hunger each night. Choose also one: *The Weight* (Partake: become crushingly heavy for a scene; cannot be moved, knocked down or carried, and doors and floors may give) or *Quiet Chest* (no breath, no heartbeat audible; +3 to Stealth; dry folk who notice must make a Dread 1 check).

**The Brink (Regrowth 10–11).** Gain *Voice of the Deep:* speak in the god's register; Partake to give one command that anyone at the Appetite or beyond must resist with Caul + Resolve vs Grim, or obey as if Called. Cost: each use, roll a Tooth; on a 1 or 2, the Passenger answers the next question asked of you, in its own voice.

### Wants

- **The West.** Trigger: dusk, a west-facing window, the sound of surf. Resist with Caul + Resolve or go and stand and watch until full dark.
- **The Water.** Trigger: sleep near any basin, well or barrel. Resist or wake with your face in it.
- **To Keep Under.** Trigger: learning a secret, finding something lost. Resist or hide, sink or bury it, and tell no one.
- **The Silence.** Trigger: being asked where someone went. Resist or say nothing at all for the rest of the scene.

### The Seating

At Regrowth 12 the Tenant takes the whole house. The character is Called on the next night that comes, without a roll, and nothing can hold them. If they come back, it is with their face and their memories, as a piece of Dromm. The character is lost. The player narrates the last walk west, and may write down one courtesy the Tenant will extend to the companions forever, which the GM must honor.

### Background Concepts

- **Netwatch Hooker.** You have dragged the Called home by the hair. You know the Mile at night, and the look they give you.
- **Wreckwife's Child.** One of your parents came back. You were the one who first noticed. Nobody believed you.
- **Uncovered Salvager.** You make your living on the dead seabed among upright wrecks and things with too many ribs, and you have seen lights out there you have never reported.
