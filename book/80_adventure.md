# The Ninth Chair

> A good host lays one place more than he expects. A great host never asks who it is for.
>> — carved over the hearth at Thimble Cross, Rim Road Company waystation, 402 A.G.

[fiction]
From the station ledger of Thimble Cross, kept by Warden Josse Haverick of the Rim Road Company, under oath. Entries for the last week of Lack, 641 A.G.
Seventh day before Tablenight. Snow since noon, if it is snow. It does not melt on the hand. It sits on the skin and goes soft, like lard left by a stove. Convoy of the Renderers' Union arrived at dusk out of the south, eleven wagons, forty-four barrels, Cullmaster's seal on the lead wagon. Sixth day before. Road closed both ways. The white lies a hand deep on the yard and has veins in it. Stores: flour for nine days at half bread. Guests: twenty-two, family four, myself. One drover missing from the yard at dusk. No tracks. A smell of baking where he stood.
Fifth day before. There is a table in the common room. It was not there at midnight. I sat up with the lamp and the ledger and I did not sleep and I did not see it come. It is long and dark and set for nine and the cloth is whiter than anything I have seen in my life. The bread is warm. Nobody has touched the bread. There are cards at the places. One of them has my name on it. I have entered it in the inventory under Furniture, Not Company Property. I do not know what else to enter it under.
Fifth day before, later. Mistress Thimble has polished the table. Nobody asked her to. She says she could not help it. She says a table like that ought to be kept nice.
Tablenight eve. I am still bound to hold this station until relieved. I wish it entered that I was never relieved.

## An Introductory Adventure

*The Ninth Chair* is an introductory adventure for **The Long Grace**, written for three to five new characters of mixed lands and playable in two or three sessions of four to five hours. It takes place in the last week of Lack, 641 A.G., at a Rim Road Company waystation called **Thimble Cross**, where the great ring road bends between the warm meat-plains of the Fatlands and the gold, empty edge of the Fast. A storm of something that is not snow strands the characters there with a convoy of Fatlands meat, a houseful of strangers, and a long table that nobody carried in.

It is a siege, a murder mystery, and a famine, all at once, and it ends on the longest night of the year. It is designed to teach the core of the game: the Grace Roll, Dread and Fray, Hunger and Partaking, the way each land's curse travels in the blood of its people, and the first rule every child of the Fast learns, which is that there are invitations you must not accept.

It is also designed to hurt. The adventure's climax is a sacrifice that the community demands, that somebody gives, and that buys nothing at all. Players should leave the table understanding, in their stomachs, what kind of world this is: a world in which the gods are not angry and cannot be bargained with, in which good people do the brave thing and it does not matter, and in which the Host, the only god never eaten, is kind.

[box: Content and Care]
This adventure contains graphic gore and transformation, cannibalism, the desecration of graves, a ritual murder, a killing within a family, an inherited bloodline curse with implied incest between adults, contagious madness, and a suicide. Talk with your players before you begin. The suicide in Day Three is written to be played as grief, never as spectacle: its method is never described and need never be, and the scene is about who finds the body, what was in the note, and what the people left behind begin to say to themselves afterward. Use the tools your table prefers to step around anything that cuts deeper than the game should. Horror works best among people who trust each other.
[/box]

## The Secret Truth

Read this section before anything else. Nothing in it should reach the players directly. All of it can reach them in pieces.

### Four Things Are Happening at Once

**The Reaping has followed the convoy.** Forty-four barrels of Fatlands meat came up the Rim Road under the seal of Cullmaster Hedley Brawn. Thirteen are notched with a small knife-mark under the stamp, and they hold the harvest of the Dusk Acres: Rootkin, field-grown meat with faces, and, packed in brine among them, the remains of people the Fatlands soil took and then, being a generous field, gave partly back. The soil of the Fatlands is the flesh of Ummer, the Laden, and that meat is still, in a sense the Board would rather not examine, ground. It has carried its weather with it. **Marbling**, the Fatlands' first frost, the whitening of the fat in the soil, has followed the wagons north for six days and settled over Thimble Cross like snow. Where the marbling lies, the ground is Fatlands ground. And on Fatlands ground in the dark of the year, at dusk, people are Reaped.

**The Host has come to dinner.** Orrum, the only god never eaten, has been walking out of the Fast along the Rim Road, laying tables where people are hungry. Thimble Cross is the most perfect invitation it has ever been offered. The inn is starving. The convoy's hunger is the bottomless hunger of the Fatlands. And the family who keep the inn are bound, by a Company charter sworn in Oathen two hundred and thirty-nine years ago, to "turn no traveller away, nor refuse any guest, while the blood of Thimble endures." A house that cannot refuse is a house the Host can enter. On the night after the convoy arrives, a table appears in the common room, laid for nine. On Tablenight, the longest night of Lack, the Host will come to sit at its head, and everyone who takes their place will be Seated: alive, smiling, eating from empty plates, never to rise.

**The guests are not innocent.** A poacher means to sell a caged Unfed woman to the Second Table, two of whose nobles are here under false names to measure the Host's table for carving. A Purgation preacher is looking for a high-Cut throat to open. The innkeeper is about to feed his guests his own mother. A Vigil courier on her last rouse is seeing things, and the things are catching.

**The ninth chair is not for anyone here.** Orrum's newest tables on the Rim Road are always laid with one place more than there are guests to fill it. That place has a chair pushed in square to the table, and at Thimble Cross, for the first time anyone has recorded, it has a card. The card bears no name. It bears a single mark in the oldest form of Table-speech, the root word that means both "to worship" and "to eat," with a stroke beneath it that scholars read as "after," or "last," or "late." The people of Thimble Cross will come to believe that the ninth card names someone who must be given to the Host so that the rest may be spared. They will argue about who. They will choose. Someone will sit in the ninth chair. And the Host will come anyway, lift the volunteer gently out of a chair that was never theirs, lay a new place for them, and go on with dinner. The ninth chair was not laid for any guest of Thimble Cross. Welcome Ardent, the Host's steward, sets it at every table himself, and in six hundred years he has never once seen who it is for.

[box: What the Ninth Chair Is (Do Not Say)]
The ninth chair is the place every old carving keeps for the Eighth, the figure chiseled out of the world's history. This adventure never says so, and neither should you. If players connect the empty chair to the eighth place in old carvings, to the stone table under the sea with eight chairs, or to the eight sides of Hallowboard, let them. Do not confirm it. Welcome Ardent does not know. The Host, if it knows, does not discuss its guest list. The horror of the climax is that the chair belongs to someone who has not arrived, that everyone at Thimble Cross can feel it waiting, and that nothing they offer fills it.
[/box]

## Background

### Thimble Cross

Thimble Cross stands where the Rim Road, coming north out of the Fatlands, bends hard west to skirt the edge of the Fast. A rutted track, the **Hungry Lane**, leaves the bend and runs north into the gold country of the Unfed, toward the hearthless town of Wanting; it is used by Crumb-Runners, Hostwatch trackers, and nobody else. The station is old, solid, and plain: a two-storey inn of grey fieldstone with a slate roof and seventeen chimneys, a walled yard, a long stable, a smokehouse, a toll house with a Company bell, and, behind a low wall, a little graveyard of nine stones.

It was founded in 402 A.G. by **Hadley Thimble**, a Crumb-Runner who had spent her life carrying grain into the Fast on her back and been spat on for it by the people it kept alive. She left the Fast, married a Fatlands drover, bought the crossing, and swore her charter with the Rim Road Company in Oathen before the Company's notary of the day. The charter granted the Thimble blood the keeping of the station in perpetuity in return for a single clause, which Hadley, who had grown up among people who refused everything, insisted on writing herself: *that the keepers of Thimble Cross shall turn no traveller away, nor refuse any guest, while the blood of Thimble endures.*

Promises sworn in Oathen follow the body that swore them, and the bodies that come after it, if the drafting says so. Hadley's drafting said so. Her descendants cannot refuse a guest without their bodies breaking to match. Mistress Hepzibah Thimble barred the door once, in her youth, against a leper in the rain, and two fingers of her right hand folded backwards to the wrist with a sound like kindling; she wears them that way still. And because the charter binds only "the blood of Thimble," and a Clausewright consulted in 488 A.G. opined that a mixed line might be argued to have ended, the Thimbles have for five generations kept their blood carefully, narrowly, at home. Company ledgers list Absalom and Hepzibah Thimble as husband and wife. Three lines higher on the same page, in an older hand, they are listed as born to the same mother. Guests notice the family's sameness: the same pale-lashed grey eyes, the same small chin, the same strawberry birthmark at the throat, the same habit of laying an extra spoon. Guests do not ask.

### The Convoy

The convoy is eleven wagons of the Renderers' Union under **Teamster Abel Crumm**, carrying forty-four barrels north from the Chute toward the Vigil, where Fatlands salt pork is bought by the ton to feed a city that eats hourly to stay awake. It travels under the seal of **Cullmaster Hedley Brawn**, who rides with it to see the far-field barrels safely sold, because the Board is beginning to worry about inspection at the Vigil border and wishes its paperwork to be beyond reproach. The convoy's manifest lists forty-four barrels of "pork, salted, first quality." The thirteen notched barrels were packed at the Dusk Acres under Brawn's eye. Abel Crumm knows they are notched. He has not let himself know why.

Six days ago, as the wagons climbed out of the warm country, the ground behind them began to whiten in long, branching veins. Each night the marbling crept a little further up the road. One drover was lost on the second night. Crumm entered it as wolves, as the Board's forms require. On the evening before the adventure begins, the convoy reached Thimble Cross, and the marbling came with it, and in the morning it was lying a hand deep over everything, and it had begun to fall from the sky.

### Why Now

Tablenight is the anniversary of the Gorging, the night six peoples ate their gods and one did not. Orrum has been patient for six hundred and forty-one years. This year, the Hostwatch say, it is walking faster. Its steward, Welcome Ardent, usually lays a table a mile ahead of his master and waits for him. At Thimble Cross, for the first time, the Host has not waited for its steward. The table was laid before Ardent arrived, and that frightens Ardent more than anything has frightened him in six centuries.

## Running The Ninth Chair

### Structure

The adventure runs across seven days, from **Day One** (six days before Tablenight) to **Day Seven** (Tablenight itself). A day-by-day timeline gives what happens if the characters do nothing; the characters will, of course, do something, and the timeline bends around them. Scenes are written to be dropped in when the moment is right. Roughly:

| Session | Days | What It Is About |
| One | Days One to Three | Arrival, the table, the cards, the first Reaping, hunger, the suicide |
| Two | Days Four to Six | The graveyard, the kin-slaying, the ritual killing, the madness, Ardent |
| Three | Day Seven | The demand, the sacrifice, the Host, the endings |

For a two-session game, compress Days Four to Six into a single long night of discoveries, and begin the second session on the morning the consensus forms.

### The Shape of Dread

Thimble Cross gets worse in a particular order: first strange, then hungry, then frightened, then cruel, then devout. Pace the Dread checks accordingly. The first session should cost each character perhaps 4 to 6 Fray; by the climax most should be within reach of Breaking, so that the arrival of the Host can tip one or two over the edge. Contagion of madness is a central mechanic here: when anyone Breaks, everyone who sees it makes a Dread 2 check, and the inn's own population is Breaking too.

### The Count

From Day Two, **Merrit Sloe**, the Brinehollow bail-girl, begins the count, as is the custom of her country: each morning she walks the upstairs corridor and calls every name through every wall, and waits for an answer. Use the count to open every day after the first. It tells the players who is gone without needing a scene, and the silences after names are the adventure's heartbeat. Keep a list. Cross names off where the players can see you do it.

[box: Hunger at Thimble Cross]
The inn's stores run short at once. From Day Two, everyone at Thimble Cross is on half bread. From Day Four, there is no bread. A character who does not eat a proper meal on a given day gains 1 Hunger at dawn whether they have Partaken or not (Unfed characters, trained to it, ignore the first two days). From Day Four, a hungry character suffers −1 on all Hand and Lung rolls per full day without food, to a maximum of −3. Hunger gained from starvation counts toward Regrowth like any other. Fatlanders, whose hunger was never in the stomach, gain 2 Hunger a day instead of 1, but suffer no penalty: they are used to it.
[/box]

[box: Eating from the Barrels]
The convoy's forty-four barrels are the only food at Thimble Cross worth the name. Thirty-one are honest salt pork. Thirteen are notched (see the Convoy Manifest handout). Meat from a notched barrel is the best thing any character has ever eaten: sweet, yielding, rich, with a fat that melts at blood heat and coats the tongue for an hour. It removes all starvation penalties. It does not remove Hunger. Instead, a character who eats it must roll Gut + Feast against Hard 14. On a failure they acquire the **Laden Taste** until the end of the adventure: they gain 1 Hunger every time they see food, smell cooking, or watch someone else eat, and they must make a Caul + Resolve roll (Hard 14) to stop eating once they have begun. A character who eats from a notched barrel three times dreams the memories of the person the meat once was, and must make a Dread 3 check on waking. A Fatlander who eats it suffers nothing new at all, which is the worst thing about it.
[/box]

[box: The Marbling]
The marbling looks like snow until you are close to it. Then it looks like the white veining in a cut of beef, branching and rejoining across the ground, with a pinkish translucence between the veins that gives slightly underfoot. It smells of cold fat and, faintly, of bread. It does not melt in the hand. It softens. It falls from the sky in soft white flakes that land on the skin and cling, greasy, and must be scraped away with a knife-back. Where it lies, the ground is Fatlands ground. Between sunset and full dark, anyone standing on marbled ground alone may be Reaped (see the Reaping, below). Each day the marbling covers more: on Day One the road and the yard; on Day Three the stable floor and the graveyard; on Day Five the cellar; on Day Seven the boards of the common room, everywhere except the strip of floor beneath the Host's table, which stays bare and warm.
[/box]

#### The Reaping at Thimble Cross

The Reaping here obeys its Fatlands rules. A character alone on marbled ground between sunset and full dark first notices a stillness (Eye + Search, Routine 10). Then the ground leans in, the smell of new bread rising from it (Dread 3 to witness; Dread 2 for a Fatlander). Then it softens: the character must succeed at Caul + Resolve (Hard 14; Grim 18 if their Hunger is 6 or more) or stop wanting to leave. Companions may drag a character free with Gut + Labor against Grim 18, but each round in the soil deals 1d6 Flesh as the ground begins, gently, to digest. A character Reaped is gone. What is pulled out after full dark is a shape of clothes and the smell of bread. Never use the Reaping as a surprise kill on a player character; describe it slowly, and give their friends the chance to pull.

### Portions and Advancement

At the end of each session, award each character **5 to 10 Portions** of experience, as described under Aftermath. A character who survives the whole adventure should have earned between 15 and 30.

## The Cast

Twenty-odd people are snowed in at Thimble Cross, and every one of them is hungry. The major figures below have full stat blocks; the rest have quick lines. Each entry notes what the person wants, what they are hiding, and what happens to them if the characters do nothing. Remember that the characters' own names are on the place cards, and that every person in this list will, sooner or later, want to know why.

### The Keepers of the House

#### Absalom Thimble, the Innkeeper

A big, soft, slope-shouldered man of sixty-one with the family's pale lashes and small chin, an apron that has not been fully white since the Thin Rising, and a voice like a warm spoon. Absalom has kept Thimble Cross for thirty-eight years and has never in that time turned anyone away, because he cannot. He loves the work. He loves the moment a cold traveller's face changes at the smell of his pottage. The charter does not feel like a curse to him; it feels like who he is. That is why the famine breaks him. A host with no food is a contradiction his body cannot hold, and from Day One his hands tremble when he passes the empty pantry shelves. On the night of Day One he digs up his mother, Old Mother Ivy Thimble, and on Day Two he serves her, and weeps in the kitchen while the guests ask for more.

#### Hepzibah Thimble, the Keeper's Wife

Small, upright, sixty, with her ruined right hand held against her apron like a folded bird. Hepzibah runs the house: the linen, the rooms, the ledger of who sleeps where. She is the family's memory and its law. She is also Absalom's sister, a fact she has never spoken aloud in her life and will defend with a carving knife. She regards the charter the way a priest regards scripture. The family has suffered for it for two hundred years; therefore it must be holy. When Orrum's table appears she polishes it, every morning, with beeswax, and cannot say why.

[quick] Hepzibah Thimble — Threat 3 · Flesh 11 · Guard 11 · Attack +4 (carving knife 2) · Armor 0 · Dread 0 (Hearth-Bound, as Absalom)

#### Linus Thimble, the Son

Thirty-four, heavy-handed, quiet, a good farrier and a bad liar, with his father's build and his mother's eyes. Linus has always known that he and his sister are expected to marry, because there are no other Thimbles left, and because the Clausewright's opinion is framed in the parlour. He has been trying for ten years to find a way to be content with it, and he very nearly has, and that frightens him more than the expectation itself. Linus discovers his father's crime on Day Four, when he finds his grandmother's wedding ring in the bottom of the pottage pot. He kills Absalom that night in the cellar with the cleaver his father taught him to use. Afterward he sits with the body until dawn and holds its hand.

[quick] Linus Thimble — Threat 3 · Flesh 15 · Guard 13 · Attack +6 (farrier's hammer 3, or cleaver 3) · Armor 1 · Dread 0 (Hearth-Bound)

#### Dorcas Thimble, the Daughter

Twenty-nine, sharp-tongued, tall, the only Thimble who reads anything but the ledger. Dorcas has read the charter every night since she was fourteen, looking for an exit clause, and there is none. She wants out: out of the inn, out of the blood, out of the marriage the parlour wall has arranged for her. She is the person most likely to ask the characters, quietly, to help her leave, and the person for whom leaving is least possible. If she crosses the station's boundary stones without the charter being released, her body will break. She knows this. She has worked out precisely how badly. She has decided she might be willing to find out.

[quick] Dorcas Thimble — Threat 3 · Flesh 12 · Guard 13 · Attack +4 (bread knife 2) · Armor 0 · Dread 0 (Hearth-Bound)

[box: Playing the Thimble Curse]
The Thimbles' inherited curse should stay in the corners of the eye. The players learn it through the sameness of their faces; through the ledger that lists Absalom and Hepzibah as born to the same mother; through the framed Clausewright's opinion in the parlour with its phrase "the blood kept narrow, the line kept whole"; through the single wide bed in the family wing and the second one beside it that has never been slept in; through Dorcas looking at her brother across a room and then looking away. Never make it explicit. Never make it a joke. It is a family that has been doing a terrible thing to itself for two hundred years because a dead woman wrote a kind clause badly, and the most frightening thing about it is how ordinary they have made it.
[/box]

#### Warden Josse Haverick, of the Rim Road Company

Forty-four, lean, a long jaw shaved blue each morning in cold water whatever the weather, an Oathener of minor Tongue-line blood who speaks the way all Oatheners do, slowly and in conditionals, with all the exits built in. Haverick holds Thimble Cross for the Company. He swore before the notary Jessamy Quill, in Tacit, to "hold this station for the Company until relieved, keep its peace, and keep its ledger true." Sworn in Oathen, the promise travels with him. He cannot abandon the station; he cannot falsify the ledger; he cannot fail to keep the peace without his body coming apart to match. He is honest because he has no choice and decent because he chose it. He is the closest thing to law at Thimble Cross, and he is trapped there more completely than anyone except the Thimbles.

### The Convoy

#### Cullmaster Hedley Brawn, of the Board of Plenty

A vast, courteous, beautifully dressed man in plum wool and a waistcoat with forty bone buttons, all straining. Brawn is a Gut-line cadet and a career officer of the Board's quietest department, the one that decides which vagrants, debtors and troublemakers are assigned the dusk shift in the far fields. He eats constantly and without hurry, from a lacquered box of candied fat he keeps in his coat, and he speaks of the Reaping as a farmer speaks of rain: something to be planned for, measured, and directed where it will do the most good. He is not cruel. He finds cruelty wasteful. He believes, sincerely, that the Reaping can be steered, that a field which is fed one chosen person at dusk will not take a random one at midnight, and he is, horribly, partly right. From Day Two he proposes a **dusk roster**: each evening, one person will go out on the marbled ground and stand there, and the rest will be safe. He will happily draw up the roster. He will not put himself on it. He has a Board warrant that says he need not.

#### Teamster Abel Crumm, of the Renderers' Union

Fifty-two, square, sunburned in a country with no sun to speak of, with the Fatlands' hunger behind his eyes and a teamster's habit of counting things twice. Abel has driven the Chute road for thirty years, and he has always known some barrels are notched, and he has always decided not to ask. He is kind to his drovers, generous with his pipe, and on the first evening he stands the whole common room a round of pear spirit and says "Lack keep away" with real feeling. On the night of Day Two he opens barrel 31 and finds, in the brine, a forearm with a tattoo he paid for himself: a sheaf of wheat and the name **ROBB**, for his sister's boy, who went to work the dusk shift in Low Tilth last Carving for a debt of thirty lacks. He is found in the cold store on the morning of Day Three.

[quick] Abel Crumm — Threat 3 · Flesh 15 · Guard 12 · Attack +4 (pry-bar 3) · Armor 1 · Dread 0

#### The Drovers

Six Renderers' Union drovers travel with the convoy, all Fatlanders, all perpetually, quietly eating. **Big Oona Harl** is the oldest, a woman of fifty built like a dray-horse who sings field hymns and loves Abel Crumm like a brother. **Nib Furrow** is eighteen, freckled, on his first run, and so hungry he chews the leather of his own reins; he is first on Brawn's roster. **Gus Haslet** and **Tam Pettle** are cousins who argue about everything except food; **Walt Sallow** is silent and missing two fingers; **Lise Trotter**, kin to the drover Sal Trotter, does not name the cattle. The convoy's twenty-two oxen stand in the yard, and on the second night, when no one is watching the stable, some of them stand on two legs.

### The Poacher and His Prize

#### Kale Dragoman, the Poacher

A long, sallow, rangy Fatlander of forty with a black beard trimmed to a point, a fine Vigil-glass spyglass on a chain, and the easy manner of a man who has found a trade that suits him. Kale hunts the Unfed across the border for the Blank trade. He takes them alive, because a living Blank fetches four times a dead one at the surgeries where blank blood and blank organs are sold to the nobility. And he feeds them well on the road, cooks for them himself, tender things, warm things, because his buyers prefer a plump specimen and because, he says, there is no reason to be unkind. His captives find this the hardest thing to forgive. He travels with two hired men and a wheeled iron cage. Kale is witty, morally weightless, and good company, and he will try hard to be liked, because people who like him are easier to sell things to.

## The Places of Thimble Cross

### The Bend and the Approach

[fiction]
The road comes up out of the south between low hedges gone white, and for a mile you think it is snow, and are glad of it, because snow is clean. Then your boots begin to slide in something that is not ice, and your horse will not put its head down to it, and the smell reaches you: cold dripping, a butcher's slab in the morning, and under it, faint and impossible, new bread. Ahead, where the road bends west, stands a grey inn with seventeen chimneys, and only one of them is smoking. Beyond it, to the north, the land goes gold, as if it were late afternoon in early Carving there, though it is noon in the dead of Lack here. The white stops at the edge of that light like a tide.
[/fiction]

The marbling covers the Rim Road for six miles south and three west; travel out is Grim 18 on Lung + Wayfaring by day and impossible at dusk, and the Reaping walks the road. North, the Hungry Lane runs into the Fast, and the ground there is bare and warm wherever the Host has walked. The characters will realise, if they test it, that the safest ground in the region is the god's road.

### The Yard and the Convoy

A cobbled yard behind a seven-foot wall, crammed with eleven wagons drawn into a ring. The oxen stand head-down, breath steaming, and do not move when touched. The barrels are stacked under oiled canvas: forty-four oak casks bound in iron, each stamped with the Chute's mark struck over twice, thirteen with a small knife-notch beneath. Marbling lies thickest here. By Day Three it has grown veins up the wagon wheels.

[fiction]
Under the canvas the barrels sweat. A slow pink brine beads at the staves and runs and freezes into soft white whorls on the cobbles. When the wind drops you can hear them settling, a creak and a long wet shift, the sound of something in a hammock turning over in its sleep.
[/fiction]

Search (Eye + Search, Hard 14) finds the notches. A Fatlander, or anyone with Reckoning 2+, can see that the notched barrels are heavier than pork should be. Laying a palm on a notched barrel at night (Caul + Instinct, Hard 14) reveals that it is warm.

### The Common Room

A long, low, smoke-darkened room with a flagged floor, three tables of scarred pine, a great hearth carved with the founding motto, and a row of west windows looking toward the Fast. From the morning of Day Two it holds a fourth table.

[fiction]
It runs down the middle of the room where no table was, dark wood polished to the depth of still water, so long the lamp at its head and the lamp at its foot cannot see each other. The cloth is white the way bone is white when it has been boiled clean. There are nine places. Nine plates of thin cream porcelain rimmed in gold, each with a folded card. Nine chairs; eight of them pulled out a hand's width, as if for someone about to sit. The ninth, at the foot, is pushed in square to the table, its back to the lamp. Down the centre: a loaf, broken, steam still rising from the break. A dish of butter sweating gold. A tureen with its lid on. The room is warm for the first time in two days. Nobody is standing near the hearth. Everyone is standing near the table.
[/fiction]

The table cannot be moved: it is not heavy, it simply does not go. Axes split it (Refrain's axe always does; others need Hand + Labor, Hard 14) and it is whole again the next dawn, with fresh bread. The food is the Host's; eating any of it makes the eater a guest, and the eater must sit at their own place before dawn (Caul + Resolve, Grim 18, each hour to resist). The bread is never cold. Its smell reaches every room in the inn, and every character who passes through the common room hungry must roll Caul + Resolve (Hard 14) or gain 1 Hunger.

#### The Place Cards

There are nine cards. Eight bear names, in a fine, round, careful hand that each reader recognises as the handwriting of someone who loved them: a mother, a grandmother, a first teacher. Fill the eight with every player character's name (correctly spelled, including any secret or childhood name), plus, in order to reach eight, **Merrit Sloe**, **Linus Thimble**, **Lord Casimir Hollowe-Vane** (under his true name, which exposes him), and **Absalom Thimble**. For three characters, use all four; for four, drop Absalom; for five, drop Absalom and Linus. The ninth card bears no name; see the handout.

### The Kitchen and Pantry

A stone-flagged kitchen with a hearth big enough to roast a pig, copper pans on hooks, and a pantry of sixty empty shelves. On Day Two the great pot hangs over the fire with the pottage in it. A character with Stitching 1+ or a Fatlands upbringing who examines the pottage's meat (Eye + Search, Hard 14) can tell it is not mutton and not pork; on a Grace, they find a small gold ring with an inscription, *A.T. to I.M., kept*.

### The Cellar and Cold Store

Steep steps down to a vaulted cellar of casks and racks, and beyond a heavy door the cold store, where meat hangs on hooks in winter. From Day Five the cellar floor is marbled, and the white climbs the barrel-staves in veins. Abel Crumm is found in the cold store on Day Three. Absalom Thimble dies in the cellar on the night of Day Four.

### The Guest Rooms and the Long Room

Upstairs, nine narrow rooms off a single corridor with thin plank walls, and at the end the Long Room, a dormitory for drovers. Here Merrit walks every morning, knocking, calling names. The west-facing rooms are the warmest, and the Fast's gold light lies across their floors all night, and those who sleep in them dream of being invited.

### The Family Wing

A locked door off the kitchen opens onto the Thimbles' rooms: a parlour with the charter framed under glass beside the Clausewright's opinion of 488; a plain room with two beds, one wide and worn and one narrow and never slept in; Dorcas's room, stacked with books on Oathen law. Searching the parlour (Eye + Search, Routine 10) finds the family register, which tells the attentive reader everything about the Thimble blood without saying anything at all.

### The Stable and Kale's Cage

A long stone stable, its floor marbled from Day Three. At the far end, under a tarpaulin, Kale Dragoman's cage: iron bars, a wooden floor with straw, a slop bucket, and Refrain Dole sitting with her back straight and her eyes open. Kale's men take turns sleeping across the door. Picking the padlock is Hand + Filch, Hard 14; the key is on Kale's belt.

### The Smokehouse

A squat brick tower across the yard, black inside with three centuries of smoke, hung with iron hooks and racks. It is where Sister Clemency performs the Ninefold Purge on Lord Casimir on the night of Day Five.

### The Little Graveyard

Behind a waist-high wall, nine stones for nine generations of Thimbles, the oldest, Hadley's, carved with a single word: *Welcome*. The newest, Old Mother Ivy Thimble's, set in Carving. From Day Three the marbling lies across it. On the morning of Day Four three graves are open: Ivy's dug down from above by a spade, and two older ones, Hadley's and her son's, broken open from beneath.

### The Toll House

Haverick's post: a stone hut at the gate with a bell, a strongbox, a crossbow rack, and the station ledger on a lectern. The ledger records everything the warden sees, and he cannot make it lie.

## Hooks: Why You Are Here

Each character needs a reason to be on the Rim Road in the dead of Lack. Choose or adapt one per character. Every hook should bring them to Thimble Cross on the afternoon of Day One, just as the road closes.

- **Sallowreach.** A Seamer's journeyman or Heir carrying a sealed jar north from the Fatlands for a client in Lastgate. Or: the Mortuary Court has sent you to find a runaway Hand-line cadet, Casimir Hollowe-Vane, before he touches someone important; he was last seen heading east with a widow in grey.
- **The Fatlands.** A Renderers' Union clerk sent by Mistress Brisket Annalow to quietly check one convoy's barrels against its manifest, because Quartermaster Ivo Stockpot has been notching more of them than she likes. Or: a Thin dissident fleeing north toward the Fast with the Board's bailiffs a day behind.
- **Brinehollow.** A Netwatch deserter walking inland, or a salvager carrying a sketch of Cray Lobbett's eight-chaired table to sell in the Vigil. Merrit Sloe recognises a fellow coaster and bails you the first night.
- **The Vigil.** A Waker escort assigned to meet Courier Lisle at Thimble Cross and see her dispatch safely to Lastgate. Or: an apprentice of Mirela Sconce sent to find the source of the Seeming of a tall figure laying a table.
- **Cradlewrack.** A border-runner for Petra Quick fleeing the highlands, or an Assembler sent to buy a Part rumoured to be among a poacher's stock. Doors you pass drift open behind you, and Lord Casimir shuts every one.
- **Oathen.** A Surety travelling to the Company's Vigil office, carrying the risk of someone else's contract in your body, or a junior Clausewright sent by Jessamy Quill's office to audit the Thimble charter, which the Company would like to end.
- **The Fast.** A Tablebreaker hunting the one who took Refrain Dole, your partner. Or a Hostwatch tracker following warm ground south along the Hungry Lane. You know the rules. You will spend the adventure watching other people break them.

That should be the first Dread check of the adventure (Dread 2), and it should be the one they remember.

## Act One: The Laid Place (Days One to Three)

### Day One: The Road Closes

The characters arrive through the falling marbling in the last grey hour of afternoon. Absalom meets them at the door with a lantern and the words his charter requires: "Come in, come in, there's always room." Let them meet the house: Haverick at his ledger, Brawn eating in the best chair, Kale at knucklebones, the widow in grey and her gloved secretary, Clemency refusing supper, Merrit carrying ale, Bell Lisle standing because she does not dare sit. It is the last warm evening.

**Dusk: the first Reaping.** As the light goes, the drover Walt Sallow steps out to check the oxen. Any character near a window or in the yard notices the stillness (Eye + Search, Routine 10). Then Walt stops walking. The ground around his boots has risen into soft white folds, like dough proving.

[fiction]
He does not shout. He looks down at his feet with an expression of mild interest, the way a man looks at a dog that has put its head in his lap. The white is over his ankles, then his shins, and it moves in a slow pulse, and where it touches him the cloth of his trousers darkens as if wet. He says, quite clearly, "Oh, that's warm." He puts his hands in his pockets. The smell of new bread is so strong that across the yard someone's stomach answers it aloud.
[/fiction]

Witnessing is Dread 3 (Dread 2 for Fatlanders). Characters who run out can try to pull him free (Gut + Labor, Grim 18; see the Reaping rules), but Walt does not want to be pulled, and the ground is fast. If they fail, by full dark there is a shape of clothes on the cobbles and two fingers' worth of empty glove. Brawn, at the window, wipes his mouth and enters the time in a small book.

### Day Two: The Table

[fiction]
Handout: The Ninth Card.
The same cream card, but cold, and slightly damp, as if it had been left out overnight. No name. In the centre, a single mark in the oldest form of Table-speech, made with a broad pen in one stroke: the root that means both *to worship* and *to eat*. Beneath it, a second, shorter stroke, drawn by a different hand, or the same hand much later. The second mark can be read, depending on the scholar, as *after*, *last*, *late*, or *still to come*. There is no third line. Where the other cards say *You are expected*, this one has a small, perfect thumbprint, pressed into the card as if into soft fat.
[/fiction]

Reading the ninth card needs Eye + Lore against Grim 18; on a success the reader knows the readings above. Refrain Dole reads it without a roll, if anyone shows her, and goes very quiet.

**The first count.** Merrit walks the corridor calling names. Use it. Note who does not answer.

**The pottage.** At midday Absalom serves a thick, fragrant pottage of barley and onion and tender pale meat, and the house, which has not eaten properly in two days, falls on it. It is wonderful. Characters who eat it, and later learn what it was, face Dread 3 at the revelation.

**The dusk roster.** In the afternoon, Cullmaster Brawn addresses the common room, pleasantly, with a ledger. He explains the Reaping as Board science: the field will take one at dusk regardless; a field that is fed by choice is a quiet field; he proposes to choose. He has already drawn up a list of seven, one per night to Tablenight, and every name on it is a drover, a servant, or a guest of low Cut. The first is Nib Furrow, eighteen. Brawn is persuasive (Tongue + Persuade 8); the starving room is frightened; unless the characters argue him down (an opposed Tongue + Persuade, or Haverick's support, which he will give if asked, because his oath includes the peace), the roster is accepted by a show of hands. Kale abstains with a smile. Refrain, from the stable, laughs until she coughs.

[fiction]
Handout: The Convoy Manifest.

Barrels 1 through 30, 32, 33: Pork, salted, first quality. Tilth Commons.

The manifest is in Abel's wagon. The struck-out name at the bottom, **Hayle, R.**, is Abel's nephew, Robb, who went to the Dusk Acres last Carving; a clerk copied his name from Brawn's culling roster onto the wrong form. A Fatlander, or anyone who asks the drovers, can learn that Robb was Abel's sister's boy.

**Dusk: Nib.** Nib Furrow walks out onto the marbled yard at sundown, shaking, because the room voted and he is a Fatlander and Fatlanders eat what they are served. The characters can stop him, stand with him (two or more people together are not alone, and the Reaping does not take them), or watch. If they stand with him all through the dusk, the field takes no one that night, and Brawn writes in his little book, without irritation, that the roster has been disrupted and the field will be hungrier tomorrow.

**Night: barrel 31.** Late, the drovers beg Abel for meat. A character awake in the yard sees him prise the lid off barrel 31 by lantern-light, stand very still for a long time, and close it again.

### Day Three: The Teamster

In the morning count, Abel Crumm does not answer. Big Oona finds him in the cold store. Do not describe how he died. Describe what she does: how she sits down on the flags beside him and takes off her own coat and puts it over him, though he is past cold; how she says his name, the way she has said it for thirty years, to call him to breakfast; how she will not let anyone else come in for the first hour. Finding him or coming upon Oona with him is a Dread 2 check. Abel left a note pinned to the inside of his coat, addressed to his sister.

[fiction]
Handout: Abel Crumm's Note.
Mags.
I opened one. I wasn't meant to and I did. It was Robb's arm, the one with the wheat on it that I paid for. I'd know it anywhere. I've known it was something for thirty years and I didn't look, and the not looking was the thing I was best at. I've fed it to people. I've eaten it myself on the road and said it was good.
I don't know how to drive the wagons back and I don't know how to drive them on.
Don't let them tell you it was wolves. It was me. I'm sorry I won't be home for the Weighing. Tell Oona the pipe is hers and she's to stop pretending she doesn't smoke it.
Lack keep away from you. It never kept away from us.
Abel
[/fiction]

**Afterward.** Abel's death is the first time the inn's madness spreads person to person, and it should be shown through the living. The drovers sit together in the Long Room and do not eat. That evening, Gus Haslet says he can hear the barrels in the yard "saying grace, like at home, thanking the field by name." By night, Tam Pettle can hear it too. Big Oona stands at the window over the yard with Abel's pipe, unlit, in her mouth, and says, to nobody, "He always went first through a gate so the beasts would follow." Any character who spends time with the drovers must make a Dread 2 check; on a failure they too hear the barrels murmuring, very softly, all night, the names of fields.

**The barrels opened.** By afternoon the inn has had no bread for a day and the pottage pot is scraped dry. Brawn has the barrels broached. He does not say which are which. Unless the characters stop it, the starving inn eats from the notched barrels by evening (see Eating from the Barrels), and it is the best meal anyone has had in their lives. Hepzibah weeps at the stove. Kale cooks for Refrain from an honest barrel, carefully, and she can smell the difference, and eats.

**Dusk: Oona.** Big Oona is not on the roster. At sundown she walks out onto the marbled yard anyway, with Abel's pipe, and stands where Walt stood, and waits. She is not making a choice anyone else can make for her, she says, if the characters reach her; she is only going after him, the way he went first through gates. This is the adventure's echo of the suicide, and it should be played as the pull of grief, not as a test the players pass or fail. Characters who stay beside her, talking, keep the field off her for the night (Tongue + Persuade, Hard 14, to bring her back inside; Grace: she gives the pipe to the character, and lives to the end). If no one goes, she is Reaped, singing a field hymn, slowly.

**Night: the Steward.** An hour after full dark, someone sees a lantern on the Hungry Lane, coming south, and the marbling melting away from the road around it in a widening black margin. Welcome Ardent walks into the yard, knocks at the door though it is open, and asks Absalom, very politely, whether the house has been laid. Then he sees the table. Seeing Ardent understood is Dread 3. He answers questions honestly and wearily. He confirms the rules of the Seated. He says he did not lay this table, and that he does not understand why the Host has gone ahead of him. Asked about the ninth chair, he says only, "I set that one myself, at every table. I always have. I've never set the card." He looks at the card for a long time and does not touch it.

## Act Two: Hunger Indoors (Days Four to Six)

### Day Four: Open Graves

**The graveyard.** In the morning, whoever goes out first sees the graveyard wall split and three graves open. Old Mother Ivy's has been dug from above; the spade is still leaning on the wall, and the shroud lies in the trench empty and folded, because Absalom could not bear to leave it in a heap. The two oldest, Hadley's and her son's, have burst from beneath: the marbling has come up through the coffins in soft white veins, and of the bones only the long ones remain, licked smooth and pink, as if something below had sucked them like sweets.

[fiction]
The coffin wood has gone soft and fibrous, like the inside of a loaf. In the bottom of Hadley Thimble's there is a hollow in the shape of a lying woman, lined with a fine white down, warm when you put your hand into it. Something is still breathing through it. The breath smells of the pottage.
[/fiction]

The graves are Dread 2; understanding that Ivy's was dug by a spade, and connecting it to yesterday's meal, is Dread 3. Following the spade's prints (Eye + Search, Hard 14) leads to the kitchen door.

**The great sermon.** At noon, in the yard, Sister Clemency preaches to the starving, and the inn listens. Roll her Sermon. On Day Four she sways most of the drovers and a few of the guests.

[fiction]
Handout: The Sermon of Sister Clemency Voide, Preached in the Yard at Thimble Cross.
Children. Brothers. Sisters with your bellies cramping. I will not lie to you, because you have been lied to enough. You are full. You are the fullest people who have ever lived. Your grandmothers' grandmothers swallowed God and you have been carrying It ever since, warm in your guts like a stone in a hen. That is why you can never eat enough. You are not hungry. You are pregnant with a corpse.
And now the one God that was never eaten has come to supper. Why here? Because It can smell Its brothers in you. Because It is a Host, and a Host goes where the larder is. It has come to fetch Its family home out of your bellies, and It does not care if It must take your bellies with them.
There is one way, and it is the oldest way. Give It back. Open your mouths and give It back. Fast and bleed and purge until the god in you comes up black and stinking and lies on the stones where it belongs, and when the Host comes on Tablenight, let It find nothing in this house but clean, empty people, and pass us by.
And those who are fullest, those whose blood is thickest with the stolen meat, the high and the gloved and the veiled: they must be emptied first, and most, because the Host can smell them from the Fast. It is not murder to open a jar. It is housekeeping.
Lack keep away? No. Lack come in. Lack is the only clean guest we have ever had.
[/fiction]

At the climax of the sermon she walks into the common room, kneels at the head of the Host's table, puts her fingers down her throat, and vomits a long black string of bile across the white cloth. Witnessing this blasphemy is Dread 2. In the morning the cloth is spotless, and Clemency's place in the room has a card. It has her name on it.

**The ring.** In the afternoon, scrubbing out the pottage pot, Linus finds his grandmother's wedding ring. A character who is in the kitchen sees him stand with it in his palm for a full minute. He says nothing. He goes out to the graveyard, and looks, and comes back.

**Dusk: the Gleanings.** At sundown, the people the field has taken come back to the windows. See Combat Encounters. They ask to be let in, in their own voices, and Absalom, Hepzibah, Linus and Dorcas cannot refuse a guest.

**Night: the kin-slaying.** After the Gleanings are driven off, Linus goes down to the cellar where his father is broaching a notched barrel for tomorrow. If no character follows, the house hears only one sound, a wet chop like a joint going through on the block, then a second, and then a long silence. If characters follow, they arrive as Linus brings the cleaver down.

[fiction]
Absalom is kneeling, as if at a hearth, with both arms in the open barrel to the elbow. He does not turn round. He says, "She always wanted to be useful, son. She'd have wanted to feed them." The first blow takes him where the neck meets the shoulder and goes in to the spine, and the sound is the sound he taught his son to listen for, the clean give of a joint. He falls into the barrel. Brine and blood come over the rim together in a pink sheet across the flags. Linus pulls him out by the apron strings, because he cannot leave his father in there with the others, and holds him, and his father's face is shining with the brine and looks, for a moment, enormously relieved.
[/fiction]

Witnessing the killing is Dread 3. Linus does not fight unless someone tries to take the body from him; then use his quick line, at +2, until he is restrained or Ruined.

**The oath on the dead.** At dawn Hepzibah comes down. She washes Absalom's face, closes his eyes, and folds his hands over the wound. Then she makes her children kneel and lay their hands on their father's chest, and she makes them swear, in front of whoever is watching, in the Company's form, with Haverick as unwilling witness: *that the blood of Thimble shall endure; that it shall be kept whole and kept at home; that its two last children shall be joined as their mother and father were joined, before Lack is out.* Linus swears. Dorcas looks at the characters, and then at her father, and swears. Haverick enters it in the ledger, because he must, and is sick in the yard afterward. This is Dread 2 for anyone who understands what was sworn.

### Day Five: The Ninefold Purge

The marbling reaches the cellar, climbing the stairs one step an hour. The inn divides: the swayed with Clemency, the Board's people with Brawn, the Thimbles with their dead. Bell Lisle is blinking every few minutes now.

**Night: the ritual killing.** After midnight, Clemency and four converts take Lord Casimir from his room with a blanket over his hands and carry him to the smokehouse. If the characters have befriended Casimir, or are watching Clemency, they can intervene; Clemency's converts fight with fervour, and Casimir, bound with his hands in a sack, can do nothing until freed. If no one comes, Vellum finds him at dawn.

[fiction]
They have hung him from a meat-hook by the bound wrists, so that his toes just touch the brick. The floor is set with nine pewter basins in a ring, and each one holds something that came out of him, in order, the way the Purgation teaches: brine he was made to drink and bring up; bile; the black of his last meal; blood from the lancet at each elbow; blood from the throat; and then, in the last four, what she took out of him with the long knife, set out neatly, the way a cook sets out the parts of a fowl, the liver dark and glossy as a plum, the coils of him laid in a bowl and still faintly steaming in the cold. His gloves have been cut off. His grey hands have been taken off at the wrist and laid together, palms pressed, in the last basin, as if praying. Smoke hangs in the roof. Clemency is kneeling in the ring with her arms red to the shoulder and her eyes shut, waiting, with great patience, for the god to come up out of the bowl.
[/fiction]

The scene is Dread 3. Clemency does not run. She explains, kindly, that it had to be done, and that it has not worked yet, and that it needs another, a higher one; she looks at Vellum.

**Vellum's oath.** At dawn Lady Vellum Serrat takes the gilt wire from her tongue in front of the smokehouse door, kneels in Casimir's blood, lays her hand on his empty chest, and swears on the dead, aloud, without a single condition, for the first time in her life: *By this dead man, my cousin of the Table, I swear the Host shall be carved, and he shall have the first slice.* Her tongue splits a quarter-inch at the tip with an audible tick, and bleeds, and keeps her promise for her. From now on she will do anything to bring the Host to its seat at Thimble Cross, and she becomes the loudest voice for filling the ninth chair.

### Day Six: The Seeming

Bell Lisle's last vial is gone by morning. Her nudger, if she has one among the characters, is all that keeps her upright, and she is blinking every minute, then every breath, and with every blink the Seeming spreads. Everyone in the common room rolls Caul + Resolve (Hard 14) each hour; those who fail see what Bell sees.

[fiction]
At the table the dead are sitting down to dinner. Walt Sallow with his clothes on and nothing in them. Abel Crumm, tucking his napkin in. Old Mother Ivy, with the barley still in her hair. Casimir with his hands in his lap, palms together. They are passing the bread. They are so happy. They are saying, in the voices of people you have loved, that there is room, that there is always room, that you look thin, that you should come and sit down. Outside the west windows, something very tall is stooping to look in, and its breath fogs every pane at once, and it is warm, and it is counting the chairs.
[/fiction]

The Seeming is Dread 3 for each new witness, and contagious: anyone who Breaks while under it gains the derangement *I am already at the table* (they believe their body is seated, wasting, smiling, and that the self walking about the inn is a thing left behind). By afternoon, half the inn shares it. Hepzibah sets places at the pine tables for the dead.

**Bell sleeps.** In the late afternoon, with her nudger's hand on her wrist, Bell tells them quietly that she would like to stop now. She asks them not to pinch her. She gives her dispatch case to the character she trusts most. She says the room with the eye is warm and has a floor you can lie down on and that she has wanted to lie down for twenty-six years. She closes her eyes. She does not open them. Her chest goes on rising and falling, very slowly, in a rhythm no one in the room can match. This is Dread 2, and for her nudger, Dread 3. The Seeming does not stop when she sleeps. It is the inn's now.

**The reading.** At dusk, Refrain Dole asks to see the ninth card again. She reads the second stroke aloud as *Late*, and tells the room what the heretic Meek Garrity has found: that the Unfed did not refuse at Hallowboard, but arrived on the tenth day, when the table had already been cleared. "We're not the ones who said no," she says. "We're the ones who came after. The late ones. That card has my people's name on it. It's had it for six hundred years."

## Act Three: The Ninth Chair (Day Seven)

### The Demand

On the night of Day Six, or the morning of Tablenight, the inn meets in the common room around the Host's table, because there is nowhere else warm. The consensus builds out of every frightened voice in the house, and the GM should let each speak:

- **Clemency** says the Host will pass a house that offers it a clean guest: a mouth with no god in it. The ninth card is for the empty one.
- **Brawn** says this is only the dusk roster by another name, and sound husbandry: feed the table one by choice and it will not take nine by force.
- **Vellum**, sworn, says that a full table is a table the Host must come to sit at, and that she will pay any price to fill it.
- **Hepzibah** says the house cannot refuse a guest, and a chair laid empty in her house is a guest refused.
- **Ardent**, asked directly whether filling the ninth chair will spare the others, says, with terrible gentleness, "I don't know. Nobody has ever sat there." The room hears what it needs to.

The demand settles on Refrain, because she is Unfed, because she is caged, and because she belongs to nobody in the room. Kale will not give up his property for nothing. What follows is an auction in reverse: the frightened inn passes a hat for the price of a woman. Brawn puts in a platter. Vellum puts in a sworn note against her own flesh. The drovers put in Abel's lacks. Let the characters choose whether to pay, to refuse, to fight, or to offer someone else, themselves included.

[box: If the Players Fight the Demand]
They may. Freeing Refrain from the cage and holding the stable against Kale's men and Clemency's converts is a fine, desperate scene (see Combat Encounters). It changes nothing that matters. If Refrain is free, she listens to the room arguing over who else should go: Merrit, whose card is on the table; Dorcas, who has asked to; a character. And she walks into the common room on her own feet, with her axe over her shoulder, and sits down in the ninth chair. "Not because you asked," she says. "Because I'm the only one here who knows how to sit at that table and still mean no." If the players have kept her caged and treated her as stock, she is dragged to the chair instead, and that is worse.
[/box]

[box: Other Volunteers]
If Refrain is dead, gone, or the players have made another figure the heart of the story, the volunteer is whoever they love most. Merrit Sloe volunteers because her card is already on the table and she reckons she might as well sit in a better chair. Dorcas Thimble volunteers because the ninth chair is the one place on earth her oath cannot follow her. A player character may volunteer; let them, and let it cost exactly what it costs everyone else. Never force a character into the chair.
[/box]

### The Sacrifice

At dusk on Tablenight the volunteer is washed, as the Fast washes its dead, and Hepzibah lays a clean napkin over their arm. Ardent, who cannot refuse to help lay a table, draws the ninth chair out from the table for the first time in six hundred years. It makes a small sound on the flags. Everyone in the room makes a Dread 2 check.

[fiction]
Refrain does not thank it. She sits down in it the way a woman sits down in a dentist's chair, all at once, with her jaw set, and lays her axe across her knees. Nothing happens. The bread steams. The lamps burn. Somebody laughs, high and frightened, and stops. Refrain looks at the plate in front of her, which is empty, and says, "No, thank you," and then again, and then a third time, and her voice cracks on the third, because the smell of the bread is coming up off the empty plate and she has been hungry her whole life. She does not say it a fourth time. Her hand is shaking on the axe. Outside, the marbling across the yard begins, very slowly, to melt, in a long dark path from the north gate to the door.
[/fiction]

### The Host

At midnight the Host comes, as it was always going to.

The door opens without a hand on it. The room goes warm all at once, warm as a kitchen on baking day, and every hungry person in it feels their mouth flood. Something stoops in the doorway, too tall for it, and comes in. Nobody sees its face. Characters who try to look straight at it see only the impression of a broad, kind, bending shape, an apron, enormous patient hands, and the light of the lamps leaning toward it. To be in the presence of the Host is a Dread 4 check; to look for its face is Dread 5. Every character must also roll Caul + Resolve against Grim 18 to keep from walking to their own place and sitting down, and Refrain's Gift may help one of them.

The Host goes first to the ninth chair. It stands over the volunteer for a long moment. Then, gently, the way you would lift a sleeping child from a chair that belonged to a grandparent, it lifts them out. It is not angry. It is something like sorry.

Ardent, in the corner, whispers, "Oh. Oh, no. That isn't anybody's. It's not for sitting in."

The Host pushes the ninth chair back in, square to the table, its back to the lamp. Then it lays a tenth place. The table is a little longer than it was. There is a new plate, a new chair pulled out a hand's width, and a new card in a fine round hand, and the name on it is the volunteer's, and the handwriting is their mother's. The volunteer reads it. Their face changes. They sit down at their own place, and lift the empty fork, and put it in their mouth, and close their eyes.

[fiction]
"Oh," says Refrain Dole, who broke two hundred and six tables, who never once in her life ate until she was full. "Oh, it's bread. It's just bread. It's warm. Why did nobody tell me it was just bread?" She is crying. She is smiling. She takes another bite of nothing. "You should sit down," she says to you, kindly, with her mouth full. "There's enough. I didn't know there would be enough."
[/fiction]

Watching this is Dread 4. It is the moment the players understand the sacrifice bought nothing. The ninth chair is still empty. Their own cards are still on the table. The Host turns, warm and patient, toward the rest of the room, and the invitation stands. Outside, at the edges of the dark path, the marbling is still hungry, and the Reaping will take anyone who flees across it alone before dawn. Nothing has changed except that there is one more guest at the table, and she is happy, and she will be happy for a hundred years.

From here, play out the night. Each hour until dawn, every character inside the inn rolls Caul + Resolve against Grim 18 to stay out of their chair; Seated NPCs call to them by name; the hungry and the Seemed walk past them to sit. The Host does not compel anyone. It only waits, and lays more food, and is kind.

## Combat Encounters

**The Gleanings (Day Four, dusk).** Six figures come out of the marbled yard to the windows and doors: field-grown things shaped like the Reaped, with root-tangled feet, smooth rind for skin, and the faces of Walt Sallow, Robb Hayle, Old Mother Ivy and others, eyes closed. They knock and ask to come in. If refused, they press against the glass until it bows, and come through. They fight to drag the living out onto the marbled ground at dusk. They are destroyed when their face is cut away; what is left smells of bread and sap.

## Endings

None of these endings is happy. Some are survivable.

### Ending One: No, Thank You

The characters refuse. They take Refrain's lesson and turn their backs on the table, and, discovering that the dark, warm path the Host walked in on is the one stretch of ground the Reaping cannot touch, they go out and stand on it, outdoors, with their backs to every door, from midnight until dawn, as the Unfed do on Tablenight. Behind them, through the open door, they hear the Seated praising the food, calling their names, asking why they will not come in. Each hour, a Dread 3 check. At dawn the warmth goes out of the air and the marbling melts into a stinking yellow slush that runs away down the Rim Road, and the inn is silent. Inside, at a table now seventeen places long, sit everyone who could not refuse: the Thimbles first, because they never could. The characters survive. Thimble Cross becomes a little Long Table, and the Company strikes it from the ledger.

### Ending Two: The Steward's Apron

A character speaks to Ardent through the long night and learns that he can be relieved. If a character accepts his position, in so many words, Ardent takes off his apron and folds it and hands it over, and weeps with relief, and sits down at the table himself, at a place that was always there and that nobody noticed was his. At dawn the new steward walks north up the Hungry Lane a mile ahead of something tall and warm, with a box of cutlery under one arm, and does not come back. The character becomes an NPC. The Host, now, has a steward who knows the players' names. Everyone else at the inn who was not Seated survives.

### Ending Three: The Long Table

The characters sit. Perhaps the Seeming has them; perhaps the Laden Taste; perhaps they are simply tired, and cold, and it is just bread. It is the warmest night of their lives. They will be lucid for months. They will describe each course to the next travellers who reach Thimble Cross, and to the ones after that. This ending is a gentle total party loss, and players who choose it with open eyes should be allowed to.

### Ending Four: Ashes and Tablecloth

The characters burn it down: the table, the common room, the inn, the Seated in their chairs, who do not get up and do not stop smiling and go on praising the food as the cloth catches. The Thimble charter breaks with the house, and so do the last Thimbles, their bodies folding and snapping in the yard as the blood fails to endure; Haverick, failing to keep the peace, breaks too, quietly, in the toll house, having written *relieved* in the ledger beforehand. The characters run south across melting marbling at dawn, and the field takes one more of them if it can. They survive. At the next crossroads down the Rim Road, two days later, there is a table, laid, with their names on the cards.

## Aftermath

Survivors leave Thimble Cross changed. Award each surviving character a **Mark of the Laid Place**: from now on, whenever they enter a house where a meal is being served, they know, without looking, how many chairs are at the table, and which one is pushed in. Characters who ate from the notched barrels keep the Laden Taste until they spend a session's Portions to be rid of it, or never. Characters who Broke keep their Derangements; the most fitting here are *I am already at the table*, *The barrels are saying grace*, and *Everyone I love has been replaced by someone politer*.

### Portions

Award **5 to 10 Portions** per session, using these guidelines:

- 2 for surviving the session.
- 1 for each major secret uncovered (the barrels, the pottage, the charter, the Second Table's purpose, the ninth chair).
- 1 to 2 for standing beside someone on the marbled ground at dusk, or keeping Bell Lisle awake an hour longer, or any act of costly decency.
- 1 for refusing the table's food when starving.
- 1 for a moment of play the whole table will remember.
