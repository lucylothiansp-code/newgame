# The Grace Roll

> On the Table, nobody asks if a thing can be done. They ask what it will cost, and who will be made to pay it.
>> — Rim Road saying

The rules of *The Long Grace* are short at their heart. One kind of roll settles nearly everything. Around it are wrapped the systems that make this world itself: what it costs to call on the god in your blood, how the god grows back, how the mind wears through, and how the dead hold you to your word.

## When to Roll

Roll only when three things are true: the outcome is uncertain, failure would be interesting, and something is at stake. A Seamer stitching a cut in a quiet shop does not roll. A Seamer stitching a cut while the patient screams and the Kept in the next room are pounding on the wall to be let in, does.

When nothing is at stake, the Carver simply says what happens. When the outcome is impossible, nobody rolls: you cannot argue a Hush into giving back its silence.

## The Roll

To attempt something uncertain, a Guest rolls **2d10** and adds one **Attribute** and one **Skill**. The Carver says which pair fits; players may suggest another and argue for it. The total is compared to a **Difficulty**.

**Total = 2d10 + Attribute + Skill (+ modifiers) (+ Teeth, if Partaking)**

| Difficulty | Number | Example |
| Routine | 10 | Climb a knotted rope; find an inn on the Rim Road; calm a frightened child. |
| Hard | 14 | Climb a wet pier-piling at night; pick a Seamer's lock; lie to a Waker. |
| Grim | 18 | Stitch a severed hand back on in a moving cart; out-argue a Clausewright. |
| Dire | 22 | Climb a cliff of the Hanging Abbey in a gale; lie to a Tenanted judge. |
| Impossible | 26 | Talk a Sayer out of a sentence; outrun the Black Noon. |

If the total equals or beats the Difficulty, the attempt succeeds. Otherwise it fails. A failed roll does not have to mean nothing happens: it may mean you succeed at a cost, too late, or with a complication, at the Carver's judgment. Failure on the Table should always move the story somewhere worse, never to a dead stop.

### Helpings

For every full 4 points by which a total exceeds the Difficulty, the roll earns one **Helping**. A Helping is an extra: more information, more speed, a better price, a quieter entry, a cleaner cut. In combat, each Helping adds +2 damage. A player can usually spend Helpings as they like, within reason; the Carver may offer a menu.

*Example: Ressa (Eye 2, Search 3) searches a drowned house for the ledger of the Called. Difficulty Hard 14. She rolls 7 and 9: total 21. That beats 14 by 7, one Helping. She finds the ledger, and with her Helping she also notices that the last entry is written in her brother's hand.*

### Grace and Lack

When the two d10 show the same number, the roll is an **omen**.

- If the roll **succeeds**, it is a **Grace**. Something goes better than it had any right to: the strongest effect the situation allows, a Helping on top of whatever was earned, an enemy disarmed, a door that was going to be locked found open, a second piece of information. In combat, a Grace attack also inflicts a **Mangling** (see Combat).
- If the roll **fails**, it is a **Lack**. The worst plausible complication happens: the weapon breaks, the lie is not only seen through but believed to be something worse, the patient's wound opens further, the god notices.
- **Double 1s ("Licked Clean")** always fail, whatever the total, and always with a Lack.
- **Double 10s ("Second Helping")** always succeed, whatever the Difficulty (short of the impossible), and always with a Grace.

[box: Lacks Worth Having]
A Lack should never be the Carver punishing the player. It should be the world being itself. Good Lacks on the Table: your Tooth bites (gain 2 Hunger); somebody you love hears what you just said; the thing you did leaves a mark that a Cutwright will be able to read; the noise wakes something in the cellar; your knife slips, and now there's blood on the bread; the rope you tied comes undone, in Cradlewrack, as everything does; you say "I promise" without meaning to, in Oathen.
[/box]

### Modifiers

Circumstances add or subtract from the total, usually by 2 (sometimes by 4 for something overwhelming). Good tools, time, and help add; darkness, injury, fear, cold and Wants subtract. Do not stack more than three modifiers. If the situation is that lopsided, change the Difficulty instead.

- **Help:** another character who could plausibly assist gives +1 (or +2 with the *Second Chair* Knack). Only one helper counts, unless the task is physical and large (lifting a cart, pulling a Called man out of the surf).
- **Tools:** the right tool is assumed; a fine one gives +1 or +2; no tool at all may make a task impossible or Grim.
- **Time:** taking ten times as long gives +2. Rushing gives −2.
- **Injury, exhaustion, hunger:** −2 per condition, as specified in their rules.

### Opposed Rolls

When two characters act against each other (a lie against a suspicion, a chase, an arm-wrestle, a haggle), both roll. The higher total wins; on a tie, the defender (or whoever is keeping the situation as it is) wins. Helpings in an opposed roll are counted by how much the winner beat the loser by.

### Long Tasks

For efforts that take hours or days (restitching a grandmother, deciphering a seating plan, crossing the Uncovered), the Carver sets a **number of Helpings needed**, typically 3 to 8, and a time per roll. The Guests roll until they reach the total or run out of time, and a Lack along the way costs something real.

### Threat

Minor non-player characters do not need full statistics. They have a **Threat** rating from 0 to 5 that they add to all their rolls instead of Attribute + Skill (attack rolls are listed separately). A Rim Road innkeeper is Threat 1. A Netwatch lieutenant is Threat 3. Lady Corrow Vane is not a Threat-rated character.

## Partaking: The Tooth

Anyone with a Cut of 1 or higher carries a god. Before any roll, a Guest may **Partake**: call on the god in their blood to lend them its strength, its senses, its terrible certainty. When you Partake:

- Roll **one Tooth** (a d6, in a different color from your d10s) and add it to the total. A character of Cut 3 or higher may roll **two Teeth** if they wish. Teeth are rolled with the 2d10. They do not count for doubles.
- Gain **1 Hunger** for Partaking.
- Gain **1 additional Hunger** for every Tooth that shows a 1. The god bit back.
- Describe how the god shows itself. Each land's chapter describes its **Tooth**: a Sallowreach Guest's hands go cold and their fingers seem one joint too long; a Fatlander's mouth floods with saliva; a Brinehollower's ears pop and their voice drops an octave; a Vigil-born's eyes stop blinking; a Cradlewracker feels something turn over inside them; an Oathener hears their own heartbeat repeating their last words.

Partaking is powerful. A Tooth adds 3.5 on average, the difference between a Hard roll and a Grim one. It is also how the end comes. Every Guest who Partakes is choosing, roll by roll, to bring their god closer to the surface.

The Unfed and Blanks cannot Partake. The Unfed have Want instead.

## Hunger

**Hunger** is a track from 0 to 10. It represents how awake and active the god in your blood is right now: the rise of appetite, compulsion, and wrongness in the body. Hunger rises when you:

- Partake (+1, and +1 per Tooth showing 1).
- Suffer a Lack while Partaking (+2).
- Eat godtainted food or handle relics with Taint (see the Rim Market).
- Spend time in a place where your god is strong, such as a Hush, the Trench or the Dilation (+1 per hour or more).
- Give in to a Want (+1).
- Refuse your Sop for too long (see below; +1 per day after the third).

When Hunger reaches **10**, the god takes a step forward. **Regrowth rises by 1** and Hunger empties to 0. Describe the change. Something in the body is different now, and it does not change back.

### The Sop

Each land's god can be quieted, for a while, by giving it a little of what it wants. This is the **Sop**: closing something in Sallowreach, eating in the Fatlands, bailing or submerging in Brinehollow, the dangerous blink in the Vigil, opening a door in Cradlewrack, keeping or making a vow in Oathen. Each land's chapter defines its Sop precisely.

Performing the Sop takes a few minutes to an hour. It removes **1d6 Hunger**. But the Sop is the god's own habit. Each time a Guest Sops in a single day after the first, they must make a **Caul + Resolve** roll (Routine 10, Hard 14 for the third time, and so on), or the next Sop becomes a Want they cannot easily stop.

A Guest who goes **three days without Sopping** begins to gain 1 Hunger per day as the god grows restless.

## Regrowth

**Regrowth** is a track from 0 to 12. It measures how far the god has come back in you. It rarely falls. The four stages, and the **Brink** before the last, are common to every land, though each land's chapter describes what they look and feel like there:

| Regrowth | Stage | What Happens |
| 0 | — | The god is asleep. Blanks and the Unfed are here forever. |
| 1–3 | The Taste | Small wrongnesses. Dreams, cravings, a tell in the body. Gain the land's Taste Gift. |
| 4–6 | The Appetite | Compulsions. The body wants what the god wants. Gain the Appetite Gift and the first Want. |
| 7–9 | The Course | Flesh changes. You are visibly becoming something else. Gain the Course Gift and the second Want. Dread 1 to those who see you unclothed. |
| 10–11 | The Brink | The god is nearly here. Gain the Brink Gift. All Wants are Hard (14) to resist. Dread 2 to those who see your changes. |
| 12 | The Seating | The god is, in some real portion, present again, wearing you or making a place of you. The character is lost. |

**Gifts** are the god's power leaking through. They are real, useful and terrible, and they are listed in each land's chapter. A Guest has every Gift from every stage they have reached.

**Wants** are compulsions. Each Want has a **trigger** (an open door, a closed eye, food left on a plate). When a Want is triggered, the Guest must either act on it or roll **Caul + Resolve** against Routine (10) in the Appetite, Hard (14) in the Course and Brink. Acting on a Want removes nothing and adds 1 Hunger. Resisting it successfully costs nothing; failing means acting on it anyway, or suffering 2 Fray if the player and Carver agree the Guest stood firm by sheer horror.

### Can Regrowth Be Reversed?

Almost never. A Purger's Grace can roll it back by one, once per stage. The relics of some lands can delay it. Orrum's tables can stop it entirely, in the way that death stops a fever. There are rumours of a dozen cures on the Rim Road, and every land is certain that one of them works. None of them do. The Carver may allow a single, terrible, story-driven exception in a campaign. It should cost more than the character wants to pay.

### The Seating of a Guest

At Regrowth 12, a Guest is **Seated**. Their god is, for a moment and in some portion, back in the world. What happens depends on the land: a Sallowreach Guest becomes a Hush; a Fatlander becomes a warm hill with, perhaps, a mouth; a Brinehollower's Tenant takes the whole house; a Vigil-born lies down and becomes a sleeper who moves; a Cradlewracker bears what they were always going to bear, or becomes the door; an Oathener becomes a Sayer, whose every word is so.

The player and the Carver play out the Seating together as a final scene. It should be awful and it should be beautiful. Afterward the character is an NPC, a place, or a power, and the player makes a new Guest. A Seated Guest's former companions should meet them again.

## Fray and Dread

The Table breaks minds as surely as bodies. **Fray** is a track from 0 to 10 measuring how far a Guest's composure has worn through.

### Dread Checks

When a Guest encounters horror, the Carver calls for a **Dread check**: roll **Caul + Resolve** against a Difficulty set by the horror's **Dread rating**:

| Dread | Difficulty | Examples |
| 1 | Routine (10) | A rotting body. A Kept man restitched at the dinner table. Toothed wheat. Being Called by a stranger's voice. |
| 2 | Hard (14) | Watching a murder. A Kept woman coming apart in your hands. The Reaping taking someone you have just met. A Tenanted stranger looking at you. |
| 3 | Grim (18) | A loved one Called, Tenanted, Reaped or Breaking. A ritual killing. Eating a person, knowingly. A face you know on a place card. |
| 4 | Dire (22) | A person becoming a place. A Hush swallowing a village. The god in your blood speaking. Kin-slaying by your own hand. |
| 5 | Impossible (26) | A Seating. A god's face. The Host turning to look at you. Knowing that what you did changed nothing. |

- **Success:** gain 1 Fray (or none, on a Grace). You hold.
- **Failure:** gain Fray equal to the Dread rating. On a Lack, add 2 more, and the Guest freezes, flees, screams, vomits or laughs, at the Carver's choice, for a moment that matters.

A Guest only checks once against the same source in a scene, and the Carver may rule that a horror seen too often stops counting (a Seamer does not check for corpses). Guests may Partake on Dread checks. The god does not mind if you are frightened, and sometimes it is the god that makes you brave.

### Fray Effects

- **Fray 0–4:** steady.
- **Fray 5–7:** **Rattled.** −1 to all Eye and Caul rolls. Sleep is thin, appetite strange, and you jump at doors.
- **Fray 8–9:** **Fraying.** −2 to all rolls except to flee, hide or fight. You see things at the edge of the lamp. You are short with the people you love.
- **Fray 10:** **Break.**

### Breaking

When Fray reaches 10, the Guest **Breaks**. For the rest of the scene they are not in control of what they do: the player describes the Break with the Carver (running into the night, attacking the nearest person, curling under a table, laughing until they choke, saying every secret they have). Afterward, the Guest gains a **Derangement** and Fray drops to 3.

Roll on the table below or choose one that fits what broke them. Derangements are permanent unless treated. Each has a **trigger** and an **effect**; when triggered, roll **Caul + Resolve** (Hard 14) to master it for the scene.

| d20 | Derangement | Effect when triggered |
| 1 | **The Dead Man** | You believe you are already dead: your flesh is rotting, your heart has stopped, you smell it. You feel no fear of harm and take no care of your body (−2 to Endure, +2 to Dread checks against death). Triggered by any reminder of your own mortality. |
| 2 | **The Impostors** | Someone you love has been replaced by something wearing their face, their voice, their memories. You cannot be convinced otherwise. Choose the person. You cannot cooperate with them, and may act against them. Triggered by their kindness. |
| 3 | **Not My Hand** | A part of your body is not yours: it belongs to someone else, or to the god. You will not use it willingly (−2 to rolls that need it) and sometimes it moves on its own. Triggered by exhaustion or Hunger 6+. |
| 4 | **Lost Time** | You lose minutes or hours. You come back somewhere else, with dirt under your nails and blood on your sleeve, and no memory. The Carver may narrate what you did. Triggered by Fray 5+ or by the Sop. |
| 5 | **The Watchers** | Everyone is watching you, reporting on you, planning against you. −2 to Persuade; +2 to Search for real threats. Triggered by being alone with strangers. |
| 6 | **The Count** | You must count things: steps, teeth, the dead, the living. You cannot stop until you finish, and the number is always one short. Triggered by stress. |
| 7 | **The Voice at Table** | You hear a voice that speaks in the tone of your god. It is reasonable. It gives advice. Some of it is good. Triggered whenever you Partake. |
| 8 | **The Hollow Plate** | Food tastes of ash, of rot, of people. You cannot eat without a Hard (14) Gut + Feast roll; you begin to starve in full larders. Triggered at every meal. |
| 9 | **The Echo** | You repeat the last words spoken to you, under your breath, again and again. In Oathen this is lethal. Triggered by shouting or by oaths. |
| 10 | **The Seeing** | Hallucinations: the Seeming without a city to share it. You see figures laying tables, the dead walking, your own body changing. −2 to Search. Triggered by darkness or exhaustion. |
| 11 | **Shut Doors** | Open things are unbearable: doors, eyes, mouths, wounds, books. You must close them. Triggered by any open door or eye. (In Cradlewrack this is a death sentence.) |
| 12 | **Kept Grief** | You cannot let go of a dead person. You speak to them, set their place, keep their belongings, and, in Sallowreach, may keep them. Triggered by any reminder. |
| 13 | **The Devotion** | You attach to a person, cause or god with absolute loyalty and will do anything they say. Choose the object. Triggered by their presence or their absence. |
| 14 | **Blood-Hunger** | You crave the taste of blood, of meat, of a particular kind of meat. Triggered by the smell of cooking or of wounds. |
| 15 | **The Splitting** | You stand outside yourself and watch your body act. Calm, distant, unreal. +2 to Dread checks; −2 to Persuade and to anything requiring care. Triggered by violence. |
| 16 | **The Unclean** | You are contaminated: by godsign, by others' touch, by rot. You wash compulsively until you bleed. Triggered by touching another person or a body. |
| 17 | **The Gaslight** | You no longer trust your own memory. Others can convince you of anything you saw or did not see (−4 to resist being deceived about the past). Triggered by being contradicted. |
| 18 | **The Rage** | Small slights become murderous. Triggered by being mocked, cheated, or refused food. Roll or attack. |
| 19 | **The Guilt That Bends the Room** | Your guilt is so heavy it warps what you see: the person you failed is everywhere, in faces and crowds and reflections. When triggered you gain 1 Fray. Triggered by success. |
| 20 | **The Pull** | You feel the god's invitation in its fullest form: the sea, the Hush, the bed, the chair. You want to go. When triggered you must roll or move toward it. See *Despair*, below. Triggered by Fray 8+ or by being near the place. |

### Contagion

Madness on the Table is catching. It always has been: the Seeming of the Vigil is only the most organized form of it. When a Guest or NPC **Breaks** in the presence of others, every witness makes a **Dread 2** check. Anyone who fails and is at Fray 5 or more also takes the same Derangement, in a shared form: they see what the Broken one saw, believe what they believe, count the same count. The Carver should make this feel like a cold draft moving through a room.

As the gods regrow, contagion grows stronger. In a place where a Seating has occurred, or near a Brink-stage noble, witnesses check at Dread 3.

### Despair

Of all the Derangements, **The Pull** is the most dangerous, and the Table is full of people who have answered it: the Finishers singing their way into the Hushes, the Called walking into the surf, the sleepless of the Vigil lying down on purpose, the starving of the Fatlands crossing into the Fast to find a table where they can finally feel full.

When a Guest with the Pull is triggered and fails to master it, they move toward the god's place and invitation. They can be stopped by their companions (a roll, a grip, a rope, a word, a reason). If they reach it and nothing stops them, the player and the Carver decide together what happens, out loud, with the table's consent. This book never describes such an ending as a method or a release. It describes it as what it is on the Table: a loss that lands on everyone who loved them, and a little more of the god in the world. A Guest's companions who witness it gain Fray as a Dread 4 horror, and the Carver should give the grief room.

### Recovering from Fray

- **A night of real rest**, safe, warm and fed (and awake, in the Vigil): remove 1 Fray.
- **A rite** led by a Celebrant or performed sincerely: remove 2 Fray.
- **A vice**: drink, rouse, poppy, brine tonic, gambling, sex (off-page), violence. Remove 1d6 Fray, but roll Gut + Endure (Hard 14) or develop a habit. Habits work like Wants.
- **Time with Who Waits at Home**: remove all Fray, once per story, if they are still themselves.
- **Portions**: 2 Portions removes 3 Fray between sessions.

**Derangements** can be treated with months of care, a Celebrant or Seamer who understands the mind, and 10 Portions. Some never go. Some of them, on the Table, are not wrong.

## Oaths on the Dead

Every Guest begins with an Oath on the Dead, and any character may swear another. To swear on the dead, you speak the full name of a dead person, ideally where they lie, and promise something in their name.

- **The Strength of the Dead:** once per scene, when you act directly to keep the oath, add +2 to a roll.
- **Keeping:** when you fulfil an oath on the dead, remove all Fray and gain 2 Portions.
- **Breaking:** if you deliberately break the oath, the dead come to collect. Gain 3 Fray immediately and suffer a **Visitation**, chosen by the Carver and shaped by the land where you swore: in Sallowreach the dead person, Kept, finds you; in the Fatlands you taste them in every meal; in Brinehollow their voice calls you from the water; in the Vigil they wait for you in every blink; in Cradlewrack you bear something with their face; in Oathen your body breaks to match the promise; in the Fast a place card bearing their name appears at every table you pass.

Swearing on the dead is not a light thing anywhere, and the Table is full of people who swore too easily. Grave-oaths sworn on the desecrated dead, over a body that has been opened or eaten or robbed, are said to bind twice as hard.

## Health, Hunger, and the Body

### Ordinary Hunger and Starvation

Every person needs about one day's bread a day: one lack's worth. Each day a character goes without food, they gain one level of **Starving**: −1 to all rolls per level, cumulative. At Starving 3, they cannot recover Flesh. At Starving 5, they lose 1 Flesh per day. A day of decent food removes one level. In the Fatlands, ordinary food never satisfies (see that chapter); in the Fast, nothing grows, and the Starving rules are the weather.

### Cold, Exhaustion and Sleeplessness

Each night without shelter in Lack, or without sleep, imposes **−1 to all rolls** (cumulative, up to −4) until the character rests. The Vigil has its own rules for sleeplessness, which are worse.

### Disease and Rot

Wounds left unstitched for a day may fester: roll Gut + Endure (Hard 14) or lose 1 Flesh per day and suffer −2 to all rolls until treated. In Sallowreach, festering never kills, and never stops.

### Healing

- **First aid** (Hand + Stitching, Routine 10, a few minutes, once per wound) restores 2 Flesh, +1 per Helping.
- **Rest**: a full day of rest restores Gut in Flesh; a full day under a Seamer's care restores double.
- **Injuries** from the Mangling table heal according to their own entries, and some never heal.

## Travel

The Rim Road is about 2,200 miles around. On foot a traveler makes about 20 miles a day on the road; by cart 25; by fast horse 40; by Company post-coach, changing horses at every waystation, 70. Waystations stand roughly every 15 to 25 miles, and a day's walk off the road in any land is somewhere stranger.

Each day of travel through dangerous country, the Carver may roll on the land's encounter table (see the bestiary), and one Guest may roll **Lung + Wayfaring** (Hard 14) to find a better route, avoid trouble, or make up time. Crossing into the Fast is a different matter entirely, and the rules for that are in its chapter.

## Light and Darkness

The Table is lit by tallow, oil, rushlight and the candles of the dead. Darkness imposes −2 to Search and to attacks; total darkness, −4. In the Vigil there is no darkness, by law, and that is a horror of its own.
