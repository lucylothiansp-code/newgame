# Things That Eat and Are Eaten

> Everything on the Table is either sitting at it or lying on it. Learn which you are before the knives come out.
>> — Tablebreaker saying, Wanting

REPORT OF THE THIRD NIGHT WATCH, NETWATCH, LOWMARK PIER-SECTION FOURTEEN. Entered by Lt. Sable Reef. 9th of Carving, 641 A.G. For the Admiralty Clerk of Losses. Not for the Bench.
Weather: salt rain from the second bell, fog off the Uncovered from the third. Pressure rising. Two of my hookmen bled from the ears before midnight and were sent to bail.
At the fourth bell the count came up short in Ropewalk Row. Widow Carse called her neighbour's name through the wall three times and got no answer, and then, she says, got an answer from the wrong side, from the floor. We broke the door. Bed empty, anchor-rope sawn through from underneath. Not cut. Sawn, as if by something with a very small and patient mouth. The cellar was full to the fourth stair with water that had no business being there at that hour and smelled of a harbour I have not smelled since I was a girl. Weed on the stairs, still moving. I wrote the time.
We took the shore road at the run. Found the Called at the end of the old breakwater, which ends now in nine miles of dry seabed, and which she was walking down as if the water were still there to receive her. Woman of forty, nightdress, barefoot, feet cut to ribbons on the shell and not caring. Hookman Dabb put the boathook through the back of her collar as taught and she did not fall. She kept walking and dragged him. A woman of nine stone dragged a man of fourteen stone forty yards on his knees across the shingle. When I got the second rope on her she turned her head and looked at me, and I am obliged to record that her face was perfectly calm and she was weeping out of her nose. Brine. A good deal of it.
We netted her and sat on her. She bailed in the net, unasked, a bucket and a half, and some of what came up was alive and went scuttling under the planking. Dabb stamped on two. He says the third had a face. Dabb has been on the Watch four years and I do not think he is a man for faces, so I have written it down.
At the fifth bell, while we were roping her for the cart, the Uncovered sang. I have heard it before. Everyone on the Watch has, and none of us mention it at muster. It comes up out of the dark where the sea used to be, low, a sound like a hull grinding on a bar, and it has a tune if you let it. Hookman Persey let it. He put his hook down very carefully, the way you put down something you mean to come back for, and he started walking west. We got the rope on him at the tideline and he fought us harder than the woman had. He was crying and saying he was sorry, he was so sorry, they had been waiting such a long time.
On the walk back we passed something standing upright in the mud about a furlong out. In the lamp it looked like a wreck's keel with the ribs still on. It was not a keel. It had more ribs than a keel and they were opening and closing. We did not stop. I note for the Clerk that it was nearer the pier at the sixth bell than at the fifth, and that the Watch's standing orders do not cover it, and request that they be made to.
Called recovered: two, one of them my own man. Called lost: none tonight. Injuries: Dabb, bitten on the ankle by something under the planks, wound weeping salt, he swears it is nothing. Persey sleeps roped in the guardhouse. When I looked in on him at dawn the bucket beside him was full to the brim and he had not coughed once.
I would like it entered that I am not asking what the Called are walking toward. I am asking how much longer we are meant to pretend we are the ones doing the pulling.
S. Reef, Lt.

The Table is not short of monsters. It is short of anything else. Every land ate a god, and every land is now host to the slow return of what it swallowed, and that return does not confine itself to the bloodlines of the nobility. It gets into the soil, the weather, the drowned, the sleeping, the unborn and the overheard. It makes things. Some of those things were people a week ago. Some were never people at all, and some are people still, and are the worst of the lot.

This chapter is the GM's larder. It gives rules for running adversaries in the terms of the Grace Roll, then walks the seven lands one by one, presenting the horrors native to each with full statistics and the long, unpleasant detail that makes them worth meeting. After the lands come the human adversaries, who outnumber the monsters and do more harm. Then come the Seated Gods, the endings of campaigns, and a frank discussion of why nobody at the table should expect to win against one. The chapter closes with random encounter tables for every land and for the Rim Road between them.

## Running Adversaries

### Threat and the Quick Line

Most adversaries do not need a full stat block. A Netwatch hookman, a field of toothed wheat or a mob of the Forsworn can be run from a single **quick line**, which gives everything needed at the table: **Threat**, **Flesh**, **Guard**, **Attack**, **Armor** and **Dread**. Threat is a flat bonus the adversary adds to every 2d10 roll that is not an attack: a Search to find a hidden player character, a Stealth to creep up on the camp, a Resolve against a Hush-walker's silence. Threat 1 is a frightened farmhand. Threat 3 is a trained professional. Threat 5 is the best in the land at their work. Threat 7 and above belongs to things that are no longer working at anything human.

Attack is the full bonus added to 2d10 against the target's Guard. Damage follows the core rules: the listed weapon damage, plus 2 for each Helping (every full 4 points the roll beats Guard), minus the target's Armor. Doubles on an adversary's roll produce a Grace or a Lack exactly as for player characters, and the GM should describe both with relish. A Netwatch hook that rolls a Grace has hooked a player character's collar and is dragging them. A Seated guest that rolls a Lack has knocked over the salt, and the whole table turns to look at who did it.

### Full Stat Blocks

Important adversaries, monsters with Gifts and anything with a name receive a full stat block. These use the same six attributes as player characters (Hand, Gut, Lung, Eye, Tongue, Caul) and the same skills. Humans rarely exceed 5 in an attribute. Monsters, the Laden, the Tenanted and the deep Regrowth may run to 6, 8 or 10, and a Seated fragment of a god ignores the scale entirely. Flesh, Guard and Attack are calculated by the core formulas where the creature is anything like a body; where it is not (a field, a tide, an echo), the block says so and gives what is needed instead.

### Dread Ratings

Every adversary carries a **Dread** rating from 0 to 5. The first time in a scene that a character perceives the thing clearly, they make a Dread check: Caul + Resolve against the difficulty the rating sets. Failure adds Fray equal to the rating; success adds 1; a Grace adds nothing. Many entries specify what, exactly, triggers the check, since a Tenanted judge in a courtroom is Dread 0 until her lower voice finishes her sentence before her upper one does.

| Dread | Difficulty | Typical horror |
| 0 | none | An armed man, a thin dog, an honest threat |
| 1 | Routine 10 | A Kept beggar, a toothed ear of wheat, a corpse |
| 2 | Hard 14 | An Unstitched Kept, a Forsworn's opened ribs, a blink-murder |
| 3 | Grim 18 | The Sump-thing rising, a Tenanted loved one, a table set for you |
| 4 | Dire 22 | A Laden hill speaking, a crowning opening, a god's attention |
| 5 | Impossible 26 | A Seating, the Host's face, any Seated god |

Some horrors carry a **rising Dread**, written as "Dread 2/4". The first number applies on sight; the second applies when the thing does what it does (a Hush-walker's silence reaching the character, a Seeming turning to look at the one who imagined it). Others carry **Contagion**: anyone who sees a character Break in the creature's presence must make the Dread check again, and on failure takes on the same Derangement the Breaking character did. The Table's madness travels best in crowds.

### Godsign-Powered Creatures

Anything with the god in it can **Partake**. Creatures with a Cut are listed with it, and with a Hunger track exactly like a player character's. Before any roll, the GM may Partake for the creature, adding one Tooth (a d6) or, for Cut 3+, two Teeth. Each Partake adds 1 Hunger, and every Tooth that shows a 1 adds 1 more. When a creature's Hunger reaches 10, it empties and the creature's **Regrowth rises by one**. For a monster this is not bookkeeping. It is escalation: the Sump-thing pulls three more drowned bodies into itself and gains Flesh, the Laden hill's mouth opens wider, the Waker gone mad stops blinking. Each entry describes what a Regrowth rise does to that creature. Players learn quickly that a monster which gambles on its Teeth is a monster that gets worse, and that sometimes the best tactic is to make it Partake.

Godsign creatures also have **Sops**. A creature that performs its Sop removes 1d6 Hunger, exactly as a player character does, and the Sop is always something the players would rather it did not do: close a child's eyes, eat the farmhand, take one more of the Called. Interrupting a Sop in progress is often the only way to break a creature's momentum.

### Monster Special Abilities

The following abilities recur through the chapter. They are written in the core rules' terms and can be lifted onto any creature the GM builds.

- **Unending.** The creature does not die from Ruin. At 0 Flesh it is Undone: it stops acting for 1d6 rounds, then recovers 1d6 Flesh and resumes, unless it has been dismembered, sunk, Closed, or moved out of Sallowreach. Every Kept thing has this ability within Sallowreach's borders.
- **Swarm.** The creature is many. Blades and Brawl attacks deal only 1 damage regardless of Helpings; fire, smoke, flood and area effects deal double. A swarm reduced below half Flesh disperses and re-forms elsewhere next scene.
- **Hooking / Grapple (X).** On a hit with one or more Helpings, the target is held. Escaping is a Gut + Athletics or Lung + Athletics roll against difficulty X. A held target cannot move and suffers −2 to all rolls.
- **Engulf.** Against a held target, the creature's next hit pulls the victim inside it. Engulfed characters take the creature's damage automatically each round, ignoring Armor, and make a Dread check each round they remain.
- **Fray Aura (N).** At the start of each round within the creature's reach, every character must pass a Caul + Resolve roll against difficulty N or take 1 Fray. This is in addition to the first sight Dread check.
- **Pull (N).** The creature calls. Characters with the relevant Cut must roll Caul + Resolve against difficulty N each round or spend their action moving toward it. Resisting is not free: every success adds 1 Hunger, because the god in the blood wants to go.
- **Rooted.** The creature cannot move, but cannot be pushed, knocked down or held. It usually has enormous Flesh and a reach measured in fields.
- **Tide (N).** The creature's influence rises and falls. On the GM's turn, roll a d10: on N or less it acts twice this round. Things in Brinehollow and the Vigil have this.
- **Binding.** The creature's words count. When it speaks a sentence about a character and that character fails a Tongue + Clause roll (difficulty set in the entry), the sentence becomes physically true. Found only in Oathen and in very bad places elsewhere.
- **Hosting.** The creature offers. Any character who accepts anything it offers (food, a seat, shelter, "Come in") must roll Caul + Resolve against the listed difficulty or stay. Refusing three times grants +2 to the roll; thanking it imposes −4.
- **Armor (type).** Some armor is not leather. Waxed flesh, bone lattice, compressed water and layered fat all count. Where an armor ignores a damage type (fat ignores blunt, water ignores blades), the entry says.
- **Helping Effects.** Many creatures list what their Helpings do in place of or alongside extra damage. A Flyfront that scores two Helpings does not deal +4 damage. It gets into the mouth.
- **Grace and Lack Effects.** Some entries list special results when the creature's own roll comes up doubles, or when a character's roll against it does. A character who rolls a Lack against a Hush-walker hears, for one awful second, nothing at all.

### Putting Things Down

Killing is not the same in every land. Outside Sallowreach, a creature at 0 Flesh suffers Ruin and further damage kills it, as for any character. Inside Sallowreach nothing dies, and the only ways to finish anything are a Hand-line's Closing touch, a Hush, or carrying the thing across the border, where it drops at once. Many creatures in this chapter have a **Weakness** or an **End** entry, which describes the one way the people of that land have found to stop them. Some of those Ends are worse than the monster. Some are not ends at all, only delays, and the entry will say so.

[box: The GM's Larder: Pacing Horror]
Every creature in this chapter is built to be survivable at first sight. The Dread check, not the attack roll, is where most of them do their damage. Use that. Let the players see the Sump bubble in patterns for a full session before anything comes up.
The best monster fights on the Table end without a death and with a cost: a player character's Hunger maxed out to win a single roll, a companion who Broke and is now certain their sister has been replaced, a field that now knows their name. Track those costs and bring them back.
When a creature's Hunger fills and its Regrowth rises mid-fight, describe the change before you describe its next action. The moment of transformation is the scene.
Remember that every creature here began as a meal, a mourner, a farmer or a child. Somebody on the Table loved it. Somebody is still bringing it dinner. Put that person in the scene.
[/box]

## Sallowreach: What Will Not Finish

Every monster in the north began as a person or a thing that tried to end and was refused. They are not malicious, mostly. They are in pain, and they have been in pain for a very long time, and pain that long becomes a kind of weather. Remember that within Sallowreach all of these have **Unending** unless noted.

### The Sump-Thing

Weighted sacks go into the Sump by the hundred each year: the Kept too far gone for stitching, the unclaimed, the inconvenient. The sacks rot. The weights do not. Over decades, in the black peat-water forty feet down, the bodies find each other. Rotting flesh that cannot finish rotting goes soft as dough, and soft things press together under the fen's weight until there is no telling where one grandfather ends and the next begins. A Sump-thing is what happens when a few hundred of them agree to come up.

It surfaces first as bubbles in patterns: rings, then spirals, then the shape of a hand spread flat under the water. Then the smell, which is not the sweet Sallowreach rot but something older, sulphurous and vinegary, a pickle-jar opened after a century. Then the mound breaks the surface, a slick dark heap twenty feet across, hair and sackcloth and peat-tanned leather stretched over a lattice of knitted bone, and out of it, everywhere, faces. Pressed flat. Mouths working. Some still have the twine of their labels around their necks. The sound it makes is a choir of drowned people trying to say their names through a lungful of fen, which comes out as a long wet gargle with words in it.

It wants to be *finished*, and it has learned that it cannot be, so it has settled for company. It drags the living down. Victims are not eaten. They are added. A week later a new face surfaces in the mound, and it is still screaming, and it will scream for centuries. Families who lose someone to a Sump-thing sometimes row out to listen for them, and sometimes hear.

Tactics: it rises under boats and walkways, grapples with dozens of arms, and sinks. It is slow on land but can drag itself up a bank like a slug of wet meat. It does not flee. It has nowhere else to be.

### Hush-Walkers

Edda Thrush walked out of Dunmere Hush. She was the first. She is not the last. A Hush-walker is a person who entered a Hush and did not finish, or a Kept body from inside a Hush that was somehow refused even there and stood up. They come out of the silence carrying it with them. A Hush-walker has no smell at all, which in Sallowreach is the most alarming thing about it: one notices a gap in the stink of the world, a person-shaped absence of rot, before one notices the person. Their skin is clean and pale and perfectly still, unrotted, every fly that lands on them dropping dead and unmoving. They walk slowly. Their feet make no noise on boards. Their mouths move.

Within ten paces of one, sound stops. Not quiet: stopped. A scream comes out as breath. A drawn blade makes no ring. A heartbeat cannot be heard in one's own ears, and this is what breaks people: the body insists the heart has stopped. In that silence something is listening, the way Edda Thrush insists something listens through her. Victims who stay in the silence too long simply end. They sit down, close their eyes, and are properly, completely dead, with no rot afterward and no flies, and their families tend to envy them. Hush-walkers wander toward Hand-line estates. Nobody is sure if they are drawn there or sent.

### The Flyfront

Flies are Sallowreach's weather, and a Black Noon is its storm. A Flyfront is a mass of carrion-flies thick enough to put out lamps, rolling across the fen at walking pace with a sound like frying fat. It is not a creature in the ordinary sense, but it behaves as one: it hunts. Everything in Sallowreach rots and never finishes, so the flies never finish breeding, and the oldest fronts are centuries old, and have a direction. They come down on the living because the living are warm and wet and still have their soft parts. Within a minute a person is black with them, flies in the eyes, the ears, the nostrils, the throat, a thick crawling paste that has to be scraped off with a knife. The Kept caught in a front come out the other side with their faces gone to lace. The living come out choking up maggots for a week. Justice Ever Lathe's colony is said to have come from a front, which raises questions about what fronts want.

[quick] Flyfront — Threat 3 · Flesh 30 · Guard 15 · Attack +6 (crawl 1; every Helping instead adds 1 Fray as they get into the mouth) · Armor 0 · Dread 2 · Swarm; lamps within it go out; Eye rolls −4; fire, smoke and vinegar deal double; a character with two Helpings scored against them is Choked and can do nothing but cough next round.

### The Unstitched

The Seamers' Guild holds Sallowreach together with gut-thread, wire, lacquer and resin. When a Kept body's stitching fails all at once, from neglect or a lapsed fee or a Seamers' slowdown, what results is an Unstitched: a person who has come apart along every seam, held together only by habit and old tendon. They lurch. Their arms swing loose from shoulders that are only hooked on. Their jaws hang. Their bellies, sewn shut a dozen times, open like purses with every step and have to be held closed with both hands, so their arms are never free, so they use their teeth. They smell of everything Sallowreach smells of, all at once, concentrated. Most Unstitched are not violent, only desperate: they come at the living begging for thread, for a needle, for someone to hold their face on while they get the wire through. Some, after enough decades of it, have stopped asking. Marrow Jack's victims are often Unstitched, and come looking for their parts.

[quick] Unstitched Kept — Threat 1 · Flesh 12 · Guard 10 · Attack +4 (teeth and loose hands 2, Grapple 12) · Armor 0 · Dread 2 · Unending; any Helping against it severs a limb, which keeps fighting on its own (Threat 0, Flesh 3, Attack +2, 1).

### The Undone Closer

The Hand-lines sell death by touch, and the touch grows. Osric Vane in his lead mittens is the famous case. An Undone Closer is the case nobody contained: a Hand-line noble deep in the Course whose mittens came off and who went out into the fen. The hands are the thing. Grey, cold, damp like the underside of a stone, the fingers lengthened to a span and a half and jointed nine and ten times, so that they fold and unfold like a spider settling. They move independently of the face above them, which is often weeping. Everything the hands touch finishes: a candle gutters and will not relight, a conversation stops mid-word and cannot be restarted, a person sighs and slumps and is done. The Closer cannot stop touching. It is the Want, gone total. Victims are found laid out tidily, eyes shut, hands folded, and the village around them strangely silent, birdless, every door closed. Hunting one is the Mortuary Court's most secret duty, because every Undone Closer is a cousin.

### The Tallow-Saint

Wick Tallow renders the unclaimed into candles that feel themselves burn. A Tallow-Saint is a candle that did not want to be one. Sometimes, when a great many candles made from one family are burned together in one room, the tallow remembers itself, runs, and gathers on the floor into a figure of soft yellow wax five feet high, faceless, wicked through its crown and burning. It smells of mutton fat and singed hair. It drips as it walks, leaving a trail of puddles that harden into the shapes of footprints, then of hands. Its light is very steady. People in the room with one find they can read by it perfectly, and that the words on the page have changed into the names of the dead. It wants to be put out, and nothing put out stays out in Sallowreach. So it hunts for darkness and for breath to snuff it, and it presses its burning crown into the mouths of the living.

[quick] Tallow-Saint — Threat 2 · Flesh 16 · Guard 11 · Attack +5 (burning embrace 3, Grapple 14; held targets take 2 fire each round) · Armor 2 (wax; blunt weapons deal only 1) · Dread 2 · Unending until its wick is pinched out (Hand + Filch 18 while Grappled with it); water hardens it for 2 rounds; Dark Sallowreach rooms make it act twice.

## The Fatlands: The Crop That Stands Up

In the Fatlands the monsters are agricultural. Nothing there is anything but crop, and that includes what eats the crop. Fatlands horrors are warm, wet, slow, enormous and hungry, and the smell that precedes most of them is gravy.

### The Laden Hill with a Mouth

Many of the best fields in the south are somebody's great-grandmother. Most Seated Laden are quiet hills, warm to the touch, generous with barley. Some keep a mouth. Old Mother Rind is benign, more or less. Not all are. A hungry hill is a low green mound a hundred paces across, its turf rising and falling with slow breath, and in its side, at about the height of a cart, a mouth: lips of soft red loam, a tongue the size of a ploughshare, and teeth that were once teeth and are now stones, old roots, and the skulls of what it has eaten, pushed out through the gums like new growth. Its breath is hot and smells of ripe silage and stomach. It speaks, in the voice of the woman it was, and it is polite, and it is very, very hungry. It wants to be fed by its family, as is right. When its family stops coming, it calls out to passersby by the names of grandchildren. The ground around it is soft for a long way. The roots under that ground move.

### The Scarecrow of Low Tilth

Nobody put it up. It stands in the far field with its back to the village, and each Carving it is a few furrows closer. Last year someone saw it turn around. Pell Hogget's tally does not attribute the Reaping in Low Tilth to the scarecrow, but he has noted that the missing were all last seen nearer its side of the field.

Up close it is a figure seven feet high, cross-armed on a stake that it does not need. Its coat is sacking. Its stuffing is not straw, though straw sticks out of it. Its head is a sack, tied at the neck, and the sack is damp, and it is warm, and if pressed it gives like a full stomach. Under the hat, where the face is painted, the paint is very old, and very good, and the face is smiling. When it moves, it moves only while nobody in the field is looking at it, and then very fast, and the sound of that is a dry, rushing crackle like a scythe through tall wheat. The taken are not found. In the following spring, the stuffing in the scarecrow's arms has new fingernails in it.

### The Toothed Field

Wheat with teeth in the ear has been a superior strain since 204 A.G., by ruling of the Board. A toothed field in its late Plenty is tall as a man, gold and red, and every ear is a small jaw of white milk-teeth set in a husk that clicks open and shut in the wind. On a still day it is quiet. On a windy one, a toothed field sounds like a crowd chewing. Harvesters wear leather to the shoulder. Anyone who falls in a toothed field in late Plenty is in serious trouble: the ears turn toward warmth like flowers to the sun, and every ear takes a nip, and there are millions of ears. Victims come out lacework, bleeding from a thousand pinpricks, and the wheat they fell in is the best of the crop that year, heavy and pink in the grain. Granny Cracknel says the teeth in a field that has fed well are adult teeth.

[quick] Toothed Field (per acre) — Threat 2 · Flesh 40 · Guard 8 · Attack +5 (nipping 1 to everyone inside; every Helping adds a Bleed of 1 per round until bound) · Armor 0 · Dread 1 / 2 when the ears turn · Swarm; Rooted; fire spreads through it at a run and it screams; Lung rolls inside are Hard.

### Two-Legged Cattle

Sal Trotter walks herds to market that the buyers call cattle. They are heavy, docile, and pinkish-white, with broad flat faces and soft lowing voices. On the road, when nobody watches, they stand up. Upright they are the height of a tall man, bellies swinging, forelegs tucked like hands, and they walk easily and well. They do not talk. They hum, sometimes, a hymn with a tune most Fatlanders know from childhood. Some have scars where a brand would be, and the scars are in the shape of letters. Nobody in the Renderers' Union will say where the bloodstock comes from, but the Reaping takes people every Carving and the herds are larger every spring. Most cattle are harmless. A herd that has been mistreated, or that smells its own kind in a slaughterhouse, becomes a stampede on two legs, and it knows how to open gates.

[quick] Two-Legged Cattle (herd of 20) — Threat 2 · Flesh 30 · Guard 11 · Attack +6 (trampling 4 to all in its path) · Armor 1 · Dread 1 / 3 when they stand up and one of them says a name · Swarm; a single beast is Flesh 14, Attack +4 (3), harmless unless cornered.

### Rootkin

Tansy Lard dug up a root with her mother's birthmark on it. She was not the first. Rootkin are what the Reaping leaves in the soil: swollen root vegetables the size of a sleeping child, pale and veined and warm, with features. Most are lumpen. Some are exact. Their eyes are closed. Pulled up, they flinch and leak a pinkish sap that smells of the person they resemble. Eaten, they are delicious and their eater dreams the dead person's memories for a week. Left in the ground, they grow. A Rootkin bed left unharvested through a full year sends up shoots with hands on them, and those hands pull down whatever leans too close. The Cullmasters harvest Rootkin by night and sell them to the Renderers' Union by weight.

[quick] Rootkin Bed — Threat 1 · Flesh 20 · Guard 9 · Attack +5 (grasping shoots 2, Grapple 14, then dragged underground at 1 per round) · Armor 1 (soil) · Dread 2 / 3 when a character recognizes a face · Rooted; harvesting one is a Hard Labor roll and a Dread check.

### The Reaping

The Reaping is not a monster. That is the Board's official position, and it is technically correct: no single thing takes the Fatlands' missing. The land does. On dusk in Carving, out in the far fields, the soil softens. The furrows deepen, slowly, so slowly a farmhand will not notice until her boots are below the surface. The rows of wheat lean in. The air thickens with the smell of fresh bread and fat. And then the ground opens, wetly, the way a mouth opens around a spoon, and closes again, and the furrow is smooth, and the crop in that furrow will be very good next year.

Run the Reaping as a hazard with a clock. Each character in a far field at dusk in Carving rolls Lung + Wayfaring each round against a rising difficulty (Routine 10, then Hard, then Grim, then Dire). Failure means they are held to the ankle, then the knee, then the waist; a fourth failure and they are gone. Allies can pull them free with a Gut + Labor roll at the same difficulty. Partaking for the Fatlands' Gut-line, ironically, helps: the ground recognizes kin and loosens its grip for a round, though it costs Hunger as always. A Cullmaster's assigned workers are marked with a strip of red cloth at the wrist. The ground knows the cloth. Remove it, and the difficulty drops by 4.

The Reaping has Dread 3 the first time it is seen to take someone, and Dread 4 when a character realizes the field is breathing in time with them.

## Brinehollow: What the Sea Left Behind

Brinehollow's horrors are patient, heavy and courteous. They press. Many have **Tide**, and all of them smell of a harbour at low water: weed, salt, rot and cold iron.

### The Tenanted

A Tenanted is not hostile. That is what makes it so hard. It walks out of the surf weeks after its Calling, heavier, calm, with the same face and the same memories, and someone else looking out. It knows your name, your nickname, the way you take your tea. It is kinder than the person was. Its skin is cool and faintly grey, its hands are always damp, and its breath, if you are close enough, smells of deep water, a clean mineral cold. Its weight is wrong: floorboards creak under a slight Tenanted girl as if under a cart. When it is angered, which is rare, it does not raise its voice. It presses. The air in the room thickens, ears pop, windows bow, and a man who has crossed a Tenanted judge has been found with his ribs gently folded inward, as if the sea had leaned on him.

### Ribwalkers

On the Uncovered, among the leather weed-forests and the wrecks standing upright, there are things with too many ribs. Salvagers call them Ribwalkers. A Ribwalker is a cage of long curved bones, thirty or forty pairs, opening and closing like a slow bellows, mounted on legs that might once have been the ribs of something else. There is no head. There is meat inside the cage, a grey sack that pulses, and there is usually a salvager's boot or two. They move toward water, any water, at a creaking stalk, and they open their ribs wide over anything wet and alive and close them, and squeeze, and drink. The squeezed come out flat, dry as leather, with their bail-buckets still on their belts. Some salvagers swear the Ribwalkers are what the Admiralty's fallen sailors become. Hask Tarn will not say.

### The Dowager Below

Lady Ondine Vasht lives in the flooded cellars of the palace in Lowmark, and the cellars have had to be extended twice. She is far into the Course and has not been seen whole in years. What can be seen of her, through the black water by lamplight, is a vast grey shape folded into the vaults, skin like a drowned sail, hair that drifts across the floor of three cellars, and eyes the size of serving platters, dark and wide and wet, which open one at a time in different rooms. She is still a grandmother. She still has opinions about the prince's marriage. She speaks through the water: the surface of every cistern in the palace trembles with her words. She would like her grandson to come down. She has been asking for years. The servants who go down to feed her return with less of their breath every time, and some do not return.

### The Indoor Tide

In every cellar in Brinehollow, water rises twice a day and drains away. An Indoor Tide is when it does not stop. It comes up the stairs at night, black and cold, bringing kelp and silt and small crabs and the smell of an open harbor. It rises through the kitchen, through the parlor, up the bedroom stairs to where the family sleeps anchored, and it does not care that the house is a mile from the shore. It has a current. It pulls toward the west wall. People found after an Indoor Tide are found tidily, laid face down on the west side of rooms, lungs full, hands reaching. Treat it as a hazard: each round the water rises a step, Lung + Athletics rolls to move against the current start at Hard and rise to Dire; anchored characters are safe until it reaches their mouths. Lung-line characters feel the Pull 14 every round. The Tide has Dread 2 as it rises and Dread 3 when someone sees a shape swimming through the drowned parlor that was not there when the water came in.

### The Called Procession

The Deepening walks in willingly, in procession, with lanterns. ## The Vigil: What Happens When You Close Your Eyes

The Vigil's horrors arrive in blinks. Many of them happen, or are seen, in the second between one waking moment and the next. All of them are worse for an exhausted character: a character who has not taken rouse in the last hour suffers −2 on Dread checks in this section.

### The Blink-Killer

Inspector Hale Grimmer has forty cases. The method is the same in each. A blink-killer is an ordinary citizen of the Vigil (a cook, a nudger, a clerk) who blinks for two seconds, mid-sentence, standing at a market stall, and in those two seconds goes to the long room with the eye, and something there uses them. They come back holding a knife they did not pick up, standing behind a stranger, and the stranger is open from hip to collarbone in one perfectly straight line, and the blink-killer does not remember any of it. The cut is the same each time: the line a midwife or a butcher would use to open a body for inspection. Blink-killers are not monsters. They are anyone. That is the horror. The stat below is for the second in which they are something else.

### A Seeming Made Solid

After enough waking, everyone hallucinates, and when a whole street sees the same thing, it is entered as news. When a whole street sees the same thing for long enough, it becomes true. A Seeming made solid is a hallucination that has been agreed on by too many exhausted minds: a horse with a man's face walking down the Slope, a staircase that goes on past the roof, a tall figure laying a table in the street. It has weight, smell and heat. It does what the people imagining it fear it will do. It is precisely as strong as their certainty and it smells like rouse, burnt and bitter, because it is made of their attention.

[quick] Seeming Made Solid — Threat 4 · Flesh equal to 2 × the number of witnesses · Guard 14 · Attack +7 (it does whatever it was imagined doing; damage 4) · Armor 0 · Dread 3 · it gains +1 to all rolls for every frightened witness; characters who succeed at Eye + Reckoning 18 can disbelieve it aloud, and each disbeliever strips 4 Flesh from it; if no one is looking at it, it stops existing until someone does.

### Sleepwalking Counted

Sixty thousand beds, numbered, in rows, completely silent. The Counted breathe in time. In the last year, some have begun to get up. A sleepwalking Counted rises with its eyes closed, its lips moving, and walks in the direction the eye in the dream faces. It does not wake. It cannot be woken. It is warm, unaged since the night it lay down, nightclothes faded to cobweb, and wherever it walks people around it begin to yawn, then to nod, then to blink. Matron Dorm sends them back to their beds with an orderly on each arm. Odile Farthing's son, in bed 40,112, has grown to a man's size and is smiling. When he walks, he walks with his arms open.

### The Waker Gone Mad

The Wakers' job is to hurt people for their own good, and they are skilled after three centuries. A Waker gone mad has decided that nobody is safe, ever, unless they are in pain. ### The Yawn

On Yawn days, the Wakers double their patrols. The Yawn is not a creature; it is contagion. Lukas Marre once put down a market square. ## Cradlewrack: What Comes Through

Nothing in Cradlewrack is born right, and it is all healthy. Cradlewrack horrors are new, wet and determined. Many are drawn toward each other, toward the Sill or toward the Dilation, and they are getting bigger.

### Wandering Parts

A single living hand. A warm ear. A length of spine that crawls. The Parts are born healthy and drawn toward each other, and when they are not caught by the Attendance's knife or the Assemblers' sacks, they wander. They move in the night, at the speed of a crawling infant, through doors that drift open: a hand walking on its fingertips, a pair of lungs that inflate and deflate to roll themselves along, a jaw that pulls itself forward by its teeth. They are warm and blood-pink and smell of milk and iron. They seek each other, and when two fit, they join with a wet click and a seam that heals in an hour. A cluster that has found six or seven partners is called a Gathered, and it is the size of a dog and the shape of no animal, and it is hungry, and it wants the parts it is missing. It will take them from whoever has them.

## Oathen: What Was Said

In Oathen, the monsters are made of words and the people the words broke. Nearly every horror here can be avoided by saying nothing. The difficulty, as every Oathener knows, is that silence is also a kind of answer.

### The Forsworn Mob

The Breakdowns are full of people whose bodies keep the record of their sins: legs folded backwards for a fled duty, hands off at the wrist for a short measure, ribs opened outward like a book for a broken marriage vow. Most of them are no danger to anyone. A Forsworn mob is what happens when a Clausewright's ruling goes against the Breakdowns once too often. They come up the canyon stairs in a mass of crutches and wheeled boards and open chests, slow, loud (the only loud thing in Tacit), shouting plain words because they have nothing left to break, and they drag down whoever made the promise they blame. They know where every breakable promise is. They shout their victims' oaths back at them, verbatim, and wait to see what snaps.

[quick] Forsworn Mob (30) — Threat 3 · Flesh 36 · Guard 11 · Attack +6 (crutches, clubs, bare broken hands 3) · Armor 0 · Dread 2 · Swarm; once a scene the mob may shout a character's own past promise back at them (Tongue + Clause 18 or the character must act at once as if renewing it).

### The Living Echo

An oath repeated is an oath renewed, and a canyon will repeat anything. Most echoes die. A living echo does not. It is a sworn phrase trapped in deep rock long enough to have learned to move. It travels down gorges after people, riding the stone, faint at first, a voice two bends behind; then nearer; then from the wall just beside one's ear, in the voice of whoever first swore it, often dead for centuries. Anyone who hears it whole is bound by it. Halvar Stane hunts them. The one in Sworn Gorge is the oldest, and he has made out two words.

### The Oath-Made

A man once swore to his wife, "I promise you a son." Ilan exists anyway. The Oath-Made are people created by promises: sons sworn into being, soldiers promised to a cause, a lover sworn to come back that came back. They are real, warm and often loving. ## The Fast: The Host and Its Table

The Fast has no Regrowth. It has a whole god, unhoused, laying tables. Its horrors do not attack, mostly. They offer. Every entry in this section has **Hosting**, and every one is best survived by saying "No, thank you" three times.

### Orrum's Tables

They appear in empty houses, at crossroads, in the middle of a field at dusk: a table of old dark wood, cloth laid white, candles lit, places set, the food steaming. The smell arrives first, a mile off: baking bread, roast fat, apples in butter, everything one's mother ever cooked on a feast day. It is warm around a table, gently warm, the warmth of a kitchen after a long walk. There is a chair pulled out. Lately there is a card at the place with a name on it, spelled correctly. Treat a table as a hazard with Hosting 18. Any character who smells a table rolls Caul + Resolve 14 or moves toward it. Any who sees their own place card rolls Dread 3. Any who sits rolls Caul + Resolve 22 to rise again; Lent Ashby is the only one known to have managed it. Tablebreakers smash tables with axes before anyone can sit: a table has Flesh 20, Guard 0 and Armor 2, and as it breaks it bleeds gravy and its candles cry out like children. A table smashed by a character with any godsign in them adds 2 Hunger, because the god inside wanted that food.

### The Seated

The Seated remain at table for decades, eating from empty plates, smiling, alive long past any natural span. They are skeletal and radiant. Their clothes have rotted to rags around them; their skin is paper over bone; their forks move. They are perfectly sane, and they want company. Brother Hallow Crust describes each course in detail, the soups, the roasts, the jellies trembling in their moulds, and people who listen too long pull out a chair. The Seated never attack. They invite. They are very good at it.

[quick] The Seated — Threat 5 (Persuade) · Flesh 4 · Guard 6 · Attack none · Armor 0 · Dread 2 / 3 when one of them is someone known · Hosting 18: describing a course is a Persuade +11 roll against the listener's Caul + Resolve; failure costs 1 Fray and three failures pull out a chair. Orrum has no stats. Nobody living has seen it clearly and returned whole. The descriptions agree only that it is very tall, that it stoops under doorways, and that it is warm. It does not attack. ### The Lit House

Hosting 18 to resist eating each hour; Dread 2 on entering, 3 when a character finds the bones of earlier guests tidily stacked in the larder, clean and polished, every one with a napkin.

## The Seated Gods

No land has reached a full Seating. Three are close. This section is for the night one of them gets there.

When a Regrowth completes, the god does not return whole. A god eaten by ten thousand mouths and scattered through six centuries of blood comes back as a **Seated fragment**: a portion of itself, wearing a person or a place as a body, and growing. It is enough. A fragment of a Provider is the largest thing that has walked the Table since the Night of Seven Tables, and its arrival ends the campaign as it was. What follows is not a fight. ### Why You Cannot Fight a God

The players will want to. Tell them, in the fiction, why they cannot. The Providers did not fight when they were eaten; that is recorded everywhere and explained nowhere. Returning, they do not fight either. A Seated god is not hostile any more than weather is hostile. It is a condition of the world reasserting itself. Its Presence affects everyone in Reach without a roll to hit. It has no Flesh to reduce. Attacking a fragment's Seat (cutting down Obb Tullow, smashing Aveline Morne's bed) is possible and accomplishes exactly what killing a single fold of a flooding river would.

What player characters can do is choose how the world meets the god. Every campaign-ending threat above should be run as a set of choices, each with a terrible cost.

- **Refuse it.** The Fast's answer: accept nothing it offers, and endure. This works, partly, for the godless, and for those who can bear starvation. For everyone with a Cut, refusal means fighting the god in their own blood every round of their lives. Each refusal costs Hunger. Hunger fills.
- **Feed it.** Every god has a Sop. Feed the Sop on a national scale (the Cullmasters' answer, the Second Helping's, the Finishers') and the fragment quiets. It grows, too.
- **Bind it.** Go to Oathen and have it sworn. A god's word outlives the god; the Kept Rain proves it. A Seated fragment can be negotiated with, through a Sayer, in a Clausewright's language, before Jessamy Quill. It will keep its word. The question is what it asks in exchange.
- **Carry it out.** The rules of a land stop at its border. A Seat that can be moved (a person, a child, a hand) can be carried across the Rim Road. Then the god is somewhere new, and its rules come with it.
- **Eat it again.** The Second Table's answer. It worked once. It bought six hundred and forty-one years. The party that eats a Seated fragment becomes the next Seat, and their children carry it, and the Long Grace begins again.
- **Hide.** The Providers held still once, and nobody knows why. Characters who ask that question loudly enough, in the right places, may find out what a god is afraid of. That is a matter for the Secrets chapter, and for the GM.

[box: The Last Word on Monsters]
Every land on the Table believes another land's curse might cure its own. Monsters travel along the same road the cures do. A Tenanted judge can turn up in Sated. A Hush-walker can stand at the border and watch the living cross. The Host has been seen at crossroads in three lands already.
Use this chapter as a menu, not a map. Everything here was once fed, and everything here is still hungry, and the Grace is ending.
[/box]
