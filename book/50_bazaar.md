# The Rim Market

> Everything on the Table has a price. The trick is finding out who is paying it.
>> — painted over the door of the Company counting-house at Toll Nine

[fiction]
The stall stood where the Rim Road bends at Gristmoot, between a man selling anchoring-rope by the fathom and a Seamer's boy crying "Thread, wire, lacquer, fingers!" in a voice that had broken years ago and been stitched back wrong. It was a Fatlands stall. You could tell by the width of the counter, which was built to bear a forearm the size of a bolster, and by the smell, which was gravy, clove, and something under the clove that the clove was there for.
The stall-wife was called Mardy Haunch and she was, by the custom of her country, beautiful: she spilled over her stool on both sides like risen dough over a tin. She was eating while she sold. She was always eating while she sold. She had a sausage in one hand and a knife in the other, and the sausage was a yard long and coiled on the board in front of her like a sleeping dog, and every so often it moved.
"It is not moving," said Mardy Haunch, to the customer. "That is settling."
The customer was from the Vigil. You could tell by the eyes, which were red to the rims and had stopped bothering to blink, and by the long pin she wore through the web of her left hand so she could twist it when the world went soft. She had a nudger with her, a small boy on a lead, whose whole job was to kick her ankle every twenty breaths. He was very good at it. He did not look up from her ankle once.
"Price," said the Vigil woman.
"For the yard? Eleven lacks."
"Eleven." The Vigil woman put three little black tabs on the counter, pressed and stamped with an open eye. "Rouse. Good rouse. Crane's strain, not the Slope stuff. Four hours of waking each."
Mardy Haunch looked at the tabs the way a cat looks at a painted bird. "What am I going to do with four hours of waking, my love? I sleep like a ditch. Coin."
"I have six lacks in coin."
"Then you have six lacks of sausage." Mardy raised the knife and the sausage, quite distinctly, tightened its coil away from it.
"Settling," said Mardy, and put the knife down.
At this point a third party arrived, which is how all real haggling on the Rim Road goes. He was an Oathener, tall and dry and dressed in canyon-red, with a little brass plate at his collar that said PRICE-WITNESS, LICENSED, and he spoke very slowly, the way a man walks across a frozen pond. "If it would be of use," he said, "and if neither party objects, and barring weather, I might, as I now intend, assist."
"Nobody asked you," said Mardy.
"That is so," said the Oathener, which cost him nothing, because it was true. He looked at the sausage. He looked at it for some time. "I would observe, without asserting anything as to its composition, that the casing bears a mark."
It did. A little way from the end there was a faded blue pattern pricked into the skin. Anchors. Two anchors, crossed, and under them, in a sailor's crude letters, the beginning of a name: SAL. The rest of the name was round the other side of the coil.
There was a silence of the kind you get at a market when everyone within six stalls has stopped pretending not to listen. The rope-seller had stopped measuring. The Seamer's boy had stopped crying fingers.
"That," said Mardy Haunch, with tremendous dignity, "is a pig that went to sea."
"I would not say otherwise," said the Oathener, very carefully, "since I cannot."
The Vigil woman leaned in, red eyes unblinking, close enough to read. The boy kicked her ankle. She did not notice. "Turn it over," she said.
"Turn it over yourself. Four lacks, then. For the yard. And the tabs."
"Three lacks and the tabs, and you turn it over."
"Five and I don't."
The Oathener coughed, gently. "If it would help to settle the matter, and as a neutral party, I might say that in my judgment, as I now hold it, the item has a value, and that value is not nothing."
Both women looked at him with loathing. He received it calmly. He was paid a crumb a word, and he had been very economical.
In the end the Vigil woman paid four lacks and two tabs, and Mardy Haunch threw in a heel of toothed bread, which bit the boy, and wrapped the coil in a sheet of oiled paper and tied it with string, and did not turn it over. The Vigil woman walked off up the Rim Road towards the lamp-glow on the eastern horizon, carrying the parcel against her chest like a baby, and three stalls down she stopped, and unwrapped one end, and looked.
Nobody saw what the rest of the name said. But they saw her stand there a long while. And they saw her, at last, wrap it up again and keep walking, because it was a long road and she was hungry and four lacks is four lacks.
"Lack keep away," said Mardy Haunch to the next customer, and smiled, and ate.
[/fiction]

On the Table everyone needs something that only an obscene neighbour can sell. The sleepless need oil, brine and pain. The undying need perfume and thread. The starving heartland needs everything, and hates whoever brings it. The Rim Road is the ring of highway that carries all of it, three thousand miles of rutted stone around the Fast's border, owned by the Rim Road Company, tolled at every bridge and lined with inns, wells, shrines, gibbets and markets. Most of what player characters buy, they buy on the Rim, from someone who is lying to them about where it came from.

This chapter is a working market. It sets out the money of the Table and what it is made of; how to find something for sale and how long that takes; how to argue the price down; what living costs in each land; and long lists of weapons, armour, clothes, tools, food, drugs, beasts, beds and services. It closes with the things that are not supposed to be for sale at all: relics cut from the gods, and the merchants who deal in them.

[box: Using This Chapter]
Prices are given in lacks (L) and crumbs (c). Ten crumbs make a lack; twenty lacks make a platter. Prices assume a Rim Road market in a good season. Use the Availability rules to decide whether an item is on the stall at all, the Haggling rules to settle what the characters actually pay, and the Taint rules whenever the thing they bought was once part of a god.
The GM should not let shopping become bookkeeping. Track money closely when it is scarce and it matters (in the Fast, in a siege, in debt to an Oathener). Otherwise let characters live at a lifestyle and roll only for the purchases that make a story.
[/box]

## The Coin of the Table

> We pay for bread with bone. Nobody thinks this is strange. Nobody has ever asked whose.
>> — Meek Garrity, from a suppressed lecture

### The Lack

The lack is the one coin honoured everywhere on the Table: a disc of salt-cured bone the width of a thumb-joint, a finger's width thick, pierced at the centre so it can be strung, and stamped on one face with the Plate (a full moon) and on the other with an empty circle. By law and by custom it is worth one day's bread. When the price of bread rises, people do not say that bread is dear. They say the lack is thin.

Nobody minted the first lacks. In the years after the Gorging, when the famine broke and the crops rose in a week, the peoples of the Table had a great deal of bone lying about from the meal, and the oldest tradition holds that the first coins were cut from the gods themselves: from Ummer's ribs, from Dromm's vast spine, from the knuckles of Ossel. Those First Lacks, if any survive, are not money. They are relics (see Relics & Godflesh). The coin in your purse is ordinary bone. That is the official position, and it is almost true.

#### How Bone Becomes Coin

Since 212 A.G. the only lawful mint is the Rim Road Company's Cure-House at Saltbridge, on the Rim where the Fatlands road meets the Brinehollow road. Every lack struck in the last four centuries passed through it. The process is old, simple and filthy.

- **The bone.** It comes from the Chute, the Fatlands' great export depot, in sealed carts stamped BONE: MIXED. The Renderers' Union sells the Company the long bones of everything slaughtered in the south. Everything slaughtered in the south includes the cattle, the swine, the orchard-pigs, the drover-herds of Sal Trotter, and the Fatlands dead, who are served at their own wakes and whose bones the family is paid a few crumbs to send on. The Company does not sort. Coin-cutters say you can tell a cattle lack from a person lack by the grain; the Company says that is superstition, and fines anyone who says it twice.
- **The boil.** The bones are boiled white in copper vats for three days and three nights. The fat is skimmed and sold back south as tallow. The steam from the Cure-House can be smelled from the next toll, and the people of Saltbridge are famous for their good skin and bad dreams.
- **The cutting.** Coin-cutters, most of them Scraplings bound to the Company for twenty years, saw the long bones into rounds on treadle saws and punch the centre hole. A good cutter gets nineteen lacks from a man's thigh. The cutters keep tally-sticks of their work and are paid in their own product, one lack in fifty.
- **The cure.** The rounds are packed in salt for a full season. The salt is Brinehollow salt, and since the sea retreated, most Brinehollow salt is boiled out of the water Brinehollowers cough up at dawn and dusk, collected in parish buckets and sold by the barrel. Every lack on the Table is cured in somebody's lungs.
- **The stamp.** A hot iron die burns the Plate and the empty circle into the faces. The empty circle has been on the coin since the first Company issue; the Company's charter says it represents "the bread to be bought." Some old coin-cutters say it is a chair seen from above.

A genuine lack is dense, faintly translucent at the edge when held to a lamp, cool to the touch, and tastes strongly of salt. Merchants bite coins. On the Rim this is not a figure of speech.

#### Crumbs

A crumb is a tenth of a lack: a bone bead the size of a pea, pierced and cured like its parent, cut from the knuckles, ribs and offcuts that will not make a full disc. Crumbs are strung in tens on a knotted cord called a loaf. Paying a lack in crumbs is legal but rude, and a merchant may count every bead in front of you while you wait. Children are paid in crumbs, beggars ask for them, and every Oathen price-witness charges by them.

#### Platters

A platter is a sealed Rim Road Company tablet of polished bone, the size of a palm, worth twenty lacks. Each is cut from a single shoulder blade, stamped with the Company's ring-road seal, numbered, and closed with a bead of red wax impressed by the signet of Jessamy Quill, Notary of the Rim Road. The Company has sworn, in Oathen, before Quill, that it will redeem every unbroken platter for twenty lacks at any Company house on the Rim. Because the oath was sworn in Oathen, it cannot be broken without the Company's sworn officers coming apart. This is why the platter is trusted more than any coin, and why the Company guards Jessamy Quill more closely than any king guards a crown. If she dies, every platter in circulation becomes a pretty piece of shoulder.

#### Weighing and Clipping

A true lack weighs as much as forty grains of untoothed wheat. Money-changers carry hanging scales and a pouch of reference grains, and in the Fatlands, where the wheat has teeth, they carry grains from the Rim and guard them like jewels. Shaving a lack (clipping) is a hanging offence in every land except Sallowreach, where the offender is shaved in turn, very slowly, by the same amount. Clipped coins are accepted at nine crumbs, or at eight if the clipping shows.

#### Counterfeits

Bone is everywhere, so forging lacks is the commonest crime on the Rim. The common fakes and their tells:
- **Horn and hoof.** Pressed and dyed. Too light; burns with a stink when touched with a hot needle. Detected on a Routine (10) Eye + Reckoning roll.
- **Unsalted bone.** Bone that skipped the season in brine. Tastes of nothing. A merchant who bites coin finds it at once.
- **Kept bone.** Cut from the Kept of Sallowreach, whose bodies never finish rotting. These coins are perfect in every respect but one: they never stop decaying. In a few weeks a Kept lack goes greasy and grey and smells faintly of the fen. In a purse it sweats. A purse of Kept coin attracts flies. Hard (14) to spot when fresh.
- **Wrack bone.** Bone from Cradlewrack Parts. A coin cut from a Part, it is whispered, is still growing; it thickens by a hair a month, and in a year will not fit a string. Hard (14) to spot.
- **Blank bone.** The rarest and most sinister fake: lacks cut from the bones of Blanks or the Unfed. They are said to be the only coins with no god in them at all. Collectors pay twenty times face value. They are almost always proof that somebody was killed for their skeleton.
Passing false coin knowingly is punished by the Company with a fine of ten times the face value and the loss of the passing hand's thumb, and by most lands with worse.

[box: Whose Bones?]
The question every player eventually asks is answered differently depending on who is asked. The Company says: cattle. The Renderers' Union says: whatever comes. Mistress Brisket Annalow, if she can be made to say anything, says that since the Reaping doubled the Chute ships more bone than the herds could possibly account for, and that the Company has never once complained about the quality. A character who studies lacks for long (Grim 18 Eye + Lore) can learn to read the grain, and from then on will know which coins in their purse were people. They will still spend them. Everybody does.
[/box]

### Sworn Notes

Oathen does not mint. It does not need to. In Oathen, a written promise to pay, sworn aloud before witnesses and signed, is backed by the only security that cannot run away: the issuer's own body. These are sworn notes, and they are the paper money of the eastern half of the Table.

A sworn note reads something like this: "I, Halde Vosk, of Tacit, as I now intend and barring weather, will pay the bearer twelve lacks on demand at my house in the third gallery, and if I do not, let my left hand answer." The issuer reads it aloud in front of a licensed Clausewright, signs it, and the Clausewright witnesses it with a thumbprint in ink. The note then circulates exactly like coin.

The collateral clause is what gives the note its value. It names what breaks if the issuer defaults: a hand, an eye's sight, the use of a knee, the voice, a set of teeth. The Rule of Oathen does the rest. The moment the issuer refuses or fails to pay a bearer who has made lawful demand, the named part breaks, at once, with a sound, wherever the issuer happens to be. Notes backed by large collateral ("let my heart answer") are not legal in Oathen, since a break is never fatal by itself and a heart that opens like a cupboard and goes on beating is considered bad for trade.

- **Valuing a note.** A note is worth its face value only if the issuer is solvent, whole, and findable. Oathen notes trade at full value in Tacit, at a crumb in the lack discount on the Rim, and at two or three crumbs in the lack in the far west, where the issuer is weeks away. A note by an issuer known to be close to default trades at half. Speculators in Tacit watch issuers' bodies through spyglasses from the opposite canyon wall, and a limp is news.
- **Forsworn paper.** Notes issued by the Forsworn of the Breakdowns are backed by what they have left. A man whose ribs already stand open like a book has little to pledge. Brakk's people issue notes against parts that are already broken; these are worthless and are traded as jokes, souvenirs and insults.
- **Sureties.** A merchant who lacks good body can hire a Surety to co-sign: the Surety pledges a part of their own body as second collateral, for a fee of one lack in ten. Ona Fell's notes are the best paper in the canyon. She has two hundred and eleven other oaths in her, and her signature still trades at par.
- **Default abroad.** The break happens wherever the issuer is, because the oath is in the issuer. A note sworn in Tacit and defaulted in Lastgate snaps a wrist in Lastgate. Foreign issuers who swear notes in Oathen are bound like natives. A non-Oathener who signs a note outside Oathen is not bound at all, and their notes are not called sworn notes but "dry paper," worth what the issuer's honesty is worth.
- **The new terror.** Writing has begun to bind on its own. Two clerks in Tacit have broken this year over notes they copied but never swore. The Bench of Clauses has said nothing. The discount on Oathen paper has quietly doubled.

[box: Default — What Happens]
When a character who issued a sworn note fails to pay on lawful demand, the named collateral breaks: treat it as an Injury from the Mangling table chosen to match (a hand that will not close; an eye gone milky; a knee reversed), with no roll to avoid it, and a Dread 2 check for anyone who sees it happen. The character is now Forsworn in that matter, and every Oathener who sees them knows it. Paying the debt afterwards does not mend the break. Nothing does.
[/box]

### Regional Money

Every land uses the lack, and every land also uses something else, usually something only that land could think of. A traveller who wants to be treated as a fool pays in lacks everywhere. A traveller who wants local prices learns the local money.

#### Sallowreach: Licenses, Minutes and Breaths

The only thing truly scarce in Sallowreach is ending, and so death is the money of the north. The Mortuary Court sells a **death-license**, entitling the bearer to one Closing by a Hand-line Closer, for about four thousand lacks. Few families can raise that, so for three centuries the Court has also issued fractions of a license. A **minute of Closing** is a sixtieth part of a license, a narrow lacquered slip of coffin-wood bearing Regent Grue's signature and the number of the license it belongs to. A **breath** is a twelfth of a minute: a black wax token stamped with a closed eye. Families save minutes for generations, in jars under the floor, towards the day great-great-grandmother can at last be finished. Lend a man a minute and he will thank you. Give a man a full license and he will weep. Minutes are the closest thing in Sallowreach to inheritance; they pass because their owners do not.

#### The Fatlands: Meat Scrip

The Board of Plenty pays wages, rents and taxes in **meat scrip**: printed slips of greased paper, each redeemable at any Board store or at the Chute for a stated weight of meat. The common denominations are the pound, the stone and the side. Meat scrip is the most honest money on the Table in one sense (it is backed by something you can eat) and the most horrible in every other, because its value tracks the supply of meat, and the supply of meat rises every Carving with the Reaping. In a heavy Reaping year the scrip falls by a third in a month. Pell Hogget's tally-keepers chart the scrip rate alongside their count of the missing, and the two lines match.

#### Brinehollow: Bail-Tokens

A **bail-token** is a small disc of cast lead, stamped with a bucket, issued by Brinehollow parishes to anyone who bails the infirm at dawn and dusk. Each token is good for one cup of sweet water at an Admiralty cistern or one bailing done on your behalf. Because both are always wanted, the tokens circulate as small change. Children like Tolly Brack earn more in tokens than their parents do in coin. A **Netwatch tally**, a larger lead token stamped with a hook, is paid to the patrol for every Called person dragged home alive; Netwatch tallies are worth ten bail-tokens and are always suspected of being for bodies that were not, strictly, alive.

#### The Vigil: Rouse-Tabs

The Vigil's small money is a **rouse-tab**: a pressed lozenge of black rouse stamped with an open eye, nominally good for one hour awake. Wages on the Slope are paid in tabs. Because the dose required rises every year, the tab is the only currency on the Table that suffers literal inflation of the body: a tab that held an hour in 600 A.G. now holds perhaps twenty minutes, and the Apothecary-General's office restamps the official value each season. Tabs cut with Brinehollow brine (see Drugs) are worth more on the Slope, because they work better, and are worth nothing to anyone who knows why.

#### Cradlewrack: Naming-Rights

In Cradlewrack, no child is named until it is a year old and plainly a child. Until then, the right to name it belongs to whoever bore it, and that right can be sold. A **naming-right** is a Tally-office certificate giving the holder the sole right to name a particular birth when the year is out. Rich families buy naming-rights to fill a branch of the family tree; the poor sell them for bread. Because nobody knows what a birth will turn out to be, naming-rights are speculation in the purest form: a right to name a healthy boy is worth a fortune, a right to name a hand is worth nothing, and a right to name something the Attendance does not permit is void. Kest has a naming-exchange, under the Tally hall, where rights are bought and sold by the hour as rumours come down from the birthing-rooms.

#### Oathen: Sworn Notes

As above. Small coin in Oathen is still the lack, and the price-witness is still paid in crumbs, but anything over a platter changes hands as paper.

#### The Fast: Ration Chits

The Unfed do not touch bone coin. They say the lack is cut from Godeaters and cured in Godeater lungs, and they are right, and they will not have it in their houses. Inside the Fast the only money is the **ration chit**: a square of unbleached cloth stamped by the Pantry with a single empty bowl, entitling the bearer to one day's ration at the Pantry window in Wanting. Chits are issued, never earned; the Abstinent give them out by household, by name, by list. A family left off the list has nothing to trade. The chits cannot lawfully be sold, and are sold constantly. In Lack season, when Mother Stint Avery begins cutting the lists, a chit fetches three lacks from a Crumb-Runner, who will be spat at for buying it.

### Barter

Most of the Table does not live on coin. Scrapling villages run on barter, and on the Rim a great deal of trade is goods against goods, with lacks used only to settle the difference.

- **Fair barter.** Goods trade at their listed price if both parties need them. Goods the buyer does not need are taken at half their value. Goods the buyer badly needs (oil in the Vigil, perfume in Lastgate, sweet water in Brinehollow, anything edible in the Fast) are taken at one and a half times or double.
- **Regional goods travel well.** A good carried from its home land to a distant one is worth half again its home price on arrival, before any haggling. This is the whole business of the Rim Road.
- **Bartering with Partaking.** A character may not Partake to haggle with goods unless the god they carry is one the other party fears; the Tooth is felt, not seen, and merchants on the Rim know the look of a customer who is getting help.
- **Barter in the Fast.** The Unfed accept no gift and give none. A barter in the Fast must be declined three times by both parties before it is agreed on the fourth asking. Skipping the ritual voids the trade, and the offended party will not deal again.

## Finding Things

> Anything can be bought on the Rim Road. Not everything can be found.
>> — Toll Nine proverb

### Availability

Every item in this chapter has an Availability rating. To find an item that is not simply sitting in front of the characters, one character makes a Grace Roll: **Eye + Haggle** against the difficulty below, modified by where they are looking. Other characters may help in the usual way. A character may Partake on the roll; the Tooth makes merchants talkative, or makes the searcher notice the stall behind the stall.

| Rating | Difficulty | Search time | Typical goods |
| Common | Routine 10 | an hour | bread, knives, rope, lamp oil, perfume, rouse |
| Uncommon | Hard 14 | half a day | swords, crossbows, mail, Kept-candles, laudanum |
| Scarce | Grim 18 | a day | plate, Brinehollow tonic in the Fatlands, a Tucker |
| Rare | Dire 22 | a week | godflesh, a Sayer's word, Blank parts |
| Unique | special | an adventure | named relics; never found by rolling alone |

- **Success** means the item is for sale, at listed price, in this market. Each **Helping** may be spent to find a better example (one extra quality, or no hidden flaw), to find it cheaper (one step of price in the buyer's favour, see Haggling), or to learn something about the seller.
- **Grace** means the seller is eager, or there is a second one, or the merchant offers something better that they "weren't going to show anyone."
- **Failure** means it is not here. The character may try again after the search time has passed again, at +2 difficulty for every retry in the same market.
- **Lack** means the item is found, and it is wrong: stolen and recognisable, Tainted and undeclared, a fake, or sold by an informer. The GM chooses, and does not say which until it matters.
- **Unique** items are never found with a roll. They are the end of a trail, the price of a favour, or the reward of an adventure.

### Black Markets

Every city on the Table has a market that is not on the Company's ledgers. In Lastgate it is the undercity under the lowest pilings, where Marrow Jack's boys sell limbs off drying racks. In Sated it is behind the rendering yards. In Lowmark it is out on the Uncovered at low fog. In the Vigil it is the Slope, which is a black market with houses on it. In Kest there are no locks, so the black market is simply wherever you are when somebody shuts the door. In Tacit it is conducted entirely in Handtalk, which is held not to bind, and which lately does.

Finding a black market needs a contact or a Hard (14) Eye + Search roll. Contraband bought there costs one and a half times the listed price, or double if the goods are capital crimes in that land. Selling there pays half the listed price, since the fence carries the risk. A Lack on any black-market roll means the Wakers, the Netwatch, the Bailiff's men or the Echo-Wardens were already watching.

### Haggling

Listed prices are opening prices. When a character wants to argue, the buyer rolls **Tongue + Haggle** opposed by the seller's Tongue + Haggle (or Threat, for minor merchants). The higher total wins. Ties favour the seller; it is their stall.

Price moves in steps of one-tenth of the listed price. The winner shifts the price one step in their favour, plus one step for each Helping (count Helpings from the loser's total, not from a fixed Difficulty). Price cannot fall below half or rise above double the listed price by haggling alone. A Grace for the buyer adds a small thing thrown in (a heel of bread, a name, a warning); a Lack for the buyer means they have offended the seller, who will not deal again today and will tell the next stall.

- **Regional tempers.** Sallowreach merchants have centuries of patience and are at +2 to resist haste. Fatlanders haggle while eating and are at −2 if the buyer refuses to eat with them, +2 if the buyer finishes the plate. Brinehollowers stop mid-haggle to bail and are not to be hurried. Vigil sellers are short-tempered and hallucinating; a buyer who pinches them first is at +1. Cradlewrack sellers knock on the counter before every offer.
- **Haggling in Oathen.** Every offer in Oathen is a promise. A buyer who says "I'll pay six" has sworn it. Oatheners haggle in conditionals ("If six were mentioned, it would not be refused") and a foreigner who does not is at −2 and is also likely to break something. A buyer who hires a licensed price-witness (1 crumb a word, a good one uses twenty) haggles at +2 and swears nothing.
- **Haggling in the Fast.** No price may be accepted until it has been declined three times. A character who forgets is at −4 on any further dealing that day.

A buyer who Partakes and rolls a 1 on the Tooth has let the god show at the counter: the seller sees it, and the price goes up a step for fear, or down two for awe, as fits the god.

## Taint

> Never eat the god twice. Once is history. Twice is a habit.
>> — Purgation catechism

Some goods are made from the gods. Bread from toothed wheat is grown in Ummer's flesh. Bottled brine is the Fathom coughed up by its eaters. A Kept-candle is Ossel's long refusal to finish, rendered into tallow. These goods carry **Taint**: the residue of the god, which goes on working in whoever uses them.

Taint is written as a rating from 1 to 5 and a kind: **Hunger**, **Regrowth**, or **Dread**. An item may carry more than one kind (for example, "Taint 2 Hunger, 1 Dread"). Taint triggers **on use**: once each time the item is eaten or drunk; once per scene it is wielded in anger, or burned, or opened; once per day it is worn against the skin; and once each time a relic's power is called on.

- **Hunger Taint.** The user gains Hunger equal to the rating, exactly as though they had Partaked. No roll. If this takes Hunger to 10, Regrowth rises as normal.
- **Regrowth Taint.** The user makes a Caul + Resolve roll against the matching difficulty: Taint 1 Routine 10, 2 Hard 14, 3 Grim 18, 4 Dire 22, 5 Impossible 26. On success, they gain 1 Hunger. On failure, they gain 1 Regrowth directly. On a Lack, they also gain the item's land's Want for a day, even if it is not their land.
- **Dread Taint.** The user makes a Dread check at the rating's Dread level, because the thing is horrible to use (a candle that whimpers; a loaf that bites back). Repeated use of the same item lowers Dread Taint by 1 after the first five uses, to a minimum of 1. One gets used to things.

**Foreign gods.** Godflesh from a land not your own carries a god you did not eat. Taint from a foreign god is not reduced by any Gift of your own Regrowth, and on a failed Regrowth Taint roll the gained point of Regrowth is marked as foreign: the GM may spend a foreign point at any time to give the character that land's Taste-stage tell for a scene. Characters who mix gods in their blood are the reason the trade in cures never ends and never works.

**The Unfed and the Blank.** A character with Cut 0 ignores Hunger Taint; there is nothing in them to be hungry. Regrowth Taint is worse. Each failed Regrowth Taint roll gives a Cut 0 character one mark of **Seasoning**; at three marks, the meat has found what it was looking for, and the character gains Cut 1 in that god's line, permanently. Tobias Wether and the Reckoner called Scour each have three marks.

**Declaring Taint.** In the Fatlands the law requires godflesh to be declared and stamped. Nowhere else does. A seller who knows the Taint will rarely say so; a Hard (14) Eye + Lore roll identifies the kind and rating of an item's Taint, and a Grim (18) roll identifies which god.

**Purging.** A Purgation emetic (see Drugs) taken within an hour of eating Tainted food removes the Hunger it caused. Nothing removes Regrowth that Taint has given.

## Arms and Armour

> There is no gunpowder on the Table. There are a great many hooks.
>> — Netwatch drill-sergeant's opening remark

Damage is listed in brackets. Attack with melee weapons is Hand + Blades (or Brawl for fists, clubs and grapples). Ranged weapons use Hand + Shooting. Weapons marked with a land are Common there and one step rarer everywhere else.

### Weapon and Armour Qualities

- **Brutal.** Each Helping on a hit adds +3 damage instead of +2.
- **Concealable.** Finding it on a body takes a Hard (14) Eye + Search roll.
- **Entangling.** On a hit with at least one Helping, the target is held instead of taking the Helping's damage: they cannot move away and are at −2 Guard until they spend an action and succeed at a Hard (14) Gut + Athletics roll.
- **Fragile.** On a Lack, the weapon breaks.
- **Heavy.** Needs Gut 3 or more to use without penalty; otherwise −2 Attack. The wielder is at −1 Guard.
- **Hooking.** On a hit with at least one Helping, the attacker may give up the Helping's damage to drag the target one pace, pull them off a ledge or mount, tear away a held item, or knock them prone (the target may resist with a Hard 14 Lung + Athletics roll).
- **Light.** Can be used in the off hand; +1 Attack against a held or prone target.
- **Noisy.** Everyone within a long stone's throw hears it used. In the Vigil, this is considered good manners.
- **Parrying.** +1 Guard against melee attacks while held ready.
- **Piercing.** Ignores 1 point of Armor.
- **Reach.** Strikes from two paces away. A foe closing to arm's length is attacked first.
- **Ripping.** If the hit deals 3 or more damage after Armor, the wound bleeds: the target loses 1 Flesh at the end of each round until someone succeeds at a Routine (10) Hand + Stitching roll. In Sallowreach a Ripping wound never closes without stitching, ever.
- **Silent.** Makes no sound in use; attacks from hiding gain +2.
- **Slow.** Reloading takes a full action.
- **Stunning.** On a hit with a Helping, the target loses their next action unless they succeed at a Hard (14) Gut + Endure roll.
- **Thrown.** Can be thrown up to ten paces with Hand + Shooting.
- **Two-Handed.** Needs both hands.
- **Waking.** A Vigil quality. A creature struck cannot blink, sleep or fall unconscious for the rest of the scene, even at 0 Flesh, and feels everything.
- **Tainted.** The item is made from godflesh and carries Taint as listed (see Taint).

### Addiction

Each time a character uses an addictive drug, they roll Gut + Endure against its Addiction difficulty. On failure, they gain one point of **Need** for that drug. At 1 Need they want it; at 3 Need they are addicted. An addicted character who goes a day without a dose is at −1 to all rolls, and −1 more for each day after, to −4; each day without also adds 1 Fray. Need falls by 1 for each full week without the drug, while suffering. A Lack on the Addiction roll adds 2 Need.

Rouse is special. In the Vigil, nearly everyone is addicted, and the dose rises. A character addicted to rouse must double the dose after each season of use to get the same effect. Dr. Aldous Crane knows where the curve ends. It is eleven years off, and a character who works the arithmetic for themselves (Grim 18, Eye + Reckoning) takes a Dread 2 check.

## Relics and Godflesh

> Every land believes another land's curse might cure its own. Nothing has ever worked. The trade never slows.
>> — The Rim Road, in a sentence

Relics are pieces of the Providers, or of people far enough into the Regrowth that the difference no longer matters. They are never Common. Most are illegal somewhere. All are Tainted. The prices given are what a dealer would ask, if a dealer could be found who was willing to sell.

#### A Hand-Line Finger
**Cost:** 1,200 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 2 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 2
A grey finger with nine joints, taken from a Hand-line noble in the Course, still cold. Touched to a lock, a lid or a door, it closes it, and nothing opens it again without breaking it. Touched to a candle, the candle finishes. Touched to a person outside Sallowreach, it does nothing, and the wielder takes a Dread 2 check from the way it flexes. Capital crime in Sallowreach. The Mortuary Court counts its nobles' fingers.

#### A Bottled Hush
**Cost:** 2,500 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** Dread 4
A stoppered jar of air from the edge of Dunmere Hush. Unstoppered, it makes a silence ten paces across for one minute: no sound carries, flames go out, flies drop. Any Kept inside it finishes, completely and properly. The Finishers will kill for one. The Court calls every jar a stolen license. The jar is always slightly too heavy.

#### A Biting Loaf
**Cost:** 20 L · **Avail.:** Scarce · **Taint:** 1 Hunger, 1 Dread
Toothed-wheat bread baked with the teeth left in. Set on a windowsill or in a doorway, it bites anyone who enters uninvited (attack +4, damage 1, Ripping). Granny Cracknel bakes them for widows. After a week, the loaf has grown a second row.

#### A Jar of Tenanted Brine
**Cost:** 400 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 3 Regrowth (Dromm)
Water coughed at dawn by one of the Tenanted. Held to the ear, it can be heard talking, slowly, in a voice like a hull settling. A character who listens for an hour may ask one question about the sea, the Trench, or the Tenant, and is answered truthfully. They must then pass a Grim (18) Caul + Resolve roll or walk west the next night.

#### A Lash from the Eye of Iss
**Cost:** not for sale (offers start at 10,000 L) · **Avail.:** Unique · **Taint:** 4 Regrowth (Iss)
A black hair a yard long, stiff as wire, one of the few that survived the Eye-lines' meal. Laid across a sleeper's lids in the Vigil, it lets them sleep and wake again, once, safely. The eye in the long room will have seen them. The Warden-Prime knows where every lash is, and there are three.

#### A Caul-Scrap Veil
**Cost:** 600 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 2 Regrowth (Vey)
A square of membrane cut from a Caul-line noble, stitched to a hat-brim. Through it the wearer sees a few heartbeats ahead, blurred: once per scene, reroll any one die. Each day it is worn, roll a d10; on a 1, the veil has grown into the skin of the brow. Duchess Morwen's veil has lately gone red. So have the scraps.

#### A Sayer's Word Sealed in Wax
**Cost:** not for sale (20,000 L if it were) · **Avail.:** Unique · **Taint:** Dread 3
A ball of gold wax the size of a walnut, sealed by the Bench of Clauses, containing a single word spoken by a Sayer into it before the bit went back in. Crack it, and the word is so. Known words in private hands: *Stop*, *Shut*, *Mend*, *Mine*. Nobody knows the scope of any of them. One is rumoured to say *Enough*.

#### An Orrum Crumb
**Cost:** 150 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** Dread 2; see text
A crumb of bread from one of the Host's tables, picked up off the cloth by a Tablebreaker before the axe fell. While carried, the holder's Hunger cannot rise above 5 and they never feel hungry. If it is ever eaten, the character is a guest, and must pass an Impossible (26) Caul + Resolve roll or sit at the next table they see, forever. It smells of fresh bread for six hundred years.

#### A Place Card Bearing Your Name
**Cost:** not for sale · **Avail.:** Unique · **Taint:** Dread 3
A stiff cream card, a fine hand, your name spelled correctly. Nobody sells these; they are found. While a character keeps it, every host on the Table must offer them a seat (+4 Persuade for hospitality), and wherever they sleep, a table is laid by morning. Burning it does nothing. It is on the next table.

#### Granny Cracknel's Matched Teeth
**Cost:** 80 L a set · **Avail.:** Scarce · **Taint:** 1 Hunger, 2 Dread
A full set of teeth milled from toothed wheat, matched by Granny Cracknel to someone she knew. Held in the mouth for a night, they give the holder that person's last meal and last sight in a dream. Pell Hogget has bought eleven sets from Low Tilth. The last sight is always the same field at dusk.

#### Ilse Marl's Chart
**Cost:** not for sale · **Avail.:** Unique · **Taint:** Dread 3
Forty years of transcriptions of the Tenanted's low-water song, laid end to end: a chart of the Uncovered, with depths no sea has ever had there. A navigator using it crosses the Uncovered without rolling and can find the Trench and a stone table with eight chairs. Studying it for a week allows a character to hum the song; anyone Tenanted who hears them stops and turns.

#### A Shard of Hallowboard
**Cost:** 3,000 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** Dread 2
A hand-sized fragment of the eight-sided stone table of the Refusal, always clean, always faintly warm. No table of Orrum's can be laid within a hundred paces of it. The Abstinent say Hallowboard has never been chipped; therefore this is a fake, or Hallowboard is lying. A Reckoner who sees it in Godeater hands will kill to return it.

#### A First Lack
**Cost:** 1,000 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 1 Regrowth (one god, by grain)
A coin cut from a Provider's own bone in the first year after the Gorging, cured in no salt at all. Worn on a cord, it gives +1 to Godsign rolls involving its god. Spent, it gives the merchant who takes it that god's Taste for a month. They circulate anyway; somebody always mistakes one for change.

#### Ossel's Knucklebones
**Cost:** 2,000 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 2 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 1
Five dice cut from a joint of the Closing Hand. Thrown for a question about when something will end, they answer truly. Thrown for a person, they show the number of years before their death; in Sallowreach they always roll blank.

#### Dromm's Ear-Stone
**Cost:** 700 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 2 Regrowth (Dromm)
A pale stone from the Fathom's inner ear, hauled up at Longslab. Its holder always knows which way the sea is and how far, and feels pressure days a full day early. In the last year it has begun to point down.

#### A Dowager Clod
**Cost:** 250 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 2 Hunger
A clod of warm earth from the Dowager Hills, cut from a Seated Gut-line matriarch. Planted, anything grows from it overnight, plump and red-veined. If the matriarch is consulted first, she may agree. If not, the crop has her opinions.

#### A Thief's Kept Hand
**Cost:** 120 L · **Avail.:** Scarce · **Taint:** Dread 2
Taken by Marrow Jack from a Kept pickpocket who was asleep on a shelf. Worn in a glove harness, it gives +2 Filch. Its owner, still in Lastgate, can feel everything it does, and is trying to find you.

#### A Dormitory Pillow
**Cost:** 1,500 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 3 Regrowth (Iss)
Smuggled out from under one of the Counted. A Dream-Diver who sleeps on it goes down and returns without a tether, once per dive, for an hour. Owning it in the Vigil is a capital crime. It is still warm, and it still smells of someone.

#### Bed One's Words
**Cost:** not for sale · **Avail.:** Unique · **Taint:** Dread 4
Matron Cecily Dorm's notebook of what Aveline Morne's lips have shaped. Most of it is a baker's daughter's recipe for bread. The last page is a list of names, and they are being crossed off in an order the Matron cannot see the reason for. One is a player character's.

#### A Living Part in a Jar
**Cost:** 200 L · **Avail.:** Scarce · **Taint:** 2 Regrowth (Vey), 1 Dread
A Cradlewrack Part, healthy and warm: an ear, a hand, a length of spine. It turns in the jar toward other Parts and toward the Assemblers' Barn, a compass to the figure. The Assemblers pay double. The Attendance seizes it and the buyer.

#### A Yard of Living Cord
**Cost:** 300 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** 2 Regrowth (Vey)
Cut, with permission, from one of the Uncut. Tie it between two people and for a day they share damage: each hit is split between them, and so is every Dread check. The cord pulses. It wants to grow back to the one it came from.

#### A Sayer's Gold Bit
**Cost:** 5,000 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** none
Garl Tome's work, worn and then outgrown by a Sayer. Fitted to any mouth, it prevents speech from binding: Oathen's rule does not touch the wearer, nor does a Sayer's word they hear. The wearer cannot eat or speak clearly. The Bench wants all of them back.

#### An Echo-Stone of Sworn Gorge
**Cost:** 50 L · **Avail.:** Scarce · **Taint:** Dread 3
A pebble from Sworn Gorge that has soaked up the echo. Held to the ear, it repeats the oath of 419 A.G., too faint to understand. Anyone who listens for a full minute is bound by it. Nobody knows what they have sworn. Halvar Stane buys them to grind into sand.

#### A Puddle of Old Wet Tom's
**Cost:** 40 L · **Avail.:** Scarce · **Taint:** 1 Regrowth (Dromm)
A corked flask of water that followed Old Wet Tom down a corridor. Poured out, it makes a puddle much deeper than the floor. Things can be dropped into it and are gone. Something in it can be heard, very far down, asking for the cork back.

#### Wren Hollowell's Hair
**Cost:** 900 L · **Avail.:** Rare · **Taint:** none
A lock of the Fasting Girl's hair, sold by someone who got close. A Blank relic: worn at the throat, it lets the wearer ignore Hunger Taint and gain no Hunger from the first Partake each day. Owning it is proof of having dealt with the Blank trade. The Thin kill for it, the Board pays for it, and the girl wants it back.

#### A Dunmere Bird
**Cost:** 60 L · **Avail.:** Scarce · **Taint:** Dread 1
A sparrow that fell at the edge of Dunmere Hush, perfectly dead and perfectly whole, uncorrupting. In Sallowreach it is the only finished thing most people have ever seen, and collectors keep them under glass. Kept who hold one weep without moisture.

#### The Scarecrow's Straw
**Cost:** 30 L · **Avail.:** Scarce · **Taint:** 2 Hunger, 2 Dread
A handful of straw from the Scarecrow of Low Tilth, taken at night by a dare. Scattered in a field, it shows on the next dusk which person will be Reaped there. Held in a pocket, it tells the land where you are.

## Merchants of the Table

> Never buy from a merchant who says the grace first. He wants something.
>> — Rim Road advice

These dealers can be dropped into any market scene. Each has a specialty: items in that line are one step more available from them, and they will buy such goods at three-quarters of the listed price instead of half.

[quick] Mardy Haunch — Threat 3 · Flesh 14 · Guard 10 · Attack +3 (cleaver 3, Ripping) · Armor 1 (suet) · Dread 0
**Stock:** Fatlands meat, toothed bread, Chute barrels, marrow-brandy. She has the widest stall on the Rim and has never once stood up behind it. Everything she sells is settling. She buys tattooed casings at a premium and will not say who for; it is Hob Gristle, who is trying to give them back to their families.

#### Abelard Myrrh, Perfumer of the Lower Pilings
[quick] Abelard Myrrh (Kept) — Threat 4 · Flesh 9 · Guard 9 · Attack +2 (awl 2) · Armor 0 · Dread 1
**Stock:** cedar, vinegar, smoke-oils, Kept gauze, Seamer's lacquer. His own nose rotted off in 340 A.G. and he blends entirely by memory, which is perfect. For 40 L he will make a scent from a dead person's clothes that convinces any Kept who smells it that they are home and loved. The Court has asked him to stop selling it to the Lofts. He has not.

#### Nan Pickering, Limb-Broker
[quick] Nan Pickering — Threat 3 · Flesh 11 · Guard 11 · Attack +4 (shelving hook 3, Hooking) · Armor 1 · Dread 2 (her drying-racks)
**Stock:** Kept limbs, seamed parts, Thief's Kept Hands, Seamer's kits. She buys from Marrow Jack and sells to back-alley seamers in Lastgate's undercity. Every limb on her racks is labelled with its owner's name, because she is honest. Some of the owners come to visit. She charges them admission.

#### Mags Weir, Salvage Queen of the Uncovered
[quick] Mags Weir — Threat 4 · Flesh 13 · Guard 12 · Attack +5 (harpoon 4, Hooking) · Armor 1 (oilskin) · Dread 0
**Stock:** wreck goods, salvage-tins, Netwatch gear, Dromm's Ear-Stones, Tenanted brine. She sells from a beached hulk two miles out on dry seabed. Her newest salvage came from a wreck that sank before the Gorging: a crate of silverware in services of eight. She has sold seven settings. The eighth keeps coming back to the crate.

#### Lucan Spill, the Oilman
[quick] Lucan Spill — Threat 4 · Flesh 12 · Guard 11 · Attack +4 (sword 4) · Armor 2 · Dread 0
**Stock:** lamp oil by the cask, mirror-lanterns, lamp-flasks. He is the supplier who doubled the price to the Vigil, and Garron Ashlock would kill him if killing him would not stop the oil. His new oil burns brighter and smells of low tide. It is rendered on the Uncovered from things with too many ribs, and the lamps that burn it have begun to drip seawater.

#### Dovey Pinch, Pin-Seller of the Slope

**Stock:** pins, pin-gloves, Waker's needles, rouse-tabs, hired nudgers by the hour. Seventy, sharp-eyed and pricked all over, she sells pain as a convenience. Her needles are recovered from dead Wakers, and each still remembers the last person it kept awake; if you stick yourself with one, you see their face.

#### Tamsin Keyless, Lock-Pedlar

**Stock:** locks, bolts, bricks, mortar, knocker's rings, counterfeit Agnes Latch locks. Her copies hold for a night, sometimes two. [quick] Ruth Ninefold — Threat 5 · Flesh 10 · Guard 10 · Attack +2 (knife 2) · Armor 0 · Dread 0
**Stock:** naming-rights, Tally extracts, Attendance birth-fees on credit, living Parts in jars. She owns the rights to name two hundred births. Several were taken to the Lying-In. One was bought from a clay-digger at the rim of the Dilation, and its year is up at the next pang.
