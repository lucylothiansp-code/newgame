# Threads Between the Lands

> Every land is certain that the cure is next door.
>> — Rim Road saying

The lands of the Table are not separate stories. They share a road, a coin, a grace and a doom, and the crises in each are beginning to touch. This chapter takes the open storylines that cross borders and lays them out as campaign threads: who is involved in each land, how the thread escalates if nobody interferes, what player characters can do about it, and how it might end. Most of the endings are bad. That is not a failure of imagination; it is the setting. A thread resolved well is a thread that ends one way rather than a worse way.

Each thread is written in stages. Advance a thread one stage whenever the characters spend a season elsewhere, or whenever the GM wishes the world to feel the weight of the clock. Threads touch: the Sayer's Sentence feeds the Deadwatch, the Blank Trade feeds the Second Table, and almost everything feeds Eight Chairs.

[box: Running Threads]
Pick two or three threads to run in the background of a campaign and let the others exist only as rumors. Keep a simple track for each, Stage 1 to Stage 4, on a card the players never see. When a thread reaches Stage 4 it resolves, with or without the characters, and the world changes. Characters who intervene can hold a thread at its current stage for a season, push it back one stage at great cost, or steer which ending it reaches. They cannot make it not have happened.
[/box]

## The Deadwatch

**Summary.** The Vigil cannot sleep and is running out of everything: beds, rouse, oil and time. Its Warden-Prime has sent Envoy Castellan Dray to Lastgate to buy the secret of Keeping from Regent Ansel Grue: if the Vigil's citizens could be killed and kept upright, a city of the dead would never need to sleep. The flaw is geography. The Kept cannot leave Sallowreach. If the plan goes ahead, the Vigil will have to move there.

**The Players.**
- *The Vigil:* Envoy Castellan Dray; Warden-Prime Ilvane Stroud; Dr. Aldous Crane, whose dose curve gives the Vigil eleven years; Benedikt Lour, already drawing plans for the whole population.
- *Sallowreach:* Regent Ansel Grue, who is tempted by a hundred thousand new taxable dead; Lady Corrow Vane, who would have to Close nobody and keep everyone; the Heirs, who see a new population of the never-dying arriving to sit on top of them forever; Tam Ruddock, who already knows how to move people across the Drop.
- *The Rim:* the Company, which would carry the migration and price it; Fogmouth, where the line is painted.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Dray negotiates in Lastgate. Small trials: Vigil volunteers are brought north, killed in Sallowreach, and kept. They do not sleep. It works. |
| 2 | The Watch votes in secret to relocate. Caravans of Vigil citizens, mostly the poor of the Slope, begin moving west along the Rim under Company contract. |
| 3 | The first mass Killing at Fogmouth: thousands of Vigil folk die north of the Drop and stand back up. The Heirs riot. The Mortuary Court demands a toll per head. |
| 4 | Lastgate's lower levels fill with sleepless dead foreigners. The Vigil's lamps begin to go out, one district at a time, as the city empties into the fen. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Escort or sabotage the trial caravans. Expose to the Vigil's poor what Keeping actually feels like (Kept bodies rot and feel it for centuries). Uncover the Court's hidden reason for agreeing: the Court believes new dead without Hand-line blood may slow the Hushes. Steal Dray's commission, which the Warden-Prime would rather die than see read aloud.

**Endings.**
- *The Dead City:* the Vigil moves north and becomes a district of Lastgate, a hundred thousand rotting, wakeful foreigners. The Dormitory is abandoned with its Counted inside, unguarded, and the lamps go out over sixty thousand beds.
- *The Broken Deal:* the Heirs drag Dray to a Hush; the Vigil, betrayed, closes the Rim at Lampwick Halt; Sallowreach loses its rouse and oil, and Lastgate's candle-makers turn to the only fuel left.
- *The Worst Arithmetic:* a Vigil sleeper is brought north, unconscious, and killed in Sallowreach. They do not wake and do not die. They lie there, Kept and Counted at once, and the Dormitory's number goes up by one.

## The Host on the Road

**Summary.** For six centuries Orrum kept to the Fast. Now its tables have been found laid at crossroads in three other lands, and the seemers of the Vigil, the scavengers of Brinehollow and the pilgrims of the Fatlands have all, separately, seen the same tall figure laying a table. That figure is Welcome Ardent, the Host's steward, walking a mile ahead of his god, and he is working his way around the Rim.

**The Players.**
- *The Fast:* Welcome Ardent, polite and exhausted; Spare Tolland of the Hostwatch, mapping the warm ground; Decline Marsh and the Tablebreakers; Envoy Prudence Lowell.
- *The Vigil:* Mirela Sconce, who recorded eleven districts hallucinating a table being laid in the street.
- *The Fatlands:* Osanna Blythe and her three hundred pilgrims, hungry every second of their lives, looking for a table where they will finally feel full.
- *Brinehollow:* salvagers on the Uncovered who have found tables laid on the dry seabed with the plates still wet.
- *The Rim:* the wardens at Saltlick Cross; every innkeeper who has seen a tall shape stoop to look in at the window.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Tables appear at Rim crossroads, one a season. Tablebreakers cross the Hem to smash them. Place cards bear the names of people who live nearby. |
| 2 | Welcome Ardent is seen in person at a Rim inn. He asks, politely, whether anyone would take over his duties. The first non-Unfed sit down: a Fatlands family, a Vigil nudger, a Kept man a yard north of the Drop at Fogmouth, who now cannot leave the table or the fen. |
| 3 | Orrum itself is seen on the Rim at night, stooping to look in at windows. Whole villages along the Fatlands border are found Seated. Osanna Blythe's pilgrims find their table. |
| 4 | Tables are laid in every capital on the same night, with a place card for every head of state. In each, one chair is left without a card. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Track Ardent and talk to him (he is the only one of Orrum's guests who can still get up, and he will answer questions honestly if he is not invited to sit). Smash tables, at the risk of finding their own names. Protect or stop the pilgrims. Discover that the cardless chair at each table faces away from the others.

**Endings.**
- *The Long Table Grows:* the Seated spread along the Rim, a second endless table around the whole continent, happy, wasting, praising the food. Trade stops because the road is full of diners.
- *The Relief:* a player character accepts Ardent's position. Ardent finally sits down and eats, and dies in moments, weeping with gratitude. The character now walks a mile ahead of the god forever, and knows exactly whom the tables are for.
- *The Unanswered Door:* the tables stop, all at once. Orrum stands still at Hallowboard facing the eighth side. The Hostwatch reports that it has never looked so frightened.

## Brine in the Rouse

**Summary.** Captain Jonah Skerry, called Dry Jonah, bottles what Brinehollowers cough up at bailing and sells it inland as tonic. It sells best in the Vigil, where the Slope dealer Rook Quarter-Bell cuts it into rouse because it is cheap and it works. The sleepless are now feeling the Calling, four hundred miles from any coast. Customers stay awake. They have also started walking west.

**The Players.**
- *Brinehollow:* Dry Jonah; Tolly Brack, who knows by the color of bailing water who will be Called next; the Netwatch, who are going to see Called strangers arrive from the east.
- *The Vigil:* Rook Quarter-Bell; Dr. Aldous Crane, desperate for anything that stretches the dose; the Wakers; Inspector Hale Grimmer, whose blink-murders begin to cluster on the western edge of the city.
- *The Rim:* Lampwick Halt and Lowmark Spur, the two ends of the walk.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | The Slope's addicts stand at west windows. Dr. Crane notices that brine-cut rouse holds an hour longer and does not report it. |
| 2 | Vigil citizens begin walking west across the plateau in their sleep-that-is-not-sleep, eyes open, straight lines. The Wakers net them at the city edge. |
| 3 | Crane formulates brine into the official rouse. The whole Vigil now carries a little of the sea. Lines of walkers cross the Rim heading for a coast they have never seen. |
| 4 | The first Vigil walkers reach the Uncovered and keep going, out toward the Trench. Some come back Tenanted: calm, courteous, rested. They are the first people in the Vigil in three centuries who sleep and wake again. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Trace the supply chain from bailing buckets in Lowmark to dealers on the Slope. Kill or bargain with Dry Jonah. Shepherd walkers home or follow them to see where they go. Present the Watch with the choice: brine buys the Vigil years of wakefulness at the cost of the sea pulling on every citizen.

**Endings.**
- *The Walking City:* the Vigil adopts brine-rouse openly and loses a tenth of its people a year to the west. The Watch calls it emigration.
- *The Tenanted Wardens:* Tenanted Vigil-folk, rested and calm, are found to be immune to the Dormitory's dream. The Watch begins appointing them to office, as Brinehollow did with its judges. Nobody asks whose eyes are looking out.
- *The Meeting of Waters:* the Trench, the draining sea and the long dim room in the Vigil's dream turn out to be closer than anyone thought. Senna Vole, on her forty-first dive, hears water.

## The First to Eat

**Summary.** Tobias Wether, a runaway raised on six centuries of refusal, crossed into the Fatlands and ate. The Reckoner called Scour spent a year undercover in the Fatlands, had to eat to keep his cover, and came home with godsign. Two Unfed bodies now carry a god that was never theirs, and the meat inside them seems to be looking for something.

**The Players.**
- *The Fast:* Scour, hiding his godsign under his sleeves and volunteering for every raid; Reckoner-Captain Scant Hollis; the Abstinent, for whom this is the worst possible news; Meek Garrity, whose research says the Unfed never refused at all.
- *The Fatlands:* Tobias Wether; Corder Lank of the Thin, who is very interested in a man who came from emptiness into hunger; Brother Glut of the Second Helping, who sees a convert.
- *Cross-border:* Odo Venn of the College, who wants to taste them; the Second Table, who want to know whether an Unfed stomach makes room for a god.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Tobias shows his first godsign. It is not purely Ummer's: he dreams of a long dim room and closes doors behind him. Scour's signs are similar. |
| 2 | The College learns of them. Unfed blood with godsign is, by the ledgers, impossible. Odo Venn tastes Tobias and goes pale and does not report the flavor. |
| 3 | Scour's comrades find out. The Abstinent strike his name from the Pantry. He flees to the Rim, where the Second Table is waiting. |
| 4 | Tobias and Scour, kept apart, begin to show identical signs at the same hour. Neither carries any one god. Both are getting hungrier for the same thing, and when they meet, they set a table. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Protect them, hide them, study them or kill them. Bring Tobias back into the Fast and see what happens to the Lack around him. Learn, from Venn's private notes, that what grows in an Unfed body is not the god the body ate.

**Endings.**
- *The Empty Vessel:* the Second Table buys Scour and serves him, as a test. The diners all sleep badly afterward, and dream of a room where someone is laying a table.
- *The Lack Breaks:* Tobias returns to the Fast and plants. The dead soil sprouts where he walks. The Abstinent call it a miracle and then realize what it means: the heartland can be fed if its people eat. Wont Bellamy wins the next vote.
- *Two Places Laid:* Tobias and Scour vanish from their guarded rooms on the same night. Two Orrum tables are found the next morning at the Rim, with their names on the cards, and the chairs pushed in.

## The Blank Trade

**Summary.** Wren Hollowell in the Fatlands, Cassian Wry in Oathen and the entire population of the Fast are worth more in parts than whole. Blank blood, marrow and organs are believed to slow the Regrowth. Dr. Mercy Thrane of the Fast is following the buyers, and the trail leads to the Second Table.

**The Players.**
- *The Fast:* Dr. Mercy Thrane; Marshal Keep Aldane, holding the border against poachers; every Unfed child sent to fetch water at dusk.
- *The Fatlands:* Kale Dragoman, poacher; Wren Hollowell, with a bounty on her weight that does not specify alive; the Board of Plenty.
- *Oathen:* Cassian Wry, the crown's Blank spy, kept in luxury and never let out of sight; the Bench of Clauses, which has received an offer.
- *Cross-border:* Master Quillon Barr and Lady Seraphine Dalgety of the Second Table; the Rim Road Company, whose wagons carry sealed brine-barrels it does not open.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Unfed disappearances double along the eastern border. Thrane finds a surgery in Sated with an Unfed man's name scratched inside a cupboard. |
| 2 | Wren Hollowell is taken. A Fatlands Gut-line matriarch is seen at the Weighing ten stone lighter and, for the first time in thirty years, not eating. |
| 3 | Oathen's Bench agrees, in a forty-page conditional, to lease Cassian Wry's kidney to a foreign buyer. The surgery is scheduled at Treaty Stair. |
| 4 | Thrane traces the trail to Master Barr's surgery, and finds Abide, nine years a donor, fat and smiling, and the eighth course on a menu pinned to the wall, still blank. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Raid a surgery. Escort Wren out of the Fatlands, which will put every bounty-hunter in the south on their trail. Free Cassian Wry, who has been kept all his life and does not know how to live unwatched. Follow the money to the Rim Road Company's sealed barrels and make the Company choose between its neutrality and its conscience. If a player character is a Blank, this thread is about them.

**Endings.**
- *The Market Holds:* the trade goes on, and prices rise. The Fast begins to sell its own people to buy grain, and calls it Pantry policy.
- *The Exposed Table:* the trail is published. In every capital, a few High Cuts are revealed to be carrying Unfed organs, and the Reckoners have a new list.
- *Room Made:* Barr's patients begin, one by one, to feel empty inside, in exactly the places where a Blank organ was sewn in. They become very hungry. They do not know for what.

## The Missing Hand

**Summary.** In a guarded barn in Cradlewrack, Dov Marrin and the Assemblers have fitted four hundred Parts from four hundred families into one figure eleven feet long. It breathes. It is missing a left hand and a voice. Hester Lowe bore a left hand and is hiding it. Juniper Dell was born saying half a sentence. Every Part, the Assemblers say, belongs to one body born piecemeal; and the figure has begun to turn its head toward the Sill.

**The Players.**
- *Cradlewrack:* Dov Marrin; Hester Lowe, who will kill for the hand; Juniper Dell, called Firstword; Ambrose the Eldest Infant, who claims to remember being Vey's left eye; Old Hinge at the Sill; Dr. Lazar Voss of the Vigil, who has drawn the figure and counted more than two of several things.
- *Sallowreach:* Marrow Jack, the part-thief, who has been buying Cradlewrack Parts on the Rim; Sister Tisane Halloway's jar of grey hinged joints, which fit together.
- *The Rim:* Petra Quick, who smuggled a Part across the border without knowing it.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | The Assemblers hire outsiders to find the hand. Hester moves house every night. The hand has begun to point. |
| 2 | Juniper Dell meets someone, somewhere, who says the other half of her sentence. Nobody hears it but them. |
| 3 | The hand is taken, or given. The figure's left arm is complete. It sits up. The pangs fall to seven days. |
| 4 | Juniper is brought to the barn. She speaks her half and the figure speaks the other half. The Sill door swings fully open for the first time since the Gorging. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Protect Hester and the hand. Find the person with the other half of the sentence before the Assemblers do. Burn the barn (the figure will not burn easily; it will begin again out of the ashes). Learn from Dr. Voss's drawing that the figure is not Vey alone: some of its Parts came from outside Cradlewrack, and some belong to no god anyone has named.

**Endings.**
- *The Firstborn:* the figure stands, walks to the Sill and goes through. Whatever was describing the view to Old Hinge comes back through the other way.
- *The Hand Withheld:* Hester refuses to the last and is killed; the hand crawls away into the hills and is never found. The figure lies incomplete and breathing, and Cradlewrack's pangs continue to shorten anyway. It was never the figure the land was in labor with.
- *The Sentence:* the full sentence is spoken aloud once. Everyone who hears it understands exactly what is being born out of the ground, and when. Nobody who hears it can be persuaded to say it again.

## The Sayer's Sentence

**Summary.** Every land with a failing cure has thought of the same thing: go to Oathen, and have Lady Imre Dath, the Sayer, say that it is fixed. What a Sayer says, is. Nobody knows what Tolm would make true if she did. The Bench of Clauses is debating, in forty-page conditionals, whether to take out her golden bit; and she has begun to write her own sentences.

**The Players.**
- *Oathen:* Lady Imre Dath; Sorrin Vael, Chief Clausewright, who would draft the sentence; Ket the Ninth, who must nod; Garl Tome, who made the gold bit; Yusra Thole, Rainwarden, whose silence about the late rain is being noticed.
- *Every land:* envoys from the Vigil (*Let the Vigil sleep and wake*), Brinehollow (*Let the sea return*), Sallowreach (*Let the dead finish*), the Fatlands (*Let the hungry be full*), Cradlewrack (*Let nothing more be born wrong*).
- *Cross-border:* the Rim Road Company, which needs the Kept Rain kept; the Second Table, which wants a single sentence of its own: *Let the second meal hold.*

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Envoys arrive in Tacit and bid. The Bench begins drafting. Lady Imre is caught with chalk and a wall full of her own sentences. |
| 2 | An envoy, impatient, bribes a guard. Someone gets into the Gilded Cells with a question. The Sayer answers. A valley in Oathen changes overnight. |
| 3 | The Bench agrees on a text, four hundred conditions long, to be spoken at the next unmuzzling. Every faction on the Table tries to alter one clause. |
| 4 | The bit comes out. She speaks. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Carry an envoy's bid, or forge one. Guard the Cells or break into them. Edit the sentence, one clause at a time, through bribes, threats and Clausewrights. Read Lady Imre's own wall, where she has written, over and over, a sentence she has never been allowed to say. Learn that she has never once been asked what she wants.

**Endings.**
- *It Is Fixed:* she says the Bench's sentence. Every Regrowth stops dead where it stands. So does every other change, of any kind. See the Secrets chapter.
- *Her Own Sentence:* she says what she has been writing. It is short. It is about her. The god in her gets what it wants.
- *The Slip:* the sentence is spoken and does not take. A god's word has slipped again, worse than the rain. Every oath in Oathen loosens by a hair, and every Forsworn body in the Breakdowns begins, slowly, to heal, and every sworn contract on the Rim begins to fail.

## Eight Chairs

**Summary.** Cray Lobbett found a stone table on the seabed of the Uncovered, older than the Gorging, with eight chairs, seven pushed in. Hallowboard, at the center of the world, has eight sides. Grandfather Peat, a bog body hauled from the Sump, is the last witness to the Night of Seven Tables and says the god was smiling. Meek Garrity has found the seating plan for Hallowboard. Someone is going to put these together. This is the thread that leads to the Secrets chapter, and it should be the spine of any long campaign.

**The Players.**
- *Brinehollow:* Cray Lobbett, who cannot stop going back; Hask Tarn, the only pilot who knows the ground; Ilse Marl, whose transcriptions of the Tenanted's singing make a chart.
- *Sallowreach:* Grandfather Peat; Old Bettany Shroud, whose forty thousand shelved dead all whisper the same word.
- *The Fast:* Meek Garrity and his plan; Welcome Ardent; Verger Sober Osmond at the Empty Chair.
- *Cross-border:* Archprovost Hesper Lune of the College, who has been reconstructing the chiseled figure; Envoy Prudence Lowell, who will do anything to keep Garrity quiet; the Second Table, who want the eighth line of their menu filled.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Garrity's plan leaks. The Abstinent call it forgery. Lobbett tries to sell the table's location again and finds a buyer who pays in Company platters. |
| 2 | Someone brings a translator to Grandfather Peat. He describes the Night in full. He describes the bank behind the god, and what was on it. |
| 3 | Rubbings from the seabed table are compared with Hallowboard and with Lune's reconstruction. They match. The eighth chair on the seabed is the only one pulled out, and it faces the same way as the eighth side of Hallowboard. |
| 4 | The word Bettany Shroud's dead whisper is the older word for *guest*. It is said aloud, in full, by someone who has put it together. Everything that has been hiding hears it. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Everything, and that is the danger. Dive to the table. Interrogate Peat. Steal Garrity's papers. Visit Hallowboard and count the sides. Each discovery should cost Fray. The right questions in the right order lead, inevitably, to the Secrets chapter's answers, and the answers are not survivable in any comfortable sense.

**Endings.**
- *Counted:* the chairs are counted aloud. It does not matter by whom. The eighth chair, on the seabed, at Hallowboard, at every inn on the Rim, turns from the wall to face the table.
- *The Silence Kept:* the characters destroy the evidence and kill or silence those who know. The world goes on not knowing for a few more years. The characters do not get to.
- *The Place Card:* at the end of a long investigation, a character finds a table laid for them, with a card in the Host's handwriting, and a second card, in another hand, at the chair beside them.

## The Second Table

**Summary.** In every capital, a few of the highest Cuts are sharpening knives for a meal they intend to eat twice. When the gods finish regrowing, they will eat them again, a few diners instead of a mob, and inherit another six centuries of grace. Wont Bellamy, a heretic on the Fast's own council, wants the Unfed to join them, starting with the one god that is still whole.

**The Players.**
- *Every land:* the Carvers: Lord Absalom Thrale (Grand Carver, Sallowreach); Lady Seraphine Dalgety (the Vigil); Master Quillon Barr (surgeon); and in every capital two or three Trenchermen of the highest Cut, some of them named in land chapters and never suspected.
- *The Fast:* Wont Bellamy, who argues the Refusal was only a postponement.
- *Cross-border:* Lysander Coyle of the Company, selling them the price of the world; Silas Fenwright, whose stolen ledgers would tell them which gods are ripe.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Practice dinners. Characters of standing are invited to one. The dish is not named until afterward. |
| 2 | The Table commissions its knives: eight, of grey bone, from a Sallowreach bone-carver who is found Closed afterward. |
| 3 | Wont Bellamy carries a motion before the Abstinent: the Unfed should do now what the others did then. Orrum is seen, for the first time, standing still while the Hostwatch approaches, as if waiting. |
| 4 | The Second Table gathers at Hallowboard on Tablenight, all of them, around the eight-sided table, with knives, and the Host lies down. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Infiltrate a dinner and finish the plate. Expose a Carver. Steal a knife. Warn the Fast, which may not believe them. Discover that the Head of the Table presides from an empty chair, and that nobody at the dinners has ever seen anyone sit in it.

**Endings.**
- *The Second Meal:* they eat the Host. See the Secrets chapter for what this does. It is the worst thing anyone has ever done.
- *The Table Turned:* the characters expose the cult; the Reckoners and the Purgation do the rest. Every High Cut in the world is now suspect, and the Lancings begin on a scale not seen since Brookhythe.
- *Served:* at the final dinner, the Carvers discover that they are not the diners. The empty chair is occupied.

## The Undying Flame

**Summary.** *A new thread.* The Vigil has ninety days of oil. Lamplighter-General Garron Ashlock has a supplier who doubled his price and a standing order to tell no one. In Sallowreach, fires smolder for decades and nothing can finish, and Wick Tallow renders the unclaimed dead into candles that feel themselves burn and give a very steady light. A Sallowreach flame does not go out. The Vigil has noticed.

**The Players.**
- *The Vigil:* Garron Ashlock; Benedikt Lour; the Lamplighters' Guild.
- *Sallowreach:* Wick Tallow, the Chandler; Regent Ansel Grue, who would sell the unclaimed dead by the cartload; the Loft-Wardens, whose forty thousand shelved dead are also, technically, unclaimed.
- *The Fatlands:* Mistress Brisket Annalow of the Renderers' Union, who supplied the Vigil's tallow and is being undercut; Quartermaster Ivo Stockpot, who has noticed what goes into the Vigil's barrels.
- *The Rim:* the Company, which must decide whether candles of people are freight or Hush-toll.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Ashlock buys a crate of Tallow's candles in secret. They burn for a month without shortening. He weeps with relief. |
| 2 | A contract is sworn in Oathen. Wagon trains of Kept-candles go east along the Rim. The Lofts begin to empty. Bettany Shroud reports her shelved dead are being taken in the night. |
| 3 | The Vigil's street-lamps are lit with people. Citizens can hear them at night, a faint high keening from every bracket. The Seeming begins to show faces in the flames. |
| 4 | A candle is carried out of Sallowreach and should, by the Rule, finish at the Drop. It does not. It keeps burning. Something has changed about what can end, and where. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Guard or hijack a candle train. Speak to a candle (they can hear and, with care, be made to answer). Help Ashlock find another way, or help him justify this one. Bring the Loft-Wardens' witnesses into it: the Set-Aside are aware, and they have opinions about being burned.

**Endings.**
- *Light Eternal:* the Vigil lives in a permanent glow of its neighbors' dead, and Sallowreach is paid in rouse and mirrors. Nobody in either city sleeps well.
- *The Lofts Rise:* the Set-Aside, learning what is planned, refuse. Forty thousand dead begin to whisper the same word louder. The Loft-Wardens open the doors.
- *The Finished Light:* the candles all go out at once, everywhere, on the night the first Hush opens in the Vigil, four hundred miles from Sallowreach. The Hushes are not bound to the fen anymore.

## The Slipping Seal

**Summary.** *A new thread.* Oathen's Kept Rain came three days late, which means a god's promise slipped. Every Rim Road Company contract, every platter, every bond of every Bonded Hand is sworn in Oathen and rests on the word of one notary, Jessamy Quill. If Tolm's word can slip, so can hers. The Company is quietly auditing everything and looking for something better to swear by.

**The Players.**
- *Oathen:* Jessamy Quill; Yusra Thole, Rainwarden, silent for a month; Ottoline Vant, whose miscut word broke a man; Ona Fell, the Surety, one default from death.
- *The Rim:* First Spoke Odalys Penhallow; Auditor-General Lysander Coyle; Reeve Calder Stokes, the Company auditor in Brinehollow who tasted salt this morning.
- *Everywhere:* every government that has ever sworn a treaty in Oathen, which is every government.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | A Bonded Hand steals from the Company and does not break. The wardens hang him anyway, quietly. The Company begins re-swearing contracts. |
| 2 | Jessamy Quill becomes ill. Her body begins to show marks that match no oath she has broken. The Company doubles her guard and hires a second notary in secret. |
| 3 | A treaty fails: two lands discover that a border sworn in Oathen two centuries ago no longer binds either side. War is spoken of, carefully. |
| 4 | Quill breaks, or dies. Half the trade of the Table comes unbound in an afternoon. Every platter is worth its weight in bone and nothing more. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Protect Quill. Investigate why the Kept Rain was late (Yusra Thole knows and cannot lie, so she will not speak). Find the Company's secret second notary and decide whether to expose them. Carry the Company's frantic re-swearing across the Rim. Discover that Odalys Penhallow is preparing for the Company to survive the lands, and what she means to feed the wagons.

**Endings.**
- *Unbound:* the Rim dissolves into seven tolls and seven armies. Every land must now get what it needs from neighbors it finds obscene without a neutral road in between, and it gets it by force.
- *The New Witness:* the Company finds something else to swear by. It is listening, and it is not Tolm, and it is very glad to be asked.
- *The Last Wagons:* Penhallow's sealed wagons set out, carrying the Spokes and the archive, around and around the Rim, and the Company becomes a procession with no destination, lit at night, stopping at no inn.

## The Stolen Ledgers

**Summary.** *A new thread.* Silas Fenwright, a renegade Lineman of the Cutwrights' College, walked out of Quire with three quires copied from the Master Concordance: the full lines of the highest houses of three lands, and the College's graph of every Regrowth bending upward at the same moment after 600 A.G. Everyone wants them. Whoever holds the ledgers knows where each god is regrowing fastest, and who is next.

**The Players.**
- *The College:* Archprovost Hesper Lune; Odo Venn; Provost Carrion Sedge in the north, who has already learned what it is to hold a dangerous ledger.
- *Cross-border:* the Purgation, who want a massacre list; the Second Table, who want a menu; the Reckoners, especially Deny Fallon, who wants a target roll.
- *Every land:* noble houses who would pay anything to have their own line removed, and their rivals', added.

| Stage | What Happens |
| 1 | Fenwright offers a single page for sale at the Groaning Board. The buyer is murdered before dawn. |
| 2 | A Lancing in the Fatlands follows the ledger's exact order of houses. A Reckoner raid in the Vigil follows the same page. |
| 3 | The College's graph is copied and nailed to toll-houses on the Rim. The Bait doctrine takes it as proof. Riots in three capitals. |
| 4 | The third quire, the one Fenwright has not shown anyone, is opened. It is a list of every Blank born on the Table in two centuries, with their present whereabouts. |

**What PCs Can Do.** Catch Fenwright, buy from him, or protect him. Return the quires to Quire, or not. Read them, which is a Grim Eye + Lore roll and a Dread 3 check; a character who succeeds learns their own line, and what it ends in. Track the Blank list before the Second Table does.

**Endings.**
- *The Lists Are Read:* massacre and counter-massacre along family lines in every land. The Regrowth slows a little where the High Cuts are gone. The College records the numbers.
- *The Ledgers Burn:* the characters destroy the quires; the Master Concordance at Quire burns soon after, in a fire that no one admits setting. Nobody now knows where the gods are, which helps no one.
- *Your Own Line:* the ledger shows that a player character is closer to a Seating than they knew, by far. Every faction that holds a copy knows it too.
