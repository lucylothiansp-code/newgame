# Leavings of Sallowreach

> Everything here keeps. That is the whole trouble, and the whole trade.
>> — sign above a part-broker's hatch in the Sinks, Lastgate

[bigquote] In any other land a relic is a dead thing that remembers. Here it is a living thing that cannot stop. | — Provost Carrion Sedge, marginal note in the northern ledgers

In the south they dig their relics out of the ground. In the west they cough them up. In Sallowreach, relics walk into the shop on their own feet and ask what they will fetch. Nothing in the fen finishes, and so nothing taken from the fen goes still: a finger cut from a Kept thief goes on picking pockets, a candle rendered from a grandmother goes on feeling the flame, a sod of grey turf lifted from the edge of a Hush goes on being silent in a sack on a cart all the way to the Finishing Post. A northern relic is not a souvenir of the god. It is a piece of the god's long refusal, still refusing.

The trade has no single market and no honest name. The Seamers' Guild calls it "the loose parts business" and disowns it while buying from it. The Mortuary Court calls it theft of licensed goods, which is the charge for anything that can end or be ended without a stamp. The Sinks call it "the leavings," as the rest of the Table now does, and the Sinks know it best, because the Sinks are where it is bought: in back rooms behind wire-drawers, in the flooded cellars of the Drowned Storeys where Little Nell Ash's Smalls run errands for a ribbon, on the long punts that come in from the Sump at dusk with something under a tarpaulin that is moving a little. A leaving changes hands in perfume. Buyer and seller each hold a pomander to the face, and the price is spoken through cedar and clove, because what is on the table smells, and always will.

Carving in the north is easy and terrible, because nothing that is cut will die of the cutting. A Kept man can be relieved of a hand on his shelf in the Lofts and wake in the morning able to feel it being used three streets away. A Hand-line noble deep in the Course can be trimmed like a hedge, and the trimmings go on closing things. The difficulty is never the knife. It is the smell, the screaming that has no air behind it, the guard, and the law. The Court counts its nobles' fingers, the Loft-Wardens count their shelved, and the Seamers' Guild keeps a book of every part it has ever sewn on, so that a stolen part is a stolen part until the end of time, which in Sallowreach is the same as never.

What the fen-folk fear most is not the relics' power but their persistence. A thing carved in Sallowreach does not spoil the way meat spoils elsewhere. It spoils by finishing: by going quiet, going cold, going properly dead, which is the one thing every northerner has been taught both to long for and to dread. A relic that finishes in your hand is a small funeral, and the fen-folk hold it like one. They close its eyes, if it has eyes. They say *may you finish*, and mean it, and are a little jealous.

Outside the north, almost every relic here weakens or ends at once, because what keeps them going is the land. Buyers from other countries learn this the expensive way, at the Finishing Post, holding a hand that has suddenly become an ordinary dead hand. The entries below say which leavings survive the crossing. Few do. Those that do are worth ten times their northern price.

## From the Hand-lines

[sigil: sallowreach]

The Hand-lines are the god's hands, and their bodies are the north's richest and most dangerous seam. A Hand-line at the Course sheds what other people only grow: nails like horn, slate-grey skin that flakes away in cold sheets, extra joints that must be trimmed so the gloves still fit. The Court burns every trimming in the Hall of Hands, by statute, before witnesses, with the ash sunk in the Sump in a lead box. The statute has been honored perhaps nine times in ten. The tenth is the trade.

### The Ninth Joint of Osric Vane

Osric Vane's hands grow. Every finger has nine joints, and every year or two a tenth buds grey under the knuckle, and the Court's surgeon must take the outermost joint away so that the lead mittens will still close at the wrist. The trimmings are counted, logged and burned. One was not. In the Lack of 637 the surgeon's boy, a Threader named Coyle, walked out of Glovehall's west wing with a joint of the left forefinger in his cheek, cold enough to make his teeth ache for a week, and sold it in the Sinks. He is in the Jar Room now. The joint is not.

It is the length of a thumbnail, slate-grey, smooth as river stone, with a black nail at one end and a pearled grey socket at the other. It does not warm in the hand. Once an hour it curls, slowly, as though beckoning. Osric, told it was missing, asked only whether it was being put to good use. He is at the Brink, and what his hands finish now is finer than wood or wick: the things a person carries inside them.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); Grim (18) for anyone who has served at Glovehall. A Grace learns that Coyle still talks, in his jar.

**SPOILAGE:** None in Sallowreach. In a lead thimble it holds anywhere on the Table for a year; carried bare outside the north, it finishes in a day. If it ever touches Osric's other hands it goes home, and his forefinger has ten joints again.

**CARVING:** Only with Osric's consent, through a padlocked mitten, in a room without flies: Hand + Stitching vs Grim (18), with cold-iron snips. A Lack means bare skin brushes the hand: Caul + Resolve vs Grim (18) or suffer Ruin and rise Kept. Watching the joint go on curling on the tray is a Dread 3 check.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Touch it to a brow and Partake (Hand + Rites vs Hard 14; the target may resist with Caul + Resolve). On success, close one thing inside them until the next full Plate: a memory, one Want, one Derangement, or one grief (remove all Fray tied to that loss). Used on an Oath on the Dead, it closes the oath: no Strength, no Keeping, no Visitation if broken, and the bearer can never again speak the dead person's name. Three uses a season; a fourth closes something of the GM's choosing, in the user.

**WORTH:** 3,000 L in the back rooms; Absalom Hethe would pay double. Unique. Capital theft of a Court asset.

### Glove-Thread of the Left Hand

Lady Corrow Vane's gloves are sewn on. They come off once a year, at Lack, when her Glovebearers unpick the seams by lamplight with bone hooks, wash the hands she has never used to hold her children, and stitch on new gloves before dawn. The old thread is supposed to be burned. It has spent a year in the seam of the most expensive touch in the north, eleven thousand Closings' worth of cold, and it has learned something.

It is grey gut-thread, waxed, kinked from the seam. It frosts in a warm room. Under a lens, every twist of it is stiff with a fine white rime, like breath on a winter window, and the rime does not melt. Northern Seamers who have stitched with it say the needle grows heavy in the fingers, and that patients go quiet under it in a way that is not sleep.

The Glovebearers sell it by the inch, carefully, to Seamers they trust. Corrow knows. She has never said a word. One of her bearers believes she wants it used; that the only mercy her hands are permitted to give without a license is the mercy her thread gives, by stealth, on Needle Street, to strangers.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) among Seamers; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** A season, in a twist of waxed paper. Wet, it loses its rime in a day. Outside Sallowreach it is only thread after a week.

**CARVING:** Taken at the yearly unpicking by a Glovebearer, or bought from one. Stealing it requires getting into Glovehall's east wing on the longest night of Lack, past Hand-line guards: Lung + Stealth vs Grim (18). A Lack means Corrow sees the thief, says nothing, and the thief is her creature from then on, whether they know it or not.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Ossel).

**EFFECT:** A wound stitched with a yard of glove-thread (Hand + Stitching vs Hard 14) closes and stays closed: it cannot reopen, bleed or fester, and restores 2 extra Flesh. The pain of it closes too, for a day. The scar is grey, cold and numb forever. A Kept body stitched with it gains no Rot for a full season, and is very quiet. A yard covers one wound.

**WORTH:** 30 L the inch, 900 L a yard. Scarce on Needle Street; Rare elsewhere. Illegal, but every Seamer of standing has a twist of it in a drawer.

### A Drop of the Regent's Lacquer

Ansel Grue is four hundred and twelve years old and mostly wire, lacquer and habit. The lacquer is laid on by Guild masters every Plate, layer on layer, black and amber, like the varnish on a very old violin, and as the old coats craze and lift, they are scraped from his cheeks and wrists and the backs of his hands with silver knives, and the scrapings are supposed to be sunk in the Sump with the rest of the Court's leavings.

The scrapings are flakes of dark amber, glossy on one side and on the other faintly furred with what was under them. They smell of pine resin, camphor and something very old and dry, a smell Lastgate calls *office*. Melted in a spoon over a Kept-candle, they run like honey and give off the smell of a counting-house on a hot afternoon.

The Regent has signed every death license in Sallowreach for three hundred years and has never once considered signing his own. Whatever keeps him upright has been soaking into those coats for centuries. Painted on the Kept, a drop of it does the same thing to them: it keeps them. Not well. Not kindly. Just upright, and at their desks.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18). Unvacated families know of it at Hard (14) and do not speak of it to their heirs.

**SPOILAGE:** Indefinite, sealed in a lead-stoppered vial. Once melted, it must be used within the hour.

**CARVING:** The scrapings are taken at the Regent's monthly lacquering in the Hall of Hands. A Seamer of Master rank may palm a flake (Hand + Filch vs Hard 14). Anyone else must bribe one, or take it from the Regent directly, which means a knife against the most guarded body in the north (Hand + Blades vs Dire 22 for a clean scrape; on a Lack, the Regent notices, and remembers, and will remember for another four hundred years). Seeing the Regent unlacquered under the scrapings is Dread 2.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Ossel), 1 Hunger.

**EFFECT:** Melted and painted over a Kept body (an hour's work), a drop removes 2 Rot and prevents any Rot gain for a year. The Kept so treated cannot be Closed by touch for that year; the Closer's hand slides off the lacquer. They also cannot change their mind about anything they held to on the day of painting. On a living person, it does nothing but stain. The Unvacated pay for it to make sure they never have to vacate.

**WORTH:** 400 L a drop. Rare. The Heirs would pay more to have the supply destroyed. The Court has hanged two Seamers for selling it, and kept them.

### Mitten-Lead

Lead that has been worn for years around a Course-hand goes cold, and stays cold. Osric Vane's mittens are recast every few years because the lead goes brittle, crazed with white frost from the inside, and so do the mittens of every Hand-line too far gone to be trusted in gloves. The old lead goes to the Court's smelter. Some of it goes, instead, to a smith in the Sinks named Hessa Coombe, who casts it into sling-stones.

A mitten-lead stone is grey, dull, heavy for its size, and cold enough to blister an unprotected palm. Frost forms on it in Plenty. It is silent in flight: no whistle, no hum, and the sound of its strike does not carry. Coombe's customers are the Heirs, the occasional Reckoner, and Finishers who want to finish something that is not themselves.

The Sinks tell of an Heir named Tobin Rook who in 612 dropped his Unvacated grandfather with one stone at forty paces, and the family stood over him all night, and in the morning he was still down. He got up on the third day. For those three days the whole street paid Coombe in advance.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in the Sinks; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** The cold lasts a year from casting. Within a year of leaving Sallowreach it is only lead.

**CARVING:** The lead must be stolen from Court stores or a Hand-line house (Lung + Filch vs Grim 18) and recast by a smith willing to handle it (Hand + Craft vs Hard 14 with iron tongs; bare-handed, it is a Dread 1 check and 1 Flesh of frostbite). A Lack at the casting means the mold cracks and the smith's hand is caught by the spill: it goes grey to the wrist and does not come back.

**TAINT:** Dread 1 (per scene in which the stones are used).

**EFFECT:** A sling-stone of mitten-lead deals 3 damage instead of 2 and its strike makes no sound. Against a Kept target, each Helping on the hit also adds 1 Rot, and a Grace closes the Kept outright, as the Closing Touch. Against a living target in Sallowreach, a Grace forces Caul + Resolve vs Hard (14) or the target suffers Ruin. A pouch holds six stones; each can be recovered and reused until the cold goes.

**WORTH:** 25 L a stone, 120 L a pouch of six. Uncommon in the Sinks, Scarce elsewhere. Possessing one is "carrying an unlicensed ending," a flogging offense.

### Pim Sorrel's Shed Tooth

Pim Sorrel is a Scrapling of twelve whose touch ends things, which should be impossible for her blood. The Court wants her opened on a table to find out how. The Finishers want her crowned. Her mother wants her across the border. And because she is twelve, her milk teeth have been coming out, one at a time, for two years, and somebody has been collecting them.

The teeth are small, white, a little chipped, and faintly cold, like pebbles from a spring. Held in a closed fist near a crying child, they stop the crying. Pim's mother kept the first two under her pillow, for luck, the way mothers do. Both were stolen in the same week. Pim hides her teeth now, and three have still gone missing.

Nobody knows what a Scrapling-born Closing means. Carrion Sedge has offered a fortune for a single tooth, to grind and test. Sister Lorn would set one in a reliquary. Marrow Jack has one already, and has been very careful about who he shows it to.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18). Finishers and Smalls know of them at Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** Indefinite. Unlike Hand-line leavings, the tooth keeps its chill anywhere on the Table. Nobody knows why. That fact alone is worth a fortune to the Cutwrights.

**CARVING:** Pim's teeth are not carved. They fall out. Obtaining one means stealing it from her, from her mother, or from whoever stole it last. Anyone who would pull one from her mouth is beyond the scope of this book and beneath the contempt of the Smalls, who will find them.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Ossel), and see below.

**EFFECT:** Held in the closed fist, the tooth lets the bearer use the Closing Touch once, as a Course-stage Hand-line would, regardless of Cut or land. Each use, the bearer rolls a d6: on a 1, the tooth is spent and crumbles to chalk, and Pim, wherever she is, feels it and weeps. A character with Cut 0 who uses it gains a mark of Seasoning, as if from Regrowth Taint, and the GM notes that the Scrapling line has found a door.

**WORTH:** Never sold openly. Sedge offers 2,000 L; the Finishers would trade a death-license minute for each. Unique per tooth (perhaps six exist). Owning one marks the owner, in the eyes of every faction, as someone who has been near the girl.

## From the Kept and the Lofts

[sigil: sallowreach]

Nine Sallowreachers in ten are Kept, and the Kept are inexhaustible. This is the sordid heart of the leavings trade: the dead do not need their parts, the dead cannot lock their doors, and the dead do not stop feeling what is done with what was taken. Marrow Jack's whole business rests on it. So does the Seamers' Guild's, from the other side of the counter. The leavings in this section are taken from the long dead, and most of them can still hear you.

### Shroud-Linen of the Lofts

The Set-Aside are wrapped in linen and shelved in the Lofts, aware. The wrappings are changed every decade, when the old cloth has gone brown and stiff with what has soaked into it. The Loft-Wardens are supposed to burn the old shrouds. Old Bettany Shroud, who has read to the shelved every night for fifty years and has written down everything they whisper back, does not burn them. She folds them, and keeps them in a cedar chest, because she cannot bear to.

A length of Loft linen is the color of strong tea, stiff and greasy, printed with the faint outline of the face it lay over. It smells of camphor and old meat. Wrapped around the head, it whispers the Lofts' whisper, like rain on reeds: names, grudges, the gossip of six generations. And lately, under all of them, one word.

The word is *company*. Every shelf in the Lofts has been whispering it for a year, as though someone were coming up the stairs. The linen whispers it too, wherever it is taken. A pedlar who carried a length of it to Gristmoot said that it whispered louder on the road east, toward the Fast.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14). Loft-Wardens know at Routine (10) and are ashamed.

**SPOILAGE:** The whisper fades over a year, faster in sunlight. Folded in cedar and kept in the dark, it lasts a decade. It survives the border, faintly, for a season.

**CARVING:** Bought from a Warden, or cut from a shelved body by a thief on the ladders at night (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14; the shelved feel the cloth go and begin, very quietly, to beg). Unwrapping one of the Set-Aside to take the shroud is a Dread 2 check; seeing the face under it is a Dread 3.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** A character who wraps their head in the linen for an hour (Eye + Search vs Hard 14) may learn one secret about any person who lived or was Kept in north Lastgate: one secret, one Helping each for another. Each hour so spent also gives 1 Fray from the voices. On a Lack, the listener hears their own name in the whisper, said by someone shelved who loved them, and must make a Dread 3 check. Anywhere on the Table, the linen murmurs *company* a little louder in the presence of an eighth chair, a laid table, or a place card.

**WORTH:** 60 L a yard in Lastgate. Scarce. Not illegal, merely shameful; the Wardens will be very hurt.

### A Cell of the Honorable Maggot

Justice Ever Lathe of the Court of Wrongs has been Kept since 389 and has rotted down to bone, a few black tendons and a resident colony. His rulings are delivered in the buzzing, and a clerk translates. Nobody is sure any longer which of them is deciding. The colony is the court. And the colony breeds, and when it breeds it sheds: a living, seething clot of grubs and flies that drops from the hem of his robe in the robing room, and is swept up by an usher, and is sold.

A cell is a jar of a few hundred white grubs and a dozen fat blue flies, nested in black matter best not asked about. Unlike every other fly in Sallowreach, these move in order. The grubs coil and uncoil together. The flies rise and settle as one, like a choir standing. If you hold the jar up and say something untrue, the buzzing in it rises in pitch, thin and angry.

A widow in the Sinks keeps one on her husband's chair at dinner and asks it whether he still loves her. She says it never lies.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); Routine (10) for anyone who practices before the Court of Wrongs.

**SPOILAGE:** A cell lives as long as it is fed: a mouthful of rotten meat a week, which in Sallowreach is the easiest thing to find. Starved, it dies in a month. Outside Sallowreach the flies behave as ordinary flies within a day, and the grubs pupate into nothing.

**CARVING:** An usher sells them at the robing-room door for a crown of bribes; or the colony can be taken from Justice Lathe himself, by a bold hand under his robe in court (Lung + Filch vs Grim 18; a Lack and the colony notices, and the Justice rules, through his clerk, that the thief is in contempt, which in the Court of Wrongs is a sentence of a year in a jar). Handling the clot bare-handed is a Dread 1 check.

**TAINT:** Dread 1. Those who sleep beside the jar dream in buzzing, and start translating it.

**EFFECT:** In the jar's presence, any deliberate lie spoken aloud makes the colony shrill. Characters gain +2 to Instinct rolls to detect lies, and anyone who knows what the jar is suffers −2 to Deceive. Placed on a festering wound, a cell eats the rot clean in an hour (the wound stops festering; a Kept body loses 1 Rot), but the user must make a Dread 1 check, and the cell grows by a third.

**WORTH:** 45 L a jar. Scarce. Legal; mildly disreputable. The Court of Wrongs considers sales a perquisite of the ushers.

### A Deserter of Captain Morrow's

Captain Dace Morrow, Commander of the Fen Guard, has been killed sixty times and stitched back after every one. He is more thread than soldier now, a tall figure bound in cord and wire, his face held on by a lattice of brass. And he has begun deserting in small pieces. His soldiers find parts of him in the barracks, under cots, behind the latrine, in the boots of men about to go on watch: a finger, an ear, a slab of shoulder with its stitching unpicked from the inside. The parts are always found facing south.

The deserter is whatever part walked away this time: a finger, a hand, once a foot that reached the Smoking Ground before a reed-cutter stepped on it. Each is bound in grey cord, smells of oil and old leather, and creeps toward the border, a room's width in a night. Turned round, it turns back.

The Guard return what they find. The Captain thanks them, and puts it in a drawer. He does not have it sewn back. One of his sergeants believes the Captain is sending himself away, piece by piece, so that one day the last of him can step over the stakes and lie down beside the others at the Finishing Post. Another sergeant has been selling the pieces.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); Routine (10) for the Fen Guard.

**SPOILAGE:** In Sallowreach, it never spoils, but it walks; it must be kept in a closed box, or it leaves. Taken across the border, it finishes at once, completely, as all the Kept do, and lies still. It is always found facing home.

**CARVING:** Found, or bought from the sergeant (Tongue + Haggle vs Hard 14). Taking a piece from the Captain directly is unthinkable to the Guard and very dangerous to anyone else; he is Kept, he is still a soldier, and he notices.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** A deserter is a perfect compass to the nearest border of Sallowreach, unaffected by fog, the Breath or a lost road. A traveler carrying it gains +2 to Wayfaring in the fen, and the Hard check for leaving the road becomes Routine when heading out. Carried in battle, it lends the Captain's habit: once per scene the bearer may ignore one failed Dread check from violence, as a soldier sixty times killed ignores it. Each time this is used, roll a d6; on a 1, the deserter has slipped its box and is gone.

**WORTH:** 80 L for a finger, 300 L for a hand. Scarce. Selling a piece of a serving officer is treason in the Fen Guard's code, which is enforced by the Fen Guard.

### Peat-Leather of the Last Witness

Grandfather Peat was hauled out of the Sump in 611. He had drowned the week of the Gorging, and for six hundred years he had lain under the black water, tanning. He is leather-brown, flattened, shining wet, with a fringe of red hair and a rope still knotted at his throat, and he speaks a dead dialect that only a few Cutwrights can follow, and he repeats one thing: the god was smiling.

His skin flakes. Drying in the Cutwrights' warm rooms, it comes away in brown scales the size of a fingernail, tough as boot-sole, smelling of peat and tannin. The College keeps the flakes in a numbered box. Some have left the box.

A flake chewed slowly softens and tastes of iron and bog, and the taste becomes a memory that is not the chewer's: black water closing overhead, the weight on the chest, cold, and before that, a firelit shore and a long table, and a vast grey shape laid out on the mud with its hands folded on its chest, waiting. And its face. The chewer always wakes from it with their own face aching, from smiling.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18). A Cutwright knows of it at Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** Indefinite, kept dry. Damp, it softens into a brown paste and the memory goes out of it in a week. It survives the border perfectly well; it was tanned long before the Kept were Kept.

**CARVING:** Stolen from the College's box (Lung + Filch vs Hard 14), or flaked from Grandfather Peat himself with a scraper, which he permits if asked courteously in his own dialect (Tongue + Lore vs Grim 18). Peeling him without asking is a Dread 2 check; he weeps, and the tears are brown.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** A chewer sees the Night of Seven Tables from the shore of the Ferrying, for the length of a dream. They may ask the GM one question about the Meal, the Providers, or the carving of the eighth place on the Ferrying Post, and receive a true answer, seen through a drowning man's eyes. They also learn to speak a few dozen words of the dead fen dialect (+2 to Lore on the oldest carvings of the north). Each flake gives one dream. On a failed Dread check from the vision, the chewer also takes the Derangement *The Voice at Table* for a week.

**WORTH:** 150 L a flake to a Cutwright; 400 L to the Second Table, which is buying. Rare. Theft from the College is a College matter, and the College has long memories and longer ledgers.

### A Tallow-Saint's Wick

Wick Tallow renders the unclaimed dead into candles that feel themselves burn, and half of Lastgate reads by people it used to know. When too many candles made from one family are burned in one room, the tallow sometimes remembers itself, runs, and gathers into a Tallow-Saint: a faceless figure of soft yellow wax, wicked through its crown and burning, that walks. The only way to stop one is to pinch out its wick, in its embrace, while it burns you.

A wick so pinched does not go out. The flame shrinks to a bead of blue no bigger than a lentil, and stays, on a hand-long braid of tallow-black thread, giving no heat and a very steady light. Hold it near a face and the light shows the face as the family that made the Saint would have seen it, beloved and familiar, for a moment, whatever face it is.

A chandler's widow on Wick Lane keeps one under a glass bell, and says it is her husband and his mother and his mother's sister, and on Tablenight she sets three places beside it.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** The bead of flame lasts until the wick is touched to water, or carried over the border, where it finally goes out with a sound like a long breath let go. Kept under glass in Sallowreach, it lasts forever.

**CARVING:** Only from a Tallow-Saint, in its grapple, by pinching (Hand + Filch vs Grim 18, while taking 2 fire damage a round). On a Lack, the wick comes away and the burning wax closes over the pincher's hand; the hand is scarred to a waxen stump unless a Seamer rebuilds it. Taking the wick from a Saint and watching it slump into a puddle that shapes, as it cools, the outlines of faces is a Dread 2 check.

**TAINT:** Dread 2, each night it is lit.

**EFFECT:** The wick gives light for ten paces that cannot be put out by wind, water or a Hush, and that no Kept will lie in: any Kept person in its light who speaks an untruth must make a Caul + Resolve roll vs Hard (14) or confess it. Held near any person, it shows them for a breath as loved; the bearer gains +2 to Persuade with anyone who sees their own face in its light, once per scene. Carried into a Hush, it is the only light that burns there.

**WORTH:** 600 L. Rare. Wick Tallow will buy one back at double, and wants very much to know who has been making Saints out of his stock.

### The Mother of Mother Gall's Brine

Mother Gall can keep a body sound for a century in the right brine, and her cellar under Brinemoss holds four hundred customers in barrels, paid up in advance, all wide awake. The recipe she will not share. Her brine is sold, at twenty lacks a pot, and it works for a year. But the real thing, the thing that keeps four hundred people perfect, is not a recipe. It is a culture. It is a mother.

Every vinegar has a mother, a slick of living matter that turns wine to sour. Mother Gall's has come down, she says, from her own mother, in a crock not empty since 402. It is a grey-white, rubbery, glistening disc the size of a dinner plate, layered like the leaves of a book, and it smells of dill and garlic and brine and, under them, very faintly, of lavender water, which was the scent her mother wore. It moves in the crock. It turns, slowly, toward whoever opens the lid.

Brinemoss says the old woman's mother is not in the crock so much as of it. Mother Gall laughs when asked. She cuts a slip for one customer in a generation; the only slip ever sold came from a barrel whose occupant passed it to a thief through a knot-hole.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Hard (14) in Brinemoss.

**SPOILAGE:** Kept in its own brine and fed a cup of sour beer each week, a slip lives indefinitely, and it travels: the mother is the one Sallowreach leaving that seems not to care where it is.

**CARVING:** A slip is cut from the crock with a wooden knife; Mother Gall will do it for the right person (Tongue + Persuade vs Dire 22, or a favor she names). Stealing one means getting into her cellar, among the barrels, which knock as you pass (Lung + Stealth vs Grim 18). A Lack means a lid lifts, and the occupant asks you, very politely, to let them out. Dread 2.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Ossel).

**EFFECT:** A slip of the mother, set in a barrel of salted vinegar, makes a brine that keeps anything submerged in it exactly as it went in, with no rot, no age, no healing, for as long as it stays under. A living person kept under breathes through it, unaging and wide awake (Dread 3 to be immersed, Dread 2 to witness). A severed part kept in it can be sewn back on a century later with no penalty. A Kept body in it gains no Rot. In a year the slip can be cut again.

**WORTH:** Never sold by Mother Gall. The stolen slip changed hands for 5,000 L. Unique, or nearly. Not illegal; the Seamers' Guild would very much like it to be.


## From the Hushes and the Fen

[sigil: sallowreach]

The fen keeps everything that ever went into it, and the Hushes keep nothing at all, and between the two lies the north's strangest trade. Reed-cutters, Sump-dredgers, Finisher pilgrims and Cutwright surveyors all bring things back from the edges: from the black water where the drowned move a little, from the grey line where birds fall, from the warm ground where the Glad Century's fires still burn. The Court claims every Hush as its own and every leaving from one as a stolen license. The Bottled Hush (see the Rim Market) is the famous one. These are the others.

### A Hush-Reed Pipe

At the edge of every Hush the reeds go from green-brown to ash-grey in the space of a step. They do not rot or bend. Cut one and it stays exactly as it was cut, with no sap, no smell, and a hollow core as clean as a bone. Reed-cutters of Dunmere's neighbor villages, who lost their trade to the grey, have found a new one: they bore the reeds into pipes, six holes, and sell them to the Heirs, the Finishers and anyone who needs a crowd to stop.

A Hush-reed pipe makes no sound. Its player feels the breath go in and feels the fingers move, and the people around them hear nothing at all, and that is the music. Those near the player feel it: a drop in the room, as though a door had shut somewhere, and then voices falter, a song in the next street loses its tune, a quarrel forgets itself. Dunstan Reave, the last of Dunmere, carves them, sitting at the edge with his spyglass, and gives them away to anyone who asks him nothing about his family.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A year. Then the reed takes on color again, slowly, from the mouthpiece down, and when it is green it plays an ordinary note, which in Sallowreach is the saddest sound a reed-cutter knows.

**CARVING:** Cut at the very edge of a Hush with a long-handled sickle, one foot outside the grey (Lung + Athletics vs Hard 14 to reach in and back cleanly). On a Lack, the cutter's hand crosses the line: Caul + Resolve vs Dire (22) or the hand finishes, numb, grey and dead forever. Standing at the edge is a Dread 2 check.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Ossel).

**EFFECT:** Played for a round (Hand + Craft vs Routine 10), the pipe ends every song, argument, speech or spoken Rite within twenty paces; those affected cannot resume it for a scene without a Tongue + Resolve roll vs Hard (14). It silences a Sayer's word in Oathen for one breath, no more. Every Kept who hears the silence must make a Caul + Resolve roll vs Hard (14) or stop what they are doing and listen for the next note.

**WORTH:** 50 L in the Sinks. Scarce. Unlicensed Hush-goods; the Court seldom bothers.

### A Glovehall Pear

Last Carving a small Hush, the size of a kitchen garden, appeared in the orchard behind Glovehall, the house where Osric Vane is kept. The gardeners will not go near it. The pears in it are perfect. They hang on grey branches in the grey grass, golden, unblemished, heavy, and they do not fall, because falling is a kind of ending and nothing in a Hush is waiting to end. There are forty-one of them. Someone at Glovehall counts them every morning, and some mornings there are fewer.

To pick one, you must reach into the silence. The pear comes away without a sound. It is cool and dense and smells of nothing. Its flesh, when cut, does not brown. Its taste, by every account, is the end of a long day: not sweet so much as finished. Those who have eaten one describe a feeling they have no word for in Sallowreach, and those from other lands recognize it at once and weep. It is the feeling of something being over.

Hand-line servants whisper that the pears are Osric. The Hush grew where his window looks.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); Grim (18) for Glovehall staff and Absalom Hethe's circle.

**SPOILAGE:** It never spoils, never browns, never softens. Once bitten, it must be eaten whole within the hour or the bitten flesh goes grey and tasteless and is only grey.

**CARVING:** A long pole and a net from outside the line (Hand + Craft vs Grim 18), at night, past the Vanes' guards and dogs (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14). A Lack on the reach drops the picker's arm across the line: as for the Hush-reed, at Dire. Picking under Osric's window, where he can see you, is a Dread 3 check. He waves.

**TAINT:** 4 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Eating a pear ends one thing in the eater, permanently, chosen when they eat: one Derangement, one Want (for the rest of their life), one addiction, one Oath on the Dead (kept, as if fulfilled, with all its rewards), or all their current Fray. A Kept who eats one finishes, gently and completely. A living Sallowreacher who eats a second in one year begins their own Hush: the GM marks a silence the size of a doorstep where they next sleep.

**WORTH:** 2,000 L. Rare. Stealing Vane fruit is a capital matter. The Finishers count them as holy relics and will not sell. Lady Corrow has been seen to stand at the Hush's edge with a pear in her gloved hand, not eating it.

### A Sump-Face

Six centuries of weighted sacks have gone down into the Sump, and over decades, under forty feet of black peat-water, the bodies find each other and press together into a Sump-thing: a slick dark mound twenty feet across, hair and sackcloth and tanned leather over a lattice of knitted bone, and everywhere in it, faces. Pressed flat. Mouths working. The Seamers' Guild pays a standing bounty of six hundred lacks for every face recovered, and gives it back to the family.

Not every face goes home. Cut free, a face is a mask of soft, peat-tanned skin, faintly warm, still moving: the lips work, the brow knots in the old grief a weighted sack gives everyone. Dredgers who keep them say a face worn over one's own fits as if it were made to, and that while it is worn, the fen recognizes you. The drowned in the black water let go of your ankle. The Sump-things let you pass. The water lets you breathe.

The face goes on speaking, for the wearer alone: a name, a street, a debt, and the words *I can still feel the rope*.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) among dredgers and Sackmen; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** In Sallowreach, never. Kept damp in fen-water, it travels a week outside the border before it finishes and becomes a scrap of leather with a face on it.

**CARVING:** From a Sump-thing, in the water, while it tries to Add you: Hand + Blades vs Grim (18) with a flensing knife, taking a round of its Many Hands for each attempt. A Lack means the carver's own face is pressed against the mound for a breath, and comes away with the impression of another face on it, which takes a month to fade. Taking a face is a Dread 3 check; recognizing it is a Dread 4.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 2 (each day worn).

**EFFECT:** While worn, the bearer can breathe underwater in the fen, cannot be grappled by the drowned or by a Sump-thing (they treat the wearer as one of their own), and gains +2 to Stealth in water. Each day worn, roll a d6: on a 1, the face's grief bleeds through and the bearer gains The Kept Grief Derangement for a day, mourning someone they never knew. Returned to its family, a face gives its finder a Portion and their gratitude, which in the Sinks is worth more.

**WORTH:** 600 L from the Seamers (who then return it); up to 1,500 L to smugglers. Rare. Wearing a face rather than returning it is desecration under Guild law, and the families take it personally.

### A Sod of Walker-Hush

A Hush-walker is the silence that got up: someone who went into a Hush and did not finish, and came out carrying the stillness with them. Reduced to nothing, a Hush-walker lies down and is finished at last, and the ground under it becomes a Hush a yard across. It is the only Hush small enough to dig.

A sod of walker-Hush is a square of turf a spade deep, grey grass in grey soil, lifted whole and boxed in cedar. It weighs a little too much. Inside the box the grass does not stir however the box is shaken, and a fly that lands on it falls over and stays. Set on the ground, it is a hole in the world the shape of a doormat: step onto it and the sound of your feet stops, your heartbeat stops in your own ears, and you become aware, very suddenly, of how loud the inside of your body has always been.

The Finishers covet them, and Sister Lorn keeps one in her house in the Sinks, under a cloth, in front of her own door. She says she uses it to pray on. Her flock have begun to ask why she does not use it for anything else.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** The sod keeps its silence for a year and a day after lifting, unless watered, which shrinks it by a hand's width each time. Outside Sallowreach it holds a single season and finishes nothing that is not Kept, which there are none of.

**CARVING:** Dug from the yard-wide Hush where a Hush-walker lay, with a spade and a cedar box, from outside its edge (Gut + Labor vs Hard 14; the digger must Partake or stand on the grey to finish the cut, which is a Caul + Resolve roll vs Grim 18 each round or take 2 Fray as the silence presses into the ears). The digging is a Dread 2 check.

**TAINT:** Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** A sod laid on the ground is a silence a yard across. A Kept person who lies on it for a full minute finishes, completely, as if Closed: no license, no Hand-line, no fee. A living person who stands on it cannot be heard and cannot hear, and may not Partake. A living Sallowreacher who sleeps on it dreams nothing at all, and wakes having lost 1d6 Hunger and gained 1 Regrowth Taint roll at Grim (18). Each Kept finished on it shrinks the sod by a quarter.

**WORTH:** 1,800 L. Rare. A stolen license four times over in the Court's reckoning; the Finishers would give a pilgrim's whole savings, and have.

### A Coal from the Smoking Ground

In the Glad Century, when the fen-folk were dancing because nobody died, peat fires were lit in the marshes east of Lastgate for the celebrations, and they are still burning. Fire in Sallowreach burns down but does not go out. The ground there is warm and spongy and steams from long cracks, and things lost in the fires walk there, black and cracking.

A coal dug from the Smoking Ground is a lump of peat the size of a fist, black-crusted, with a slow red heart that pulses like a sleeper's breath. It has been burning for five hundred years. It does not consume itself and does not grow, and it cannot be put out: water steams off it, sand smothers it to a dull glow that brightens again the moment the sand is shaken off. It smells of sour smoke and, faintly, of the roasting meat of the Glad Century's feasts.

The Heirs, who meet on the Smoking Ground because no Court agent will go there in good boots, carry coals in iron boxes as badges. They say a coal from the Ground is the only thing in the north that the Unvacated did not get first.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in Lastgate; Hard (14) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** In Sallowreach, never. Carried over the border, it burns normally, which is to say it burns out in a night, as fast as five hundred years can catch up with it, with a roar and a stink and a great deal of heat.

**CARVING:** Dug from a crack with iron tongs and a box (Gut + Labor vs Hard 14 while taking 1 fire damage a round). The Smoking Ground's burned walkers resent the digging; a Lack draws one, cracking and smoldering (as an Unstitched Kept, but dealing fire). Seeing one is Dread 2.

**TAINT:** none in Sallowreach. Burned over the border, Dread 1 to watch it go.

**EFFECT:** A coal lights any fire, in any weather, and that fire cannot be put out by any ordinary means within Sallowreach; only a Hand-line's Closing or a Hush ends it. A coal in an iron box warms a room and keeps off cold-weather penalties for a party. Carried across the border and thrown, it bursts into a blaze that fills a room with fire for one scene (4 fire damage a round to all within it). The Heirs call this "sending grandfather south."

**WORTH:** 15 L in the Sinks; 120 L abroad. Uncommon. Legal, though a Court agent who finds one on an Heir will find a reason.

## Preparations of the North

[sigil: sallowreach]

Not every leaving is carved. Some are made: brewed in physicians' back rooms from the one ailment the north still suffers, written down by the last family to remember a rite, struck from the coins pilgrims leave behind. These are preparations, and they are the leavings most often sold to foreigners, because some of them survive the border, and all of them are easier to explain to a customs clerk.

### Quease's Number Four Hundred and One

Dr. Halloran Quease, the physician of a land where no illness is fatal, has catalogued four hundred kinds of pain, from *the long bell* (a seam pulling through) to *the attic* (being shelved and forgotten). His Number 212, which removes one kind of pain for a day, is sold in every apothecary in Lastgate. His Number 401 is not sold anywhere, and he denies it exists.

It is a tincture distilled from the nerve-ends of the Kept who have stopped feeling: the very rare Set-Aside, far up the shelves in the Lofts, who have been in pain so long that the pain has worn through, like a step worn through by feet, and gone quiet. Quease buys their nerves from Marrow Jack, by the ounce, and does not ask how he gets them. The tincture is clear, oily, and smells of carbolic and lemon, like the doctor. A drop on the tongue tastes of nothing and, a moment later, of nothing at all, everywhere.

The four hundred and first pain, in Quease's private notebook, is the pain of not being able to feel anything. He catalogued it by taking the tincture himself. He has not written in that notebook since.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); a physician or Seamer knows of it at Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A year in a stoppered brown bottle, kept cool. It survives the border.

**CARVING:** Brewed only by Quease (Eye + Craft vs Dire 22 for anyone who steals his method). The ingredient must be taken from the deepest Set-Aside in the Lofts (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14 past the Wardens, then Hand + Stitching vs Grim 18 to take the nerve). Unwrapping a shelved body that cannot feel the knife is a Dread 3 check, because it does not scream.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 1. Addiction: Grim (18).

**EFFECT:** For a scene, the drinker feels no pain at all. They ignore Injury penalties, cannot be forced to act by pain or torture, and are immune to effects that cause pain (including a Waker's needle). They also cannot feel whether they are hurt: the player does not learn their Flesh total until the scene ends, and the GM tracks it. They lose all Fray gained in the scene, and gain double that amount the following morning, when everything comes back.

**WORTH:** 60 L a dose, if Quease can be persuaded. Rare. Not illegal, because no law imagines it.

### A Last Verse

Songs in Sallowreach have no last verse. Nothing does. Bram Hollin's family were professional mourners, a trade six centuries extinct, and by an unbroken line of fathers teaching sons for a job none of them could do, he alone knows the whole funeral rite: the keening, the washing, the coin on the tongue, the six last songs. And the six last songs have last verses. They are the only ones in the north.

Bram writes one out, very rarely, for a family that cannot afford him and has been granted a Closing at last: one verse on a slip of black-edged paper, in a small tidy hand, folded twice. The verse is short. It is not beautiful. It is plain, old-fashioned fen-speech about putting down the tools at dusk and going in. Sung over someone who is ending, it lets them end. Sung over anything else, it ends that.

A forged Last Verse is common, and does nothing. The real ones carry Bram's thumbprint in lamp-black on the fold, because his father's did, and his father's father's. Several Finishers have tried to buy one. Bram has refused. He says the verse is for the people left behind.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18). Telling a real one from a forgery is Eye + Search vs Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** The paper keeps; the verse is spent once sung all the way through. A verse once sung cannot be sung again from the same slip. The words on it fade to grey before the singer has finished.

**CARVING:** Not carved. Given by Bram Hollin, for a reason he accepts (Tongue + Persuade vs Dire 22 if the reason is anything but grief), or stolen from those he gave it to, which the north counts among the lowest acts a person can commit.

**TAINT:** Dread 2, to the singer, who feels it end.

**EFFECT:** Sung (Tongue + Rites vs Hard 14) at a Closing or at any death, the verse lets the dying go cleanly: a Kept person finishes without pain, and every mourner present removes 3 Fray and gains a Portion. Sung over anything that is not dying, it ends it: a fire, a song, a quarrel, a curse, an ongoing Seeming, the voice of a Tenant for a scene, or one Gift of a Brink-stage creature for a scene. Sung over a Hush, it does nothing, and the singer hears, for one moment, every sound that ever ended there.

**WORTH:** Never sold by Bram. 800 L when stolen. Rare. Owning a stolen verse is not a crime in law; it is a crime in every kitchen in Lastgate.

### Shut-Eye Pennies

The Finishers walk into the Hushes singing. Before they go, Sister Lorn of the Shut Eye lays a pair of black wax pennies on each pilgrim's eyes, stamped with a closed eye like the Court's breath-tokens, and prays, and lifts them off again, and the pilgrim walks into the grey with their eyes open. She has done this nine hundred times. She keeps every pair. They are in a long cedar box in her rooms, nine hundred pairs, labeled, and she cannot bring herself to melt them down.

A pair of Shut-Eye pennies is as warm as an eyelid and soft enough to take a thumbprint, and each still holds the shape of the eye it lay on, down to a lash-mark. Laid on a sleeper's eyes, they bring a sleep with no dreams in it, deep and black and complete. Laid on the eyes of the Kept, they bring something the Kept have not had since they died: a few hours of nothing at all.

The pilgrims who wore them did not come back. That is the point. The pennies remember the last thing those eyes saw before the grey, and on very still nights, those who sleep under them sometimes see it: a line of grey reeds, a door standing open, and Sister Lorn outside, waving.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Routine (10) among the Finishers.

**SPOILAGE:** A pair works for a year of nights, then softens into ordinary wax. Outside Sallowreach the warmth lasts a season.

**CARVING:** Stolen from Lorn's cedar box (Lung + Filch vs Hard 14), or begged from her (Tongue + Persuade vs Grim 18; she will give a pair only to someone she believes will never use the Hushes). A Lack while stealing them means the thief reads the label on the box: a name they know. Dread 3.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** A sleeper under the pennies rests completely: a night's sleep under them removes 2 Fray instead of 1, and no Visitation, dream or Derangement can reach them that night. A Kept person under them is free of pain and Rot gain for the night. Used in the Vigil, they do nothing to stop the room; the sleeper goes to the long room and does not dream there either, and does not come back. On a Lack on any Regrowth Taint roll they cause, the user wakes with the Pull (see Despair) toward the nearest Hush for a day, and companions should hold on to them.

**WORTH:** 90 L a pair. Scarce. The Finishers will not sell them; the Smalls of the Drowned Storeys trade them among themselves as their most precious coin, and nobody has asked them where they get theirs.

# Leavings of the Fatlands

> Everything here is meat. So everything here is for sale by the pound.
>> — Renderers' Union saying, painted on the Chute's long ramp

[bigquote] The relic trade in the south is only butchery with better manners. We do not carve the god. We serve it. | — Mistress Brisket Annalow, to a Company auditor at the Chute

In the Fatlands nobody calls them relics. A relic is something a foreigner pays too much for. In the south a piece of the god is simply produce: it is grown, cut, weighed, stamped, salted and shipped, and the only questions anyone asks are what it weighs and what it is good for. The Board of Plenty requires every piece of godflesh to be declared and stamped, the only land on the Table that does, and the stamping-houses at the Chute keep a column in their ledgers headed *Remarkable*, for cuts that do more than feed. The column is long. The Renderers' Union handles it, at a premium, and asks the Board not to read it.

This makes the south's leavings the most available and the least trusted on the Table. Almost anything can be had in Sated for meat scrip, but almost everything is mislabeled. A barrel marked *pork, Low Tilth* may be pork. It may be what the field Reaped. A jar marked *orchard sap* may scream when opened. Fatlanders shrug at this. To them everything that grows is the god, and the god is good to eat, and if a cut of it does something wonderful besides, so much the better: that is what a breadbasket is for.

Carving in the south is agricultural. Leavings are reaped with sickles from fields that flinch, dug with spades from soil that bleeds, pruned from trees that scream, plucked from the elbows of the Laden by the Washers who tend them, and sliced, with real tenderness, from the volunteered bodies of the Second Helping. The worst of the work is not the blood. Fatlanders are not squeamish about blood. It is the recognizing: the root with a birthmark, the cut that tastes of a name, the hill that calls the harvester by a pet name only their mother used.

What the south fears is not the leavings' power but the hunger in them. Every Fatlands relic is food, in the end, and every one of them wants to be eaten, and every one of them makes the eater hungrier. Southern children are taught to wash their hands after touching a Remarkable cut and never to lick their fingers. Southern adults lick their fingers anyway. The Thin say that the Fatlands' whole export economy is a way of spreading Ummer's appetite to every table on the Rim, one barrel at a time, and the Board's answer has always been that the barrels sell.

Southern leavings travel well, in salt and fat and jelly. That is the danger. They keep their hunger wherever they go.

## From the Gut-lines and the Laden

[sigil: fatlands]

The Gut-lines took Ummer's stomach and entrails, and in their bodies the god grows like a crop. A Gut-line noble at the Course sheds shoots, sweats a sticky sweetness that draws bees, grows roots through the floor of their dray; at the Brink, they are a field; Seated, a hill. Everything that comes away from them is fertile, and the Board, which is made of them, has a hundred quiet laws about who may take it. Washers who tend the Laden are sworn to burn what they pluck. Washers are also paid very badly.

### A Tip of the Chairman's Root

Chairman Obb Tullow weighs nineteen hundred pounds and is moved by dray and eight oxen, or was, until last spring, when his feet rooted through a seam in the dray's iron floor. The Board has agreed not to notice. The dray has not moved since, and the Board now meets around it. What the Board does not know is that the roots go all the way down into the Weighhouse cellars, and that the Chairman can feel the Reaping through them, every one, as a warm full mouthful.

The roots are trimmed, where they can be reached, by a hoof-trimmer of the Board's stables who has sworn an oath on his mother's wake. He trims at night. The tips are white, glossy, finger-thick and warm, ringed like an earthworm, and they bleed a clear sweet sap that tastes of gravy and pennies. Held in the mouth, a tip pulses slowly, at the pace of the Chairman's heart. Those who have held one in a negotiation report that they felt very heavy and very calm, and that everyone in the room seemed, suddenly, to be leaning toward them.

The hoof-trimmer sells to the Renderers' Union. Mistress Annalow holds a tip in her cheek when she meets the Board.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); a Board clerk or Union officer, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A month wrapped in damp sacking. Dry, it shrivels to a white thread in a week. It travels, and keeps its weight.

**CARVING:** At night, in the Weighhouse cellars among the roots, with pruning shears (Hand + Craft vs Hard 14; Lung + Stealth vs Grim 18 to get in past the Bailiffs). The roots flinch when cut. A Lack means the Chairman, above, feels it: the floor heaves, and the carver is held to the ankles by roots (Gut + Endure vs Grim 18 to tear free, 1d6 Flesh).

**TAINT:** 3 Hunger.

**EFFECT:** Held in the cheek for a scene, a tip lends the Chairman's weight: the bearer cannot be moved, pushed, knocked down or Reaped, gains +2 to Intimidate and Persuade with anyone of the Fatlands, and, in any vote or assembly, is treated by everyone present as the heaviest person in the room. A tip lasts three scenes before it is chewed through. Swallowing it gives the Hunger at once, and the eater roots, for an hour, wherever they stand.

**WORTH:** 350 L. Rare. Taking any part of a sitting Board member without license is a crime against the vote, punished by the Gavage.

### A Strip of the Unending Cloth

Dame Suet Marrable's Unending Dinner has been in continuous service at Marrable Hall for eighty-one years. Guests have been born at her table, and several have been served at it. The table is three hundred feet long, and the cloth on it has never been changed, only added to: new linen laid over old as the old goes under, so that the cloth is now a thick, soft, many-layered thing, like a mattress of pressed pastry, soaked through with eighty-one years of drippings, wine, sauce and what the servers do not discuss.

The cloth is cut, rarely, when the table is extended. The strips are sold to Gut-line houses as heirlooms. A strip of Unending Cloth is a slab of yellowed linen an inch thick, heavy, greasy, smelling of roast and cloves and something older and sweeter, and it is never cold. Spread on any table, it changes the table. Food set on it does not cool. Guests seated at it do not want to leave. A dinner laid on Unending Cloth goes on.

Dame Marrable does not sell it herself. She gives strips to those she wishes to keep: debtors, rivals, daughters-in-law. Lord Chastain Loin has a strip in his dining room, and his guests stay for days.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) among the Gut-lines; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** Indefinite, kept greased. It travels, and its hunger does not fade. Washing it ruins it, and the water stinks of gravy for a mile.

**CARVING:** Cut from the table during the Dinner, which never stops (Lung + Filch vs Grim 18 with a carving knife, while seated as a guest, and a guest at Marrable Hall must eat five courses an hour or be noticed). On a Lack, the carver cannot rise from the table until the next extension, a month hence, and is fed throughout.

**TAINT:** 2 Hunger (to each guest at a meal laid on it).

**EFFECT:** At a meal laid on the cloth, each guest must make a Caul + Resolve roll vs Hard (14) to leave before the host rises; failure means they stay and eat, for another hour, and roll again. Hosts gain +2 to Persuade over guests who have eaten on it. A Fatlander who eats a full meal on it gains the Sop's benefit as if the meal were the flesh of the dead. Abroad, it makes any dinner a siege.

**WORTH:** 500 L. Rare. Legal; the most coveted wedding gift in Sated. Some families refuse it, politely, and then move house.

### Elbow-Cress

At the Course the Laden sprout. Small green shoots come up in the creases of the elbows and behind the knees, pale and tender, and the Washers pluck them every morning, and every night they grow back. They are supposed to be burned. In the Sated market they are sold by the bunch, under the counter, as *elbow-cress*, and they are delicious: peppery, juicy, faintly sweet, with a taste under the pepper like a lover's skin.

Elbow-cress heals. Eaten raw, a handful closes cuts and knits bruises overnight, and Sated's prize-fighters and Renderers' bruisers eat it like candy. It also feeds. The eater wakes, after a night of eating it, with their own elbows itching, and a craving for more that is very hard to explain to anyone who has not felt the Fatlands hunger. A Washer named Pelly Dripping was jailed for selling her lady's cress, and testified that the lady knew and was glad: it meant somebody else was eating the god for a change, and not her.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in Sated; Hard (14) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** Three days fresh; a week in cold water. Dried, it is only a weak tea that heals nothing.

**CARVING:** Plucked at dawn by a Washer, with fingers, from a sleeping Laden (Hand + Filch vs Routine 10 if a Washer; Hard 14 otherwise, because the Laden wake when it is pulled and say your name). Seeing the shoots come back as you watch is a Dread 1 check.

**TAINT:** 1 Hunger, 1 Regrowth (Ummer).

**EFFECT:** A bunch eaten (a full meal's worth) heals 1d6 Flesh by morning, or 2d6 if eaten before sleep, and removes the penalty of one festering wound. A Fatlander may count it as a Sop meal. Anyone who eats elbow-cress three nights running must make a Gut + Endure roll vs Hard (14) or begin to sprout themselves: tiny shoots at the elbows, harmless, which must be plucked daily and are worth 2 L a bunch.

**WORTH:** 5 L a bunch. Uncommon in Sated; Scarce elsewhere. Illegal to sell as food (it must be declared and stamped, and never is). The fine is a meal's weight in lacks.

### A Pear of Mother Rind's

Old Mother Rind rooted forty years ago, and now she is a small orchard hill outside Kettle Furrow, with nine pear trees growing from her back and a working mouth in the clover at her foot. Her lips are brown and cracked like bark and her teeth are wheat-teeth, many rows. Her village asks her advice on marriages. She is always right, and always hungry, and her fee is meat. Lately she has asked for particular people.

Her pears are small, brown-russeted and very sweet, with flesh the color of cream and a core like a tiny knuckle of bone. They are warm when picked. Eaten slowly, each one carries her voice: the eater hears, in the soft crunch of each bite, a dry old woman's laugh, and at the core, a sentence. It is an answer. It is always an answer to the question the eater most wanted to ask and did not.

Mother Rind gives them away, or so the village says. She gives them in return for meat. And she has begun, lately, to give them only to particular people, the same people she asks for, as though she were sweetening them first.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); Routine (10) in Low Tilth.

**SPOILAGE:** Two weeks off the tree; a season in honey, though the voice grows sly. Over the border, the voice fades within a month.

**CARVING:** Picked from her back, which is climbing on a woman (Lung + Athletics vs Routine 10, and asking first, or she bites). Picked without paying her fee, the pear is sour and the answer is a lie. Her fee is a joint of meat brought to her mouth, and lately a name; paying it is a Dread 2 check if the player knows whose name it is.

**TAINT:** 2 Hunger, 1 Dread.

**EFFECT:** Eating a pear gives a true answer, in Mother Rind's voice, to one question the eater is afraid to ask; the GM answers honestly, in a sentence, and may choose the question if the player will not. The answer is true. It is also, always, somehow about food. Each pear eaten by the same person makes the next more expensive: Mother Rind remembers them, and asks after them, and begins to ask for them.

**WORTH:** 40 L a pear in Kettle Furrow; 150 L in Sated. Scarce. Legal. The village of Kettle Furrow has been noticeably smaller since she started asking for people.

### Paunch's Chewed Weight

Dr. Emmerich Paunch, the Board's physician, opened a Gut-lines stomach under license to measure its capacity, and lowered a plumb line into it. He ran out of line at two hundred feet. When he drew the line up again, the lead weight on the end of it had been chewed. He has since obtained longer line. He has not been able to bring himself to use it.

The chewed weight is a cone of grey lead the size of a hen's egg, deeply scored with tooth marks: not one set but dozens, overlapping, some flat like grinders, some narrow like a child's, some long and curved like nothing that eats in daylight. It is wet. It is always wet, however long it is dried, and the wetness smells of gastric acid and warm bread. Laid on a table, it rolls toward the nearest person. Dropped down a well, it reaches the bottom, whatever the depth, and the line comes up chewed.

Paunch keeps it in a jar in his study, and someone, while he was at the Weighing, took it out and put a different weight in its place. He has not yet noticed. He hasn't wanted to look.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22). The Board's physicians, Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** It does not spoil. It does, slowly, get lighter, as if something were still eating it. In a decade it will be gone.

**CARVING:** Stolen from Paunch, or from whoever stole it (Lung + Filch vs Hard 14). A new one can be made only by doing what Paunch did: a plumb line lowered into a Gut-lines stomach, under license or otherwise (Hand + Stitching vs Grim 18 to open; the patient is awake), and what is at the bottom must be willing to chew. Opening a Gut-line and hearing an echo is a Dread 4 check.

**TAINT:** 1 Hunger, Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** Lowered on a line into any hole, well, pit or mouth, the weight reaches the bottom and tells the holder what is down there, by the marks it comes up with: depth, whether anything living waits, and whether it is hungry. Lowered on a thread into a sleeper's open mouth, it reports their exact Hunger and Regrowth to the holder. Lowered into the Trencher, or a Seated hill, it comes up with a single human tooth in the lead, and the GM decides whose.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Paunch would pay 2,000 L to have it back, and more to have it never mentioned. Unique.

## From the Fields and the Reaping

[sigil: fatlands]

The soil of the south is the god's flesh, and it grows flesh. Wheat comes up with teeth, roots come up with veins, and in Carving the fields take people and give back what they were. The leavings of the fields are the commonest in the south and the cheapest, because there is so much field. They are also the ones Fatlanders least like to talk about, because the fields are their families, quite literally, and the Reaping has doubled in ten years.

### Corner-Soil of the Dusk Acres

The Cullmasters send vagrants, debtors and troublemakers to work the far fields at dusk. Those fields are called the Dusk Acres, a belt of Board land rotated every Carving, and each plot carries a small numbered stone at its corner. They are the most productive fields in the country and the most carefully ploughed. Cullmaster Varn Sweetbread thinks of the whole business as agriculture.

The soil from a Dusk Acre corner, dug from under the numbered stone, is black, sweet-smelling and warm, with a greasy shine, and it moves. Very slightly, in the palm, it settles, and settles again, as if something under it were breathing. It sticks to the skin. Fatlanders do not keep it in houses. The Thin steal it to prove what the Board is doing: a pot of corner-soil, left on a windowsill in Sated, opens at dusk into a small wet mouth, and closes, and the windowsill is a little lower.

Mixed into a floor, a road or a doorstep, corner-soil teaches that ground to Reap. The Renderers' thugs call it *a dusk shift*, and use it in disputes.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Cullmasters and Thin know it at Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A season in a sealed iron pot. In wood or cloth it soaks through in a week. Abroad, it keeps its hunger, but the hunger is weaker: it Reaps only in Carving.

**CARVING:** Dug under a numbered stone at dusk, in a Dusk Acre in Carving, while the Reaping is under way (run the Reaping clock for each round spent digging; Gut + Labor vs Hard 14 to fill a pot). Digging up something besides soil is a Dread 3 check. It usually happens.

**TAINT:** 2 Hunger, Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** A pot of corner-soil, spread across a doorway or six paces of floor, makes that ground Reap at dusk for a week: anyone helpless, sleeping or bound on it at sundown must make Lung + Wayfaring rolls as for the Reaping (Routine, then Hard, then Grim, then gone). Anyone awake and moving gains a Dread 2 check from the feel of the floor. Spread in a field, it doubles that field's yield and its Reaping.

**WORTH:** 70 L a pot. Scarce. Possession outside the Cullmasters' office is "theft of Board soil." The Board has hanged no one for it, because a trial would require naming the soil.

### Bottled Rootkin Sap

What the Reaping takes, the field gives back as Rootkin: swollen root vegetables the size of a sleeping child, pale and veined and warm, with features. Most are lumpen. Some are exact. Pulled up, they flinch and leak a pinkish sap that smells of the person they resemble. Tansy Lard found one with her mother's birthmark and replanted it behind the house, and waters it every day. Others are less sentimental.

The Renderers' Union sells the sap in phials to Lastgate perfumers and grieving families. It is pink, thin and faintly warm, and it smells of one specific person: their skin, their hair, their soap. A drop on a pillow brings them back into the room for a night. A drop on the tongue brings back more: a memory, out of order, out of context, as vivid as a blow. The Union labels phials by village and does not label them by name, because the families would find them.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in the Fatlands; Hard (14) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** A year stoppered. Opened, the smell fades in a week.

**CARVING:** Pulled from a Rootkin bed (Gut + Labor vs Hard 14, against grasping shoots), then cut and drained (Hand + Stitching vs Routine 10). Recognizing the face is a Dread 3 check. A Lack while cutting means the Rootkin's eyes open.

**TAINT:** 1 Hunger, 1 Dread.

**EFFECT:** A drop on the tongue gives one of the Reaped person's memories (roll below). A whole phial drunk at once gives all six, and the drinker must make a Dread 3 check and gains The Kept Grief Derangement for a week, mourning themselves. Used as bait at dusk in a Reaping field, a phial poured on the soil guarantees the field takes the nearest person who smells of it.

| d6 | The Memory |
| 1 | A meal with family; the drinker can taste every dish, and is hungry for a day after. |
| 2 | A secret the Reaped kept: the GM gives one true fact about someone they knew. |
| 3 | Their last walk out to the field at dusk; the drinker knows the Dusk Acre's number. |
| 4 | Their wedding, or their child's birth; the drinker removes 1 Fray and weeps. |
| 5 | The moment the soil took them, from below. Dread 3. |
| 6 | Something they saw in the far field before the end: a scarecrow, closer, turned round. |

**WORTH:** 8 L a phial. Uncommon. Legal if stamped. Families who find their own dead in a phial on a Lastgate perfumer's shelf have been known to burn the shop.

### A Singing Cutting

Marigold Chine keeps the oldest grove in the Bleeding Orchards and refused the deafening. She sings to her trees through the pruning, while ten thousand others scream, and her trees have begun to sing back, in tune, in her voice. The other keepers, who cannot hear it, feel it in their teeth. She wants to learn what the trees are trying to say. She fears she already knows: the last verse is the wake-song, and it is about her.

A cutting from her grove is a whip of red-barked wood as long as a forearm, with three buds, wrapped in wet moss. It is warm. It bleeds, faintly, where it was cut. And it sings: very softly, in a woman's alto, one line of an orchard hymn, over and over, without breath. Planted, it roots in a night, and the young tree sings louder. Animals love it. Two-legged cattle stop and listen with their heads tilted. People sleep beside it like children.

Marigold sells cuttings only to people who promise to sing back.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); orchard-keepers know at Routine (10), by their teeth.

**SPOILAGE:** A month in wet moss; planted, it lives as long as an orchard tree. It travels, and sings in every land, though in the Fast it sings nothing at all and drops its leaves.

**CARVING:** Cut from Marigold's grove at pruning time in Grace, while the Bleeding Orchards scream (Hand + Craft vs Hard 14; Caul + Resolve vs Hard 14 to stand the noise without stuffing your ears). Cutting without her leave makes the grove fall silent, which every keeper feels at once. A Lack means the cutting sings the wake-song's last verse, with the carver's name in it. Dread 3.

**TAINT:** 1 Hunger. A Fatlander gains 1 Hunger each day the cutting is in earshot.

**EFFECT:** The song calms: animals within earshot will not attack unless hurt, two-legged cattle follow the cutting's bearer as a herd follows a drover, and listeners gain +2 to Dread checks (fear, not horror) while it sings. Planted, it grows a tree in a year whose fruit, eaten, quiets a Fatlander's Hunger by 1d3 without the Sop's compulsion. And the tree learns: in a year it sings in the voice of whoever planted it.

**WORTH:** 120 L. Scarce. Legal. Orchard-keepers will not buy one; they cannot bear the sound.

### A Lettered Hide

Sal Trotter walks herds to market that the buyers call cattle. They are heavy, docile, pinkish-white, with broad flat faces and soft lowing voices, and on the road, when nobody watches, they stand up. She has stopped naming them, because they started answering. Some have scars where a brand would be, and the scars are in the shape of letters.

At the Chute the hides go to the tanners, and the lettered ones are pulled out and sold. A lettered hide is a square of thick white leather, soft as kid, with a raised pink scar across it spelling part of a word, or a whole one. The words are names. Sometimes a first name, sometimes a village, once in a while a full name with a date, as though a brand had been written by someone who wanted the beast to remember. Rendered through a tannery, a lettered hide stays warm and smells faintly of milk. Worn against the skin, the scar itches. Worn for a day, it itches in the shape of the word.

Fatlanders buy them to find out who. Sal Trotter does not. She says she can guess, and that the herd is all the same name, really.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) at the Chute; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** Tanned, it lasts as leather lasts. Untanned, it rots in a week, and smells of a person.

**CARVING:** Bought from the Chute's tanners (Tongue + Haggle vs Hard 14), or taken from a beast on the drive (Hand + Blades vs Hard 14 to kill and flay; Dread 2, and Dread 3 if the beast stands up). Sal Trotter will kill anyone who touches her herd on the road.

**TAINT:** 1 Hunger, Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** A hide bearing a name lets its holder find what became of that person: carried for a day, it leads the holder (as a compass of itching) to the field where they were Reaped, or to their nearest kin. Worn as a jerkin, a hide of the herd marks the wearer as one of them: two-legged cattle follow, protect and obey the wearer, and on the road at night will stand and walk beside them, humming. The wearer gains 1 Hunger each night they are worn.

**WORTH:** 30 L, or 200 L if the name is a known one. Scarce. Legal if stamped *beef*. They are all stamped *beef*.

### First Marbling

At the turn of Carving comes Marbling, the first frost of the south, when the fat in the soil whitens and the plains look like a cut of meat from horizon to horizon. For one morning, before the sun softens it, the white fat stands up out of the ground in veins and ribbons, and the poor go out with spoons and buckets to skim it before it sinks back. First Marbling is the year's best fat. It is creamy-white, sweet, cool and very clean, and it melts at the touch of a palm. Rubbed into skin, it vanishes, and the skin stays warm for days.

Southern mothers rub it into children against the cold. Renderers' bruisers rub it into their knuckles and their bellies before a fight, because a body rubbed in First Marbling gives under a blow like soil under a spade, and does not bruise. Brother Glut's faithful baste themselves with it before an Offering. It makes them, they say, a better meal.

Rubbed in too often, it does not wash off. The skin goes soft, loamy and faintly mottled, field-colored, and keeps the print of a thumb for an hour.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in the Fatlands; Hard (14) abroad.

**SPOILAGE:** A season in a sealed crock, kept cool. In warmth it goes rancid in a week and smells of a wake.

**CARVING:** Skimmed at dawn on the first Marbling morning, before sunrise, with a flat spoon (Hand + Craft vs Routine 10; a crock a person). A Lack means the ground under the skimmer softens and the soil takes them to the knees (one Reaping roll, Hard).

**TAINT:** 1 Hunger, 1 Regrowth (Ummer) after the third use in a season.

**EFFECT:** A crock gives ten rubbings. Each rubbing grants Armor 1 (stacking with worn armor) against blunt weapons and fists for a day, removes cold-weather penalties, and makes the user's flesh soft as loam. After the tenth rubbing in a single season, the user's skin stays soft: permanent +1 Armor against blunt weapons, −1 to Stealth (they leave prints on everything they lean on), and an unmistakable warm, savory smell.

**WORTH:** 3 L a crock in the Fatlands; 15 L abroad. Common at home, Uncommon abroad. Legal, and stamped.

### A Trencher Creeper

The Trencher is the field where Ummer was eaten: a great shallow bowl of black soil a mile across, west of Sated, where nothing is planted and everything grows, in a dense tangle of toothed grain, veined roots and fleshy creepers that heal over any path cut through them in a night. At its center is a rise of soil shaped, from the air, very like a sleeping figure lying on its side. The Board forbids entry. Pilgrims of the Second Helping go anyway, and lie down, and are found weeks later, smiling and partly absorbed.

A Trencher creeper is a length of pinkish vine, thick as a thumb, smooth, warm and pulsing, with small round leaves like fingernails. It grips. Laid against skin, it curls, and fixes, and within an hour has sent fine white tendrils under the skin, and the bearer feels it as a warm, full, sleepy contentment that is nearly the opposite of the southern hunger. The Second Helping wraps it around the wrists of the Offered. Smugglers sell it to the desperate of other lands as the only thing on the Table that makes a Fatlander feel full.

It does. While it grows.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Second Helping, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A month in a jar of warm drip-fat. Once on skin, it lives as long as its host.

**CARVING:** Cut from the Trencher's edge (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14 past Board wardens; Hand + Blades vs Hard 14 to sever it, as it grips the cutter's wrist). Each hour in the Trencher is +1 Hunger. A Lack means the cutter lies down for a moment, smiling, and must be pulled out by others (Gut + Labor vs Grim 18). Seeing the half-absorbed pilgrims is a Dread 3 check.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Ummer).

**EFFECT:** Wound round a limb, the creeper roots in an hour. While it grows, the bearer feels no hunger at all: Hunger cannot rise above 3, the Wants of the Fatlands do not fire, and Starving penalties are ignored. Each week it is worn, roll Regrowth Taint, and the bearer must stand still on soil for an hour a day or lose 1 Flesh. Removing it after a week is Hand + Stitching vs Grim (18) and 1d6 Flesh, and the hunger returns all at once: +5 Hunger.

**WORTH:** 200 L. Rare. Trespass in the Trencher is a Board offense; selling its growth abroad is a Union matter. The Second Helping gives it away.

## From the Kitchens and the Knife

[sigil: fatlands]

The south's dead are served at the wake, by the family, with the best plates; to be buried uneaten would be the deepest insult to a life. So the south has no graveyards, and its holiest places are its kitchens. The leavings of this section are made, not grown: by cooks, butchers, chefs and feeders, from the god's flesh and the people's, with the same skill and pride a Lastgate Seamer brings to a restitching. Most are sold openly. All of them are delicious. That is the difficulty.

### Hob Gristle's Tasting Spoon

Hob Gristle, the Honest Butcher, can tell by taste what a cut used to be, down to the name: a mouthful of sausage tells him the pig, the field it ate from, and the name of anyone Reaped into that field. It has ruined him for the trade. He still joints at wakes, and apologizes to every joint, quietly, by name. He has already tasted his brother, in a pie in Sated, and has told no one.

He has worn out eleven tasting spoons in thirty years. They are horn spoons, dark and polished, worn thin at the bowl from his tongue, and the gift has soaked into them as gravy soaks into a board. His old spoons were thrown out with the kitchen scraps and fished out by an apprentice who understood. A Gristle spoon dipped in any dish and licked tells the licker what is in it, all of it, by name. Most buyers use one once, and put it in a drawer, and do not eat out for a while.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Sated; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** It never spoils, but the gift wears with use: after twenty tastings, it is only a spoon.

**CARVING:** Bought from the apprentice, now a cook named Ruddle Furrow (Tongue + Haggle vs Hard 14), or taken from Hob, who would give one to anyone who asked kindly and promised never to use it on a pie.

**TAINT:** 1 Hunger per tasting. Dread 2 the first time the spoon names someone the taster knew.

**EFFECT:** A tasting (Eye + Feast vs Routine 10) names every creature in the dish, where it grew or grazed, and, for any meat that was a person or was fed on one, the person's name. A Helping gives the field or the barrel's origin; a Grace gives how they died. Used on a Chute barrel, it reveals what the double stamp hides. Used on Rootkin, it names the face.

**WORTH:** 75 L. Scarce. Legal, but any Renderer who sees one in a customer's hand will remember the face.

### An Offered Portion of Brother Glut

Brother Glut, prophet of the Second Helping, is enormous, radiant and happy. He preaches that being eaten is the highest form of being loved, and he has volunteered parts of himself to prove it: his left hand, both ears, three toes, a long strip from the flank. The wounds are bound in clean linen and he shows them off like medals. The parts were cooked at Gladbelly with garlands and song, and most of them were eaten there. Not all.

An Offered Portion is a slice of the prophet, cured in salt and honey in the Second Helping's way, wrapped in vine leaves and tied with wheat-straw: a rosy, translucent, sweet-smelling sliver the size of a playing card. Eaten, it tastes of honey and a summer meadow and of nothing else in the world. And the eater, for the first time in their life if they are a Fatlander, stops being hungry. Not full. Loved. That is how the faithful describe it. A warmth that settles in the belly and spreads, and the absolute certainty that being eaten would be the kindest thing that could ever happen to them.

The widows at the back of Glut's meetings, holding their plates, have each eaten one.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Second Helping, Routine (10).

**SPOILAGE:** A year cured. It survives the Rim Road in its leaves.

**CARVING:** Given by the Second Helping to a convert, or stolen from Gladbelly's slaughter-pavilion (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14). Taking one from Brother Glut himself requires only asking: he will offer a slice gladly, and the carver must cut it (Hand + Stitching vs Hard 14; Dread 3 to cut a living man who is weeping with joy).

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Ummer), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Eating a portion empties the eater's Hunger to 0 and keeps it there for a day: no Partaking costs Hunger, no Wants fire. Then the eater must make a Caul + Resolve roll vs Grim (18); on failure they gain the Devotion Derangement toward the Second Helping and its teaching, and will volunteer something of themselves (a finger, a strip) at the next Gladbelly table unless their companions stop them. Non-Fatlanders feel the love and none of the relief.

**WORTH:** Never sold by the faithful. 300 L from thieves. Rare. Not illegal; the Board considers the Second Helping lawful and enthusiastic. That is the worst of it.

### Dellamore Aspic

Lady Aspic Dellamore's specialty is preservation. Her rivals are displayed in her gallery, set in clear golden jelly, garnished with herbs and quail's eggs, and blinking. The jelly keeps them; the Gut-line blood keeps them hungry. They watch visitors eat at her table. She is working on a new piece for the gallery, and has been measuring Dame Marrable.

Her aspic is the clearest on the Table: a golden, quivering, faintly sweet jelly clarified twelve times through linen and egg-white, set with a stock whose base she will not name. Anything set in it is kept as it went in: not dead, not living, simply held. A severed hand set in Dellamore aspic is a fresh hand a year later. A cut flower is a cut flower. A person is a person, awake, unable to move, unable to starve, watching. Dellamore sells small pots of it to surgeons and to collectors. She sells large pots to no one, and her rivals' families have stopped asking where their relatives are.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) among Gut-line society; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** The aspic holds as long as it is kept cool and unbroken: in a cellar, for decades. Warm or cracked, it melts in a day, and whatever was in it resumes exactly where it left off, including bleeding.

**CARVING:** Bought from Dellamore's kitchen (Tongue + Haggle vs Grim 18; she prices by the client's weight), or stolen from it (Lung + Filch vs Grim 18, past a gallery of rivals who watch every step and cannot raise the alarm except with their eyes). Walking her gallery is a Dread 2 check.

**TAINT:** Dread 1 (to use); Dread 3 (to set a person).

**EFFECT:** A pot preserves one severed part perfectly until it is reattached (no penalty to Stitching; a Seamer needs only a Routine roll). A larger vat holds a person in the state they were in when set: the bleeding stop, the dying do not die, the Reaped soil on their boots does not take them. A wounded companion set in aspic can be carried for weeks to a surgeon. They are awake the whole time. Each week set, they make a Dread 2 check.

**WORTH:** 50 L a pot; 1,200 L a vat. Scarce. Legal. Lady Dellamore is an artist.

### A Notched Stave

Quartermaster Ivo Stockpot feeds the army and stamps the export barrels at the Chute. He is portly, fussy and honest, and he has started marking a small notch on the barrels he would not eat from himself. There are a great many notches. He wants someone outside the Fatlands to know. He fears that if they knew, they would keep buying.

A notched stave is a single oak barrel-stave from the Chute, tarred inside, double-stamped on the outside, and carrying near the rim a small, neat, V-shaped notch cut with a penknife. The wood is soaked through with whatever was in the barrel, and it has kept a little of it. Dropped into a cooking-pot, a notched stave floats if the meat is honest beef, pork or mutton. If it is not, the stave sinks, slowly, to the bottom, and stays there, and the broth turns very slightly pink.

Reckoners carry notched staves into the Fatlands' customer lands. Purgation cells hand them out at the Rim Road inns. Stockpot does not know. He would be relieved.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) on the Rim; Grim (18) in the Fatlands, where nobody wants to know.

**SPOILAGE:** A year, before the wood dries out and forgets.

**CARVING:** Pried from a notched barrel at any Rim market (Eye + Search vs Hard 14 to find one; most notched barrels are emptied and burned by Union men).

**TAINT:** none. Dread 2 the first time it sinks in a meal you have eaten.

**EFFECT:** Dropped into any dish, stew or barrel for a minute, the stave reveals whether it holds human flesh or flesh fed on the Reaped: it sinks if so. No roll. A party carrying one never eats a Reaped meal unknowingly. A Fatlander who uses one finds the Hollow Plate Derangement easier to catch: Dread checks at meals are +2 difficulty for a week.

**WORTH:** 10 L abroad; nobody sells them in the Fatlands. Uncommon on the Rim. Illegal in the Fatlands as "slander of produce."

### A Gavage Funnel

At the Gavage, the Board's house of correction for the Thin, a hundred cells each hold an oak-and-iron chair, a set of leather straps, a funnel-stand and a drain. Its kitchens never close. Feeder Callow Pudge, soft-voiced and motherly, has polished her own funnel for thirty years and hums lullabies as she pours, and sincerely believes she saves lives. She keeps a book of the Thin she has fed, with their weights in and out, and letters from grateful families.

A Gavage funnel is a cone of tinned copper a forearm long, with a narrow spout wrapped in soft leather so that it does not break teeth. The leather is dark with years of porridge and lard. When held, the funnel is warm. When raised toward a mouth, it pulls a little, like a lodestone toward iron, and the mouth opens. Thirty years of forced meals have taught the funnels the god's own lesson: that every mouth wants to be fed, and every refusal is only fear. A funnel brought near a Fatlander makes them salivate. A funnel brought near one of the Thin makes them weep.

Callow Pudge has begun, secretly, to leave one cell door unbarred each Lack. She has also been seen to throw a funnel into the Little Gravy, and stand watching it sink.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in the Fatlands; Hard (14) abroad.

**SPOILAGE:** It does not spoil. Abroad, the pull fades after a season.

**CARVING:** Stolen from the Gavage (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14), or bought from a feeder who has had enough (Tongue + Persuade vs Hard 14).

**TAINT:** 1 Hunger per use, to the one fed. Dread 1 to the feeder.

**EFFECT:** A held, grappled or bound person can be fed through the funnel (Hand + Stitching vs Routine 10). The fed person cannot refuse what is poured: food, drink, medicine, poison, Tainted meat, a Purgation emetic. A starving companion who cannot keep food down can be saved with it (remove 2 levels of Starving). Used on a Fatlander, it counts as their Sop. Used on a Thin, it costs them 2 Fray, and a day of their fast.

**WORTH:** 12 L. Uncommon. Legal in Bailiffs' hands; owning one privately in Sated is, oddly, a mark of respectability.

### Hesk's Wake-Glaze

Wakemother Pudding Hesk is the most sought-after wake-cook in Sated. Families wait months for her. She bastes the dead with their own dripping and a little honey, roasts them slow over applewood, and sends them to the table glazed and crackling, and she weeps every time, openly, while she carves. She knows everyone's secrets, because the dead taste of them. She wants to cook the Chairman. She has been asked to.

Her glaze is the drippings of the roasted dead, reduced with honey, clove and a splash of sour wine from the family's own cellar, until it is a dark, glossy, sticky amber that smells like a feast-day and sets like toffee. She bottles the leftovers in small crocks and gives them to the bereaved, to put on their bread through the winter. Families say that while they eat it, they hear the dead at the table. Others have learned that brushed on anyone's meat, wake-glaze carries more than a memory: it carries a confession. The dead taste of their secrets. So does their glaze.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in Sated; Grim (18) abroad.

**SPOILAGE:** A season in a sealed crock; a year in Lack's cold.

**CARVING:** Bought from Hesk (Tongue + Persuade vs Hard 14, and she must like you), or skimmed from the roasting-pan at a wake (Hand + Filch vs Hard 14 under a grieving family's eyes). Taking it from the dead's own pan, at their own wake, is a Dread 1 check and a mortal insult if seen.

**TAINT:** 2 Hunger.

**EFFECT:** Eating a spoonful of a person's wake-glaze gives the eater one of that person's secrets (the GM chooses; on a Grace, the secret they would have most wanted kept). A Fatlander with Taste the Name gains two. A crock holds six spoonfuls. Brushed on the meat of a living guest at a meal, it gives nothing, but the guest must make a Caul + Resolve roll vs Hard (14) or confess one secret of their own before the meal ends, as if the dead were asking.

**WORTH:** 20 L a crock. Uncommon. Legal. Selling another family's glaze is a feud, and in the south feuds end at a wake.

# Leavings of Brinehollow

> What the sea gives back, it is only lending.
>> — salvager's proverb, Saltwick

[bigquote] Every relic in this country is something the water left behind on its way out. Be careful what you pick up. The water will want it back, and the water is in you. | — Hask Tarn, the Pilot, to a party of salvagers at the Mile's end

Brinehollow's leavings are the sea's lost property. They wash up, or they are coughed up, or they are found standing upright on the dry seabed with their hatches open. The country was a nation of fishers and wreckers for five centuries, and it has kept the fisher's habits now that its sea is gone: everything that comes out of the water is the finder's, by old salvage law; nothing that comes out of a person is anyone's, by older custom; and anything taken from the Tenanted is not spoken of at all, because it is rude to ask a returned person where they went, and ruder to ask what they brought back.

The trade lives in three places. Saltwick, thirty miles inland, is the open market for everything the Uncovered yields, and its salvage halls deal in leather-weed, wreck-timber, sea-glass and the odd gold ring from a skeleton's finger without much interest in what else they buy. Lowmark's Mile is where bodily leavings change hands: bail-water by the jar, Tenanted salt, the hair that falls out in the bath in long dark ropes. And the far Uncovered, past the Wreck Orchard, is where the dangerous things are found, by salvagers who go out with ropes and hooks and come back fewer.

Carving in Brinehollow is mostly collecting. The body gives up its water twice a day for nothing; the sea, leaving, gives up its floor. The hard part is what is attached. A salvager on the Uncovered must contend with Ribwalkers and cart-sized crabs and lights in empty wrecks. A bail-water collector must contend with the Netwatch, the Wreckwives and the Drowned Bench, all of whom take an interest in who is buying the water of the soon-to-be-Called. And anyone who carves from the Tenanted must contend with the Tenanted, who are calm, courteous, very heavy, and remember.

What the coast fears is the pull. Every Brinehollow leaving carries the sea's wish to go home, and the sea's home is west and down, and the people who carry its leavings find themselves standing at west-facing windows. Salvagers keep their finds in the east rooms of their houses. Old Brinehollowers anchor their relics as they anchor their sleepers, roped to the bed-frame. And everyone knows a story of someone who kept a pretty shell from the Uncovered on the sill, and woke one night already walking.

## From the Lung-lines and the Tenanted

[sigil: brinehollow]

The Lung-lines ate Dromm's lungs and now hold court from glass tanks; the Tenanted came back from the sea with someone else looking out. Between them they hold Brinehollow, and between them they shed the coast's most potent leavings: the water they live in, the hair they lose, the tears they weep, the silt they leave in courtrooms. None of these are taken by force. They are taken from drains, from bath-water, from the floors of the Sounding Hall, from the long hours in which nobody important is watching the servants.

### Tank-Water of the Prince

Prince Aurel Vasht, heir of the Lung-lines, is twenty-two and cannot breathe air for more than an hour. He rules from a glass tank in Fathomhouse and has learned to read lips through eight inches of green water. The tank is drained and refilled every week by servants in rubber aprons, because the prince's lungs breathe it as other lungs breathe air, and what he breathes out turns the water cloudy and cold and alive.

The drained water goes down a pipe to the cellars, where the Dowager Below is. Some of it goes, in buckets, out of the kitchen door. Tank-water is pale green, faintly glowing in the dark, so cold it aches in the teeth, and in it hang fine, slow, silver threads that coil and uncoil like breath made visible. It tastes of nothing. Drunk, it fills the lungs and stays there, and the drinker discovers that they can breathe it: that their chest has become, for a while, a prince's chest.

The servants call the trade *the prince's leavings* and think it harmless. The prince knows. He reads it on their lips.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Fathomhouse servants, Routine (10).

**SPOILAGE:** A day in an open bucket; a month in a sealed glass bottle kept cold and dark. Exposed to sun, the threads die in an hour and it is only seawater.

**CARVING:** Taken from the drain-pipe or the kitchen buckets on drainage night (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14 inside Fathomhouse). A Lack means the Dowager's hair comes up the pipe toward the bucket.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Dromm).

**EFFECT:** A cup drunk lets the drinker breathe water as easily as air for a day, and see in dark water as in dim light. While under its effect, the drinker gains +2 to Instinct and Search in or under water and can read lips through glass or water. For a non-Brinehollower, it also gives a Brinehollower's tide for the day: they must bail at dusk or begin to drown.

**WORTH:** 60 L a bottle. Scarce. Theft from the royal household; the Admiralty hangs for it, in theory.

### A Rope of the Dowager's Hair

Lady Ondine Vasht, the Dowager Below, lives in the flooded cellars of Fathomhouse and has not been seen whole in years. What can be seen of her, through black water by lamplight, is a vast grey shape folded into the vaults, skin like a drowned sail, and hair that drifts across the floor of three cellars. She sheds it. It comes loose in long dark ropes, like weed after a storm, and the servants who mop the stairs at the indoor tide's ebb find it wound around the newel-posts, and sell it.

Dowager hair is black-green, slick, thick as a finger when wet and thin as a thread when dry, and stronger than any rope spun in the Ropewalk. It cannot be cut by an ordinary blade; it parts only under a saw. Brinehollowers who can afford it have it braided into their anchoring ropes, because a sleeper roped with the Dowager's hair has never once been Called. The rope will not let them go. Wreckwives whisper that it is because the rope belongs to someone, and that someone is keeping them for herself.

Those who sleep tied with it dream of a long dark room full of water, and a grandmother's voice, and a hand stroking their hair.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) among the rich of the Headland; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** It does not rot. Dry, it becomes brittle in a year unless wetted with seawater once a week.

**CARVING:** Gathered from Fathomhouse's flooded stairs at low indoor tide (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14). Taken from the Dowager directly, under the water, with a saw: Lung + Athletics vs Dire (22), with her hair attacking as she defends it. Seeing one of her eyes open in the dark water is Dread 4.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Dromm), on each night slept in it.

**EFFECT:** A sleeper anchored with Dowager hair cannot be Called: no Calling roll is made. The rope cannot be cut except by a saw and a Grim (18) roll. But it is hers. Each night slept in it, roll a d6: on a 1, the sleeper wakes in Fathomhouse's cellars, wet, unharmed, roped to a pillar, with no memory of the walk. Lung-line characters roll a d6 and fail on a 1 or 2.

**WORTH:** 150 L a fathom. Rare. Theft from the royal household. Most of the Headland's finest anchoring ropes contain some.

### A Tenanted Tear

At the Brink, a Brinehollower's tears are salt enough to sting cuts. The Tenanted weep rarely, and when they do, it is said, it is the person underneath weeping, not the Tenant: a long slow tear, very heavy, that runs down the cheek and does not dry, and falls, and makes a sound when it lands like a pebble dropped into a well. The Wreckwives collect them. They sit with their returned spouses in the evenings and hold a glass phial to the cheek, and wait. Sennet Gull, whose husband came back kinder and lives in her cistern, has seven.

A Tenanted tear is a single drop of water in a stoppered phial, heavier than the phial, perfectly clear. It does not evaporate. Tilted, it rolls like quicksilver. Put to the eye, it lets you see the way the Tenant sees: through water, through flesh, into the deep of a person, where their sea is. The Wreckwives use them to look into each other and see how far gone they are. They use them, too, to look into their spouses, and see who is down there, and how small.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Wreckwives, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A tear lasts forever in its phial; it is used up when put to the eye.

**CARVING:** Waiting by a Tenanted person until they weep (Eye + Instinct vs Grim 18, and days of patience) and catching the tear before it lands. A Tenanted person asked to weep, and told why, may oblige (Tongue + Persuade vs Dire 22). Seeing who is underneath when the tear is used is Dread 3, and Dread 4 if it is someone you loved.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Dromm), Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** Placed in the eye for a scene, a tear shows the user the depth of every person in sight: their Regrowth stage, whether they are Tenanted, and if Tenanted, a glimpse of the person still underneath (whether they are there, and how much of them). It also shows anything hidden under water within a mile. The user's eye stings with salt for a day after (−1 to Search).

**WORTH:** 250 L. Rare. Wreckwives will not sell; tears change hands only when a Wreckwife dies, and the sisterhood hunts the buyer.

### A Cup of the Sounding Pool

The courthouse of the Drowned Bench is a round stone hall whose floor is a shallow pool, ankle-deep, fed by the indoor tide. The accused stand in the water. The Tenanted judges sit on a stone bench at the far side, utterly still. Justice Maren Oake, Called at thirty and returned after forty days, has never given an unjust verdict, and speaks in two tones at once, and the lower one sometimes finishes first. The pool is always cold. Witnesses say that when a lie is spoken in the Hall, the water rises.

They are right. The pool has been listening to testimony for four hundred years. A cup of it, carried out in a waterproof flask, still listens. Poured into a basin and set between two people, it lies flat as glass while the truth is spoken, and when a lie is told, it rises: a finger's width for a small one, over the rim for a great one, cold and green and smelling of the deep. Merchants in Saltwick use it to settle contracts. Lovers on the Mile use it to settle worse.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Lowmark; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** A season in a sealed flask; it loses its ear if it ever dries. Outside Brinehollow it hears only Brinehollowers.

**CARVING:** Scooped from the Sounding Hall's pool during a session, in plain sight of the Bench (Hand + Filch vs Grim 18). The Tenanted judges see it. They say nothing. On a Lack, Justice Oake turns her head and speaks the carver's name in the lower voice only. Dread 2.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Dromm).

**EFFECT:** Set in a basin, the water rises at every deliberate lie spoken within arm's reach of it, with no roll and no defense; Deceive rolls are impossible across it. Used in a negotiation, it gives +2 to Haggle and Clause for the honest party. A Tenanted person speaking across it makes it rise slowly, whatever they say; nobody knows whether this means they lie, or only that the sea in them is very deep.

**WORTH:** 180 L a flask. Scarce. Contempt of the Bench, if it can be proven; nobody has ever tried.

### A Sliver of the Beached God

Commodore Lysander Vell of the Admiralty keeps in his cabin aboard the propped *Gannet* a lead box holding a piece of Dromm's flesh, preserved since the Beaching, which still weeps cold water. He is a member of the Second Table, and he believes the god is reassembling at the bottom of the Trench. He has been eating a sliver of the flesh each Tablenight. He no longer needs to bail.

The flesh is grey-white, dense, translucent at the edges like a scallop, and cold enough to burn. It weeps constantly: a slow seep of clear, heavy water that fills the box an inch a day and must be poured off, and the water runs west. A sliver shaved from it with a razor curls like an apple-peel and quivers on the blade. Eaten, it tastes of the bottom of the sea: of pressure, of iron, of a cold so complete it is almost peace. The eater's chest goes quiet. The tide does not come. And something, very far down and very far west, turns its attention, slowly, toward the eater, the way a sleeper half-wakes at a sound.

The box has no lock. Vell has never thought it needed one.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22). The Second Table, Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** In its lead box, indefinite. A shaved sliver must be eaten within a minute or it melts into a cup of cold water that runs west across any floor.

**CARVING:** Shaved from Vell's piece, in his cabin aboard a fully crewed, propped warship (Lung + Stealth vs Grim 18; Hand + Blades vs Hard 14 to shave cleanly). A Lack means the razor slips, and the flesh closes over the carver's thumb: Gut + Endure vs Grim (18) to pull free, losing the thumb's skin. Handling the god's flesh is a Dread 3 check.

**TAINT:** 4 Regrowth (Dromm), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** A sliver eaten frees the eater from bailing for a year: their tide does not come, and they cannot drown. They gain Armor 1 against crushing and pressure, and ignore pressure days. Each sliver also adds one automatic Calling roll on the next licked-clean moon, at Dire (22), whatever the eater's land, and the GM notes that the god has seen them.

**WORTH:** Never sold. The Second Table would pay any price for the whole. Unique.

## From the Body's Tide

[sigil: brinehollow]

Twice a day every Brinehollower brings up the sea. It is the commonest leaving on the Table, a pint or two per person per tide, poured into gutters that run west. Most of it is nothing: cold, clear, faintly green water with a little sand in it. Some of it is not. The bail-water of the soon-to-be-Called turns sea-blue and heavy. The chests of the drowned turn out to have currents. Sometimes a person brings up something small and living, and sometimes a word, and sometimes a knife.

### A Tenanted Sweetheart's Bucket

Every Brinehollower carries a bail-bucket. Losing yours is like losing your shoes. Sweethearts exchange them. When one of a pair is Called, and comes back Tenanted, the returned one asks for their bucket at the door, and bails into it at dawn and dusk as they always did, and the water they bring up is deeper, colder and much heavier than before. The bucket learns this water. In a year it is heavier empty than full ones are.

A Tenanted sweetheart's bucket is an ordinary wooden or tin bail-bucket, often painted with names or hearts, stained green to the rim, and too heavy for its size. It is cold. Brinehollowers can feel it across a room. The Tenanted can feel it across a city. A bucket given away by a Tenanted spouse, as a sweetheart's token, binds the receiver to the returned one in a way the Wreckwives argue over: the Tenant always knows where its bucket is, and treats whoever holds it with the full courtesy it extends to its family. Sennet Gull's husband gave her his. She has started to prefer him. That is what frightens her.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); Wreckwives, Routine (10).

**SPOILAGE:** It keeps its weight as long as the Tenanted owner lives. If they are drained, it is only a heavy bucket.

**CARVING:** Given, by a Tenanted person, freely (it cannot be taken; a stolen bucket is only a bucket, and the Tenant comes for it, courteously). Persuading a Tenanted person to give it is Tongue + Persuade vs Grim (18), and is a Dread 2 check when they smile.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Dromm), each night it sits beside the bed.

**EFFECT:** The holder is treated by the bucket's Tenant as family: protected, helped, answered honestly about anything but the sea. Every Tenanted person who sees the bucket recognizes the bond and extends the same courtesy (+2 to all Tongue rolls with the Tenanted). A holder who bails into the bucket finds the sea easier to bring up: bailing takes half the time. And the Tenant always knows where the holder is.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Unique per Tenant. The Wreckwives call accepting one *marrying the water*.

### Reave's Shell

Dr. Isolde Reave, lung surgeon, opened a drowned chest at high tide and found that the water in it had a current. In the next one she found a shell, alive, of no known species. She has found eleven more since, in the chests of the Called brought home dead by the Netwatch. She keeps them in a tank. They have bred.

A Reave's shell is a whorled spiral the size of a thumb, pearl-grey outside, pink and wet within, and something lives in it that has never been seen whole: a soft pale foot, two fine feelers that taste the air, and a sound, very faint, like breath through a reed. Held to the ear, it does not make the sound of the sea. It makes the sound of lungs. And it wants to go back into a chest. Placed on the lips of a sleeping Brinehollower, it crawls in, down the throat, into the lungs, where it settles, and from then on it bails for them: it drinks the tide as it rises, every drop, and the sleeper never wakes gurgling again.

Dr. Reave has one in her own chest. She has noticed that she now coughs up, at dawn, very small shells.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Lowmark physicians, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A week out of salt water; indefinitely in a jar of brine. Once in a chest, it lives as long as its host.

**CARVING:** From a drowned Called body, in Reave's surgery or a Netwatch mortuary, at high tide (Hand + Stitching vs Hard 14). Bought from Reave, who will sell to anyone willing to report back (Tongue + Persuade vs Hard 14). Watching it crawl into a mouth is a Dread 2 check.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Dromm), once, on implanting; then 1 Regrowth Taint each season.

**EFFECT:** The host need never bail again, and cannot drown in their own tide. They breathe a little wet. Each season, roll a d6: on a 1, the shell has bred, and the host coughs up a dozen tiny shells at dawn, each of which can be given to another person. On a 1 rolled twice in a row, the host's chest begins to have a current, and their Calling rolls are +2 difficulty.

**WORTH:** 300 L. Rare. Not illegal; nobody has thought of a law. The Purgation's Wringer would burn every one.

### The Invitation, Transcribed

Nerys Colm walked into the surf up to her chin and then, uniquely, turned around. She heard the invitation in full and can repeat it. Nobody who has listened to her has stayed ashore. She lives alone, now, in a house on the old shoreline with no windows facing west, and speaks to visitors through a door, and does not say it.

Somebody wrote it down. In 627 a Salt Assay clerk, sent to take her deposition, sat with his back to her door and transcribed what she said in shorthand, and stopped after a page, and went home, and walked west that night. The page was found in his satchel by the Netwatch, who could not read shorthand. It has been copied since. A copy of the Invitation is a sheet of paper covered in a tight, slanting clerk's shorthand, water-stained, smelling of salt; read aloud, by anyone who can read the old Assay hand, it becomes a voice. Listeners say it is not words so much as being known: something patient and kind and very, very old, which has always known your name, telling you that you may come home now, and that you will not be cold.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22).

**SPOILAGE:** The paper keeps. Every copy made by hand is weaker than its original; a copy of a copy of a copy calls only Lung-lines.

**CARVING:** Copied from an existing copy (Eye + Lore vs Grim 18 to read the shorthand; Hand + Craft vs Hard 14 to copy it). Or taken from Nerys Colm herself, who will speak it to anyone who asks three times, and will weep afterward. Reading or hearing it is a Dread 4 check.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Dromm), to everyone who hears it read.

**EFFECT:** Read aloud (one minute), the Invitation forces every listener with Lung-line blood, or brine in them (see Dry Jonah's tonics), to make a Calling roll at once, at Dire (22), regardless of anchoring; failure means they rise and walk west, now. Others make a Dread 4 check and must resist the Pull derangement for a night. The reader is not immune. It has been used as a weapon twice. Both times the Netwatch could not haul back enough.

**WORTH:** Never sold openly. Unique per copy (perhaps five exist). Possession is treason under Admiralty law; the Deepening would treasure one, and Mother Limpet has said she does not need it.

### The Little Knife from Below

Ottiline Hawse, Mistress of the Ropewalk on Mile Three, keeps a ledger of every anchoring rope cut in Lowmark and can tell by the cut which ropes were cut by the sleeper, which by the beloved, and which by no hand at all. Forty of the ropes she sold last year were cut by the same small blade, angled from below. She knows the blade. She has not said whose it is.

It is a short, slender knife with a curved blade no longer than a finger, the kind net-menders use, with a handle of grey bone gone smooth and green from wet. It is always damp. It is found, again and again, in sleepers' bedclothes, under the mattress, in the toe of a boot beside the bed: wherever it can be reached by a hand from below. Each time it is found, it is thrown into the gutter, which runs west. Each time, a few weeks later, it is found again, in another bed.

The Wreckwives believe it is a Tenant's tool, sent ahead. The Netwatch believes it is a person. Ottiline Hawse believes it is a child's knife, and that is why she will not say.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Ropewalkers and Netwatch, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** It does not rust or rot. It cannot be kept: each Plate, roll a d6; on a 1, it is gone from wherever it was locked, and the GM knows whose bed it is under now.

**CARVING:** Found in a bed after a Calling (Eye + Search vs Hard 14). Taking it from the hand that holds it at night is Lung + Filch vs Dire (22), and the hand, the GM decides, may be the taker's own.

**TAINT:** Dread 2, each night it stays in the house.

**EFFECT:** The knife cuts any rope, chain or binding with a single stroke, including Dowager hair and the Anchorites' iron; no roll. It also cuts promises: used to sever a cord tied between two people as a token, it ends one oath between them, unkept and unbroken. But each night it is in the bearer's house, the GM rolls a d6; on a 1, a sleeper in the house (the bearer included) wakes with their anchoring cut, and must make a Calling roll.

**WORTH:** Never sold; nobody keeps it long enough. Unique. The Netwatch pays a tally for every sighting.

## From the Uncovered and the Deep

[sigil: brinehollow]

A new mile of seabed is exposed every year, and with it six centuries of wrecks, bones, reefs gone to rot and things that came up as the water fell and did not die properly. By old salvage law, what the sea gives up belongs to the finder. By newer custom, salvagers do not go out alone, do not go past the Wreck Orchard after dark, and do not bring home anything that is still warm. Saltwick's salvage halls buy everything else.

### Ribwalker Bellows

On the Uncovered there are things with too many ribs: cages of long curved bones, thirty or forty pairs, opening and closing like slow bellows, mounted on legs that might once have been the ribs of something else. Inside each cage is a grey sack that pulses. Ribwalkers move toward water, any water, and they press it out of whatever they catch: a salvager held in the closing cage loses the water of their body a cup at a time, and is found afterward flat and dry as a pressed flower.

The grey sack is the bellows. Cut out and cured in salt, it is a fist-sized bladder of rubbery grey hide with a tube of soft bone at each end, and it still pulses, slowly, when warm. Pressed to a mouth, it draws out water. The Netwatch learned this first: a Called man dragged from the surf with his lungs full can be saved with a Ribwalker bellows, pumped by hand, faster than by any bailing. Every Netwatch boat carries one now. Every Netwatch hooker has stories of the times it drew out more than water.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in the Netwatch; Hard (14) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** A year salted. Unsalted, it rots in a week and smells of a beach in Plenty.

**CARVING:** From a Ribwalker, killed or disabled (Hand + Blades vs Hard 14 to cut the sack free intact, inside the cage). A Lack means the cage closes on the carver's arm: 2 Flesh a round until freed (Gut + Labor vs Hard 14). Seeing the dry thing still in the cage's last catch is Dread 2.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Used on a drowning or un-bailed person (Hand + Stitching vs Routine 10), the bellows clears their lungs in a round, saving them; it counts as bailing. Used as a weapon on a grappled or helpless foe (Hand + Brawl vs their Guard), it draws out 1d6 Flesh of water; each Helping adds 1. Used on a Tenanted person, it draws out a gallon a round, more than the body can hold, and the Tenant asks, politely, that you stop.

**WORTH:** 45 L. Uncommon in Lowmark. Legal; Netwatch issue.

### Lobbett's Silt

Cray Lobbett, a scavenger on the far Uncovered, found a stone table on the seabed, older than the Gorging, with eight chairs. Seven were pushed in. The eighth was pulled out a little, as if someone had just stood up, or was about to sit down. He has not been able to sell the location, or to stop going back. Salvage crews have searched for it for two years. Three did not come back.

Lobbett brings back silt. He fills his pockets with it every trip, from under the eighth chair, without meaning to, and empties them in Saltwick taverns, and sometimes sells a twist of it for drink. Lobbett's silt is fine, grey and soft as flour, and dry, although everything else on the far Uncovered is wet. It smells faintly of bread. Held in a closed hand, it is warm, the way a chair is warm after someone has risen from it. A pinch of it scattered on any table makes the room uneasy: diners look up, all at once, toward the empty end of the table, as though someone were expected.

Lobbett says the silt is how he finds his way back. He says he wishes it weren't.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18) in Saltwick; Dire (22) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** It never changes. It is never quite all used up: a pocket emptied of it has a little in the seams the next morning.

**CARVING:** Bought from Lobbett (Tongue + Haggle vs Routine 10; he wants to be rid of it), or gathered at the table itself, which requires finding it (see Ilse Marl's Chart in the Rim Market). Sitting in one of the seven chairs is a Dread 3 check. Touching the eighth is a Dread 5.

**TAINT:** Dread 2, each time it is scattered.

**EFFECT:** The holder always knows the direction and distance to the stone table, anywhere on the Table, and can never be lost on the Uncovered (no Wayfaring rolls needed there). Scattered on a table at a meal, it makes everyone present glance at the same empty place: the scatterer gains +2 to Filch and Stealth for the scene while all eyes are elsewhere. Scattered on one of Orrum's tables, it does something the GM should decide in advance, and should not explain.

**WORTH:** 10 L a twist from Lobbett, who undercharges. Scarce. The Reckoners of the Fast pay a great deal more, and ask where it came from, and do not leave.

### A Bladder of Trench-Breath

Thirty miles west of Lowmark's last pier the Uncovered ends at a cliff edge, and beyond it lies black water that has not dropped at all, an abyss with a current that runs downward. From the edge you can hear it draining: a low, endless sound like a throat swallowing. Salvagers who go to the brink report a warm updraft that smells of breath.

They are not imagining it. The air that rises from the Trench is warm, damp, and has been somewhere. A pig's bladder held open at the edge and tied off quickly traps a lungful of it; the bladder stays warm, swells and slackens slowly, in and out, as though it were breathing on its own. Opened under the nose, it smells of a sleeping mouth, and of the deep, and of something sweet underneath. Brinehollowers who inhale it feel the tide in their chest go still and listen, as a dog listens to a whistle; the Hunger in them drops to nothing. Then they find they are facing west.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) among far salvagers; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** A month; the bladder goes cold and limp when the breath has gone out of it.

**CARVING:** At the Trench edge (two days across the Uncovered; Lung + Wayfaring vs Hard 14 each day). Catching the breath is Hand + Craft vs Routine 10, but standing at the edge is a Dread 3 check, and a Lung-line character must make a Calling roll at Hard (14) or begin to walk down the cliff.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Dromm), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Inhaled, it removes all Hunger from a Brinehollower (or anyone with Dromm in them) and gives +2 to the next Godsign roll. Then the inhaler makes a Calling roll that night at +2 difficulty. Released in a closed room, the breath makes every Brinehollower there turn to face west for a minute, motionless, listening, and lose their next action; those who fail a Caul + Resolve roll vs Hard (14) hear the draining sound in their own chest for a week.

**WORTH:** 120 L. Rare. Legal. The Deepening buys every one and opens them on Gullcry Strand at procession time.

### A Figurehead's Eye

Eight miles out on the Uncovered, in a shallow basin, nearly two hundred wrecks of every age stand upright on the mud in loose rows, as if parked by a harbormaster. Nobody can explain why they are upright, or why their hatches are all open, or why their figureheads all face west. Lantern-light can be seen in the oldest ones at night, which are empty.

Salvagers prize the figureheads' eyes. Each is a disc of painted wood or set glass, the size of a palm, prised from a carved face: a mermaid, a queen, a dog, a saint, a weeping woman with her hands to her breast. Taken from the Wreck Orchard, the eye goes on looking west. It turns in its setting, or in the hand, or in a drawer, until it faces the sea. Captains of the propped fleet at Keelrow keep them on their sea-chests as a compass. And sometimes, at night, the painted pupil of a figurehead's eye shows a tiny lit window, as though the wreck it came from had a lantern burning in it, and someone were standing at the glass.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) on the Mile; Hard (14) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** Indefinite. The painted ones flake within a decade.

**CARVING:** Prised from a figurehead in the Wreck Orchard (Lung + Athletics vs Hard 14 to climb the bow; Hand + Craft vs Routine 10 to prise it free). At night, a Lack means the lantern in that wreck goes out, and something walks down the inside of the hull toward the bow. Dread 3.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Dromm), on each night it is looked into.

**EFFECT:** The eye always shows the direction of the retreating sea (and, increasingly, of the Trench), unaffected by fog or magic. The bearer gains +2 to Wayfaring on the Uncovered. Looked into at night (Eye + Search vs Grim 18), its lit window shows the inside of its own wreck as it is now, and whoever is in it; a Grace shows their face.

**WORTH:** 25 L. Uncommon on the Mile. Legal under salvage law.

### A Longslab Stone

Longslab, the beach of the Beaching, where Dromm was hauled ashore on ten thousand hooks and took four days to stop moving, is permanently wet, though no sea has touched it in a century. Water seeps up through the shingle as if the beach were a sponge someone is always pressing. At the tide-turns the wet ground breathes: a slow sigh across two miles of stones.

A Longslab stone is a flat grey pebble of the beach, smooth and sweating. It never dries. Wrapped in a cloth, it soaks the cloth in an hour; set on a table, it leaves a ring that spreads. The water it sweats is the god's, coming up from wherever the god's blood went into the shingle six centuries ago: cold, very salt, faintly pink in good light. Pressed to a drowning person's chest, it pulls the water out of them, the way the beach pulls; pressed to a wound, it draws out the blood, cleanly, and closes it. Gulls will not land near one. Children on the Mile dare each other to lick them.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in Brinehollow.

**SPOILAGE:** A stone sweats for a year after it leaves the beach, then dries, cracks and is only a pebble. Outside Brinehollow, a season.

**CARVING:** Picked up from Longslab (no roll), but standing on the beach at a tide-turn is a Dread 1 check for anyone, and a Lung-line character must resist the Want *The West* at Hard (14). Pilgrims of the Deepening lying face-down on the shingle resent collectors.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Dromm), on each use.

**EFFECT:** Pressed to a chest, it bails a helpless person in a minute (counts as bailing, no roll). Pressed to a wound (Hand + Stitching vs Routine 10), it stops bleeding and heals 2 Flesh. Carried on a pressure day, it gives the bearer an hour's warning and +2 to Endure against the pressure. Used too long, it pulls water from whoever holds it: a bearer who keeps one in a pocket for a month loses 1 Flesh and gains a terrible thirst.

**WORTH:** 2 L. Common in Lowmark. Legal; considered unlucky.

## Preparations of the Coast

[sigil: brinehollow]

Brinehollow's crafted leavings are the work of smugglers, monks and priests, the three trades that have most to do with what goes in and out of people. They are the coast's most exported goods after salt, and two of them are the reason the Vigil's Slope has begun walking west.

### Dry Jonah's Blue Label

Captain Jonah Skerry, called Dry Jonah, bottles what Brinehollowers cough up and sells it inland as tonic. It sells well in the Vigil. He has never asked what his customers start to dream about. His ordinary brine tonic heals and is sold on every Rim market (see the Rim Market). His Blue Label is not ordinary.

Blue Label is bail-water collected from people in the last week before their Calling, when the water Tolly Brack reads goes from green to a deep sea-blue with a weight to it, like water pulled from a hundred fathoms. Jonah's collectors follow Tolly on his rounds, and buy the blue water from the families, who do not want it in the house. Bottled in cobalt glass with a hand-drawn blue label, it is cold, viscous and faintly luminous, and it tastes of tears. In the Vigil, Rook Quarter-Bell cuts it into his rouse on the Slope, and his customers stay awake for twice as long. They have also started walking west.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) on the Slope and the Mile; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** A year in cobalt glass; it goes cloudy and inert in sunlight.

**CARVING:** Bought from the family of someone about to be Called (Tongue + Haggle vs Hard 14; Dread 2, because they are in the next room, standing at the west window). Or bought from Jonah (Tongue + Haggle vs Grim 18).

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Dromm). Addiction: Grim (18).

**EFFECT:** A dose drunk removes 4 Weariness (see the Vigil) and holds Weariness gains at bay for a full day; it also removes 1d6 Fray, as a vice, with the usual risk of habit. The drinker dreams of the sea while awake, all day, at the edge of sight. On a failed Regrowth Taint roll, they must also make a Calling roll that night, as a Brinehollower of their Regrowth would, even if they have never seen the coast.

**WORTH:** 25 L a bottle in Lowmark; 80 L in the Vigil. Scarce. Not illegal anywhere, because no law imagines it. The Wreckwives would drown Jonah in a barrel of it.

### A Link of Father Ebb's Chain

Father Ebb, Abbot of the Anchorites, has been chained to the same rock on the Hanging Abbey's cliff for fifty-one years. He has felt the Calling every night of them. He says the chain is not what holds him, and will not say what does. Every decade or so the salt eats through a link, and the brothers replace it, and the old link is given to a pilgrim who has climbed the cliff to ask for it.

A link of Ebb's chain is a ring of black iron as broad as a palm, crusted white with salt, rusted to lace at one side where it parted. It is cold and wet. Worn on a cord, it is heavy. The pilgrims who wear one say they sleep unanchored and are not Called, and the Anchorites do not deny it. But the brothers who climb down to the pilgrims say, gently, that the link only works for those who wear it as Father Ebb wears his chain: as a reminder, not a rope. They will not explain what that means. Pilgrims who misunderstand it are found on the Uncovered, still wearing it.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) along the coast.

**SPOILAGE:** It rusts away over a decade; in the Abbey's salt rain, a year.

**CARVING:** Given at the Hanging Abbey to a pilgrim who climbs the cliff (Lung + Athletics vs Grim 18; a fall is a long one), and asks (Tongue + Rites vs Hard 14). Climbing past a thousand chained monks coughing in unison at dusk is a Dread 2 check.

**TAINT:** none.

**EFFECT:** The wearer gains +4 to Calling rolls and to resisting *The West* and *The Water*, but only while they hold to a reason to stay ashore: a living person they would not leave, a vow, a task. The player names it when they first wear the link. If that reason is lost (the person dies or is Called, the vow is broken, the task done), the link gives −4 instead, until a new reason is named at the Abbey. Ebb, asked about this, says that is what holds him.

**WORTH:** Never sold by the Anchorites; 100 L from pilgrims who have given up. Scarce. Legal.

### A Deepening Lantern

Mother Limpet, prophet of the Deepening, has baptized two thousand by walking them into the sea at night. She says she will follow when the last one is safely under. She is eighty-four. On procession nights the road of crushed white shell from Gullcry Strand toward the far water is lit at every post, and the faithful walk it in lines, singing, with lanterns, and the lanterns go under with them, still lit.

They do not go out. The Deepening's lanterns are made of horn and brass, their wicks soaked in a lamp-oil rendered from the fat of beached fish and blessed on Longslab, and they burn under water. Salvagers find them on the Uncovered, among the leather-weed, still burning, years after their bearers walked in. The flame is blue-green, small and steady. In its light, Brinehollowers feel no fear of the sea at all; they feel welcome. The Netwatch smashes every one it finds. Lieutenant Sable Reef, who has pulled three hundred Called off the shore with hook and rope, keeps one, unlit, in her quarters. She has begun to wonder what she is saving them from.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) on the coast.

**SPOILAGE:** A filling of the oil burns for a year, in air or water. Refilled with ordinary oil, it is an ordinary lantern.

**CARVING:** Found lit on the Uncovered (Eye + Search vs Hard 14 at night). Taken from a procession walker (Hand + Filch vs Hard 14; they will not resist, and will smile at you, Dread 2). Given by Mother Limpet to a willing pilgrim (no roll).

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Dromm), each night it is lit.

**EFFECT:** The lantern burns under water and in any wind, lighting ten paces. Anyone in its light gains +2 to Dread checks caused by the sea, water or the Tenanted, and the Called in a procession are calm and will not resist being roped out of line if the lantern is carried with them (no roll needed to rope one free). But Brinehollowers in its light for a scene must resist *The West* at Hard (14).

**WORTH:** 35 L. Uncommon. Netwatch confiscates on sight; possession is "aiding a Calling," a fine of ten tallies.

# Leavings of the Vigil

> Anything that helps you rest is contraband. Anything that keeps you up is currency. Everything else is lamp-oil.
>> — Pinchmarket saying

[bigquote] They say a relic of the god of sleep should be soft. Ours are needles, bells and mirrors. The soft ones we burn, and the ones we cannot burn we lock in the Rattlehouse, and still people queue at its door. | — Captain Brannoch Pyre of the Wakers, testimony to the Watch of Watches

The Vigil does not trade in relics so much as fight a war against them. Every leaving of Iss is, at bottom, a piece of rest: a softness, a warmth, a stillness, a way of going under and coming back. The law of the Lid makes such things capital crimes, and the Wakers hunt them with rattles and needles, and the Watch of Watches keeps the most dangerous in a sealed tin room under the Rattlehouse that is said to be the only quiet place in the city. And the city wants them more than it wants anything. A people who have not slept in three hundred years will pay a year's wages for a minute's peace. The Tuckers have built a whole criminal economy on that sentence.

So the Vigil's leavings come in two kinds, and the city treats them as opposites. There are the leavings of waking: things that hold the lids up and the eyes open, made from the lidless Eye-lines, from the lamps and the bells, from the Wakers' long needles and the Bitterhouse's black stills. These are sold openly in the Pinchmarket and the city is proud of them. And there are the leavings of the room: pieces of the Dormitory, the Pillow, the Sleepers' Mile, the dream itself, which promise rest and deliver it, and come with a price written in the long room's ledger. These are sold in the Slope's back lanes, under tin, by people who do not look you in the eye, because looking at someone too long makes them yawn.

Carving here is less a matter of knives than of nerve. Most of the Vigil's leavings must be taken from places where sleep is strongest: from the Dormitory's warm breathing halls, from the soft hollow of the Pillow, from beside the Counted. Every minute spent there adds Weariness. A carver who goes in tired does not come out. The Tuckers carve best, because they are paid to stand beside sleep and not succumb; Dream-Divers carve deepest, because they go into the room itself and are dragged back.

What the Vigil fears most is not a relic's power but its comfort. A leaving that makes the user feel warm, safe or rested is treated by every Vigilant as a loaded crossbow: useful, beautiful, and pointed at the user's own head. Pinchmarket dealers wrap such goods in burr-cloth so that they scratch. Buyers carry them with a pin through the palm.

## From the Eye-lines

[sigil: vigil]

The Eye-lines ate Iss's eyes, still shut, and their descendants are born lidless and call it purity. A lidless eye must be wetted by hand every half minute, and it sees everything, and forgets nothing. What the Eye-lines shed is the residue of seeing: the tears that run from eyes that cannot close, the brushes that wet them, and, in the families born with lids, the lids themselves, which shrink and fall away in the Course like burnt pages.

### A Wetting-Brush of the Warden-Prime

Warden-Prime Ilvane Stroud has not shut her eyes in fifty-eight years. Two attendants brush them wet every half minute, day and night, with fine sable brushes dipped in a cup of warmed salt water. She has seen everything that has happened in front of her since birth, and she can forget none of it. Her brushes wear out in a month. They are burned, by law, in the presence of a Watcher. Her attendants are paid in rouse.

A worn brush is a slim handle of pale bone, carved with an open eye, and a head of black sable gone stiff and grey-tipped with salt. It smells of tears. Its bristles have touched the most watchful eyes on the Table forty thousand times a month, and some of the Warden-Prime's seeing has dried into them. Brushed across the user's own eyes, open, the brush lets them see as Stroud sees: everything, all at once, in perfect order, and keep it. Clerks of the Watch who have tried it say the memory is unbearable after a day. Spies who have tried it say it is worth it.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Watch clerks and attendants, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A brush holds its virtue for ten uses, or a month, whichever comes first.

**CARVING:** Taken from an attendant before the burning (Tongue + Persuade vs Grim 18, or a great deal of rouse). Taken from the burning itself, in front of a Watcher (Hand + Filch vs Dire 22). A Lack means the Warden-Prime sees you do it. She will remember your face in perfect detail for the rest of her life.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Iss).

**EFFECT:** For a scene after brushing, the user has perfect recall of everything they see: they may later recall any detail of it exactly (no roll), and gain +2 to Search. For that scene they cannot blink, which means no blink checks, and they gain Weariness 1 at the end of it. A user who brushes three times in a day must make a Dread 2 check from the sheer accumulation of seeing.

**WORTH:** 140 L. Scarce. Theft of the Warden-Prime's person; a Rattlehouse matter.

### A Pair of Fallen Lids

At the Course, a Vigilant's lids thin, shrink and are gone: first a translucence, then a shrinking, the lids drawing back like a burnt page curling from its edges, and then they fall away, a pair of soft, dry, pinkish crescents, light as moth wings, usually found on a pillow that is not supposed to exist or in the dust of a stair. Eye-line midwives collect them. The Tuckers buy them.

A pair of fallen lids is kept in a twist of oiled silk, and they are faintly warm, and they still twitch, now and then, as though trying to blink. Laid over a person's open eyes and held there with a drop of gum, they close. And the person under them sees darkness for the first time since they were four years old, and does not fall asleep. Not at once. The lids remember being shut without sleep, a trick no Vigilant body knows. For the length of a held breath, a little longer, a person under them can rest their eyes. Those who have done it describe it as the most beautiful thing that has ever happened to them, and some of them lie, afterward, about how long they kept them on.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Tuckers and midwives, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A season in oiled silk; then they crumble.

**CARVING:** Gathered after they fall (Eye + Search vs Hard 14 in an Eye-line house), or bought from a midwife of the Height (Tongue + Haggle vs Grim 18). Peeling them early, before they fall, is a crime and a horror (Hand + Stitching vs Grim 18; Dread 3) and they are useless.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Iss), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Worn for a count of sixty (once per day), the lids give the Lull's benefit (remove 1d6 Hunger, and also 2 Weariness and 1 Fray) without the blink check at Hard; instead the user makes a blink check at Routine (10). If the user keeps them on past sixty, every further count of ten adds one step to the difficulty, and on a failure they are in the room, and their nudger must fetch them as if they had fallen asleep.

**WORTH:** 400 L. Rare. Possession is "equipping for sleep," a capital crime. Old Nod is said to own a drawer of them.

### Lidless Tear-Salt

The lidless weep constantly, not from grief but because an eye that cannot close must be kept wet, and the body tries. Their attendants catch the tears in little silver cups, and in the Height the tears are dried in the sun of the Bonfires into a fine, sparkling, bitter salt, like crushed glass. It is called tear-salt, and it is the most expensive seasoning on the Table, and the Eye-lines eat it on everything.

Tear-salt keeps the eyes open. A pinch on the tongue is as good as a rouse-tab; a pinch in a cup of rouse makes the rouse hold twice as long; a pinch rubbed into the eye stings like a needle, and the eye stays open, unblinking, for an hour. The Bitterhouse buys it by the pound. The Slope can afford it by the grain, and buys it anyway. In the last decade, as the rouse dose has risen, so has the price of tear-salt, and so has the number of Eye-line attendants found with their own eyes rubbed raw, trying to make more.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in the Vigil; Hard (14) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** Indefinite, kept dry.

**CARVING:** Collected from a lidless person over a day (Hand + Craft vs Routine 10 with a silver cup), with their consent or while they are held. Taking it from the unwilling is cruelty the Vigil pretends not to notice (Dread 1).

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Iss), after the third pinch in a day.

**EFFECT:** A pinch on the tongue removes 1 Weariness and halts Weariness gains for an hour, without the Addiction roll of rouse; added to a rouse dose, it doubles the dose's duration. Rubbed into the eyes (1 Flesh), it makes the user immune to blink checks for an hour, and gives −1 to Search from the stinging.

**WORTH:** 5 L a pinch; 300 L a pound. Uncommon. Legal and celebrated.

## From the Dormitory and the Room

[sigil: vigil]

Sixty thousand beds, numbered, in rows, completely silent. The Counted breathe in time. The leavings of this section are taken from the place everyone in the Vigil fears and longs for most: from the Dormitory's halls, from the long room in the dream, from the Pillow where Iss lay, and from the Sleepers' Mile, where the refugees of 342 lie under the heather. All of them are illegal. All of them make the user sleepy. Every carver who takes them gains Weariness doing it.

### A Breath of the Dormitory

The Counted all breathe in time: sixty thousand chests rising and falling together, slow, deep, perhaps four breaths a minute, and the air in the halls moves with them like a tide, a long warm sigh drawn in through the doors, a long warm sigh pushed out. Standing in the center of a hall, a visitor feels it on their face. Orderlies wear wax in their ears and keep their eyes on their boots.

A breath of the Dormitory is caught in a bladder at the exhalation, when the sigh goes out through the doors. It is warm, and it smells of clean linen and skin, the sweetish smell of bodies that do not sweat and do not age, and it is drowsy beyond any drug. Released in a crowded room, it spreads like the smell of bread, and everyone who breathes it yawns, and then everyone who sees them yawning yawns, and the Wakers come running. The Tuckers sell bladders of it to rivals of the Watch. The Bedded release it in the Pinchmarket as a sacrament.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); Grim (18) outside the Vigil.

**SPOILAGE:** A day in the bladder; then the warmth goes out of it and it is only stale air.

**CARVING:** Caught in a hall of the Dormitory at the exhalation (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14 past the orderlies; Hand + Craft vs Routine 10 to catch it). Each minute in a hall adds 1 Weariness; a blink check at Hard every minute after the third. Standing among the breathing rows is a Dread 2 check.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Released, the breath fills a room or a stretch of street twenty paces across. Everyone who breathes it gains 2 Weariness and makes a blink check at Hard (14), whatever their Weariness; foreigners in the Vigil are not spared. It is a Yawn hazard (Dread 1, becoming Dread 3 when someone goes down) for a scene. Outside the Vigil, it makes everyone who breathes it sleepy (−1 to all rolls for an hour) and gives Vigil-born travelers a blink check.

**WORTH:** 50 L. Scarce. "Spreading the Yawn" is a capital crime.

### A Sheet from the Restless Rows

As of Carving 641, the Matron's private register lists nineteen restless sleepers. Bed One, Aveline Morne, moves her lips. Bed 40,112, Odile Farthing's son, has grown to the size of a man in eleven years, and smiles. Bed 7,730 has turned its head toward the east door. Beds 212 through 219, eight members of one family who lay down together on the First Night, have begun to move as one. The orderlies change their sheets anyway, every Plate, because someone must do something.

The sheets of the restless come away creased in patterns. Not random creases: lines, loops and hatchings, as if the sleeper had been tracing something with their whole body under the linen. Laid flat on the floor of the Matron's office, the creases of beds 212 to 219, put together, make the plan of a room: long, low, with a table in it. The sheets are supposed to be burned. A laundress named Lisbet Coyle has been selling them instead, one at a time, to the Bedded and to the Dream-Divers, who pay well, because wrapped around the head of a waking person, a restless sheet whispers.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** A season, unwashed; washing erases the creases and ends it.

**CARVING:** Taken from the Dormitory laundry (Lung + Filch vs Hard 14) or from the bed itself, at the change (Hand + Filch vs Grim 18 under the orderlies' eyes; 1 Weariness per minute in the hall). Touching a restless sleeper to draw the sheet is a Dread 3 check; their face is happy.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Iss), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Wrapped around the head for a minute, under a nudger's guard, the sheet lets the wearer hear what the restless sleeper's lips are shaping: the GM gives one line, often the answer to a question about the long room, the eye, the number, or the table being laid. The wearer gains 2 Weariness and must make a blink check at Grim (18). Spread flat, a set of sheets from one family shows a map of part of the long room, with a step or two more of it each month.

**WORTH:** 220 L. Rare. Desecration of the Counted, and "equipping for sleep."

### Sleepers' Mile Moss

Below the border stones, along the old Pilgrim Road, lie the refugees of 342 A.G., who fled the plateau to sleep beyond its borders and found that the rule followed them. About four thousand of them are scattered along a mile of road and heather. They still breathe. They are grown over: moss in the hair, lichen on the cheeks, heather pushing up through the rotted rags of their clothes.

The moss that grows in a sleeper's hair is a soft, thick, grey-green cushion, warm as a scalp, and it smells of the sleeper's breath. Rim Road porters at the Last Beds pick it and sell it to Vigilants coming down from the plateau, who chew it like tobacco on the road. It works. A wad of Mile moss, chewed slowly, gives the chewer something like the rest of a night's sleep, without sleeping: the ache behind the eyes eases, the hands steady, and the long heavy pressure on the lids lifts for a day. Chewers report a taste like a pillow. Some report dreaming, awake, of heather and sky. Factor Joss Mallory sells it at the Last Beds with a straight face as "a local herb."

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) on the Pilgrim Road; Grim (18) on the Lid.

**SPOILAGE:** A week fresh; a season dried, at half strength.

**CARVING:** Picked from a sleeper's hair on the Sleepers' Mile (no roll, but a Dread 2 check: the face under the moss is breathing, and the eyes under the lichen move). A Vigil-born carver on the Mile must make a blink check at Hard (14) for each sleeper picked; the heather is very soft.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Iss).

**EFFECT:** A wad chewed removes 4 Weariness and recovers Flesh and Fray as a night of real rest (remove 1 Fray). Once a day. Each wad also makes the next Lull Sop more dangerous: its blink check is one step harder for a day. A chewer who fails three Regrowth Taint rolls from it begins to grow moss in their own hair, which does not wash out.

**WORTH:** 6 L a wad at the Last Beds; 30 L on the Lid. Uncommon. Contraband in the Vigil; legal on the Rim.

### A Chip of the Pillow

The Pillow is the hollow where Iss lay, at the exact centre of the Lid: a long shallow depression in the bedrock, a quarter mile from end to end, shaped like the impression of something enormous that lay on its side with its knees drawn up. The stone is warm in every season and faintly soft, like a cushion with the stuffing settled. Snow melts there. Yawners who stand at its rim begin to weep.

A chip of the Pillow is a palm-sized flake of grey stone, smooth on one face, warm, and soft: it gives under the thumb as a cushion gives, and slowly springs back. Laid under a head, it makes any surface the softest bed on the Table. Tuckers grind it to powder and dust their sleeping-cellars with it, because a sleeper on Pillow-dust goes down faster and, they claim, comes back easier. Old Nod forbids it. His people use it anyway, and their failure rate has risen, and the extra bodies on the Dormitory steps before the next bell are very relaxed.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Tuckers, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A chip keeps its softness for a year away from the Pillow; then it cools to ordinary stone.

**CARVING:** Chiselled from the Pillow (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14 past the Waker cordon; Hand + Craft vs Hard 14 to chisel). Each minute in the hollow adds 1 Weariness and a blink check at Hard; a Lack means the carver lies down, just for a moment. Companions must pull them out (Gut + Labor vs Hard 14) before the next breath.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Iss), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** A sleeper resting on the chip, outside the Vigil, recovers twice over: double Flesh from rest and 2 Fray instead of 1. A Vigil-born character resting on it, anywhere, makes the Lull's blink check at Grim (18) instead of Hard. Under a Tucker's guard, a sixty-second sleep on the chip counts as a full night's rest, if the Tucker can fetch them back (Hand + Stitching vs Grim 18).

**WORTH:** 350 L. Rare. Desecration of the Pillow and "equipping for sleep."

### Senna Vole's Tether

Senna Vole has made forty dives into the room, where three is the usual career. She has mapped the long room to within thirty paces of the eye. On her last dive, something behind the lid said her name. Divers lie down at the Tether Hall in harness, wired at the wrists, ankles and throat to a great brass winch and a team of eight hauliers, and when the bell is struck the hauliers drag them back. Vole's tether, the braided wire-and-silk cord she used for her last twenty dives, was retired after the fortieth. It had gone taut in a way no tether ever had, toward the eye.

It hangs in the Tether Hall's records-room, coiled, and it does not lie still. Every quarter hour, at the bell, the coil tightens and pulls, gently, toward the south end of the room, the way the eye in the dream faces. Divers touch it for luck before going under. A Diver who wore it once since then, against orders, came back on the bell with no trouble at all, faster than any haul, and told the hauliers she had not needed them. She said the tether had brought her back. She said someone on the other end had let her go.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Dream-Divers, Routine (10).

**SPOILAGE:** It does not spoil.

**CARVING:** Taken from the Tether Hall records-room (Lung + Stealth vs Grim 18), or given by Senna Vole to someone she trusts (Tongue + Persuade vs Dire 22). Handling it is a Dread 2 check: it pulls.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Iss), Dread 2, per dive.

**EFFECT:** A diver (or any sleeper) bound in the tether returns from the room when the bell is struck, automatically, without hauliers. Each dive, the diver may ask the GM one question about the long room, and on a Grace, about the eye. But each dive, roll a d6: on a 1, something on the other end of the tether has said the diver's name, and the GM gains a Regrowth point to spend on the diver at a moment of their choosing.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Unique. The Watch of Watches would seize it if they knew how often it is borrowed.

## From the Lamps and the Bells

[sigil: vigil]

The Vigil keeps itself awake with light and noise: ten thousand public lamps, a hundred thousand more in its houses, mirrors on every roof, Bonfires in the squares, bells every quarter hour, tin roofs that clatter in the rain. Three centuries of this have soaked into the instruments themselves. The city's lamps, bells and clocks are not godflesh, strictly. But they have stood against the god every minute of every day for three hundred years, and some of them have taken on its shape the way a dam takes the shape of the water.

### A Row Mirror

Lampwrights' Row is the brightest street in the world, lit from above and below and every side at once by mirrors on every roof, so that a walker there casts no shadow. The oldest mirrors were silvered in 343, the year after the First Night, and have not reflected darkness since. Each was turned toward a lamp the day it was hung, and has never been turned away.

When one cracks, its shards are prized. A Row-mirror shard is a jagged piece of old glass, its silvering gone grey and leprous at the edges, and it holds light. Carried into a dark room, it glows faintly with the reflected glare of the Row, as if it remembered three hundred years of lamps and could not stop showing them. It throws no shadow, and lets nothing near it cast one. People who look into it see their own faces lit from every side, harshly, unflatteringly, and cannot look away, and cannot blink.

Garron Ashlock, Lamplighter-General, has ninety days of oil. He has begun buying up Row shards. He has not said why.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in the Vigil; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** A shard keeps its light for a year in darkness, then must be returned to a lit street for a day to drink.

**CARVING:** Collected when a Row mirror cracks (Eye + Search vs Hard 14 to be first), or broken deliberately (a Guild offense, flogging). Prising one from a rooftop is Lung + Athletics vs Hard 14.

**TAINT:** none.

**EFFECT:** The shard removes darkness penalties within five paces and prevents any shadow within that radius, defeating Stealth that depends on darkness. A person made to look into it (Tongue + Intimidate vs their Caul + Resolve, or simply held) cannot blink, Lull or sleep for a scene, and takes 1 Fray from the glare. In Sallowreach, the shard stays lit inside a Hush, and the Kept turn their faces from it.

**WORTH:** 90 L. Scarce. Guild property; theft is prosecuted when the Guild notices.

### A Quarter-Bell Tongue

The city's bells sound every quarter hour to keep it awake, and they deafened Thaddeus Knell, the chief bell-ringer, years ago. He rings by the clock and by the shaking in his teeth. Every few decades a clapper cracks, from three hundred years of quarter hours, and is replaced. The old tongues are given to the Wakers. Captain Pyre keeps one on his desk.

A bell-tongue is an iron clapper the length of a forearm, pear-shaped, its striking face worn to a bright dent, and it is never still. Held in the hand, it hums. Struck against any surface, even a wooden table, it rings with the voice of the bell it came from, loud, cold and vast, and the sound goes into the teeth. It wakes anyone. It wakes people from the edge of the room, from a blink in progress, from a Tucker's minute that has gone a breath too long. It does not wake the Counted. Thaddeus Knell has tried, in Bed One's hall, at night, with the clapper of the Great Quarter. Bed One's lips stopped moving for exactly one minute.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); Wakers and Knell's ringers, Routine (10).

**SPOILAGE:** It does not spoil.

**CARVING:** Bought from a Waker (Tongue + Haggle vs Hard 14), or taken from a bell-tower when a clapper is replaced (Lung + Athletics vs Hard 14 to climb).

**TAINT:** none. Each use costs the striker 1 Flesh if they do not stop their ears.

**EFFECT:** Struck (an action), the tongue removes 3 Weariness from everyone within twenty paces, ends any blink in progress, and gives a nudger's fetch for a sleeper in the room an automatic success. Anyone using Lay Them Down or a Catching Yawn within earshot fails. In any land, it ends any sleep, trance, Seeming or enchantment of drowsiness within earshot. It cannot be used stealthily. Restless sleepers within earshot stop moving for one minute.

**WORTH:** 220 L. Scarce. Legal in Waker hands; illegal to sell, and sold.

### A Hank of Pendulum Wire

In the Pendulum Hall, from the top of the tower to the cellar, hangs a single pendulum, a great iron bob on a rope of twisted wire, swinging through a slow arc that once took exactly four seconds. Maud Sexton, the Hourkeeper, has proved by that pendulum that each hour is a little longer than the last. Something is stretching the night that the Vigil will not admit it is having. Every decade the wire is replaced, because it stretches too.

Old pendulum wire is a coil of twisted iron strands, oiled, and longer than it was when it was hung: measured coils come back from the Hall a span longer than they went in. Wound around a wrist, it ticks against the pulse, slow, much slower than the heart. Those who wear it feel time thicken around them. A second becomes a long second. A breath becomes room enough to think in. Clerks of the Watch wear it to finish their shifts. Wakers wear it in fights. Maud Sexton will not have it in her house.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** It does not spoil. It does, slowly, keep stretching.

**CARVING:** Taken from the Pendulum Hall's stores when the wire is replaced (Lung + Filch vs Hard 14). Standing inside the humming tower too long is a blink check at Hard each scene.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Iss), each time its power is called.

**EFFECT:** Once per scene, the wearer may stretch a moment: take one extra action before anyone else can respond, or reroll one failed Lung or Hand roll. Each use adds 1 Weariness, because the stretched moment had to be stayed awake through. Once per day, the wearer may instead stretch an hour, and do an hour's work in what others see as a minute; this adds 3 Weariness and ages the wearer's face visibly by a month.

**WORTH:** 160 L a hank. Scarce. Theft from the Pendulum Hall.

### Lukas Marre's Muzzle

Lukas Marre's yawns are catching beyond all reason; he once put down a whole market square. He lives in the Glass House, masked, alone, and apologizes through the pane to everyone who visits. The yawners there wear stiff leather muzzles that hold the jaw half closed. Marre's wear out faster than anyone's, because he fights them, and because the leather soaks up the yawn.

A Marre muzzle is a harness of boiled leather and brass, with a padded chin-cup, stained dark on the inside, and it smells of him: of sleep-breath and saliva and the warm milky smell of a yawn. Strapped on someone else, it gives them his gift. The wearer cannot yawn, quite. The yawn builds behind the leather and goes out of them through the eyes, and everyone who sees their face catches it. Tuckers have used them to empty a street of Wakers. Assassins have used them to make a whole household blink at once. Marre does not know. He thinks the Glass House burns his old ones.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Glass House keepers, Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** A muzzle holds its yawn for a season after he last wore it.

**CARVING:** Taken from the Glass House's refuse (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14). Visiting Marre to take one from him is a Caul + Resolve roll vs Hard (14) through the speaking-tube, or gain 2 Weariness.

**TAINT:** Dread 1, to the wearer (they can feel what they are doing).

**EFFECT:** Worn, the muzzle makes the wearer a Glass House yawner: once per scene, everyone who can see their face must make a Caul + Resolve roll vs Hard (14) or gain 2 Weariness and make a blink check. The wearer also gains 1 Weariness per scene. Outside the Vigil, the yawn still spreads, and Vigil-born travelers in sight must make blink checks.

**WORTH:** 180 L. Rare. Spreading the Yawn; capital.

## Preparations of the Pinchmarket

[sigil: vigil]

The Pinchmarket has been open every hour of every watch for three hundred years. What it sells best is what the Vigil makes best: the tools and drugs of staying awake, and their black-market opposites. These are the city's crafted leavings: needles, stimulants, sandglasses, bread, and one thing from Inspector Grimmer's evidence room that ought never to have left it.

### An Atlas Needle

Sister Agathe Pinch, Mistress of the Needle and author of the current edition of the Atlas of the Quick, has trained every Waker alive. She is tiny, bright-eyed and grandmotherly, with a lap-tray of needles laid out like embroidery tools. She believes that pain is a teacher, and that Wakers must learn it first on their own bodies. Her needles are steel, as long as a hand, fine as hair at the tip, and each has been used on a hundred recruits in the standing-stalls. She retires one each year, with a little ceremony, and gives it to the recruit who screamed least.

An Atlas needle finds the Quick. That is the Wakers' word for the place in a body where pain lives closest to the surface and does least harm: a point behind the ear, under a nail, at the hinge of a jaw. An Atlas needle in any hand goes there on its own, as a dowsing rod goes to water. It wakes the dead-tired as nothing else can, and it makes a liar's eyes water, and it never leaves a mark. Sister Pinch's former students treasure theirs. Jack Tenterhook, the wake-valet, has three, and says the only difference between his old work and his new is the tipping.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); Wakers, Routine (10).

**SPOILAGE:** It does not spoil.

**CARVING:** Given by Sister Pinch to a recruit after a week in the standing-stall (Gut + Endure vs Grim 18 for the week; 4 Fray), or bought from a Waker down on their luck (Tongue + Haggle vs Hard 14).

**TAINT:** Dread 1, to the user, the first time each day.

**EFFECT:** A prick (an action, or a hit on a grappled target) removes 3 Weariness and ends a blink or falling-asleep, as a Waker's needle succeeding automatically, and costs no Flesh. In questioning, the needle gives +2 to Intimidate, and a target who has been pricked suffers −2 to Deceive for the scene. As a weapon it deals 1 damage, ignoring Armor.

**WORTH:** 70 L. Uncommon. Legal for Wakers; anyone else carrying one is presumed to be a Waker, which on the Slope is dangerous.

### Crane's Strain Eleven

Dr. Aldous Crane, Apothecary-General, invented the current strain of rouse. He knows the dose curve and where it ends: there is a point at which the amount needed to stay awake is the amount that stops the heart. It is eleven years off. In his private laboratory in the Bitterhouse he has been working on what comes after. He calls it Strain Eleven, after the years. He has not slept, of course, but he has not rested either.

Strain Eleven is a black lozenge the size of a thumbnail, stamped with an open eye and a small numeral, glossy, bitter beyond bitter, and it holds. One dose keeps a Vigilant awake for a full day, clear and sharp, with no rising pressure on the lids at all. It is what the city has been praying for. It is also, by Crane's own notes, the dose at the end of the curve, taken now. Each lozenge brings the heart a year closer to the place where it stops. Four have gone missing from Crane's laboratory. Crane has noticed, and has not reported it, because he would have to explain what they are.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); Bitterhouse chemists, Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** A year sealed in wax.

**CARVING:** Stolen from Crane's private laboratory (Lung + Stealth vs Grim 18; Eye + Search vs Hard 14 to find the box). Brewing more requires his notes (Eye + Craft vs Dire 22 and the Bitterhouse stills). Reading the notes is a Dread 2 check.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Iss). Addiction: Dire (22).

**EFFECT:** A lozenge removes all Weariness above the user's floor and holds it there for a full day; no blink checks, no Seeming. The user gains +1 Eye for the day. Then they roll Gut + Endure vs Grim (18); on a failure they suffer a rouse-heart (Ruin), and each lozenge taken permanently reduces their maximum Flesh by 1. The user does not feel the damage until it is done.

**WORTH:** Never sold; 1,000 L if it were. Unique (four doses at large). The Watch of Watches would end Crane for it, and then demand the recipe.

### Morne's Loaf

Aveline Morne, the Sleeper in Bed One, was a baker's daughter who lay down on the First Night and was the first not to rise. Three hundred years unmoving. Last month her lips began to shape words, and the Matron has started writing them down (see Bed One's Words, in the Rim Market). Most of it is a recipe for bread.

Somebody has copied the recipe out. It has gone round the Slope on scraps of paper, and the Bedded bake it in their hidden cellars, and it is very good bread: a soft, round, pale loaf with a split top and a crumb as fine as a pillow, made with milk and honey and something unusual, a pinch of salt from the bottom of the oven and a slow rising in a warm place for longer than any baker in the Vigil would dare leave dough. It smells of a kitchen at dawn three hundred years ago. Those who eat it say it tastes of being a child, put to bed, in a house with the lamps turned down. And the Bedded say that every loaf baked from it is laid on a table in the long room, at the far end, under the eye, and that someone is setting places.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) on the Slope; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** Three days, like any good bread. Stale, it is only bread.

**CARVING:** Baked from a copy of the recipe (Eye + Craft vs Hard 14 and an oven held warm for a night, which in the Vigil is suspicious). The recipe is had from the Bedded (Tongue + Persuade vs Hard 14) or copied from the Matron's notebook.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Iss), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** A slice eaten removes 1d6 Fray and 1d6 Hunger, as the gentlest of vices, without risk of habit. Then the eater gains 3 Weariness, and must resist the Want *To lie down* at Hard (14) for the rest of the scene, whatever their land. A Vigilant who eats a whole loaf alone, the Bedded say, dreams the long room without sleeping, for a breath, and sees the table. Outside the Vigil, the bread is merely the best bread the eater has ever had, and they will think about it for years.

**WORTH:** 2 L a loaf on the Slope. Uncommon. Baking it is "Bedded practice," punished by a season in the Rattlehouse.

### Old Nod's Minute-Glass

Old Nod, King of the Tuckers, sells one minute of guarded sleep for a month's wage, and people queue around the block. He has never tried his own product. He says a man should not get high on his own supply. His Tuckers time the minute with sandglasses, and the best of these are Nod's own: small glasses of greenish Glass House glass in frames of black wood, filled not with sand but with grains of ground Dormitory brick.

A minute-glass runs for exactly sixty breaths of the Counted, which is a little more than a minute and a little less than enough. While it runs, a sleeper under its watch is held: not in the room, quite, but on its threshold, in the doorway, with the warmth on their face and the floor soft underfoot. When the last grain falls, the sleeper is pulled back. Mostly. Old Nod's glasses have a failure rate of one in sixty, which is the best on the Lid, and the failures are delivered to the Dormitory steps before the next bell. Every Tucker knows the number. Nobody knows which minute it is.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) among Tuckers; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** The glass lasts until broken. Each failure leaves a grain stuck to the glass, and after sixty, it never empties.

**CARVING:** Bought from a Tucker (Tongue + Haggle vs Grim 18; they do not sell their glasses), or taken from one by force (they are armed and not sentimental). Old Nod gives a glass only to a successor.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Iss), to the sleeper.

**EFFECT:** A character who sleeps for one turn of the glass, under another's watch, gains a full night's rest: remove 1 Fray, recover Flesh as for rest, and reset Weariness to its floor. When the glass empties, roll a d20: on a 1, the sleeper does not come back, and is Counted. A nudger's fetch may still be tried at Grim (18) in the next breath. A Lack on the fetch delivers the sleeper's number to the watcher, in their own hand, on a scrap of paper found later in their pocket.

**WORTH:** 400 L. Rare. "Equipping for sleep," capital; the Tuckers are already capital.

### Evidence Forty

Inspector Hale Grimmer investigates blink-murders: killings done by ordinary people during a two-second sleep, with no memory after. A cook, a nudger, a clerk blinks mid-sentence and comes back holding a knife they did not pick up, standing behind a stranger, and the stranger is open from hip to collarbone in one perfectly straight line. Grimmer has forty cases. The method is the same in each. So, he has lately realized, is the knife.

Every blink-murder has been done with a knife the killer did not own: a kitchen knife, a quill-knife, a cobbler's blade, a nudger's paring knife. Grimmer keeps them in his evidence room, numbered one to forty, and last month, laying them out on a bench, he saw that all forty have the same nick on the edge, a hair's breadth from the tip, in the same place. Different knives. The same nick. He has told no one. Evidence Forty, the most recent, a short bone-handled kitchen knife, has since gone missing from the bench. Grimmer suspects a constable. The constable suspects he took it himself, during a blink.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22). Grimmer's constables, Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** It does not spoil. It is not always where it was left.

**CARVING:** Stolen from the Inspector's evidence room (Lung + Filch vs Hard 14), or found in the hand of the next blink-killer, which means being near a murder (Dread 2).

**TAINT:** Dread 3, the first time each day it is held.

**EFFECT:** In the hand of a character who blinks, the knife cuts the Line: the GM may, on any blink result of 8 or 10, have the bearer's hands make a single attack with it at +10, and on two Helpings the target suffers Ruin, opened. The bearer has no say. Held deliberately in a fight by someone awake, it is only a knife (2 damage), but with a Grace it leaves the nick in the target's bone, and the GM notes that whatever moves the blink-killers now knows them.

**WORTH:** Never sold; nobody who understands it would buy it. Unique. Theft of evidence; Grimmer would pay anything to have it back, and then would not know what to do with it.
