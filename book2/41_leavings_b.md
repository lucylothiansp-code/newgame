# Leavings of Cradlewrack

> Keep it in grey stone. Keep it knotted. Knock before you lift the lid. And if it is warmer in the morning than when you laid it down, it was never yours to keep.
>> — chalked inside the lid of a relic-chest sold under the naming-exchange, Kest

[bigquote] Everywhere else on the Table a relic rots. In the highlands it ripens. | — Factor Merriam Toll of the Rim Road Company, in a letter to Quillhouse

Other lands carve their relics out of the god. Cradlewrack does not need to. The god delivers them. Every week the Tally records something the Attendance must enter and cannot explain: a Part, a key with wards no smith cut, a warm stone with a pulse, a scrap of membrane from the Morwen nurseries, a length of living cord, a green bottle with a breath in it, a bundle tied in the knot that does not come undone. The highlands are the only country on the Table where leavings are born rather than taken, and highlanders speak of them the way they speak of lambing: flatly, practically, and with one eye on the door.

The law is plain and universally ignored. Under the Book of Questions, anything that comes into the world in Cradlewrack is a birth, and every birth belongs first to the Attendant who asked the Two Questions over it. A relic kept without an Attendant's word is therefore *concealment*, and is punished by unhinging. In practice the trade runs in the low cellars under the Kest naming-exchange, where naming-rights are bought by the hour above and leavings are sold by the ounce below, by people who knock on the trapdoor before they lift it. A right to name a hand is worth nothing on the exchange floor. The hand itself, in the cellar, is worth a month's bread to the Assemblers and a season's to a Rim buyer. The Company's factor at Breech's Gate takes cauls by the crate for Brinehollow. The Second Table pays for roofs and asks for first refusal on anything with a voice.

What highlanders fear in a leaving is not rot. Nothing of Vey's goes bad. It goes *somewhere*. A thing of the god's kept too long in the dark will open, or bear, or begin, or crawl to the edge of the shelf nearest another of its kind. Highland keepers do not ask how long a relic will last. They ask how long before it leaves. The answer, for most, is a pang or two, and the remedies are the country's three old certainties: knot it, brick it, or keep it in grey stone. The Barren Order teaches that the grey granite carted from the Fast border is the one material in the highlands that has never borne anything, and a grey-stone relic box, the size of a loaf, costs 60 lacks in Kest. It is the commonest dowry gift among the cautious, and the commonest thing found open and empty in a widow's cellar.

Abroad, the leavings of Vey behave as the country's pregnant daughters do: they take the highlands with them. Petra Quick learned it with women, and the Rim has learned it with relics.

[box: Rules — Highland Leavings Abroad]
A leaving of Vey (any relic in this chapter with Regrowth Taint of Vey) carried out of Cradlewrack keeps the country's calendar. On each pang, wherever the relic is on the Table, roll a d10. On a 1, every door, lid and knot within a stone's throw of it opens during the night, and the ground beneath it is warm, red and faintly wet until dawn. On a double pang (the GM's call, when the interval closes again), it is a 1–3. Grey stone, bricking and the Attendance knot each prevent the roll while they hold.
[/box]

## From the Veiled Houses

[sigil: cradlewrack]

The Caul-lines carry almost continuously, and the nurses of the great houses take away what they deliver before anyone can ask what it was. Morwen Hall has a room for it with no door on the lower side, entered from above by ladder, where the deliveries are entered in a household tally kept separate from the public one. The houses do not sell what they bear. Their nurses, their valets and their bricklayers do, and the Veiled Council has spent three centuries pretending this is not where half the cellar trade under the naming-exchange begins. Leavings of the veiled are the finest the highlands produce: the god in them is thick, kept close by three hundred years of keeping the veil, and they are prophetic in the way the houses themselves are prophetic, which is to say vaguely, redly, and lately all in agreement.

### Red Weep

Beneath the veil of a Caul-line noble deep in the Course, where the membrane lies against the cheek, a fluid gathers. For most of the houses' history it was clear and faintly salt, and the nurses wiped it away and burned the linen. Since the Duchess Iphigene's veil went red, the weep under every high veil in the country has turned: rose, then watered wine, then red. It beads at the hem and drips, one drop to the slow count of sixty, like a pang. The Morwen nurses catch it now in silver thimbles. The household has found that it sells.

It is warm, and tastes of iron and the inside of a cheek. Whoever drinks it dreams the next day through a red film, as the veiled see it. A Morwen valet sold it at Knocking Gate to merchants who wanted tomorrow's prices, until in 640 a Fatlands drover drank three drops together and in the morning walked out of the Open Door and north across the moor without his cattle, telling the wardens he had seen a door in the ground with someone standing in it, arms spread, and that it was him, and that he was late. Neither has been seen since.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Cradlewrack, Grim (18) elsewhere. Caul-line servants know of it.

**SPOILAGE:** One pang. After the next, the weep grows a skin that will not lift; after the second, a small wet veil around nothing, and the thimble must be bricked. A sealed grey-stone phial in a cold spring-cellar keeps it a season.

**CARVING:** It is caught, not cut: a silver thimble and an hour under the hem of a Caul-line in the Course (Regrowth 7+) while they sleep or prophesy. With a nurse's connivance, Hand + Stitching vs Hard (14); stolen, Lung + Stealth vs Grim (18). A Lack means the veil parts and the carver sees the face beneath: Dread 3, and the noble will know them through any veil, forever.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** One drop at dusk. Once the next day, before a roll of the player's choosing, the GM must name one thing about to begin in the scene (an ambush, a lie, a door opening) and the character gains +4 on that roll or acts first that round. A thimble holds three drops. More than one in a night gives, on a d6 roll of 1–3, the Duchess's vision of the opening ground instead, with the drinker standing in it: Dread 3.

**WORTH:** 90 lacks the thimble in the Kest cellars, 250 on the Rim. Scarce in Cradlewrack, Rare elsewhere. Concealment; theft from a house is lockbreaking. The Veiled Council has begun buying it back.

### A Born Key

Among the things the Course delivers, the Attendance's records list keys. They come mostly from Caul-line nobles, after a carrying of days, small and wet and already finished: the length of a finger, dark as old horn, warm, with a bow shaped like a closed eye and wards no locksmith in Kest has ever filed. They are not iron. They are faintly flexible, like gristle, and turn in a lock with a sound like a tongue clicking on a palate. The Tally enters them as "one, key, living?" and the question mark has never been removed.

Every born key fits every lock. The harder lesson is that every born key is born *for* one door, somewhere, and knows which, and grows heavier as it nears it. A tinker named Abel Clayhand carried one for eleven years and said it never once felt like home. In 629 he walked north to the Sill with it in his fist. Old Hinge says Clayhand stood before the standing door all night, and at dawn was gone, and the key lay warm in the grass below the threshold, and Hinge put it back where he found it. Old Hinge says a great many things.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14). A Filch roll vs Grim (18) in any thieves' den on the western Rim finds someone who has used one.

**SPOILAGE:** It does not decay. Each pang, unless knotted in Uncut cord or kept in grey stone, it opens one lock, lid or knot near it of its own accord, usually the one its keeper least wants opened.

**CARVING:** Taken at delivery, or from the wool-lined nursery drawer where the great houses keep them in rows, faintly stirring. Witnessing a key born is Dread 2. Robbing a Morwen drawer is Hand + Filch vs Grim (18); a Lack means the drawer's other keys have already opened every door between the thief and the way out.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Vey).

**EFFECT:** Once per day the key opens any lock, bar, latch, seal or knot with no roll, in any land. Bricking resists it (Caul + Godsign vs Grim 18 to open a bricked way). Each use, roll a d6: on a 1 the key has learned that lock, and that lock will never hold again. The GM secretly chooses the key's own door. Within a mile of it, the key weighs as much as a man's arm; at the door it turns by itself, and whatever is behind that door knows its keeper by name.

**WORTH:** 400 lacks. Rare. Sold under the naming-exchange, by Petra Quick on the road, and by Mister Cutlery, who will not say what door he thinks his is for. Concealment of a birth; in the Vigil and Oathen, possession of a housebreaker's tool.

### The Little Bone Door

In the Lying-In's book, under a date in 509 A.G., there is an entry the Attendance teaches every Senior of the Lying as the reason a second book is kept at all: *one, door, of bone, hinged; opened onto nothing; stays.* A Calloway lord in the Course delivered it after nine days' carrying: the size of a playing card, yellow-white, frame and leaf and threshold of one piece, on a hinge of living gristle, with a knocker the size of a barleycorn. Opened, it showed not the palm it lay on but a dark beyond, and warm air came through it, smelling of candles.

The Lying-In kept it bricked for a hundred and eighteen years. In 627 a bricklayer in the last month of his term walked out with it under his apron. It is now, the Attendance believes, with Dr. Lazar Voss, who wants to press it against the Assemblers' figure and look inside. It is no longer the size of a card. It is the size of a hand, and Voss's monthly measurements show it growing at the rate of the pangs.

Most who look through it see what they hoped to. Some see a long room, warm and dim, with a table laid and a great many chairs, one pulled out, and hear someone moving around it, unhurried, laying cutlery.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); automatic for a Senior of the Lying, who will deny it.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It grows. When it is the size of a door, the Attendance believes it will no longer need a wall to open in.

**CARVING:** Unique; it can only be stolen or bargained for. Voss keeps it in a lead case under a pillow he does not use. Holding it the first time is Dread 3: it is warm, the hinge has a pulse, and the knocker taps once against the palm.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** Laid against a wall, floor, door, lid or body and opened, it lets the holder see and hear the far side as through an open doorway. Against a living body it shows the inside (+4 to Stitching to diagnose or cut; the subject feels a cold draught and takes a Dread 2 check). Each opening, roll a d10. On a 1 the view is the long warm room: Dread 4, and Caul + Resolve vs Hard (14) to close it. On a failure, something on the other side begins, kindly, to describe the view, and will do so every night until the holder comes and sees.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Voss paid 3,000 lacks to the bricklayer's widow. The Assemblers would pay double, and the Second Table has asked whether it can be made to open from the other side.

### A Second-Meal Crock

At a highland naming, a year after the birth, the family eats the afterbirth, kept salted in a crock and stewed with lamb and onions: the Second Meal, in memory of the first. When a child is *returned* in its first year, taken back for a late Second Opinion, there is no naming and no Meal, and the crock should go to the Stillyard with the bundle. Many families cannot bear to send it. They keep it on a high shelf, knotted shut, and do not look at it.

What is kept in a returned child's crock does not cure. It stays deep red and glossy, and it grows: half full at the returning, it is pushing up its lid in five years, warm, smelling of iron and milk. A spoonful, eaten, begins again whatever in the eater's body has stopped. Lost parts grow back *new*. A Foundling captain who lost his left hand at Breech's Gate ate from a widow's crock in Cleave and woke nine days later with a newborn's hand at the end of his wrist, pink, creased, flexing. Over two years it grew into a woman's hand, slender, with a callus on the ring finger no work of his had made. It strains east at night. He sleeps with it tied to the bedpost.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Cradlewrack; any surgeon of the Reopening Ward knows the captain.

**SPOILAGE:** None, if salted and knotted. Left unopened more than a year, the crock bears, and what climbs out is a Wandering Part.

**CARVING:** Taken from a grieving household by persuasion (Tongue + Persuade vs Grim 18, and Dread 2 on hearing why they kept it) or theft (Hand + Filch vs Hard 14). Knock before opening. A Lack means the contents have begun: something the size of a fist lifts its head out of the salt (Dread 3).

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Vey), 1 Hunger, Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** A spoonful, kept down with Gut + Feast vs Hard (14). Within nine days one lost part (finger, hand, eye, ear, teeth, foot) or one permanent Injury from the Mangling table begins to grow back, newborn-sized, reaching full size over a year (−2 on rolls using it until then). The new part is in law and fact a Part: each pang, roll a d10, and on a 1 the character wakes with it straining toward the Assemblers' Barn. A crock holds 1d6 + 2 spoonfuls. A second spoonful grows the same part again, beside the first.

**WORTH:** 150 lacks a spoonful under the exchange, 600 the crock. Scarce. Concealment and desecration of the Meal. Fatlands buyers pay double, believing it will fill them. It does not, but it begins something in the stomach that keeps them company.

## From the Attendance

[sigil: cradlewrack]

The Attendance is the only government on the Table that kills in an apron, and it leaves very little behind. What it uses, it keeps; what it cannot keep, it bricks. Its leavings come out into the world by three doors only: the apprentices who quit after their first Second Opinion and do not give back their kits, the bricklayers of the Lying-In who serve one year at triple pay and are watched the whole time, and the Opinions themselves, a very few, who keep something of what they did because they could not leave it in a wall. The midwives' leavings are not pieces of a god. They are tools that have done the god's work so long they have learned it, and every one of them was last held by a woman who had just answered the Second Question and would remember it.

### A Retired Second Opinion

By the Book of Questions, an Opinion's knife is retired at its thousandth answer, counted in notches inside the knife-case where no one else will look. Retired knives are bricked into the Knife Course, a band of masonry around the inside of the Old Lying-In's wall at the height of a woman's hand, where four centuries of them lie end to end and midwives trail their fingers along it the way others touch wood. Most knives go into the Course. Not all.

They are plain: a short blade of highland steel, a ewe-horn handle worn to the shape of one thumb, a smell of lye. The edge never dulls on anything born. Held over something new, the handle grows warm at the slow count of three and stays warm until the holder decides. Clemency Seele of Rudge kept hers when she retired in 590 and carved the Sunday lamb with it for forty years, and said she always knew, by the warmth, which lamb was going to be difficult. Her grandson inherited it. He was found at nineteen standing over his sister's cradle with the knife in his hand, counting, and could not say what he had meant to decide.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14). Every Attendant knows the custom; only an Opinion knows how often it is broken.

**SPOILAGE:** None in Cradlewrack. Abroad, rust blooms on the blade within a season in shapes like small palms; oiling it with spring-salt each pang prevents this.

**CARVING:** From the Knife Course: Lung + Stealth vs Dire (22) to pass the night Attendants, and Hand + Labor vs Hard (14) to unbrick a knife quietly. From a retired Opinion: never given, sometimes inherited, occasionally taken from her body. Gripping it the first time is Dread 2: the count of three, a thousand times over, in the palm.

**TAINT:** Dread 2; and 1 Regrowth (Vey) each scene it is used against a thing of Vey's.

**EFFECT:** A knife (2, Silent). Against anything born in Cradlewrack that the Book of Questions would not let stay (Parts, a Gathered, Doorborn, the Refused, the get of a crowning, Fenn's Get), it ignores Armor and each Helping adds +3 damage. Once per scene the wielder may ask the First Question aloud of such a creature: it must hold still for one round unless it beats Caul + Resolve vs Grim (18). The knife carries a Want: **Go into the Room** (Caul + Resolve vs Hard 14 on learning of a birth within a mile, or go, and stay to the end).

**WORTH:** 800 lacks. Rare. The Refused pay a fortune to have them destroyed, and have been known to pay one to have one. Possession by a non-Attendant is unhinging; use of one is bricking.

### The Knot That Will Not Come Undone

Every knot in Cradlewrack slips. Laces loosen by morning and clothes are retied at every bell. One knot holds. The bundle an Opinion carries out of the room after the Second Question is tied with the cord of the birth itself, wet, in a knot taught only at the rank of Opinion: small, ugly, many-turned, like a fist of string. It has never been known to come undone. The bundle goes into the Stillyard's Low Wall without a family mark, entered as "struck through," and the knot goes with it.

A length of that cord, cut free with the knot left whole, will hold anything it is tied around, forever. Doorborn fingers, born keys and the land itself cannot open it. In a country where nothing stays shut, it is the only shut thing there is. A lockbreaker named Tobin Furrow sold eleven to rich houses in Kest in 636, and their owners slept, for the first time in their lives, behind doors that did not drift. Furrow was found the next spring bricked into a Low Wall niche, alive, with a knot tied through the bones of his wrists. The Attendance has never said it was them.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18). Every highlander knows the knot exists; few will say what it is tied around.

**SPOILAGE:** While tied around something, none. Cut free, it must be tied onto something within one pang, or it remembers it is only cord, and slips.

**CARVING:** Unbricking a Low Wall niche at night without a sound is Hand + Labor vs Hard (14); seeing the bundle is Dread 3. Cutting the cord above the knot without loosening a turn is Hand + Stitching vs Grim (18). A Lack means the bundle knocks.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** Tied around or between anything (a door and its frame, two wrists, a lid, a Part, a wound), it cannot be untied by any means, mundane or godsign, and no pang loosens it. It must be cut (Hand + Blades vs Grim 18), and is then spent. A door so tied is the one door in Cradlewrack that holds past a pang. A wound so tied will never reopen; neither will anything else on that limb, including the hand.

**WORTH:** 300 lacks. Scarce under the exchange, Rare abroad. Agnes Latch will not touch one. Lockbreaking and desecration; the penalty is bricking, and the Low Wall is long.

### Hollan Kest's Thumbnails

On the night of the Meal, at dawn, when the floor of the old birthing-house was a drift of gods the size of hares and mice and fingernails, all still trying to begin, the last of them was no larger than a barleycorn, and the midwife Hollan Kest knelt and crushed it between her thumbnails, and wept, and said, "There. Now nothing begins." She was the founder of every line of Midwife-Paramount since. When she died in her ninety-first year, her thumbnails were drawn and set in a reliquary of horn and gold on the altar of the Old Lying-In, two curved yellow shields the size of a child's fingernail, one in each half of a hinged box.

They have never stopped trying to close on each other. With the reliquary open, they creep together across the velvet over an hour, edge first, like crab claws, and must be parted with a pin. A Kest rumour holds that the reliquary was robbed in 638 and the nails replaced with goat's horn. The Attendance has denied it, which in Kest is taken as confirmation.

**WHISPERED:** Every highlander knows the story. That the nails survive: Eye + Lore vs Hard (14). Where they are now: Dire (22).

**SPOILAGE:** None, kept apart. Left in one box they close, and closed they cannot be parted without breaking, and broken they are only nail.

**CARVING:** Unique. The Old Lying-In's doors have stood open since the Meal; leaving unseen is Lung + Stealth vs Dire (22). Touching them the first time is Dread 3: they are warm, and smell of something very small that has been crushed.

**TAINT:** 4 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** Pressed together upon something small that is beginning, they end it. Once per day, with no roll, the holder may end one of: a birth in its first hour; a Part smaller than a hand; a wound in the act of reopening; a Doorborn half-born from its door; a crowning's first pressing-forth; a fire's first flame; a Want gained within the last day. The thing simply stops, and will not start again. Each use, the holder marks a **Shut**. At three Shuts the holder can never again begin anything well: −2 on the first roll of every fight, journey, bargain or endeavor, forever. Vey remembers whose hands these were.

**WORTH:** Never sold. The Second Table has offered 12,000 lacks through three intermediaries. The Barren Order believes that pressed on the lip of the Dilation they would stop the land's labor, and has never been near enough to try. If they were stolen, Orla Kest would go into every room in Cradlewrack until she found them.

### A Stoppered First Breath

In the old highlands, before the Meal, the midwife caught each child's first breath in her cupped hands and blew it into Vey's nearest opening, as the giving back. The god is in the blood now and has no opening to blow into, but the Attendance still catches first breaths, for one kind of birth only. When an Opinion has answered the Second Question, she first holds a small green-glass bottle to the mouth of what she is about to strike through, and catches the breath it draws, and stoppers it. The Book of Questions gives the reason in a single line: *So that it is not lost.* The bottle goes into the bundle, under the knot, and into the Low Wall.

A few get out. Apprentices keep them, the way Wenna Thrale keeps names. The breath inside is the beginning of a life that was not allowed, and it is still entirely a beginning. Uncorked under the nose of the dying, it begins them again. A Foundling sergeant named Ninety, opened belly to breastbone by a Gathered on the Cleave road, breathed one from his drill-mother's pocket and stood up in the mud with the wound already glazing. He has no memory of the day before, and for nine days afterward he stood outside every house in Cleave where a woman was in labor, face to the wall, weeping without knowing why.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Hard (14) for any Attendance apprentice.

**SPOILAGE:** In Cradlewrack stoppers work loose. Each pang, roll a d10: on a 1–2 the stopper has slipped and the breath is gone, and somebody near the bottle wakes having sighed in their sleep. A bottle knotted with the Attendance knot, or kept in grey stone, keeps forever.

**CARVING:** From a bundle in the Low Wall (as for the Knot, above), or from an Opinion's apron-pocket in the minutes between the knife and the bricking: Hand + Filch vs Hard (14), in the same room as what has just been done (Dread 3).

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Inhaled by a Dying character, it ends Ruin at once: restore Flesh to half maximum, and remove all Fray. The character has begun again. They lose all memory of the previous day, and for nine days carry the Want **Attend** (Caul + Resolve vs Hard 14 on learning of a birth within a mile, or go, and stand outside). On the first pang after, they dream from inside a bundle: 1 Fray. One use.

**WORTH:** 500 lacks. Rare. The Attendance never sells; the cellars do, discreetly, mostly to the Foundling Companies and to Rim wardens. Possession is concealment. A bottle offered for sale in Sallowreach fetches nothing; nobody there has needed to begin again in six hundred years.

### A Leaf of the Lying-In's Book

The Lying-In keeps its own book, apart from the public Tally, for the births nobody speaks about. Its boards are red clay fired hard as iron, and its leaves are not vellum but caul, stretched and scraped from the Caul-line deliveries, so that each holds a pinkish light when lifted to a lamp. Entries are in the Attendance's shorthand of hooks and loops: a date, a bearer, *what is it*, *does it stay*, and the Opinion's mark.

Leaves get out under bricklayers' aprons. They are the most dangerous thing the cellars sell, because they are true and because they are not finished. A leaf cut from the book goes on writing itself: when a new entry is made in the Lying-In, the shorthand surfaces on the loose leaf too, overnight, in a brownish ink like old blood. Rufus Ochre owns one. It is how he knows the eleven hundred unaccounted births are not all walking north.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); the Assemblers know, and pay.

**SPOILAGE:** Stable if dry. In damp the caul softens and crawls by night toward the Lying-In; one left out in a wet spring was found a mile off, flattened against the Lying-In's wall, trying to get in.

**CARVING:** Hand + Filch vs Dire (22) inside the record-room, or a year's triple wages to a bricklayer. Reading the shorthand is Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); an Attendant of rank 3 reads it freely.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 3 (on first reading).

**EFFECT:** A read leaf reveals 1d6 births the Tally hides: parish, bearer, what was born, whether it stayed, and where it went. One, the GM decides, is still alive. Each night, roll a d6 to see whether the leaf writes.

| d6 | What the leaf writes tonight |
| 1 | Nothing. The leaf is warm. |
| 2 | A birth in the Lying-In: "one, door, small; opened onto nothing; stays." |
| 3 | A delivery from Morwen Hall, in the Duchess's name, answer to the Second Question left blank. |
| 4 | "One, born-grown, asked what year it was. Stays." The parish is the one the characters are in. |
| 5 | A birth whose bearer is a player character, dated nine days from tonight. |
| 6 | A line with no bearer and no answer, only the Opinion's mark, and the word *late*. |

**WORTH:** 600 lacks to the Assemblers, more to the Second Table. Rare. Possession is the gravest concealment there is; the Attendance has bricked readers for less.

## From the Born-Wrong

[sigil: cradlewrack]

The Parts, the born-grown, the Uncut and the things the Assemblers stitch are the commonest source of leavings in the highlands and the most contested. Every hand born in a Rudge cottage is wanted by four parties at once: the mother, who loves it; the Attendance, which is owed it; the Assemblers, who believe it is a finger of the god; and the cellar-dealers, who know exactly what a Fatlands buyer will pay for a warm hand in a jar. The Foundling Companies guard their dead more fiercely than any army on the Table guards its living, and the Uncut regard any cord cut from one of their own as a murder of two people. Leavings in this section are almost all taken from someone who was, in some sense, still attached to them.

### A Joining Seam

When two Parts fit, they seal along a seam that heals in a night: a pale raised line, faintly ridged like the join of a shell, warm, and smelling of milk. The Assemblers' surgeons cut seams out of the Gathered they cannot use (clusters that joined wrongly, in the dark, before the Gatherers reached them) and keep the strips in warm ewe's milk in stone jars in the Barn's middle hall. A strip the length of a finger, laid along a wound between two pieces of flesh, makes them join as Parts join. It does not matter whose flesh it is.

A Kest mason who lost three fingers to a falling lintel bought a seam and three fingers cut from a man hanged at Knocking Gate, and had them stitched on in a cellar under the naming-exchange. They took overnight. They work beautifully. At dusk they clench, all three, hard, and hold, for about as long as it takes a hanged man to stop.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Hard (14) for anyone who has had dealings with the Assemblers or the Second Table's surgeons.

**SPOILAGE:** In warm ewe's milk, one pang; in milk in a grey-stone jar, a season. Out of milk it dries within a day to a dead white string.

**CARVING:** Cut from a Gathered: Hand + Stitching vs Grim (18) if it is alive and fighting (it will Grapple), Hard (14) if dead. Witnessing a Gathered pried apart along its seams is Dread 2. A Lack means the seam closes on the carver's hand, and must itself be cut.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Vey).

**EFFECT:** Laid in a wound with Hand + Stitching vs Hard (14), it joins any two pieces of living or freshly dead (within a day) flesh by the next morning: a severed limb is restored (removing that Injury), or a foreign part is grafted (a hand, an eye, a sheet of skin). A foreign part keeps one habit of its first owner (a tremor, a reach, a Want, a clench at dusk); seeing it the first time is Dread 2 for the bearer. The seam is Vey's now: in Cradlewrack it reopens on a pang roll of 1–4 rather than 1–3, and any Part within a mile is drawn toward it.

**WORTH:** 120 lacks the strip. Scarce. The Assemblers sell only to those they trust; Master Quillon Barr buys all he can find. Concealment, and in four lands an accessory to grave-robbery.

### A Borrowed Scar

The born-grown arrive scarred: old sword-cuts, rope-burns, the white puckers of healed arrowheads, all on bodies that have never been anywhere. The Companies call them *birth-marks* and think little of them. The Cutwrights' Linemen think a great deal of them, because in every case they have been able to trace, the scars on a born-grown match the wounds of someone who died on the day the born-grown was born, somewhere on the Table. The College has not published this. Hobb Fallowmere, Warden-Captain of the Rim, was born with a warden's scar on his cheek, and does not like to be asked whose.

A scar cut whole from a dead born-grown and grafted onto living skin carries something of the one who first took the wound: a habit of hand, a trained reflex, a craft. The Rim wardens swear by them. A sergeant of the Second Company who wears a crossbow-burn on her forearm, grafted from a Foundling who went home, has not missed a shot in four years. She dreams of a tower in the Vigil and a lamp going out.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Hard (14) for anyone who has served in the Foundling Companies or the Rim wardens.

**SPOILAGE:** Rots in three days unless grafted. Kept in brine with a pinch of spring-salt, one season.

**CARVING:** From the body of a dead born-grown: Hand + Stitching vs Hard (14) to lift the scar whole. The Companies bury their dead in their own wall and guard it; robbing that wall is Lung + Stealth vs Grim (18), and the Companies do not hand such thieves to the Attendance. They deal with it as a Company. Dread 2 for the carving.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Grafted (Hand + Stitching vs Grim 18, or automatic with a Joining Seam), the scar gives +1 to one skill the original owner plainly had (a sword-cut, Blades; a rope-burn, Athletics or Labor; a burned palm, Craft), to a maximum of 5. Once per session, in a scene that resembles the original death, the bearer takes a Dread 2 check as they remember dying. When the bearer dies, the GM may decide that somewhere in Cradlewrack, that day, a born-grown arrives mid-stride, wearing the wound that killed them.

**WORTH:** 200 lacks. Rare. Traded on the Rim among wardens and caravan guards. The Companies' vengeance is not a legal penalty, but it is a reliable one.

### A Going-Home Blanket

A Newborn of the Foundling Companies is given three things: a number, a place in the Crèche, and a blanket of undyed Company wool. Some born-grown age normally. Some age backward. A few, without warning, revert to infancy in a single night, and in the morning their comrades find a baby in the blanket in the soldier's bed, and the soldier is gone. The Companies call it *going home*. The infant does not live long; it never does. It is bricked into the Company's own wall, and the blanket, which smells of milk and sweat and gun-oil and the soldier's own particular musk, is folded and kept by whoever loved them.

Some are sold, by sergeants who drink, to buyers who have heard what a going-home blanket does. Wrapped in it overnight, a sleeper wakes a year younger: smoother, quicker, lighter in the joints. They also wake lighter in other ways. Old Rim merchants who have tried it describe forgetting things in the order they learned them, last first: the newest language, then a craft, then a face. A spice-factor at the Groaning Board slept in one for seven nights in 638. On the eighth morning the innkeeper found a baby in the bed, in a fine nightshirt much too large for it, and a ledger on the bedside table in a hand nobody could read any more.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Hard (14) in the Companies, who will not discuss it.

**SPOILAGE:** It keeps while unwashed. Washed, it is only wool. The smell, and the effect, fade after nine pangs.

**CARVING:** From a comrade's keeping: Tongue + Persuade vs Hard (14), and a Dread 2 check on being told who it belonged to. From the Crèche's store: Lung + Stealth vs Grim (18).

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Each night slept wrapped in it, the sleeper wakes a year younger in body, heals 1d6 Flesh and loses one healed Injury's lasting penalty, and loses 1 point from the skill they most recently raised. On the third night they also lose a Knack. On the seventh night, and every night after, they must roll Caul + Resolve vs Dire (22) or go home.

**WORTH:** 350 lacks. Rare. Bought by the old and rich of every land except Sallowreach, where nobody ages and everyone already forgets. The Companies regard the sale as the theft of a body.

### A Root-End

Among the Uncut, the eldest let their cords grow down into the clay, and are called Roots, and through their cords the long houses feel the pulse of the land itself. Maeve Cord's mother is the oldest Root in Cordway, and her cord does not end in her tomb. When a Root's cord is cut, by the Barren Order's saboteurs or by a desperate thief, the end that went into the ground comes up: a pale knot of fibrous cord as thick as a wrist, trailing fine red rootlets like the veins of an eye, and beating. It does not beat with the Root's pulse. It beats with the land's, and under that, faster and smaller, the second pulse Madder Rudge hears at the Dilation.

In 637 a Grey Brother of Nullhouse cut a Root's cord in Cordway at the height of a pang, to sever the Uncut from the ground. Both ends bled. The Root died in the night, and her daughter on the other end of the cord died with her. The root-end came up in the Grey Brother's hands as he pulled, and he could not let go of it for a day. It is in Nullhouse now, in a grey-stone box, and the brothers who keep it say it is beating faster every pang.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); every Uncut knows, and will not say.

**SPOILAGE:** It wants soil. Bedded in a pot of red highland clay it lives indefinitely; out of clay it withers in nine days. Abroad, it puts rootlets into any soil it rests on, and the Highland Leavings Abroad roll is made at 1–3.

**CARVING:** Cutting a living Root's cord is Hand + Blades vs Hard (14) and is, to the Uncut, the murder of two people. Dragging the end up out of the clay is Gut + Labor vs Grim (18); the ground holds on. Dread 3. A Lack means the carver feels the second pulse in their own chest for a week, and gains 2 Hunger each day of it.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Vey).

**EFFECT:** The holder knows the hour of the next pang a full day in advance. Laid on bare ground, it twitches toward the Dilation and beats faster near any crowning. Held to the ear, its second pulse can be listened to: Eye + Instinct vs Grim (18) learns one true thing about what is under the Dilation, and costs a Dread 4 check every time. A Cradlewracker who carries it feels a Pull (14) toward the Dilation once each day.

**WORTH:** 900 lacks. Rare. The Barren Order buys them to keep in stone; the Second Table buys them to follow. The Uncut do not buy them back. They come for them.

## From the Ground in Labor

[sigil: cradlewrack]

The Attendance believes no single person will ever be Seated in Cradlewrack, because Vey is not regrowing in a person. It is regrowing in the country. That makes the land itself the god's body, and its leavings the nearest thing on the Table to a piece of a Provider that has come back whole. Pilgrims at Rimwatch sell the clay of the Dilation's lip by the jar; the masons of the Stillyard sell what comes out of their walls; Old Hinge sweeps up splinters below a door older than the Gorging and does not know what to do with them. Highlanders handle the land's leavings with the same flat practicality they bring to everything else, and with one difference. They do not keep them in the house during a pang.

### Lip-Clay of the Dilation

The lip of the Dilation is soft red clay that gives underfoot like flesh and slopes inward to the drop. Every morning the Rimwatch reset their painted stakes at the new edge, and every morning, before the stakes go in, the pilgrims and the poorer Rudge diggers go down onto the slick slope with buckets and scoop the clay from the very edge, where it is warmest. It smells of iron and birth-water. In the jar it flexes at each pang, a slow squeeze and release, and the lid must be tied down.

The potters of Kest learned early not to fire it. A cup made of lip-clay in 636 held tea for two pangs; at the third, its bottom dilated in its owner's hand, and something the size of a plum, wet and dark, came up through it and dropped into her lap. What the clay is good for is drawing things out. Smeared into a wound, it widens the wound, deepens it, and delivers whatever is lodged inside: arrowheads, splinters, a swallowed key, poison, the toothed seed of Fatlands wheat. The Reopening Ward uses it, under protest, on Foundling soldiers with bolts in them. It hurts more than the bolt did.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in Cradlewrack; everyone at Rimwatch sells it. Hard (14) abroad.

**SPOILAGE:** Warm and active for one pang. After the second it cools to ordinary red clay, unless kept in a sealed jar topped with water from a highland spring, which keeps it a season.

**CARVING:** Scooping from the advancing edge is Gut + Athletics vs Hard (14) on a slope that gives. The rim is Dread 2; during a pang, Dread 4. A Lack means the slope moves: Lung + Athletics vs Grim (18) or slide over the edge, which is the end of the character.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Vey), 1 Hunger.

**EFFECT:** Smeared into a wound with Hand + Stitching vs Routine (10), it delivers anything lodged in the body: a bolt-head, a splinter, a swallowed object, or one poison or drug (ending its effect at once). The patient takes 1d6 damage that ignores Armor and a Dread 2 check, and that wound reopens on a pang roll of 1–5 forever after. Used as mortar, it gives any wall a door within nine days, where there was none, and Doorborn are known to come out of such doors.

**WORTH:** 15 lacks the jar at Rimwatch. Uncommon in Cradlewrack, Scarce abroad. Not illegal. The Attendance looks at buyers in a way that makes them wish it were.

### A Splinter of the Sill

On the bare hilltop in the north, a single oak door stands in its frame with no wall around it, grey with age, iron-bound, older than the Gorging, ajar since that night and opening a finger's width further every year. As the gap widens the old wood stretches, and splinters come away from the leading edge: long pale slivers, dry and grey on the outer side, warm and faintly damp on the side that faced the gap. Old Hinge sweeps them up and burns them. Not all of them. Pilgrims steal them from the grass, and Hinge, who is old and short of money, sells a few.

A splinter carried in a pocket makes doors behave as the Sill behaves. They stand open a finger's width behind the carrier, every one, when they look back. Pressed into a keyhole, a crack or a gap between boards, a splinter shows the far side of the Sill instead of the far side of the crack. What can be seen there varies. Agnes Latch, who has studied the Sill through a glass for twenty years and built every one of her best doors to its pattern, bought four splinters in 639 and has not let anyone into her workshop since.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); anyone who has visited the Sill knows of the splinters.

**SPOILAGE:** None. On Tablenight every splinter of the Sill, wherever it is on the Table, stands upright on its end and stays so until dawn.

**CARVING:** From Hinge's sweepings: Tongue + Persuade vs Hard (14), or a few lacks. From the door itself: Hand + Craft vs Grim (18) to pry a sliver from the edge without touching the gap. Touching the gap is Dread 4. A Lack means the door opens a finger's width further while the carver's eye is to it.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Carried, it gives +2 Stealth to slip through doors unheard, and the carrier leaves every door they pass a finger's width open; Cradlewrackers gain the Want **Leave It Open** at Routine (10) if they did not have it. Pressed into any gap and looked through, Eye + Godsign vs Grim (18) answers one question about something that is beginning, from the other side of the Sill: Dread 4. On a Lack, something on the far side looks back through the crack, and knows the carrier's face.

**WORTH:** 250 lacks. Scarce in Cradlewrack, Rare abroad. The Attendance forbids approach to the Sill, and Agnes Latch outbids everyone.

### A Knocked Brick

The long walls of the Stillyard tick and settle on still nights, and sometimes they knock. Families pay masons to listen, and when a wall knocks back, the Attendance is sent for, and attends, and afterward a mason takes out the brick with the family's stamp and lays a new one. The old brick is meant to be broken. Absalom Gage, the deaf chief mason, has a stack of them in his yard, forty years' worth, and lately he can hear them.

A knocked brick goes on knocking. It is an ordinary red brick, stamped with a family's mark, a little warmer than it should be, and it carries the dead behind it the way a shell carries the sea. Mortared into a house wall, it knocks three slow times whenever anything comes through any door of that house unannounced, which in Cradlewrack is the most valuable warning there is. Laid on a table with a palm flat on it, it answers questions from whoever was bricked behind it: one knock for yes, two for no. There is a third answer. Gage says the dead give it more often every year.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Kest; masons know without a roll.

**SPOILAGE:** Removed from the Stillyard, a brick knocks for a year and a day and then is only a brick. Mortared into a wall of grey stone, it knocks forever.

**CARVING:** Unbricking a knocking brick from a Stillyard wall is Hand + Labor vs Hard (14), and desecration. Feeling it knock against the palm as it comes free is Dread 2. A Lack means it knocks from inside the carver's pack all night, and they get no rest.

**TAINT:** Dread 2; and 1 Regrowth (Vey) each time it is questioned.

**EFFECT:** Set in a wall, the household cannot be surprised by anything entering through a door. Questioned, it answers up to three questions a night from the dead behind it, by knocks; each night of questions is a Dread 2 check. On any question, the GM may answer with three knocks, which means *come and see*: the questioner must pass Caul + Resolve vs Hard (14) or walk to the Stillyard that night and open the niche.

**WORTH:** 70 lacks. Scarce. Sold by masons on the quiet. Desecration; the family whose mark it bears may take it worse than the Attendance does.

### A Doorborn's Key-Finger

Doorborn are the offspring of thresholds: thin grey things with limbs jointed like hinges, born from any door, gate or lid shut too long. They come out at night into sleeping houses and open things with long fingers like keys, neatly, along every seam a midwife knows. The finger of a dead one is as long as a man's hand, grey, cold, with five small joints that bend in both directions and a tip flattened into wards like a key's bit. Drawn along a body, it opens it along a seam, cleanly, without blood, and the patient stays awake and feels nothing but a draught.

The surgeons of the Reopening Ward are forbidden to use them and do. Tamsin Gorse keeps one in a knotted cloth in her apron and will deny it to anyone but a patient on her table. Burglars use them too. A key-finger slid into any lock turns it as if it were made for it, and then keeps turning, very gently, after the lock is open, as if feeling for something further in.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Hard (14) for surgeons and thieves in Kest.

**SPOILAGE:** Wrapped tight in a knotted cord it stays supple for a season. Unwrapped it stiffens within a pang into grey bone, which still opens locks and no longer opens bodies.

**CARVING:** Kill a Doorborn (Threat 4, Flesh 12, Guard 16, Dread 3) and cut a finger free: Hand + Stitching vs Hard (14). A Lack means the finger opens the carver's palm along its lines as it comes away: 2 damage, ignoring Armor, and a scar that will reopen.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** As a surgical tool, +4 to Stitching for any surgery, with no bleeding and no pain for the patient. As a pick, +4 to Filch against locks. As a weapon, opening fingers (3); with two Helpings the target's oldest scar opens and bleeds 1 Flesh a round until stitched. Every seam it opens reopens on a pang roll of 1–3. A Cradlewracker who carries it unwrapped gains the Want **Leave It Open** at Hard (14).

**WORTH:** 180 lacks. Scarce. Surgeons, burglars, the Second Table's Master Barr. Concealment; the Attendance regards any Doorborn part as a birth that should have been bricked.

### The Rudge Rope

In 403 A.G. the parish well at Rudge gave birth, and something climbed up the rope and was not seen again. The well has been capped with three tons of fired brick and a millstone, and the parish still sleeps badly. The rope was kept in the church loft: forty fathoms of hemp, stiff and dark, stained to a man's height above the bucket-end, and printed all along its lower length with small marks like the grip of wet hands. It is warm. It is a little longer every year.

The parish sold it in 590 to a Rim collector and Madder Rudge's family bought it back in 611, for more than the parish could explain. In the summer of 629 someone took it out to the Dilation at night and let it down over the edge, and something on it went down ninety feet, and came back. The rope came up with a handprint pressed into its lowest knot, warm, the clay still in the whorls of the fingers, and larger than any human hand. Madder says the rope was stolen. She says a great many things, upside down, through her pipe.

Let down into any hole deep enough, the Rudge Rope is taken hold of. Something always climbs.

**WHISPERED:** Everyone in Rudge knows. Elsewhere, Eye + Lore vs Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** None. It grows a hand's breadth longer each pang.

**CARVING:** Unique. Kept by the Rudge family in a bricked room under Madder's cottage; getting in is Hand + Labor vs Hard (14) and getting out past Rudge diggers is another matter. Holding it is Dread 3: the grip-marks are warm, and one of them is closing.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** Lowered into any opening deeper than a man is tall (a well, a shaft, a grave, a cistern, a crowning), within the hour something takes hold of it and climbs. Roll a d6 when it reaches the top.

| d6 | What climbs the Rudge Rope |
| 1 | Water, rising up the rope in a warm clear column that smells of the springs, and keeps rising. |
| 2 | Whatever was lost down there, whole and wet: a body, a bucket, a child's shoe, a person who fell. They ask what year it is. |
| 3 | A Part, holding on with one hand. |
| 4 | A born-grown, naked and slick, who climbs out by the knots and walks away without a word. |
| 5 | Nothing. The rope comes up with a new handprint on the lowest knot, larger than the last. |
| 6 | The thing from 403. It is much bigger now. It remembers the rope. |

**WORTH:** Not sold since 611. The Assemblers have offered 2,000 lacks. The Attendance does not know it still exists.

# Leavings of Oathen

> Nothing is sold in Tacit. Things are described, and the description is guaranteed.
>> — Silas Amend, Paper-Broker of Tacit, to a Fatlands buyer who asked whether a thing worked

[bigquote] Every leaving in the canyons is a sentence somebody could not take back. Buy it as you would buy a knife: by the handle, and without asking what it has cut. | — marginal note in the Bench's Register of Seized Goods

Very little of Tolm is left to carve. Its ears were boiled and eaten by the upper gorges, its tongue by the Tongue-lines, its heart by the Rain-keepers, and its bones were rolled into the Gullet and lie under a floor of hot red sand that the Bench forbids anyone to dig. What Oathen has instead are leavings of the word: things that heard too much, things that were broken by what was said, things that bind. A Course-Ear cut from a noble's neck still listening on its plate. A Surety's tattooed skin with a stranger's promise in it. A flask of rain a god swore to send. The canyon folk handle these things in silence, gloved, often bitted, because a leaving of Tolm can bind a person who never meant to say anything at all.

The trade is the most honest on the Table and the most frustrating. A seller in Oathen who says "this works" has sworn that it does, and breaks if it does not. Oathen dealers therefore say almost nothing. They lay the leaving on felt, set beside it a slate on which they have chalked what others have said of it ("*I am told*," "*It was said in my hearing*," "*The previous owner believed*"), and wait. Price-witnesses in brass collar-plates stand by for a crumb a word. Large sums change hands in sworn notes, which are the safest paper on earth, and the Bench of Clauses keeps a Register of Seized Goods in which every leaving that binds is entered as the property of the Bench, because every word of public record is. The Register is very long, and its upper rooms are lined with felt.

What Oatheners fear in a leaving is not rot but repetition. A thing that heard an oath can say it again, and an oath repeated is an oath renewed. Leavings are kept where they cannot echo: in felt, in wax, in lead, and for the very worst, in gold, the one metal Tolm's mouth will not bite through. They are moved on windless days and never discussed while the Tattler blows. And every Oathener knows, as they know the exits in their own sentences, that the widening is happening: that handtalk breaks fingers now, that writing binds at half its Weight, that the Quiet believe even thought is beginning to count. A leaving bought last year under one set of rules may be under another this year. The Bench has begun adding a clause to every relic sale, *as the god now counts*, and nobody can say what it covers.

[box: Rules — Leavings That Bind]
Many leavings in this chapter make something an oath that would not otherwise be one. Treat the result exactly as an oath sworn in Oathen: it has a **Weight** (given in the entry), may carry **Exits** if the character drafts carefully (Tongue + Clause, as in the Oathen chapter), and on breach brings a **Breaking** from the Breaking table, at 2 × Weight Flesh. Unless an entry says otherwise, a leaving's oath binds only within Oathen's borders; leavings that bind abroad say so, and are worth a great deal more for it. Echoes renew a leaving's oath as they renew any other.
[/box]

## From the Tongue-lines and the Sayers

[sigil: oathen]

The highest blood in the canyons is the furthest along, and the Tongue-line houses shed leavings the way old trees shed bark: small ears cut from the neck at the Course, saliva caught when a Sayer's gold is off for cleaning, tongues given to the Quiet's knife and kept in brine, vows scratched into plaster by swearers who cannot be stopped. The Bench owns all of it, in law. The houses sell a great deal of it, in fact, through physicians, bitmakers and the very discreet, to buyers who have a use for a thing that makes words count. The Second Table has agents in at least three Tongue-line houses and an interest in this section of the market that needs no explaining.

### A Course-Ear

At the Course, the small ears come: puckers in the skin of the neck and shoulders and the inner arm that open, over a season, into perfect little ears, pink and folded, turning toward sound. The Tongue-lines who grow them cannot bear crowds, and their physicians cut the ears away in felt-lined rooms with a curved blade and a pot of cedar salve. The cut ears do not die. Laid on a dish, they go on turning toward every voice, and if the room is silent long enough, they turn toward the nearest heartbeat.

Spies discovered what they were for within a generation. An ear tacked under a table, sewn into a collar or dropped into a planter at a treaty-sitting hears everything said within ten paces, and held afterward to a listener's own ear it repeats all of it, verbatim, slowly, in the voices of those who spoke. In the month the Mute King made his sound, the Bench seized every ear in the Hall of Conditions and burned them in a lead furnace. The rumour on the Shadeward Wall is that one was missed, sewn into the hem of the clerk who heard it, and that it has been repeating the sound ever since, very quietly, in a drawer.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); spies, Clausewrights and the crown's household know without a roll.

**SPOILAGE:** It lives on sound. Kept in total silence (sealed in felt) it starves in a season; kept in a busy room it lives for years, and after the first year a second, smaller ear buds beside it.

**CARVING:** Cut from a Tongue-line at the Course with Hand + Stitching vs Hard (14), with the patient's consent or under a physician's cover; stolen from a house's felted box with Hand + Filch vs Grim (18). Dread 2: it turns toward the carver as they breathe. A Lack means it has heard the carver, and will repeat what they said, at the worst moment, in their voice.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Tolm).

**EFFECT:** Left in place for up to a day, it hears everything said within ten paces. Held to the ear afterward, it repeats it all at half speed. An oath it repeats is renewed, at its original Weight, for the one who swore it, wherever they are in Oathen: an ear is a way to bind a person twice. It can be made to stop only by sealing it in felt.

**WORTH:** 400 lacks. Rare. The crown's spies buy all they can; so does the Rim Road Company. Planting one in the Hall of Conditions carries the clerk's sentence: the bit, for life.

### Sayer's Ink

Every few months, the gold bit of a Sayer must come out to be resized, because the tongue behind it keeps growing. Garl Tome does it himself, in the Gilded Cells, with the guards deafened and the Bencher of the week standing by with a fresh bit in her gloved hands, and in the moments the mouth is open, it runs: a thick, clear saliva that smells of wet slate and tastes, those who have caught a fleck on the lip say, of the moment after you have said a thing you cannot take back. Tome catches it in a gold cup, because it eats every other metal it touches. He is meant to pour it into the furnace. He has a daughter whose voice he has never heard, and a dowry to think of.

Ground with lampblack and gum, it makes an ink that binds. What is written in it is not merely written: it is said, in the Sayer's mouth, at full Weight, and the god hears it as speech. A contract written and signed in Sayer's Ink binds its signers as if they had sworn it aloud before the Ledger, and it does so anywhere on the Table. The Rim Road Company has been looking, quietly, for something better than Oathen to swear by. Lysander Coyle, the Auditor-General, bought a phial in 639 and has not told the Wheel.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22). Garl Tome, two Benchers and Lysander Coyle know for certain.

**SPOILAGE:** Sealed in gold or canyon-glass under wax, a year. Ink dried on the page binds forever. Ink more than a year in the phial still writes, but binds at half Weight.

**CARVING:** Only in the Gilded Cells, during a resizing: Hand + Craft vs Grim (18) to catch it as Tome's assistant, or Lung + Stealth vs Dire (22) to be in the room unasked. A Sayer's open mouth is Dread 3, and a Sayer with her bit out may say something.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 1 (the wet ink moves on the page like a tongue).

**EFFECT:** A promise written in it and signed is an oath of its full Weight (usually 3 or 4) for every signer, binding anywhere on the Table, with Breakings as in Oathen. A lie written in it breaks the writer's hand (Weight 3). A phial writes about twenty pages.

**WORTH:** 1,500 lacks the phial. Rare. Possession outside the Bench is treason against the Ledger. Inside the Company, it is the most interesting thing anyone has seen in a century.

### A Tongue from Mote's Vault

Brother Ansgar Mote, surgeon of the Quiet, has taken four hundred tongues, cleanly, from novices who chose the knife over the vow. He keeps them in brine, labelled, in a vault cut into the cliff above the upper Gullet, because he once threw one on the midden and it went on trying to speak there for a week, and the crows would not go near it. Each sits in its own stoppered jar, pale and thick, with a tag of goat-vellum giving the name and the date. At night they move. They shape words without breath, the same words, over and over: the last thing their owners wanted to say before the blade.

Many of the Quiet's novices were born in the Tongue-line houses and knew things. The sentences the tongues are shaping are, in a fair number of cases, the reason their owners took the knife. Mote has begun to suspect something worse, and has written it on his slate and wiped it off again: the tongues are growing. The oldest have put out a second root at the cut, and three of them, he believes, are no longer shaping the same sentence every night. They are shaping new ones.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); the Quiet know and do not speak, for obvious reasons.

**SPOILAGE:** In brine changed every new Plate, indefinitely. Dry, it withers within days, and as it withers it shapes its sentence faster.

**CARVING:** Mote does not sell. Taking a jar from the vault is Lung + Stealth vs Grim (18) among the Quiet's cliff-cells, where the slightest sound carries and every brother is listening. The Quiet regard the theft as the breaking of a vow made on another's behalf. Dread 2 on first seeing one move.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Its sentence can be read off its shapes with Eye + Lore vs Grim (18) (Dacey Furl reads them easily). It is always a secret worth knowing. Placed in the mouth of a corpse, it speaks the sentence aloud once in its owner's voice, and in Oathen what it says binds whomever it concerns, at Weight 3. Each season, roll a d10; on a 1 the tongue has grown, and the GM may have it shape, once, a new sentence that becomes a little true, as at the Course.

**WORTH:** 250 lacks to the curious; far more to whomever its secret concerns. Rare. The Second Table wants the ones that are growing.

### A Hanging Bird of Low Shale

In 598 A.G. a Tongue-line girl said "I wish it would stop" during a storm, and everything in the valley of Low Shale stopped. The river stands in its bed in a single hard green ribbon. A herdsman stands mid-stride with his crook raised and his eyes wet. Larks and wheatears hang in the air a foot above the grass, wings half-spread, exactly where they were, and have hung there for forty-three years with their eyes open. Nothing rots. The air has no smell. The Bench has posted the valley silent and forbids entry, and Bait pilgrims sneak in anyway.

A bird can be taken. It comes out of the air the way a held breath comes out of a chest, with a faint resistance and then all at once, and it lies in the palm weightless and cold. Set down again anywhere, released in midair or pressed against a wall, it stays exactly where it was put, and nothing in the world will move it: not a shoulder, not an ox-team, not a pang in Cradlewrack. An Echo-Warden named Tav Shale (the family took the valley's name) carries one on a cord to anchor ropes in gorges where no stone will hold a piton. He has held a rockfall off a fallen man with it. He does not like to look at its eye.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Echo-Wardens and climbers know at Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** None. It is stopped.

**CARVING:** Entering Low Shale past the Bench's posts is Lung + Stealth vs Hard (14). Standing among the stopped is Dread 3. Taking a bird from the air needs Caul + Resolve vs Hard (14). A Lack means the carver has spoken aloud in the valley, and the herdsman's eyes have turned to them, and will be turned that way when they next come.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 2 (its eye is open, and wet).

**EFFECT:** Released anywhere, it stays there, immovable by any force, until a bare living hand takes it back. A rope tied to it holds any weight; a door wedged with it cannot be opened, in any land; a blow struck against it is like striking a cliff (the attacker takes 2 damage). Whoever takes it back loses all feeling in that hand for an hour (−2 to Hand rolls).

**WORTH:** 600 lacks. Rare. Echo-Wardens, burglars, the Canyon Watch. Entering Low Shale is a Bench offence; taking from it, the Bench says, is theft from a Sayer's sentence.

### A Scratched Vow

At the Appetite, swearers vow without meaning to, and must be bitted for their own safety; and a determined swearer, bitted and with hands bound, will still try to scratch vows into the plaster with a fingernail. The walls of the Murmuring House are covered in them, hundreds deep, in the shaky capitals of people who cannot stop promising. The nurses whitewash the wards every month. By the end of the month the white is scored again. Since writing began to bind, the nurses have stopped reading the walls, and wear their eyes lowered on the wards.

A slab of that plaster, cut out with a mason's saw, holds a vow that binds whoever reads it aloud, because to read a vow aloud is to say it. The Breakdowns' liars-for-hire use them as snares: a scratched vow passed across a counter to an illiterate partner, read aloud by a helpful clerk to a debtor, nailed over a door for a rival to puzzle out. The Bench calls this *snaring* and punishes it with the snarer swearing the vow themselves. Dacey Furl has found, on the night ward, a vow scratched over and over in three hundred different hands: the vow all three hundred are mouthing in their sleep. Its last word is always scratched through.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Tacit.

**SPOILAGE:** Plaster crumbles in a year; under glass, a decade. The vow fades as the scratches fill with dust.

**CARVING:** Hand + Craft vs Hard (14) to saw a slab out of a Murmuring House wall, with a Caul + Resolve vs Routine (10) not to read what you are cutting. A Lack means you read it.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Reading it aloud swears it: Weight 2 (Weight 3 in Oathen, where the reader can be heard by the canyon). Reading it silently: roll a d10, and on a 1–2 the reader is bound at Weight 1. Roll a d6 for what a slab says.

| d6 | The scratched vow |
| 1 | I WILL WALK TO THE SEA |
| 2 | I WILL NEVER EAT BREAD AGAIN |
| 3 | I WILL HAVE YOUR EYES |
| 4 | I WILL NOT WAKE |
| 5 | I WILL GO DOWN TO THE GULLET AND LIE ON IT |
| 6 | The night-ward vow, its last word scratched through. The GM decides what it was. |

**WORTH:** 30 lacks. Scarce. Snaring is a Bench crime in Tacit and a growing trade on the Rim, where nobody reads the slabs before buying them.

## From the Broken and the Breakers

[sigil: oathen]

The Breakdowns are the one place in Oathen where a person can lie, because the Forsworn have nothing left to break in the mouth, and so they are also the one place in Oathen where leavings are sold with patter. The broken trade in what broke them and what broke others: tattooed skin from Sureties, bones from the Unmade Road, the receipts of a back-canyon fixer, the hands of the first man the god ever took at his word. The Bench's executioner leaves very little behind him, and what he leaves is the most frightening thing in the canyons. The Breakdowns treat all of these as a dockside treats rope: as the stuff of a hard trade, handled by people who have already paid.

### A Surety's Patch

A Surety carries other people's oaths tattooed on the body in dense fine script, one bond to a patch of skin, until they are a ledger of other families' risk. When a Surety dies, the oaths they carried are released, as all a dead person's oaths are. The skin does not know that. A patch of tattooed skin cut from a dead Surety and tanned still holds its bond, and the bond is still looking for something to break.

Sewn to a living body, a patch will take a Breaking in its wearer's place, once, the way its Surety would have. The skin tears along the script, the bone beneath cracks like a dropped plate, and the wearer is left whole underneath, bruised and shaking. The Breakdowns sell patches by Weight. The Surety House calls the trade carrion-work and has twice sent members, at fee, to stand surety for oaths sworn against the patch-dealers in the Gathering of the Broken. A patch-dealer named Aubin Hollow had his hands come off on Fair Weight Row in 638 as a result, and sells patches still, with his feet.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); anyone of the Surety House or the Breakdowns knows.

**SPOILAGE:** Untanned, a week. Tanned with canyon salt and cedar oil, years.

**CARVING:** Sureties are entombed upright in the Witnessing Vaults with the rest of the dead. Flaying one is Hand + Stitching vs Hard (14) and a Dread 2 check. Desecration of a niche is a Bench crime; the Keeners say a Surety flayed in her niche has been known to Testify. A Lack means the patch tears in the cutting and is worthless.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Sewn or strapped against the skin, it absorbs the next Breaking its wearer suffers of a Weight no greater than the bond tattooed on it (1 to 4). The wearer loses only half the Flesh, gains no Forsworn mark, and makes the Dread check as a witness rather than as the broken. The patch is then spent. Wearing more than one at once makes them all fail.

**WORTH:** 50 lacks per Weight of the bond. Scarce in the Breakdowns, Rare elsewhere. Sold openly on Back-Before-Dark; possession is not a crime, only its getting.

### The Merchant's Hands

In the third year after the Gorging, a merchant of Tacit who had sworn to deal fairly with a customer shorted him by a thumb's weight of salt. His hands came off at the wrist in the marketplace, cleanly, with a sound like a dropped pail, and lay on the stones among the salt. It was the First Breaking, and the customer's family dried the hands and kept them as proof, and gave them to the Bench in the Honest Age. For three centuries they lay on the great scales in the Hall of Weights, palms up, curled as though holding a scale-pan, brown and hard as dried figs, and no merchant in Tacit cheated within sight of them.

In the Forsworn Riots of 506 they were stolen, and they are in the Breakdowns now, in a niche at the head of Fair Weight Row, the lane named for the oath the merchant broke. The Forsworn call him *the first of us* and lay copper crumbs before the niche. Merchants who deal in the Breakdowns come and swear their weights in front of the hands, which is the only place below the Walls where anybody's word is good. The Rim Road Company has offered four thousand lacks to set them on the scales at Toll Nine. Brakk has said no in four words, plainly.

**WHISPERED:** Every Oathener knows the story; that the hands survive and where, Eye + Lore vs Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** None. They have not changed in six hundred and thirty-eight years.

**CARVING:** Unique. Taking them from Fair Weight Row is Lung + Stealth vs Dire (22) through a slum that never sleeps, and would start the Second Forsworn Riot. Touching them is Dread 1: they are warm, and the fingers tighten.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Set on a counter or a pair of scales, the hands make false weight and false measure impossible within sight of them: loaded scales read true, clipped and counterfeit lacks are pointed out by a twitch of a finger, and a merchant who has made any promise of fair dealing and breaks it in their presence suffers the Breaking at +1 Weight. Haggling in their presence uses Eye + Haggle for both parties, never Deceive.

**WORTH:** Never sold. The Bench wants them back; the Company wants them on the road; the Breakdowns would burn Tacit's lower bridges first.

### A Knuckle from the Unmade Road

In 627 A.G. the four hundred soldiers of the Fourth Regiment swore to hold Wending Pass to the death, obeyed their captain's order to fall back, and broke on the road behind him, each opening at every joint by which he had bound himself to the place. Their remains were never cleared. The bone along the switchbacks below the pass is still sorted, roughly, into the shapes of men lying where they fell, and the drovers walk their animals single file down the centre of the road and do not look to either side. Renn Hold is still at the top. He has to be.

A knucklebone from the Unmade Road is small, brown, polished by fourteen years of wind, and carries in it the soldier's oath to hold. Canyon Watch soldiers carry them, illegally, sewn into the lining of a sleeve, and swear by them before a fight. A man with a Wending knuckle in his fist does not get knocked down and does not run. Sergeant Mags Carrow, who keeps the eleven unstrung survivors in the Breakdowns, has beaten three knuckle-sellers half to death with her spear-butt and thrown their stock into the river. She says the boys gave enough. She says it plainly. She has the eye to spare.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); any soldier of the Canyon Watch knows.

**SPOILAGE:** None. But each Tablenight a knuckle moves, a little, wherever it is kept: it is found nearer the eastern side of the room, toward Wending.

**CARVING:** Reaching the road means passing below Renn Hold, who will let nobody through who has not sworn to hold the pass (see the Oathen chapter). Finding a whole knuckle in the sorted bone is Eye + Search vs Hard (14) and a Dread 2 check. A Lack means the nearest shape of bone has shifted, in the time the carver was bent over it, to face them: Dread 3.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** The bearer says aloud "I hold here" (a Weight 2 oath, which the knuckle makes binding in any land). Until the danger in that place has passed, they gain +4 to resist being moved, knocked down, Grappled away, Pulled or routed, and +2 to Dread checks. If they leave of their own will before the danger has passed, they break as the four hundred broke: the knees, at Weight 2.

**WORTH:** 120 lacks. Scarce. Sold in Canyon Watch barracks and on the Rim to caravan guards. Desecration of the war-dead; the Watch does not prosecute, but Mags Carrow does.

### A Vow-Eater's Receipt

Grandam Cess, the back-canyon fixer, can release a client from an oath by having them swear a greater one over it. Her clients go away free of what they came with, and owe her something worse. Each is given a receipt: a strip of goat-vellum on which the old oath is written out in full and struck through in red, and beneath it the new oath, in a shorthand of Cess's own that no Clausewright has managed to read, signed in the client's blood. The receipt is the debt. Whoever holds it when Cess comes to collect is the one who pays.

Her clients learn this late. A whole Oathener cannot sell one honestly without saying what it is, and so they sell them through the Breakdowns, where the liars-for-hire describe them, with a straight face and an opened mouth, as *a free release from Grandam Cess*. That part is even true. Present a receipt to Cess, and she will release the holder from any one oath of theirs, of any Weight, for nothing more. She will simply note, in her soft dry voice, that the holder now owes her what the last holder owed. She knows what it is. She is the only one who does.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Hard (14) in the Breakdowns, where everyone has been offered one.

**SPOILAGE:** Valid until Cess collects, or dies. If she dies, the Breakdowns believe, every receipt she ever issued comes due in the same afternoon.

**CARVING:** Bought from a desperate client or a liar-for-hire with Tongue + Haggle vs Routine (10); the sellers are very eager. Reading the shorthand is Eye + Clause vs Dire (22), and most never try.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Presented to Grandam Cess, it releases the holder from one oath of their choice, of any Weight, without Breaking. The holder then owes the receipt's debt, which binds as a Weight 4 oath. Roll a d6, or let the GM choose, for what is owed.

| d6 | What you owe Grandam Cess |
| 1 | A year of silence, to begin when she says so. |
| 2 | The name of the next child born under your roof, before its parents choose it. |
| 3 | Your left hand's work for a season. It will do her business at night, and you will not remember. |
| 4 | To carry one oath of hers, unread, as her Surety. |
| 5 | A Sayer's word, sealed in wax, brought to her door. |
| 6 | To promise nothing ever again. Your next oath of any Weight breaks you as it is spoken. |

**WORTH:** 20 to 500 lacks, depending on how desperate the seller is. Scarce in the Breakdowns. Perfectly legal, because nobody can prove what it says.

### The Breaker's Glass

The state executioner of Oathen owns no weapon. Jude Crake visits the condemned in the Ninth Room beneath the Ledger, turns an hour-glass at the first word, and talks pleasantly until they promise him something small. It has never taken longer than an hour. The glass he turns is not his. It belonged to the Breaker before him, who was condemned in his turn, and whom Crake, then a young man awaiting his own Breaker, talked to death in forty minutes across the same table. Crake kept the glass. It is plain canyon glass in a frame of olive-wood, and its sand is fine and red, and it has run, by the clerks' count, for eleven hundred dissolutions.

The room is warm when it runs. People who have sat across from it describe a pressure like the pressure in the throat before a sneeze, a wanting to agree, to reassure, to say *yes, I'll try*. The sand has heard every small promise made in that room for sixty years, and it remembers how they are got. Crake has said, pleasantly, to more than one Bencher, that he will give the glass to anyone who can talk with him for a full hour while it runs without promising him anything at all. Nobody has taken him up on it.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18). Every Oathener knows the Breaker; few know the glass matters.

**SPOILAGE:** Each turning, one grain of sand fails to fall and stays in the upper bulb. When the upper bulb is stopped, the glass will run no more. The clerks estimate forty turnings remain.

**CARVING:** Unique. It is kept in the Ninth Room beneath the Ledger, and stealing it is Lung + Stealth vs Dire (22). Winning it from Crake means an hour's talk with him while it runs: four Tongue + Clause rolls vs Grim (18), each failure a small promise he will hold you to.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** While it runs (one hour), everyone who speaks within its hearing must pass Tongue + Clause vs Hard (14) at each quarter of the glass, Grim (18) in the last quarter, or make a small promise (Weight 1) of the turner's choosing in the course of conversation. In Oathen these bind. Elsewhere they do not, but the turner gains +4 to Persuade and Haggle to extract commitments for the hour.

**WORTH:** Never sold. The Bench regards it as Crake's personal property, which frightens the Bench more than anything else about him.

## From the Canyons and the Vaults

[sigil: oathen]

The country itself keeps the god's word better than any of its people. The canyon walls repeat everything, the Gullet's sand swallows it, the Ledger holds four miles of it in stone, and the dead stand upright in the Witnessing Vaults to hear it. Rain falls once a year because a god promised. The leavings of the canyons are the stuff of that keeping: sand, stone, water, bone and horn that have spent centuries hearing oaths and holding them. The Echo-Wardens regard most of them as hazards to be packed in felt. The Bench regards all of them as public record. The Rim Road Company regards them as the most interesting commodities in the east, and has begun to ask the Register of Seized Goods for an inventory.

### Gullet Sand

The Gullet is the gorge where Tolm was eaten, at the head of the river system west of Tacit, and its floor is deep red sand over the god's bones. Sound does not carry there. It sinks, as into a pillow, and a man shouting at the top of his lungs in the Gullet can be heard by nobody five paces off. The sand is hot all year, hot enough now on the deep floor to blister bare feet, and pilgrims who walk it barefoot on Tablenight eve come out with soles that weep for a week. Those who lay an ear to it hear a slow sound like breathing. The Bench rules it is the river. Blasphemers who curse Tolm aloud are found next morning with their tongues gone and their mouths full of fine red sand, and the sand, those who have cleaned the mouths say, is warm.

A pouch of it carried out of the Gullet keeps the Gullet's quiet for a while. Scattered in a ring, it makes a place where nothing echoes and nothing said carries beyond the ring. Smugglers use it to talk in canyons. Lovers use it to make vows that cannot be renewed by the walls. The Echo-Wardens use it by the sack, and the Quiet sleep on it.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in Oathen; it is sold at the Gullet's mouth by the ladle.

**SPOILAGE:** It cools over a season away from the Gullet and stops working. Kept in a felt pouch against the body, it stays warm for a year.

**CARVING:** Hand + Labor vs Routine (10) to scoop, but it must be done in silence on the Gullet floor, as custom and caution require: Lung + Stealth vs Hard (14). Hearing the breathing under the sand is Dread 2. A Lack means the sand took a sound from the carver, a word or a cry, and will give it back, later, from inside the pouch.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Tolm).

**EFFECT:** A handful scattered in a ring three paces across makes, for a scene, a place where nothing echoes and no sound leaves the ring (+4 Stealth against hearing; oaths sworn inside are not renewed by echo, and cannot be overheard by any mortal ear). Anyone who curses Tolm aloud inside the ring loses their tongue, as the blasphemers do. A pouch holds five handfuls.

**WORTH:** 25 lacks the pouch in Oathen, 80 on the Rim. Uncommon in Oathen. The Bench forbids its export; it leaves by the cartload anyway.

### A Flask of Kept Rain

Before it died, Tolm promised the canyons rain on the first day of Grace, and a god's word outlives the god. The Kept Rain has fallen on that day for six hundred and forty years: warm, heavy, a single day's downpour that fills the cisterns and floods the canyon floors and drowns the careless. The Rainwardens keep a cellar under the Rain Shrine where a sealed flask of canyon-glass has been filled on every one of those days, six hundred and forty flasks in rows, each tagged with its year. It is the god's word, bottled. Lovers make the year's vows in the downpour. Merchants anoint contracts with old vintages.

This year the rain came three days late. Yusra Thole, the Rainwarden, has filled the six hundred and forty-first flask and set it at the end of the row, and has not spoken since. Its water is clear and tastes of nothing at all. Three other flasks of Late Rain are known to exist, and the Second Table, the Rim Road Company and the Cutwrights' College each want to know the same thing: what happens to a promise that is anointed with a promise that slipped.

**WHISPERED:** Kept Rain: Eye + Lore vs Routine (10). Late Rain: Eye + Lore vs Grim (18), and whispered only.

**SPOILAGE:** Sealed in canyon-glass, forever. Once opened, it must be used before sunset.

**CARVING:** Anyone can catch Kept Rain on the first day of Grace, though stealing from another's cistern-share is the one theft the Bench treats as a crime against the god. Old vintages from the Rain Shrine cellar are Hand + Filch vs Dire (22). Late Rain is somewhere in Yusra Thole's keeping, and three other places.

**TAINT:** Kept Rain: 1 Regrowth (Tolm). Late Rain: 2 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** *Kept Rain*, touched to the tongue or the page while an oath is sworn, gives that oath one free Exit, "barring what none could help," which the god will always honour. *Late Rain*, so used, lets the swearer break that oath once without Breaking at the moment of breach. Three days later, the Breaking arrives, at +1 Weight, wherever they are.

**WORTH:** Kept Rain 40 lacks a flask for a recent year, 400 for one of the first century. Uncommon. Late Rain: never sold lawfully, and the bidding on the Rim began at 1,000 lacks.

### The Miscut Stone

Ottoline Vant, Scribe of the Ledger, carves the public vows into the cliff from a scaffold. Last year she miscut one by a single word, and the man it named broke that night. Writing is not supposed to count. The Bench had the slab cut out of the cliff face and a true copy carved in its place, and the miscut slab, a block of red stone the size of a hearthstone, was sealed in felt and lead and carried into the Ledger's record-rooms, and entered in the Register of Seized Goods under a number and no description.

The man was a carter named Tacet Varrow, and he had sworn, at his marriage, to cherish his wife *in her house*. The miscut made it *in his house*. She had left him a month before. He broke at the chest, Weight 4, in a house that was not his, with the door open. The slab has not forgotten. It accepts carving. The rumour on the Sunward Wall, which the Register's keepers do not deny, is that a vow chiselled into the miscut stone binds the person it names as though they had sworn it at the Ledger, whether they swore it or not, and that the wrong word on its face is cutting itself deeper every month.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); Ottoline Vant and the Register's keepers know.

**SPOILAGE:** None. The miscut word deepens a hair's breadth each new Plate.

**CARVING:** Unique. Reaching it in the Ledger's record-rooms is Lung + Stealth vs Dire (22). Moving it takes two strong backs. Uncovering it is Dread 2: the miscut word is wet.

**TAINT:** Dread 3, and 3 Regrowth (Tolm) for each vow carved into it.

**EFFECT:** A vow carved into the slab (Hand + Craft vs Hard 14, an hour's work) naming a person binds that person as a Weight 4 oath sworn at the Ledger, from the moment they read it or have it read aloud to them, in any land. They may plead their reading as for any oath. The carver takes a Dread 3 check for each vow, and the slab will hold it there forever.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Lord Varro Esk has asked the Register's keepers, generously, what it would cost to view it. They have not yet named a sum.

### A Warden's Horn

An Echo-Warden of the rank of Tracker carries a horn of signal-silence: a canyon-goat's horn with its bell packed tight with felt from the Gullet's pilgrim-mats. Blown, it does not sound. It un-sounds. For the length of a breath, everything within a bowshot goes silent, the wind and the river and the voices and the echoes still riding the stone, and Wardens across the gorge feel the silence in their one good ear and know where their comrade is. Against a living echo, a horn is a club: the silence breaks the echo's footing in the rock, and it bleeds noise.

Old horns are dangerous. A horn blown against echoes for forty years has eaten forty years of oaths and kept them in its felt, and the felt has to be repacked with fresh every season or the horn begins, faintly, to sound. What it sounds is everything it ever silenced. Warden Hollis Grane's horn went unpacked for one winter in 619 while he lay sick, and in Grace his wife woke in a room full of four hundred overlapping oaths, every one sworn in the eastern gorges in the last forty years, all said at once, all binding. Her hearing did not come back. Neither did some other things.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** The felt must be repacked each season with Gullet felt. Unpacked, roll a d6 each week; on a 1 the horn sounds (see Effect).

**CARVING:** Horns are given at the rank of Tracker, and a Warden is buried with hers. Taking one from a Warden's grave in the eastern gorges is Hand + Labor vs Hard (14) and the Wardens hunt the thieves by ear. Dread 1.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Blown (Lung + Endure vs Hard 14), all sound within a bowshot stops for one round. A living echo in range takes 2d6 damage. Words spoken in that round are heard by no mortal ear and carried by no echo; the god hears them at half Weight. The blower is deaf for an hour. If the horn sounds instead, everyone within a bowshot hears every oath it has ever silenced: Dread 3, and Tongue + Clause vs Grim (18) or be bound by one of them (the GM's choice).

**WORTH:** 300 lacks. Rare. Smugglers pay most, to blind pursuers' ears; the Wardens pay nothing, and take them back.

### A Testifying Jaw

The dead of Oathen stand upright in the Witnessing Vaults beneath Tacit, faces outward, to witness the oaths of the living. When an oath sworn on one of them breaks, the dead answer for Tolm, and the commonest answer is the jaw: it opens in its niche and speaks the oath aloud in the dead one's own voice, loud enough to be heard for a mile through the rock. Old Sabra Wends, the Keener, has heard it forty-two times. Nine of them were this year. The jaws that have spoken hang loose afterward on their hinges, and the Keeners tie them shut with ribbon.

A jaw that has Testified, taken from its niche, is still a witness. An oath sworn with a hand on it is an oath sworn on the dead, wherever it is sworn, and the dead will answer for it as they answer in the Vaults. The Rim Road Company would like several. A jaw in a strongbox at a Fatlands waystation would make every bond sworn there as good as one sworn under the Ledger. Sabra Wends has noticed that her niches are being counted by men in good grey coats.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Keeners and the Company's factors know.

**SPOILAGE:** Bone, and lasting. But a jaw that is not sworn on for a year stops witnessing; the Keeners say the dead grow bored.

**CARVING:** Entering the Vaults unattended is Lung + Stealth vs Grim (18) past the Keeners' lanterns. Removing a jaw is Hand + Stitching vs Hard (14) and a Dread 3 check. A Lack means the jaw speaks the carver's name, once, in the dead one's voice.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** An oath sworn with a hand on the jaw, anywhere on the Table, is an Oath on the Dead in the Oathen manner: +1 Weight, no Surety may carry it, and if it breaks, the swearer suffers a Breaking even outside Oathen and the jaw Testifies (roll on the Oathen chapter's Testify table). Once, on first being taken, the jaw speaks the oath it Testified, which is always someone's secret.

**WORTH:** 700 lacks. Rare. Robbing the Vaults is desecration of the witnesses, and the Bench sends Jude Crake.

## From the Bitted and the Sworn-Into-Being

[sigil: oathen]

Oathen's children are bitted from the first tooth until twelve, its swearers are bitted for their safety, and its kings are bitted for life. Its widows keep promises they made at deathbeds, and some of its sons exist only because somebody swore they would. These are the smallest leavings in the canyons and the most intimate: things that have lived in mouths, things kept alive by a word, things that remain when a word is no longer kept. Oatheners do not sell them lightly. Most come onto the market when a family line ends, and the dealers on the Sunward Wall who handle them do so in white gloves, without speaking, as if attending a funeral.

### An Heirloom Bit

Every Oathen child wears a soft leather muzzle from the first tooth until twelve, decorated by its parents with beads, ribbons and the family mark. In the old families the same bit is passed down, mended and re-lined, through generation after generation, so that a child of the Sele line or the Fell line wears in its mouth the leather that has held back the words of its grandmother's grandmother. Heirloom bits taste of everyone who wore them. Oathen adults who see one in a dealer's case go quiet, and touch the corners of their own mouths, where the groove is.

An old bit has held back a very great many promises. It has learned how. An adult who wears one cannot be made to swear by the god in their blood, and the bit will take the god's anger for the wearer's hands when they sign: a vow made in handtalk while wearing it breaks a bead off the leather instead of a finger. When the beads are gone, it is only leather, and the family it belonged to is properly over.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10); every Oathener knows the custom, and Hard (14) to know what an old one can do.

**SPOILAGE:** Leather; it lasts as long as it is oiled with cedar. A bit worn by fewer than four generations has no virtue.

**CARVING:** Families never sell a bit while the line lives. Heirloom bits come to market when a family dies out, and the dealers find them first. Tasting one the first time is Dread 2: a child's fear, a great many times over.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** While worn, the wearer cannot speak, ignores the Oathen Wants **To Swear** and **To Be Heard**, and gains +2 to Caul + Resolve against any compulsion to speak. Each time the wearer would suffer a Breaking from a handtalk vow, a bead falls from the bit instead and the fingers are spared. A bit has 1d6 + 3 beads.

**WORTH:** 60 to 200 lacks, by age. Uncommon in Tacit. Selling a living family's bit is not a crime in law; in the canyons it is the next thing to one.

### An Ember of the Widow's Fire

Asha Kindle promised her dying husband she would never let the fire go out. That was thirty-one years ago. She has not slept more than an hour at a stretch since, and the fire on her hearth has grown particular about what it is fed. It sulks on wood. It flares on letters. It burned for three days, blue and roaring, on a lock of hair her husband gave her when they were courting, and the house was warm as Plenty in the depth of Lack. It cannot go out. Asha has tested this, once, with a bucket, at four in the morning after a bad night, and the coals lay under the water glowing like eyes.

An ember from that fire, carried in a horn box packed with ash, does not go out either. Rain, wind, a river, a smothering blanket: none of it matters, because a promise is keeping it lit. The Vigil has heard. Garron Ashlock, Lamplighter-General, with ninety days of oil and a supplier who has doubled the price, has sent an agent east with a letter of credit and a horn box. Asha gave one ember away, years ago, to a carter on the Rim, so that some of the fire would be somewhere else if hers failed. The carter has not slept more than an hour at a stretch since.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Tacit; the whole Shadeward Wall knows the widow's fire.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It cannot go out. Each new Plate, it wants something more precious.

**CARVING:** Asha will not give one. She does not sleep, so stealing a coal from her hearth is Hand + Filch vs Grim (18). The fire leans toward a thief, curious about what they have promised: Dread 2.

**TAINT:** 1 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** It gives light and warmth that no wind or water can put out, enough to banish darkness penalties within a few paces. Fed a token of a promise (a ring, a letter, a lock of hair given in love), it blazes for a scene as a torch (3 damage, fire) and warms a house through a night of Lack. Fed only wood, it sulks to a coal, and never dies. Whoever carries it cannot sleep more than an hour at a time (−1 to all rolls each night, to −4, as for sleeplessness).

**WORTH:** 500 lacks. Rare. The Vigil would pay ten times that for enough embers to light a district, and does not yet understand what they eat.

### The Coat of a Faded Oath-Made

The Oath-Made are people created by promises: sons sworn into being, soldiers promised to a cause, a lover sworn to come back who came back. They are warm and real, and they exist only for as long as the promise is kept. When the one who swore it dies, or wavers, or forgets, they begin to fade, first from memory, then from sight, and at the end there is nothing left but the clothes they were wearing, lying in a heap on the floor where they stood, still warm. Ilan, who was promised to his mother as a son, is nine, and his father died in the spring, and he has started to become difficult to see.

The coat of one who has already faded is hard to look at. Eyes slide off it. It hangs in a wardrobe and is overlooked by everyone searching the wardrobe. Worn, it makes its wearer as hard to see as its first owner was at the end, for as long as someone is keeping a promise to the wearer. The crown's spies have three. A Clausewright named Mira Dath, who owns another, has noticed that she can no longer remember the face of the boy who wore it first, though she knew him from birth, and she was the one who drafted the promise.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** The coat itself fades. Each year it is harder to find again once put down: Eye + Search vs Hard (14) to find it in one's own room, Grim (18) after a decade.

**CARVING:** It is simply picked up where its owner faded, from a family who usually wants it gone and cannot bear to let it go: Tongue + Persuade vs Hard (14). Dread 2, when the carver realises they cannot picture the one who wore it.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Tolm), Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** While worn, and while someone is keeping a promise of Weight 2 or more made to the wearer, the wearer gains +4 Stealth and +2 Guard. If that promise is broken, or its maker dies, the wearer begins to fade as an Oath-Made fades: each day thereafter until a new promise is made to them, a companion of the GM's choice forgets their face (Dread 2 when the companion notices), and the wearer gains 1 Fray.

**WORTH:** 450 lacks. Rare. Spies, thieves and the crown. The Bench has no law against it, because it has never been able to find one to seize.

# Leavings of the Fast

> It will keep. That is the whole trouble with it. Everything out here will always keep.
>> — Hollow Ann Pruett, the Card-Keeper, of the place cards in her oiled box

[bigquote] Everything else on the Table is a piece of something that was eaten. Out here it is all still whole, and all of it is being offered. | — Spare Tolland of the Hostwatch

The Fast has no relics in the sense the other lands mean. Nothing of Orrum was ever eaten, and so nothing of Orrum was ever carved. What the Host leaves behind it is not flesh but hospitality: crumbs on a cloth, a candle that does not shorten, a hearth-coal that needs no wood, a silver fork in a Seated hand, a card with a name on it in a loved and lost handwriting, a chair pulled out at an angle. Every one of these was set out for someone. Every one is still, quite literally, on offer. The Unfed have a word for the leavings of the Host that has no plural and no polite form, and they spit after saying it.

They do not trade them. To pick up a thing that has been laid is to accept it, and so the only leavings the Unfed themselves carry are the ones they have broken first: the splinter a new Tablebreaker brings home from her first table, the cards Hollow Ann gathers from the wreckage. Everything else leaves the Fast through the despised. Crumb-Runners carry Ash Mill flour out over the Hem in sacks marked as oats. Blank-poachers carry candles and cloth back with their catches. Hostwatch trackers come home with things they swear they found on the road. And the Rim buys all of it, at prices that would keep a Wanting household for a year, because every other land on the Table believes another land's curse might cure its own, and the starving Fatlands above all believe that somewhere in the middle of the world is a table where you finally feel full.

The Unfed have a second kind of leaving, and it is the only kind they honour: the leavings of their own refusal. Teeth from the chalk trench at Hallowboard. The slate from Lammas Bottom. A Starved Saint's crumbs, saved by a novice who cannot bear to tell her. The book kept at the Lean House. These are starvation's relics, and no Unfed would sell one to save their own life, which is precisely why, every winter of the list, some of them are sold.

What everyone fears in a leaving of the Host is the opposite of what the other lands fear. Nothing of Orrum's rots. Nothing goes stale, cools, tarnishes or runs out. It does not need preserving. It needs *containing*: keeping from being set down, served, spread or laid, because every leaving of the Host remembers what it was for, and if it is given the chance it will make a table of wherever it is.

[box: Rules — The Host's Leavings]
Leavings of Orrum carry **no Hunger or Regrowth Taint**: nothing of the Host was ever eaten, and there is nothing in them for the blood to recognise. Their Dread Taint works as usual. In place of the other kinds, every use of one is an **offer**. The entry gives the offer's difficulty; the user makes an **Invitation check** (Caul + Resolve), adding their Pangs to the difficulty as in the Fast's chapter. Godeaters roll it too. On a failure, the user takes a step toward the Host: the next laid table they see, within a day, they must check again at +2, and on a second failure they sit. An Unfed who carries a leaving of the Host and refuses to use it may claim it once a day as an offer refused (+1 Want), and most Tablebreakers do exactly this with their splinters. Spoilage for these leavings is always *none*; the entries say instead how they are contained.
[/box]

## From the Laid Tables

[sigil: fast]

The tables come in every size: a single plate in a high meadow, a family's supper in a kitchen that has had no hearth in six hundred years, eight places steaming at a crossroads on the Rim, nine miles of cloth along the valley road. They are always perfect. The Tablebreakers smash them with axes before anyone can sit, in a set order (lamp, plates, legs, top, chairs last, every chair), and the tables bleed gravy as they go and their candles cry out like children. What comes away from that work, or is snatched before it, is the commonest leaving of the Fast and the most dangerous to carry, because it was so recently a meal.

### A Breaker's Splinter

To join the Tablebreakers, an Unfed must break a table alone and bring back a splinter. It is the only rite of entry the order has, and it has never been made easier. The splinter is pale, of a fine close wood that no Rim joiner has been able to name, and sticky on one side with gravy that never dries. It is warm. It smells of roast fat and onion, faintly, for ever. A new Axe wears hers on a cord around the neck under the shirt, against the skin, and an Old Breaker of a hundred tables wears a whole rattling string of them, and the smell of an Old Breaker coming down a lane in Wanting makes children cry.

The Hostwatch discovered what else a splinter does in 512, the second winter of the Great Short. A splinter laid on bare ground at dusk is found at dawn with a whole table grown up around it, laid and lit, as if the wood had remembered what it belonged to and called the rest of itself back. Since then the Hostwatch have used splinters as bait: a splinter planted a mile from a village, on the far side of a hill, draws the next table there and not into somebody's kitchen. It does not always work. Sometimes the table is laid at the splinter and also in the kitchen.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) in the Fast; everyone knows the rite. The bait trick: Hard (14), Hostwatch only.

**SPOILAGE:** None. Contained by being worn against the skin, never set down on wood or ground.

**CARVING:** Breaking a table alone: approach it (Invitation, Hard 14), then smash it (Flesh 20, Guard 0, Armor 2) while its candles cry (Dread 2). A godsigned breaker gains 2 Hunger as the god in them grieves for the food. A Lack means the nearest chair pulls itself out, toward the breaker.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Worn, +2 to Invitation checks against tables, cards and voices (it is proof of a table refused). Planted on bare ground at dusk, it draws the next table the Host would lay within a mile to the splinter instead; the planter makes an Invitation check (Hard 14) at dawn if they go to see.

**WORTH:** To the Unfed, never sold: a Breaker who sells her splinter is a Breaker no longer. On the Rim, 40 lacks to Fatlanders who suck the gravy-end. Scarce.

### A Yard of the Long Table's Cloth

Along the valley road in the old heart of the country, eleven thousand of the Seated sit on both sides of one continuous table, nine miles end to end, and the cloth that covers it is unbroken and spotless for all nine. It can be cut. It is tough as sailcloth and very white and smells of starch and lavender, and the Seated on either side watch the knife with polite interest and ask whether the cutter is staying. By morning the Long Table's cloth is whole again, without a seam. The cut yard stays cut, and white, and pressed, forever.

Spread on bare ground, a yard of it is a table. Food set on it stays as it was set: hot food hot, cold food cold, bread new, butter beaded, for as long as it lies there. The Crumb-Runners would give their teeth for that and do not touch it, because food that has lain on the cloth has been *served*. But its true use was found by a widow of Brindle Cross named Bare Lammas, whose husband sat at the Long Table in 621. The Seated die when they are dragged beyond the length of the cloth. She cut a yard, and laid it under his plate, and lifted him, plate, cloth and all, and carried him home on her back, and he did not die. He sits at her bare floor in Wanting now, on his yard of white, eating air, and tells her about the soup. She says it is better than the alternative. Most days she means it.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); every family with someone Seated knows the widow's story and argues about it.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It never stains; gravy beads and runs off it. Contained folded, in ration-cloth, never spread.

**CARVING:** Walking in among the Seated is a Hosting 18 matter: each hour, the nearest guest describes a course (Persuade +11 vs Caul + Resolve; each failure 1 Fray, three failures and a chair is pulled out). Cutting the cloth is Hand + Blades vs Hard (14). Dread 2. A Lack means the carver has cut the yard in front of an empty chair that is pulled out, for them (Invitation, Dire 22).

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Food laid on it keeps exactly as it was laid, indefinitely; anyone who eats it makes an Invitation check (Hard 14). A Seated guest with a yard of cloth under their plate may be lifted and carried anywhere, and lives, and remains Seated, wasting, delighted, wherever the cloth is spread.

**WORTH:** 300 lacks. Rare. Fatlands nobles want it for the larder; families of the Seated want it for something worse.

### A Host's Candle

On every laid table there is a lamp or a candle, and it never burns down. The candles are pale beeswax, smelling of honey and warm milk, and when a table is broken they cry out as it falls, in voices like children's. A Breaker who is quick enough can snatch one whole before the axe comes down. It goes on burning in the hand. Pinched out, it relights itself at dusk. It is never shorter in the morning than it was at night.

In its light, nobody starves. The light has the warmth of a kitchen at the end of a long day, and the hunger in the bodies inside it eases, and does not grow. In the Great Short of 511 a family of Ash Lane left off the list survived the whole winter in a cellar by a stolen candle, with nothing to eat at all. The neighbours who broke open the cellar in Grace found them alive and pink-cheeked and sitting very upright around the candle, at a table nobody had built, laid for five, with their hands folded, waiting to be served. The eldest daughter looked up and asked if the neighbours would like to join them. She was polite about it. She had always been a polite girl.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); the Ash Lane story is told to every Unfed child in a winter of the list.

**SPOILAGE:** None. Contained by being kept unlit in daylight and pinched out at dusk, every dusk.

**CARVING:** Snatching a candle from a table before it is broken: Hand + Filch vs Hard (14), reaching across a laid place (Invitation, Hard 14). The voice from the door, if there is a door, is Dread 2.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Within its light no one gains Pangs or levels of Starving, and existing Starving penalties are halved. For each night anyone spends asleep in its light, everyone in the light makes an Invitation check (Hard 14). If any fail, a table has formed around the candle by morning, laid for everyone who was there.

**WORTH:** 200 lacks on the Rim. Rare. To the Unfed, keeping one is acceptance, and the Pantry strikes the family's names.

### A Coal from a Kept House

*Never enter a house where the hearth is already lit.* In the empty farmhouses of the heartland, hearths are found burning with no wood in them and no ash beneath: a steady low glow like a banked coal that warms the hand at a yard and never burns down. The houses around them are Lit Houses, and a guest who crosses the threshold finds that the door will not open again from inside until they have eaten. A coal lifted out of such a hearth with tongs, by someone who did not come in by the door, keeps burning in an iron pot.

It is the warmest thing in the Fast. A Crumb-Runner caught on the drove roads in a blizzard of Lack, with oxen dying in the traces and his hands gone white, will look at a coal in a pot and think very hard. It needs no fuel. It cooks. It dries boots. And any roof it sleeps under becomes, a night at a time, a roof over a lit hearth. The walls begin to sweat butter. The door begins to stick. The floor creaks with a sound like a stomach. On the third night, the shelter is a Lit House, and its door does not open from inside.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); every Crumb-Runner has heard of someone who tried.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It cannot be quenched. Buried in chalk from the Barrows it goes dark, warming the ground above it, and lights again when dug up.

**CARVING:** The carver must reach the hearth without crossing the threshold: through the thatch or a window, Lung + Athletics vs Hard (14). Lifting the coal is Hand + Craft vs Routine (10) with iron tongs. Dread 2: the room is clean, the kettle is on, and there is a place laid.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** In its pot it warms a room or tent as a good hearth does, ignoring cold and exposure penalties in Lack, and cooks without fuel. Each night it stays under the same roof, everyone sleeping there makes an Invitation check (Routine 10, then Hard 14, then Grim 18). After the third night the shelter is a Lit House (Hosting 18, as in Things That Eat and Are Eaten).

**WORTH:** 120 lacks. Scarce on the Rim; bought by Rim innkeepers in Lack, who learn. To the Unfed it is an abomination, and a household found with one is never entered again.

### Ash Mill Flour

In the middle country, on a chalk stream, stands a great stone watermill abandoned since the Refusal, whose wheel still turns. The millstones grind day and night with nothing between them, and from the chute falls a fine pale flour, warm to the touch, which smells of nothing at all. It is Orrum's flour. The Unfed will not go near the mill. Desperate Crumb-Runners will, with sacks, and Rim smugglers buy what they bring out and sell it in the Fatlands as Fast-flour, the cure for hunger.

It does what it is sold to do. A spoonful stirred into water and drunk makes the drinker full: for the first time in the life of a Fatlander, perhaps, really and entirely full, for an hour. They weep. Many of them laugh. They describe it afterward the way the Seated describe the soup. And at the end of the hour, the fullness drains away, and in a great many cases the drinker turns, without a word, toward the north and the middle of the Table, and starts walking. Osanna Blythe's three hundred pilgrims began with a single sack of Ash Mill flour shared out at a Low Tilth harvest-home.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) on the southern Rim; it is sold openly at the Groaning Board's back door.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It never goes stale and no weevil will touch it. Contained in a sealed crock, never poured out onto a board.

**CARVING:** Filling a sack at the chute is Hand + Labor vs Routine (10), but the flour is warm and falling into the carver's hands, and the mill-room is swept and has a bench by the door (Invitation, Hard 14). Dread 1.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** A spoonful in water: for one hour, the drinker's Hunger cannot rise, all Starving penalties vanish, and the Fatlands Want to feast falls silent. Then an Invitation check (Grim 18; each later dose +2) or walk toward the Fast for a day. An Unfed who drinks it loses 3 Want and has, in their own eyes and their people's, accepted.

**WORTH:** 8 lacks the cup at Brindle Cross, 30 in Sated. Uncommon on the southern Rim. The Board of Plenty has never quite decided whether a cure for hunger is sedition. The Thin burn it where they find it.

## From the Seated

[sigil: fast]

The Seated remain at table for decades, eating from empty plates, smiling, wasting, praising the food. They cannot be dragged away without dying. They can be robbed, quite easily, because they are as light as dry leaves and their hands are weak, and what they hold, they let go of to reach for the next course. The leavings of the Seated are the things they ate with and the things they said, and the pieces of themselves that came away as they wasted. Every one of them was taken from a person who was, at that moment, inviting the thief to stay.

### A Seated Guest's Fork

The Seated eat with silver: old heartland plate, heavy forks with four long tines and a pattern of wheat-ears on the handle, the tableware of a people who worshipped a Host. They lift the forks laden with nothing and put the nothing in their mouths and chew it slowly, with every appearance of delight. A fork taken from a guest's hand is no lighter than any other silver fork. Lifted empty to the lips, it tastes of the course its guest was eating at the moment it was taken: real taste, rich and hot and perfect, filling the mouth.

Brother Hallow Crust, who has sat at a table on the valley road for forty years, was relieved of his fork in 637 by a Rim tinker who wanted the silver. He looked up, said "Oh, thank you, I'd finished with that one," and was given another by no one, between one breath and the next. The tinker sold the first in Sated for its weight. The buyer was a Gut-lines widow who could no longer taste anything she ate, after sixty years of eating without stopping. She now eats nothing but the fork. She has lost two stone, and has never been happier.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14).

**SPOILAGE:** None; it never tarnishes. Contained wrapped in ration-cloth, tines down.

**CARVING:** Taking a fork from a Seated hand is Hand + Filch vs Routine (10). The guest looks up and invites the thief to sit: Seated Persuade +11 vs Caul + Resolve, Dread 2, or Dread 3 if the guest is someone the thief knew. A Lack means the guest's hand closes on the thief's wrist, lightly, and they are at the table's edge (Invitation, Dire 22).

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Lifted to the lips, it gives the full taste of a perfect course. For an hour afterward the user ignores all penalties from Starving and Pangs, and feels fed. They are not. Pangs and Starving continue to accrue as normal, unfelt, and the GM tracks them in secret. Each use is an offer (Invitation, Hard 14). Lent Ashby, who cannot taste anything, would give a great deal to try one once.

**WORTH:** 90 lacks. Scarce. Sold on the Rim to the jaded, the sick and the starving, who are all the same customers by the end.

### A Whispered Menu

The Seated describe each course in loving detail to anyone who will listen: the mutton with caper sauce, the eel in green jelly, the little birds roasted whole in vine-leaves, the pudding with a burnt-sugar skin. The descriptions are dreadful to hear because they are so good, and people who listen too long pull out a chair. In 618 a Hostwatch clerk named Meagre Odell sat at the far side of the valley road with his ears stopped with wax and a spyglass to his eye and read Brother Hallow Crust's lips for a whole season, and wrote down every course, forty pages of it, in a neat clerical hand.

He gave the pages to the Hostwatch. The Hostwatch locked them in the Pantry cellar under the Great Map and did not read them twice. Copies have since been made, by Crumb-Runners who could read and needed money, and are sold on the southern Rim, and they work as the Seated work. Read aloud, the menu makes listeners hungry in a way bread will not touch, and makes them look toward the middle of the Table. Osanna Blythe's pilgrims read it at their fires. A Cullmaster in Low Tilth has been reading it aloud in the far fields at dusk, and finds the Reaping goes more smoothly when the workers are thinking about something else.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Hard (14) on the southern Rim among pilgrims.

**SPOILAGE:** None. The paper smells of gravy and the ink never fades.

**CARVING:** Transcribing a menu means sitting within sight of a Seated guest for days: the guest's Persuade +11 vs the transcriber's Caul + Resolve each hour (each failure 1 Fray; three, and a chair is pulled out). Wax and lip-reading lower it to once each day. Dread 2.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Read aloud (Tongue + Persuade, +4 from the menu), it acts as a Seated guest's invitation: each listener rolls Caul + Resolve against the reader's total, and each failure costs 1 Fray; three failures, and the listener walks toward the nearest laid table they know of. Read silently, it is an Invitation check (Routine 10) for the reader.

**WORTH:** 60 lacks a copy. Scarce. Sold among pilgrims and Second Helping congregations. The Hostwatch would like the original destroyed; it will not burn.

### A Guest's Lifted Hand

At the head of the Long Table, where the oldest of the Seated sit, there is almost nothing left of them: clothes holding a posture, light as a wasps' nest, a smile hung between two cheekbones. They no longer lift the fork. But when a living person walks past them on the valley road, every one of them turns, together, with a sound like a stand of dry reeds moving in a wind, and looks. A hand broken from one of these weighs nothing. It is paper stretched over twigs, the colour of weak tea. And it goes on turning.

Hung on a thread, a lifted hand turns to face the nearest laid table within a day's walk, steadily, like a weather-vane. Among the living, it does something worse: it turns to face whoever in the room is hungriest. The Tablebreakers use them to find tables. The Hostwatch use them to find the Host's work. Kale Dragoman's poachers bought two in 639, and use them on the Rim Road to pick the Unfed out of a crowd at an inn, because nobody on the Table is as hungry as an Unfed on the road, and the hand turns to them like a flower to the sun.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); Tablebreakers and poachers know.

**SPOILAGE:** None. But it grows lighter over the years, until there is only the sleeve.

**CARVING:** Walking to the valley head means passing nine miles of the Seated (as for the Long Table's cloth). Breaking the hand away is Hand + Brawl vs Routine (10), and Dread 3: every guest along the table turns to look at the carver. A Lack means the guest's jaw moves, once, and says the carver's name.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Hung on a thread, it points to the nearest laid table within a day's walk (+4 to Search and Wayfaring to find or avoid one). Among people, it turns to the one with the most Pangs, or, if none are starving, the most Hunger. Each dawn its keeper wakes to find it turned toward them: Dread 1.

**WORTH:** 150 lacks. Rare. Tablebreakers, Hostwatch and poachers; the Abstinent count the trade in hands as the trade in Unfed lives, which it is.

### Little Fain's Ribbon

Little Fain, an orphan of six, went missing for a week and came back plump, clean, rosy and happy, with a ribbon in their hair and the smell of warm milk on their breath. Someone had said "Come in," and Fain had. Fain will not say who cooked, only that there was a big kitchen and a fire and that they cried when it was time to go. The ribbon is blue silk, finer than anything woven in the Fast or bought at the Rim, and it still smells of warm milk, and of something sweeter underneath, like honey on a spoon.

The Abstinent wanted it burned. It will not burn. They wanted it taken from the child. Fain held on to it with both hands and did not cry, and none of the elders, it turned out, wanted to be the one to pull. So Fain has it still, tied at the wrist, under a sleeve, and on cold nights in the orphans' hall Fain is the only child who sleeps warm and wakes with milk on the lips. The other children have noticed. Two of them have begun saving their crusts for the windowsill, as Abby Rooke does.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Wanting; everybody knows Fain.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It never frays, never fades and will not burn.

**CARVING:** Unique. It must be taken from a six-year-old who does not want to give it up (the GM should let the players feel that), or given by Fain, who might, to someone kind who is very hungry. Dread 2 on first smelling it.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Whoever wears it is a child of the house. They cannot starve to death: their Pangs cannot rise above 8, because each night something feeds them in their sleep, and they wake with milk on their lips. An Unfed wearer loses 1 Want each such morning. Each dawn, the wearer makes an Invitation check (Hard 14) or spends the day looking for the big kitchen, and the tables laid for them are the sweetest in the Fast: honey, custard, little iced cakes.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Osanna Blythe's pilgrims would give everything they have for it, which is nothing.

## From the Steward and the Hill

[sigil: fast]

Between the Host and its tables walks the Steward, Welcome Ardent, a mile ahead, laying places. Behind them both, at the centre of the continent, stands Hallowboard on its chalk hill, with the Barrows below it and the Stoopway running east. The leavings of this ground belong to two traditions that the Unfed would prefer not to see side by side: the things of the god's service, which the Hostwatch handle with a devotion they do not admit to, and the things of the Refusal, which every Unfed holds sacred. A rope braided from grass that grows over the starved; a tooth from the trench where they stood; a compass that has followed the Host for thirty years; a pewter plate laid nightly at the last temple; an apron. They are all, in their way, about service. That is what makes them hard to look at.

### The Steward's Apron

Welcome Ardent wears a waiter's long apron, gravy-stained, over a thin black coat, with a cloth over his arm and very clean white gloves. He has worn an apron for six hundred years. On a morning in Carving of 640, the wardens at Wanting Post found one folded on the Hem wall, facing inward: long, white, starched, gravy-stained at the hem, folded in precise thirds, with a card tucked into the waistband. The card said, in a beautiful round hand, *For whoever would like it.* The wardens did not touch it. By noon it was gone, and nobody would say who had taken it.

It has passed through at least three pairs of hands since, and each of its owners has learned the same two things. The first is that the Host's tables do not offer to staff. A person wearing the apron can walk the Long Table and the Seated ask them for more bread instead of inviting them to sit; they can pass a laid table, a smoking chimney, a voice at a door, and nothing calls them by name. The second is that staff have duties. Lent Ashby's place card, which said *Lent* for thirteen years, now says *Steward*. Welcome Ardent has left an apron out on the road. He may be hedging.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); the Hostwatch suspect, and the wardens at Wanting Post know there was an apron.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It is never more stained than it was.

**CARVING:** Unique. It is somewhere on the eastern Rim, with someone who has begun laying tables in their sleep. Once put on, it cannot be taken off by another's hand; only by its wearer saying "No, thank you" to it three times aloud and meaning it (Caul + Resolve vs Grim 18). Dread 3 on first tying the strings, which tie themselves.

**TAINT:** Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** While worn, the wearer makes no Invitation checks of any kind: tables, cards, smells, voices and the Seated treat them as staff. Each night, they must pass Caul + Resolve vs Hard (14) or rise in their sleep and lay a table, a real one, laid and lit, with a place for everyone within sight, which is an offer to all of them (Hosting 18). After a number of nights the GM decides, Welcome Ardent comes to thank the wearer in person, and to discuss terms.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Every Tablebreaker who has heard of it would trade their axe for it, and every one who has worn it would trade a great deal more to be rid of it.

### A Tooth from the Trench

Around seven of the eight sides of Hallowboard, the chalk of the hilltop is worn into a shallow trench where the forty thousand stood for nine days and starved. The trench is pale and hard as a step, and in some lights the prints of bare feet and knees can be seen in it, layered. The bones of the Refusal were gathered and buried in the Barrows below, but the chalk still turns up teeth after rain: small, worn, yellow-white teeth from jaws that clenched on nothing for nine days. Unfed who find them rebury them where they lay, with their bare hands, saying the Decline. *By the ones who stood.*

Not all of them are reburied. A tooth from the trench is the holiest thing an Unfed can hold, and holding one makes refusal easier: the Unfed say that the one it belonged to stands beside you, and holds your other hand, as on Tablenight. Reckoners sew them into their collars before a raid. Scour, the Returned, carries three, and they have not helped him. On the Rim, collectors who do not understand what they are buy them as curiosities, and the Unfed who see one strung on a Fatlands merchant's watch-chain have been known to follow the merchant for a very long way.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) for any Unfed; Grim (18) for anyone else.

**SPOILAGE:** None. Chalk-dry and lasting.

**CARVING:** Found on Hallowboard hill after rain with Eye + Search vs Routine (10). Being on the hill is Dread 2, chiefly because of the eighth side, whose chalk is smooth and white and untrodden. Taking one away is the worst desecration in the Fast, and an Unfed who does it can never again stand on Tablenight with their people.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Carried, +2 to every Invitation check, and +2 to resist any Hosting. An Unfed carrying one may use **Steady** without spending Want once per day. An oath sworn holding the tooth is sworn by the ones who stood: for an Unfed, binding by shame as such oaths are; for anyone else, breaking it brings the Fast's Visitation (a place card bearing the dead one's name at every table the breaker passes).

**WORTH:** 30 lacks to Rim collectors, who do not know. Scarce. To the Unfed, priceless, and the selling of one is spoken of in the same breath as saying "Come in."

### Barrow-Hair Rope

The grass on the Barrows, where the dead of the Refusal lie nine and ten deep, is the only untrimmed grass in the Fast: long, coarse, pale as straw-coloured hair, warm in the evening. The Hostwatch say that Orrum comes and sits among the mounds some nights, as a mourner sits at a grave, and the warm patch it leaves is shaped like someone lying down with their arms around something. The Tablebreakers cut the Barrow grass at the end of Carving, by permission of the Abstinent, and braid it into rope.

A Rope-Holder's tether is the line that pulls an Axe back from a chair, and a tether of Barrow-hair is the best there is. The dead of the hill, the Breakers say, hold on from the other end. A Breaker on a Barrow-hair rope has never yet been lost to a table, though several have been pulled back so hard their shoulders came out. The rope is warm to hold, the warmth of an arm, and those tied with it overnight dream of standing in the snow for nine days with their neighbours on either side and a god lying on the table in front of them, smiling.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); any Tablebreaker knows.

**SPOILAGE:** Cut grass stays pale and supple for a year, then goes brittle and must be rebraided.

**CARVING:** Cutting the grass is Hand + Labor vs Routine (10), and braiding it into rope is Hand + Craft vs Hard (14). Only Tablebreakers may cut, by the Abstinent's leave. Dread 1, at the warm places.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** A character tied with it gains +2 to Invitation checks. Once per scene, a companion holding the other end automatically succeeds in holding them back from a table, a door or a chair (no opposed roll). Anyone who sleeps tied with it dreams of the hill: 1 Fray, unless they are Unfed, for whom it is the best sleep they will ever have.

**WORTH:** 25 lacks the fathom. Uncommon in Wanting among the Tablebreakers; Scarce on the Rim, where Fatlands pilgrims buy it to tie themselves to the road at night, and then cut it.

### A Mile-Walker's Compass

At the rank of Mile-Walker, a tracker of the Hostwatch is given a brass compass on a thong. It is an ordinary Rim compass, bought by the gross from a Lampwick Halt instrument-maker. It does not stay ordinary. After a few years following warm ground at a mile's distance, the needle stops pointing north and points to the Host, and after twenty it warms in the hand as the Host draws near, and after thirty, as Spare Tolland's has, it begins to tremble when the Host stops and waits.

A Mile-Walker's compass passes to the Hostwatch when its owner dies, which they do in the field, usually alone, and usually smiling. Some never come back. In 633 a Tracker's body was found sitting on a stile two miles from Wanting with her compass in her lap and her face turned west, and the needle was pointing at her. The Hostwatch keep the old compasses in a drawer under the Great Map. Lately, Spare Tolland has noted in the restricted ledger that his own needle has twice, on Rim Road nights, swung away from the Host for a whole night, and pointed steadily at Hallowboard: at its eighth side, he believes, though he did not go to see.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); Hostwatch only.

**SPOILAGE:** None. A compass never used to follow the Host goes back to pointing north within a year.

**CARVING:** Given at rank 3 of the Hostwatch, or taken from a dead Mile-Walker in the field (Eye + Search vs Hard 14 to find the body; Dread 2). The Hostwatch ask for them back, politely, and then not politely.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** It always points to Orrum, wherever on the Table the Host is walking, and grows warm within a mile (+4 to Wayfaring to track or avoid the Host). Each day the needle is warm, the bearer makes Caul + Resolve vs Hard (14) or spends an hour walking toward it, to close the mile.

**WORTH:** 200 lacks. Rare. Never sold by the Hostwatch; bought by poachers, pilgrims and the Second Table, who would very much like to know where the Host is going to be on Tablenight.

### The Verger's Platter

On a wooded rise six miles west of Wanting stands the Empty Chair, the last temple of Orrum, a long low hall of honey-coloured stone with a single table running its length. Its Verger, Sober Osmond, lays one place every night at the head of the table, against all law: a pewter platter, a horn cup, a linen napkin folded into the shape of a bird. He lays nothing on it. Every morning the platter is clean. He believes this is why the god has stayed gentle, and the Abstinent, who have condemned him eleven times, have never once sent anyone to stop him.

The platter is old heartland pewter, dull, dented, its rim worn thin by a thousand years of hands. It is not a leaving of the Host's in the way the tables are; nobody laid it but Osmond. But something has eaten from it every night for fifty-three years, and the Verger has noticed, as nobody else has, that on the mornings it is cleanest, there are no tables found laid anywhere within a day's walk of the temple. He has never sat up to see what clears it. He says it would be rude.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); everyone in Wanting knows the Verger's habit. That it matters: Dire (22).

**SPOILAGE:** None.

**CARVING:** Unique. Osmond would give it only to someone who promised to lay it every night after him; stealing it from the temple is Hand + Filch vs Hard (14). What happens at the Empty Chair the first night no place is laid is the GM's to decide, and the Hostwatch would like to know in advance.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Laid each night, empty, at the head of a table in any house, it keeps Orrum's tables and place cards from appearing anywhere in that house. Each morning it is clean. If a single night is missed, the next morning every chair in the house is found pulled out, and the household must make Invitation checks (Grim 18) before they may leave the room.

**WORTH:** Never sold. The Abstinent would like it destroyed and dare not. The Hostwatch would like it watched. Nobody has asked what the platter is feeding.

## From the Unfed

[sigil: fast]

The Unfed honour no relics but those of refusal, and those they honour by never touching. Their sacred leavings are the litter of starvation: a slate scratched white, a book of meals never eaten, the crumbs on a Saint's mouth, an apple from the last autumn of the old world, and one green blade of wheat inside two fences and a ring of tin cans. Most of them are kept by the Abstinent in the Pantry, between the ration-ledgers, or by the few Unfed who have the standing to keep such things, and they reach the Rim only in the worst winters, through the omitted and the desperate. A Rim collector who owns one of the leavings of the Unfed owns a piece of the only clean history on the Table, and the Unfed believe, not without reason, that it does not stay clean in their hands.

### Saint's-Lip Crumbs

Sister Nay Corliss sits on a hurdle in a field south of Wanting and has not eaten in three years. She should have died in the first two months. She is translucent; lamplight shows the bones of her hands like twigs in amber. She does not know she is a miracle of the wrong kind: each morning she wakes with crumbs on her lips, and the novice who wipes them away, a girl of fifteen named Hesitate Fennick, has never told her. Hesitate has started to save them, in a twist of cloth, in her shirt. She does not know why. She has eleven.

The crumbs are of a very fine white bread, sweet, with a crust that cracks like sugar. A single one is a day's meal. It is also the Host's food, given in the night to someone who did not accept it, and that is the trap in it: whoever eats a crumb knowingly has accepted on the Saint's behalf. They are fed. They will be fed again. They wake the next morning, and every morning after, with crumbs on their own lips, and they cannot starve now, ever, however long they refuse, and every Unfed who has heard of it thinks it the most frightening thing the Host has ever done.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18). Only Hesitate Fennick knows for certain, and she has told one person, who has told the Second Table.

**SPOILAGE:** None. They do not go stale.

**CARVING:** From Hesitate's twist of cloth, by Tongue + Persuade vs Hard (14); she is frightened and wants to be told what to do. Watching the crumbs appear at dawn on the Saint's lips is Dread 2.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** One crumb removes all levels of Starving and 3 Pangs, with no refeeding sickness. The eater then makes an Invitation check (Grim 18). On a failure, they are **Fed in the Night**: from then on, their Pangs never rise above 3, they wake each morning with crumbs on their lips, and an Unfed so fed can never again earn Want.

**WORTH:** 100 lacks a crumb to Fatlands buyers. Scarce, and finite. The order of Starved Saints would be destroyed by the knowledge, and some of the Abstinent would prefer that than to let it be known.

### The Lammas Bottom Slate

In the last month of the eighth winter of the Lack, the reeve of the village of Lammas Bottom scratched on a slate a list of what the village ate, in a hand that grows larger and less steady down the page: *bark of the elm, boiled. Shoe-soles, boiled. Moss. The dog Brindle. Clay with salt. The cat. The other cat. The rush matting.* The last line is a single word, scratched over and over until the slate is white and then worn through, so that what was once a word is now a hole the size of a thumb: *no.* The slate is kept in the Pantry at Wanting, in the Chair's own cupboard, and is brought out on Tablenight.

Meek Garrity asked to see the slate in 638, for his researches, and was refused three times, and asked a fourth, and was let in. He turned it over. On the back, in the same reeve's hand, older and steadier, there is a second list: the households of Lammas Bottom, in an order of walking, and beside each a number of the day. The Lammas families were to go up the hill on the tenth. Garrity copied it and put the slate back face-up. The Abstinent do not know he turned it.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) for the Unfed, who are told about it as children. The back: Dire (22), and only from Garrity.

**SPOILAGE:** None. The hole grows a little each time a thumb is pressed into it.

**CARVING:** Unique. It is in the Chair's cupboard at the top of the Pantry, behind an iron door with three locks whose keys are held by three people. Dread 2 for an Unfed to hold it, Dread 3 for anyone else, who feels the cat and the other cat in their own throat.

**TAINT:** Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** Once per session, the holder may press a thumb into the hole and refuse one thing absolutely: an offer, a Hosting, a Want, a Pull, a Sayer's sentence about them, a Seated guest's invitation. No roll; it does not touch them. The holder gains 1 Pang. An Unfed holding the slate gains 1 Want each day they also go hungry.

**WORTH:** Never sold. If it were stolen from the Pantry, the Abstinent believe, the Refusal itself would be stolen with it. If it were read on its back, they would be right.

### A Ninth-Winter Apple

The heartland held on longest in the Lack. Its orchards were the last on the Table to set fruit, and in the ninth winter the last apples fell from the trees and broke open on the frozen ground: a skin of perfect red around a puff of grey dust, and nothing else. A few were not broken. A family of Lammas Bottom kept one in a box of beech-wood sealed with wax, as the last fruit of the old world, and their descendants have kept it since. There are said to be four others, in the Pantry, in the College at Quire, in Mister Cutlery's silverware case, and one that nobody can find.

It is wrinkled now, the colour of dried blood, and much lighter than an apple should be. Inside it is the Lack: not a memory of it but a pinch of it, the dust of the ninth winter, the thing that ate the world's increase before the world could have it. The College's single attempt to study one, in 590, involved opening it under a bell-jar. Every grain and every scrap of food in the laboratory was ash within the hour. The Cutwright doing the work, a Gut-line cadet, found that he could not, for a whole day, feel the god in his blood at all. He said it felt like a hare holding very still in long grass. He never asked to see the apple again.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); the College has a single paper on it, sealed.

**SPOILAGE:** None, but the skin is drying, and will one day split of its own accord.

**CARVING:** Unique, or nearly. It must be bought, begged or stolen from one of its keepers; the Lammas descendants are omitted from the Pantry list this winter and may be persuaded (Tongue + Persuade vs Hard 14). Holding it is Dread 3: it is very cold, and something in it is hungry.

**TAINT:** Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** Broken, it releases the ninth winter in a ring ten paces across. All food in the ring becomes ash; seed rots; milk sours; Orrum's tables within a mile vanish. Every godsigned character in the ring cannot Partake for the rest of the scene, because the god in their blood is holding perfectly still. Everyone in the ring takes a Dread 3 check from the sense of being, for a moment, very small and very nearly found. One use.

**WORTH:** 2,000 lacks. Rare. The Second Table has bought one, and has not opened it. Some of them have begun to wonder why the gods are afraid of it.

### The Lean House Book

At the far end of Wanting, beyond the Cold Yards, the Lean House is where the omitted go to wait. Withy Gaunt keeps it. She does not feed them; she washes them, changes the straw, holds their hands and listens to them talk about food, as they all do at the end, in loving detail: the pie at a wedding in their childhood, a pear eaten in a ditch, an onion roasted in the ashes of a Cold Yard fire. She writes it down. The book is thick, bound in ration-cloth, and every entry is a name, a date, and a meal, and the last words of each entry are always the same, because she asks them: *and was it good?* They always say yes.

Read aloud to a starving person, an entry feeds them a little. Not truly; the body believes it, for an hour, and the belief is worth something at the end. Withy does this sometimes for the very last. Lately she has found entries in the book she did not write. They continue the entries of people who have died, in a round, loving hand, describing what they are eating now: at a long table, in a warm room, with everyone they knew. She has not shown the Abstinent. She has started, at night, to read those entries aloud to herself.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Wanting; the Lean House is not secret, only unmentioned.

**SPOILAGE:** Paper, kept dry. It has never once been damp, even in the wet of Lack.

**CARVING:** Unique. Withy will lend it to someone who asks four times. Reading it through is Dread 2; reading the entries in the second hand is Dread 3.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** An entry read aloud to a starving listener removes 1 Pang from them for the day, and the reader gains it. Read through, the book names every person omitted from the Pantry list since Withy began, and against eleven of the names, in the margin, she has noted who struck them. The entries in the second hand, read aloud, are an offer (Invitation, Hard 14) to the reader and every listener.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Forbear Wend would pay anything to know it exists; Wont Bellamy would pay more.

### The Green Blade of Hartsell's Field

Yield Hartsell has ploughed, harrowed and sown her ten acres north of Wanting every spring for fifty years, and every spring the seed has rotted. This year, in one corner, one seed came up. It is a single blade of green wheat, now five inches tall, inside a fence of hurdles, inside a second fence of thorn, inside a ring of tripwire hung with tin cans. It is the only living crop in the Fast. It is watered every night. Yield does not do it. She has sat up three times to see who does, and fallen asleep each time, and woken at dawn to wet earth around the stem.

The Hostwatch have looked for warm ground around the fence and found none. What they have found, each morning, is frost: a crisp white patch on the grass just outside the thorn, though the Fast has no frost in Carving, in a shape they have drawn in the restricted ledger many times and do not care to name. It is the shape a chair leaves in frost when it has been pushed back from a table, and someone has got up, and gone. The Abstinent want the blade burned. The Hostwatch want it watched. The Second Table wants it stolen. The pilgrims want to kneel to it. Yield wants to know who watered it, and is beginning to be afraid that she does.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Wanting; the blade is a public secret. The frost: Dire (22), Hostwatch only.

**SPOILAGE:** It is alive. Kept in its own clod of earth, watered, it lives. Uprooted, it dies in a day.

**CARVING:** Unique. Reaching it means passing Yield, two fences and a tripwire (Lung + Stealth vs Hard 14), and lifting it with its clod intact (Hand + Craft vs Grim 18). Standing by the frost-shape at dawn is Dread 3 for anyone who understands it, and Dread 1 for anyone who does not.

**TAINT:** Dread 3.

**EFFECT:** It is food that no one offered. An Unfed may eat it without accepting, and lose no Want, and it is the first grain of the heartland eaten since the Lack. Planted elsewhere in the Fast with its clod, seed sown within a stone's throw of it comes up. In Hartsell's corner, the morning after it is moved, the seed in the earth there is found rotted again.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Every faction on the Table would pay for it, and none of them have asked the question the Hostwatch are asking, which is not who watered it but who used to sit there, and where they have gone.

# Leavings of the Rim and the Empty Chair

> Every land is certain the cure is next door. The Rim is where next door keeps its stock.
>> — Mister Cutlery, to a customer at Gristmoot, closing his silverware case

[bigquote] Count the chairs if you must. But do not count them aloud, and never count them twice. | — a saying of Rim Road innkeepers, older than the Company

The Rim Road is where leavings go to be sold. Every relic in this book passes along it sooner or later: in a Company wagon under a false bill, in a smuggler's boot, in the black coach of the soft-voiced man with a silver fork embroidered on his mask, who will buy back anything he sold at triple its price, provided the seller names exactly where it came from. The Company declares nothing and opens nothing. Its standing order at the Chute, *do not open anything*, is the closest thing the Rim has to a relic law, and every factor on the ring observes it as piously as the grace.

But the road has leavings of its own. The great institutions that cross every border make instruments, and their instruments learn things: a rope that has hanged four hundred people under Company law, a quire of the College's ledgers, the bowl the Purgation was born in, a menu with eight lines and seven courses. Older than any of them, the Rim carries the oldest things on the Table: fragments of the Providers themselves, from before the Gorging or from the night of it, which travel the ring endlessly because no land will have them home. A strip of the Closing Hand's palm cannot go back to Sallowreach; the Mortuary Court would call it a stolen license. A flake of the Lidded's eye cannot go back to the Vigil; its owner would be hanged. They go around and around, from inn to inn, and are worth more every circuit.

And there are a very few leavings that no dealer on the road will name, that Mister Cutlery has refused at any price, and that the Cutwrights' College has a single sealed ledger about. They come from the eighth place. They are presented last in this book, for the GM alone.

## From the Road and the Ledger

[sigil: rim]

The Rim Road Company owns the road, every inn and post on it, and every toll; the Cutwrights' College owns the record of every bloodline that walks it. Neither owns a god. Both have handled the Table's horror for six centuries as a pricing problem and a filing problem respectively, and their instruments have absorbed the habit. They are practical, ruthless, and very slightly wrong in ways their makers have not noticed. The Company's leavings are sworn in Oathen, and so carry Tolm's attention around the ring. The College's are written in blood, tasted and graded, and so carry every god at once.

### A Stone of the Hem

On its inner side, facing the Fast, the Rim Road is bordered by a low wall of fitted grey Oathen stone about knee-high, running the whole two thousand four hundred miles. It is called the Hem. It does nothing; anyone can step over it. Nobody does. The Company replaces any stone that goes missing within a day, from stockpiles at every post, and its masons are under standing orders to lay each stone with its dressed face *inward*, toward the middle of the Table, as the original builders did. Nobody remembers why.

On the morning after the table was found laid with eight places at Saltlick Cross in 639, the wardens found that the forty Hem stones nearest the crossroads had been turned around in the night, every one, and lay with their dressed faces outward, toward the Rim. The masons turned them back. They have had to turn them back eleven times since, at Saltlick Cross, at Wanting Post, at the Groaning Board, and once at Fogmouth. A Hem stone pried loose and carried away keeps whatever face it had. Laid across a threshold or at the head of a bedroll, it does what the Hem was perhaps once meant to do: nothing on the side it faces can be accepted across it.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18). The turning of the stones: Hard (14) for any Company mason or warden.

**SPOILAGE:** None. Each night, roll a d10: on a 1 it has turned around by morning.

**CARVING:** Prying a stone from the Hem is Gut + Labor vs Routine (10), and theft of road. The wardens hang thieves of cargo and make cargo of thieves of road. Dread 1, if the stone's face is outward when it comes up.

**TAINT:** Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Laid on the ground, its dressed face toward something, it makes a line. No offer from that side can be accepted across the line: a laid table, a voice saying "Come in," a Seated guest's invitation, a Hosting creature's food. Characters behind it make no Invitation checks against things beyond it. If the stone turns in the night, the line faces the other way, and the characters are on the wrong side of it.

**WORTH:** 60 lacks. Scarce. Crumb-Runners, Hostwatch and pilgrims who have changed their minds. The Company prefers not to discuss why a wall that does nothing should be worth stealing.

### The Rope at the Post

*Pay the toll. Keep the peace. Say the grace.* Murder on the Rim Road is punished by the Company's wardens with a rope at the nearest post, and every post's rope is sworn, like every other Company instrument, before the Company's notary at Treaty Stair. It is good Fatlands hemp, tarred, a little thicker than a thumb, and every warden-sergeant ties a small knot in the tail of the post's rope for every hanging it has done. Retired ropes are meant to be burned at Treaty Stair. The rope from Knocking Gate, which Hobb Fallowmere retired in 638 with four hundred and nine knots in its tail, was not.

A sworn rope knows the Company's three articles the way a dog knows its master's step. Looped loosely around the wrist of someone who has broken one of them within a bowshot of the road (killed there, stolen Company cargo, refused the grace at a Company table), it tightens. Around the wrist of someone who has not, it lies slack. Around a captive's wrists on Company ground, it cannot be slipped. Fallowmere used the Knocking Gate rope as an instrument of justice for nineteen years, and his sergeants say he never once hanged an innocent man. They also say that the rope creaks at night, in the barracks store, and that when it does, it creaks four hundred and nine times.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14); any Company warden knows the knots, and Grim (18) to know what the rope does.

**SPOILAGE:** Off the Rim, it is only rope. On it, it keeps as long as it is tarred each season.

**CARVING:** A retired rope must be got from a barracks store before it reaches the Treaty Stair fires: Hand + Filch vs Grim (18), among wardens. Holding it is Dread 2: it is heavy in a way that has nothing to do with its weight.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Within a bowshot of the Rim Road, a captive bound with it cannot escape by any means short of cutting it (Hand + Blades vs Grim 18). Looped around anyone's wrist on the Rim, it tightens if they have done murder, theft of cargo or the refusal of the grace on the road, and lies slack if they have not. It cannot be deceived. It cannot judge anything that happened off the road.

**WORTH:** 400 lacks. Rare. Bounty-hunters, inn-factors and Reckoners waiting for nobles at inns. Theft of Company property; the wardens retrieve their ropes, and use them.

### A Quire of the Master Concordance

The Cutwrights' College keeps the record of every bloodline on the Table in the Master Concordance, in a vault cut into the hill under the library at Quire, kept cool by spring water. A *quire* is a gathering of its leaves: forty sheets of College paper folded and stitched, written in the Concordance's tiny hand, giving the full lines of one land's highest houses from the Meal to the present, with the Blood-Taster's grades, the Cut of every member, their stage of Regrowth, and the College's private rate of advance. Silas Fenwright, the Ledger-Thief, walked out of Quire in a Copyist's grey with three of them. He is trying to sell them to whoever will let him live.

A quire is a target roll for the Reckoners, a massacre list for the Lancets, a menu for the Second Table, and for the College the most dangerous thing in the world to have loose, because of the last page. The last leaf of every quire is a graph in red ink, drawn in the Archprovost's own hand: that land's Regrowth across six centuries, a long gentle slope, and then, after 600 A.G., a bend upward. Laid side by side, the graphs from the seven quires bend upward in the same year.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); every faction on the Rim is looking for Fenwright.

**SPOILAGE:** Paper, well made. The red ink of the last leaf darkens every year, as though the line were being drawn over again.

**CARVING:** Copying from the Concordance is Eye + Lore vs Dire (22) inside Quire, under the eyes of Linemen who know exactly what every Copyist is. Buying from Fenwright is a negotiation with a terrified man whom four factions want dead.

**TAINT:** Dread 2; Dread 3 on reading the last leaf.

**EFFECT:** With the quire for a land, +4 to any Lore or Search roll touching its noble houses: proving or disproving a line, finding a bastard heir, naming the houses nearest a Seating. Reading the last leaf with Eye + Reckoning vs Grim (18) shows the reader that every land's Regrowth bent upward at once, and so that the seven are one event: Dread 3.

**WORTH:** 5,000 lacks a quire. Rare. Possession is a College death warrant, quietly executed. The Reckoners would pay in blood rather than coin; Deny Fallon already has one, and wants the rest.

## From the Second Table and the Purgation

[sigil: rim]

Two movements cross every border with opposite answers to the same question. The Purgation believes the gods can be thrown back up; the Second Table believes they can be eaten again. One has an altar-bowl for its emblem and the other a signet ring engraved *It worked once.* Both are full of nobles who know exactly how far along their own blood is, and both have made instruments of the meal: the bowl that catches what comes up, the menu that lists what will go down, the knife that does the carving. The Rim's dealers handle their leavings more carefully than any god's, because the people who want them back are not gods. They are on the road, and they keep notebooks.

### A Menu of Seven Courses

The first trace of the Second Table in any record is a menu found in the coat of a murdered Gut-line lordling in 412 A.G. The College has since collected forty more. They are cards of heavy cream stock with gilt edges, written in a fine sloping hand that the College has matched to no one living or dead, and they always list seven courses, each named for a god, in an order that varies a little from card to card. A typical one, taken from a Brinehollow admiral's widow in 630, reads: *I. A clear broth of the Fathom. II. The Laden, roasted, with its own bread. III. Lids in aspic. IV. The Opening, stuffed. V. A tongue, pressed. VI. The Closing Hand, served cold. VII. The Host, carved at table.*

On the menus found in the last ten years there is an eighth line at the bottom, ruled in the same hand, and left blank. Diners of the Second Table believe the blank is the place for the dish they have not yet found. The College tried, once, in 637, to learn more by writing on it. A Lineman wrote a word on the eighth line of a seized menu in ink. In the morning the line was blank again, and the Lineman had not been able to eat breakfast, because every mouthful he lifted tasted of nothing at all, and the feeling did not pass for nine days. Nobody at Quire has tried a second time.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); members of the Table know without a roll.

**SPOILAGE:** None. The card never yellows.

**CARVING:** Taken from a Second Table member (they carry them sewn into a coat lining), or bought from Mister Cutlery, who has several. Dread 1 on reading the courses; Dread 3 on noticing the eighth line.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** Presented at any Second Table dinner, it admits the bearer as a Taster, no questions asked; they will be served a dish, and expected to finish. Among members, +4 to Persuade and Deceive. Anything written on the eighth line is gone by morning, and the writer's food tastes of nothing for nine days (−1 to all rolls; Starving cannot be removed by eating). The card cannot be burned or torn.

**WORTH:** 500 lacks. Rare. The Second Table buys its menus back, and its sellers.

### A Carver's Knife of Grey Bone

The Second Table has commissioned knives. Every Carver, the rank that leads a table in a land, keeps one: a long, thin, single-edged blade of grey bone fitted to a silver handle engraved with a course-name, sharpened on stones that cost more than the knife. Lord Absalom Thrale, the Grand Carver, keeps the knife from the first practice dinner, and the College would identify its blade, if shown it, as one of Ossel's knuckles. The others are less certain in origin. The Table bought Provider bone wherever it could find it, from the treasuries of old Hand-line houses and Morwen strong-rooms and the bottom of the Gullet, and had the knives made by a single cutler in Sated who died, conveniently, after the eighth.

A grey bone knife cuts godflesh as a good bread-knife cuts bread. It goes into a Laden hill, a Tenanted's chest, the caul of a Morwen duchess, as easily as into butter, and what it cuts does not knit well afterward. It also carves leavings better than any tool on the Table, and the Second Table's practice dinners have been supplied, for two centuries, by Carvers who could take a finger, a lid or a lash from a god-touched body without wasting a sliver.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); Grim (18) for anyone who has dined with the Table.

**SPOILAGE:** None. Bone that was a god does not age.

**CARVING:** Taken only from a Carver, by violence (a Second Table Diner, per Things That Eat and Are Eaten) or by inheritance within the Table. Holding it is Dread 2: it is warm along the edge, and very slightly hungry.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (of the god whose bone it is; most are Ossel).

**EFFECT:** A knife (3). Against any creature with a Cut or a Regrowth, or godsign of any kind, it ignores Armor and each Helping adds +3 damage; Flesh lost to it heals at half the normal rate. Used on any Carving roll in this book, it gives +4 and halves the Dread of the work, because the hand holding it has stopped thinking of what it cuts as anyone.

**WORTH:** 6,000 lacks. Rare. Never sold by the Table, which hunts its lost knives with the same patience it brings to the menu. There are eight. The Table has only ever seated seven Carvers at once.

### The First Bowl

In 233 A.G., at the Weeping Post, a Fatlands physician named Hurlo Sweyn took a draught of his own devising before a congregation of Confessors and retched for six hours into a brown earthenware basin, and at the end of the sixth hour brought up a hard white thing the size of a thumbnail which he swore was one of Ummer's teeth. That was the Great Retching, and the basin is the First Bowl. It is plain, glazed, cracked and riveted in three places, with *What goes down can come up* painted around the rim in Sweyn's own hand, and it sits on the altar of the Confessors' chapel at the Weeping Post, in the brown water, under a lid of beaten tin.

Penitents who take the Black Draught over the First Bowl bring up more than food. Sister Tisane Halloway, Mistress of the Draught, has seen it eleven times in six years, in Penitents from four lands: a small grey hinged thing like a finger-joint, which moves. They are not the same god's. They come from Fatlanders, from a Brinehollow cooper, from a Vigil clerk, from a Sallowreach girl. They are all the same thing. Halloway keeps them in a sealed jar. They have grown. They fit together, and the jar, she has noted in a hand that is not quite steady, is getting full.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10) for any Purger; the jar, Dire (22), and only from Halloway.

**SPOILAGE:** None. The rivets are replaced every century.

**CARVING:** Unique. It sits on an altar at a crossroads full of penitent travellers and Purgation brothers; stealing it is Lung + Stealth vs Grim (18), and would put every Lancet on the Rim on the thief's trail. Holding it is Dread 1.

**TAINT:** Dread 2.

**EFFECT:** A Black Draught taken over it is Gut + Endure vs Grim (18). On a success the Penitent loses all Hunger and brings up a leaving of their own god: a pebble-sized thing (Taint 1 Regrowth of their land) worth 50 lacks to a dealer. On a Grace, they also lose 1 Regrowth, once per stage, as the rules allow a Purger. On a failure, Ruin. On a Lack, what comes up is one of Halloway's grey hinged things, and it moves toward her jar.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Saint Ancel Ruel preaches over it twice a year, and does not look into it.

## From the Seated Gods

[sigil: rim]

On the Night of Seven Tables six gods were butchered and shared out, and nearly all of each was eaten. Not quite all. In every land someone kept something back: a strip of hide, a hook with flesh on it, a flake of eyelid, a phial of what the wound ran with, one of a thousand newborn gods carried out in an apron. Those are the oldest leavings on the Table, older than any Cut, and they are pieces of the Providers as they were when they lay down, before six centuries of blood thinned them. The trade calls them *Seated leavings*, because the College holds that when a land completes its Seating the god will want every piece of itself back, and these pieces, wherever they are on the Rim, will go. Six are described here. There are others, in strongboxes and reliquaries and Mister Cutlery's case. As the Regrowth quickens, every one of them has begun to stir.

### A Strip of Ossel's Palm

The Closing Hand lay in the fens with its hands palm up on the peat, each one wide enough to cup a cottage, and for three thousand years the fen-folk carried their dead to it on reed hurdles and laid them by a hand, and the hand closed their eyes and folded their arms, and they were sunk in black water and gone in a season, leaving the water sweet. On the night of the Meal, the families who took the hands became the Hand-lines. A few took strips of the palm-skin first, grey and dry and lined like a riverbed seen from a hill, and kept them in their treasuries as the last thing of the god's that had closed a pair of eyes. The Mortuary Court counts them, as it counts its nobles' fingers. It does not count them all.

A strip is the length of a forearm and the width of a hand, grey leather, cold, the lines of the palm running across it like roads. Laid over the eyes of the dying anywhere outside Sallowreach, it gives them the death the fen-folk had before the Meal: the eyes close, the arms fold, the body goes into the ground and is gone in a season and the earth above it is sweet. Laid over one of the Kept, inside Sallowreach, it finishes them, slowly, over a year of perfect quiet. Sister Lorn of the Shut Eye has wanted one for forty years. So has Henna Farrow.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Grim (18); the Mortuary Court's Clerk of Hands knows every strip by number, and exactly which three are missing.

**SPOILAGE:** None outside Sallowreach. Inside, it begins very slowly to finish, and is smaller every decade.

**CARVING:** From a Hand-line treasury: Lung + Stealth vs Dire (22). Holding it the first time is Dread 2: the holder's own fingers want to close something.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Ossel), Dread 1.

**EFFECT:** Laid over the eyes of a dying person outside Sallowreach, it gives them a clean death: no Visitation, no lingering, no rising; mourners who see it remove 2 Fray. Laid over a Kept in Sallowreach and left, it Closes them over a year of silence. The holder's hands go grey to the second knuckle for a day after each use, and they gain the Sallowreach Want to close lids and eyes, at Hard (14), for that day.

**WORTH:** 3,000 lacks. Rare. The Court calls each one a stolen license, and each finished Kept a theft from the Regent.

### Sweyn's Tooth

What Hurlo Sweyn brought up into the First Bowl in 233, after six hours of retching, was a hard white thing the size of a thumbnail, and he swore it was one of Ummer's teeth. Ummer was the Laden, the god of harvest, and its iconography is a full belly with a sheaf rising out of it, and a mouth, always a mouth, smiling. If a piece of that god could be thrown up, then all of it could, and on that proof the Purgation was founded. The tooth sits in a silver cup beside the First Bowl at the Weeping Post. The Purgation calls it the Proof.

The College examined it in 590 with the Purgation's permission and the Purgation's Lancets standing by, and filed a report that has never been released. It notes that toothed wheat first came up in the Fatlands in 204, twenty-nine years before the Great Retching, and that Sweyn was a Fatlands physician with a taste for bread. It notes also that the tooth has, in the three and a half centuries since, grown a root. It is a long, fine, red root, like a hair, and it has gone down through the bottom of the silver cup and into the altar. Whatever it is, it is still Ummer's.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Routine (10); every Purger knows the Proof. The root: Grim (18).

**SPOILAGE:** None. It is growing.

**CARVING:** Unique. Taking it means cutting its root out of the altar at the Weeping Post (Hand + Blades vs Hard 14) in front of a congregation; the Lancets would follow the thief to the edge of the world. Holding it is Dread 2: it is warm and it bites, gently.

**TAINT:** 3 Hunger, 1 Regrowth (Ummer).

**EFFECT:** Placed under the tongue during any meal, it lets the eater taste exactly what the food was before it was food, and name it (+4 to any roll to identify meat, poison or Taint by taste). Held in the mouth during a Black Draught, it gives +4 to the Gut + Endure roll. Planted in any soil, the soil begins to grow teeth.

**WORTH:** Never sold. The Board of Plenty would pay to have it destroyed and the Second Helping would pay to have it eaten.

### A Beaching Hook

The western coast dragged Dromm up onto the shingle at Longslab on ten thousand hooks, and it took four days to stop moving. The hooks were forged in the fifth year of the Lack from every scrap of iron on the coast, harpoon-barbed and as long as a man's forearm, and the crews who drove them in hauled on ropes from the dunes in shifts. Longslab has never dried. Every winter storm turns up a few hooks in its wet sand, rusted, still barbed, still with a rag of grey flesh on the barb that has not rotted in six hundred and forty-one years, and is cold, and pulls.

A Beaching hook holds what it is set in as the deep holds. A thing hooked by it is pressed down, held under a weight that is not there. The Netwatch would give anything for them; a single hook on a pole holds a Called man as surely as ten hookmen. Lieutenant Sable Reef has one. She has noticed that the Called she holds with it do not fight it the way they fight rope. They go still, and look at the hook, and weep, as if it were something they recognised. The Second Table has quietly bought seven, which is more than anyone needs to hold a person.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Hard (14) in Brinehollow; Grim (18) elsewhere.

**SPOILAGE:** None, while the flesh on the barb is wet. Dried, it is only rusted iron; it must be kept in a jar of seawater or Tenanted brine.

**CARVING:** Dug from the wet sand at Longslab, where gulls will not land, with Eye + Search vs Hard (14). Longslab is Dread 1; touching the flesh on the barb is Dread 2. A Lack means the hook catches the digger's hand, and must be cut out.

**TAINT:** 2 Regrowth (Dromm).

**EFFECT:** Mounted on a pole (boathook 4, Hooking), a target it hooks is held at Grapple 22 and can be hauled by one person as easily as by ten. A Tenanted or Called creature hooked by it cannot act, only weep. A Lung-line who carries it feels the Pull (14) toward the Trench once a day.

**WORTH:** 900 lacks. Rare. The Netwatch, the Admiralty, and the Second Table, which has not explained what it means to hold down.

### A Scale of Iss's Lid

Iss, the Lidded, lay curled in the high valley above old Ludmere, and no one in recorded history ever saw its eyes open. The lids were immense, grey and soft as a moth's wing, and they shed, very slowly, in flakes like the scales of a fish, which drifted down into the valley and lay among the beds of the dreaming. Ludmere's oracles gathered the flakes and burned them as incense, and slept in the smoke, and woke with prophecy. On the night the Eye-lines ate the eyes, still shut, a few oracles kept a few flakes. Three are known. The Warden-Prime knows where two of them are.

A scale is the size of a thumbnail, translucent, faintly veined, and always warm, as if it had just been lying against something asleep. Held up and looked through at a sleeping person, it shows their dream, plainly, as if through a window. Looked through at one of the Counted, in the Dormitory, it shows a long dim room with a single enormous closed eye in the far wall, and the sleeper standing in it, among sixty thousand others, all facing the same way. Senna Vole has looked through one. She says the room was quieter than she remembered, and that for the first time some of the sleepers had turned around.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22). The Warden-Prime knows of all three without a roll.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It cools if it is kept away from a sleeping body for more than a month, and then shows nothing until it has lain beside one for a night.

**CARVING:** Unique, or nearly. One is in the Vigil's treasury, one with the Eye-line House Dalgety, and one is unaccounted for. Holding it is Dread 1: the holder yawns, and cannot stop.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Iss).

**EFFECT:** Looked through at any sleeper, it shows their dream (+4 to any roll to learn their fears, secrets or intentions). Looked through at the Counted, it shows the long room. Each use, the looker rolls Caul + Resolve vs Hard (14) or blinks, as the Vigil's Appetite blinks: for a second they are in the long room too, and come back having moved.

**WORTH:** 2,500 lacks. Rare. Possession is a capital crime in the Vigil. Matron Cecily Dorm would pay with something other than money to know whether the number in the room matches hers.

### One of the Drift

On the night the highlands killed Vey, it would not stay killed: from each wound a smaller Vey crawled, and by dawn the floor of the old birthing-house was a drift of gods the size of hares, of mice, of fingernails, each with its arms spread, each still trying to begin. Hollan Kest crushed the last, the size of a barleycorn. The rest went into the copper. One did not. A girl of twelve carried it out of the birthing-house in the pocket of her apron, the size of a mouse, because she could not bear it, and dried it in a salt-box, and it is in the vault under the library at Quire, in a box of grey stone, beside the Master Concordance, and it has never stopped.

It is soft and red and warm, and faceless, and it lies with its arms spread to either side like a figure standing in a doorway. Every pang in Cradlewrack, wherever it is, it begins again: it is a little different, newly wet, as if just born. It is a god, alive, in a box. The Archprovost has never written down what she uses it for. Her Linemen say that the College's oldest copyists, the ones who should have died a decade ago, visit the vault on the first of every month and come out looking as if they had just begun.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Impossible (26); Hesper Lune and two of her Linemen know.

**SPOILAGE:** None. In grey stone it keeps; out of it, the Highland Leavings Abroad roll applies, at 1–5.

**CARVING:** Unique. The vault at Quire is cut into the hill under the library and guarded by the College's whole apparatus; reaching it is Lung + Stealth vs Impossible (26). Seeing it is Dread 4: it is very small, and it turns its faceless head toward the one who opened the box.

**TAINT:** 4 Regrowth (Vey), Dread 4.

**EFFECT:** Once each Plate, laid against something that has stopped, it begins it again: a heart stopped within the minute, a dry spring, a dead fire, a barren field or womb, a stopped clock, a broken oath's Forsworn mark (it heals). Each use, roll a d6: on a 1, what begins is not what was meant, and the GM decides what it is.

**WORTH:** Never sold. If the Assemblers knew it existed, they would go to war with the College for it. If the Attendance knew, they would bring the knife.

### A Phial of the Witness's Water

When the canyon folk cut Tolm's throat in the Gullet, what came out was not blood but something thick and clear that smelled of wet slate and tasted, those who caught it on their lips said afterward in handtalk, of the moment after you have said a thing you cannot take back. The poor licked it off the stone and were Scraplings ever after. A woman named Deverel Ashe, who laid her forearm open to the bone that night and knelt with her teeth clamped on her sleeve until her jaw cracked, caught some of it in a horn cup, one-handed, without a sound. Her family has kept it in a stoppered phial of canyon-glass ever since. Five drops are left.

A drop on the tongue makes the drinker's speech the Witness's for a day. What they say, plainly and without condition, is so. That is the gift. The cost is that the Witness does not distinguish between what the drinker says aloud and what they say to themselves, and nobody alive goes a whole day without telling themselves something. The last of the Ashe line to drink, in 601, told herself at noon, in a moment of despair, that she would never be loved. She was a very beautiful and very warm woman with a husband and four children, and by sundown not one of them could bear to stand near her, and none of them could say why.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Dire (22); Sister Verity Amn of the Quiet knows, and has asked for a drop.

**SPOILAGE:** Sealed, forever. Unstoppered, the whole phial evaporates in an hour, and everyone within a room's breadth hears their own last words come back to them, slightly flat, for a week.

**CARVING:** Unique. The Ashe house is a minor Tongue-line on the Sunward Wall with a strongroom of felt and lead; Hand + Filch vs Grim (18), with the house silent around the thief. Holding it is Dread 2: the holder hears their own last sentence, half a second late.

**TAINT:** 3 Regrowth (Tolm).

**EFFECT:** For one day after a drop, once per scene, a plain statement the drinker makes about something they can see becomes so, as the Course's **Say So**, with no roll and at any scale up to Grim. For the same day, the GM may make one thing the drinker thinks about themselves come true, in a small and fitting way.

**WORTH:** 4,000 lacks the drop. Rare. The Bench would seize it, the Second Table would drink it, and the Ashe family has been asked, by a polite man in a good grey coat, what it would take.

## The Empty Chair

[sigil: eighth]

[box: For the GM Alone]
The two leavings that follow come from the eighth place: the gap in the ring of figures on every carving older than the Gorging, and the chair that is always pulled out. They are not for sale and not for use. Do not give them to players as treasure. Let the characters find them at the end of long trails, hold them, feel what they do, and want very badly to put them down. Their stat lines are complete, because the players will ask, but the honest answer to "what does it do?" is in the Secrets chapter, and the honest answer to "how do we use it?" is that you do not. Use them as signs. Every time one is touched, the world is a little nearer to its last meal.
[/box]

### Chisel-Dust

The eighth figure has been cut from every carving older than the Gorging: hacked with a boathook in Brinehollow, ground out with a rasp in the Vigil so patiently that the recess shines, gouged in Kest so deep that the hole breathes warm air on cold days, smoothed from Hallowboard's panel as if by a thumb in wet clay. Different tools, different centuries, some before the Gorging and some after. In a few old places nobody has swept beneath the recess since it was cut, and there is a little drift of dust on the floor below the gap. It is not stone-dust. It is grey, and very fine, and slightly too heavy, and it is never cold.

Hesper Lune has collected it for twenty years, from beneath four hundred carvings, in a stoppered jar in her bedroom at Quire. Dust from two carvings, set in one dish, draws together overnight into a single line. Dust from many draws together into a shape. She has not let anyone see the shape in the jar. She has stopped going to dinners. Her kitchen staff report that bread left on the table in the room where the jar is kept is found in the morning still in its shape, crust and all, and empty inside: not stale, not spoiled, but hollow, as if the inside of it had been eaten without the outside being touched.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Impossible (26). Hesper Lune knows. So, perhaps, does whoever chiseled the first carving.

**SPOILAGE:** None. Wherever it is kept, there is slightly more of it each month.

**CARVING:** Sweeping the floor beneath an unswept eighth recess is a moment's work with a brush and a paper, and requires no roll. It requires a Dread 3 check. A Lack means that for one heartbeat, as the brush touches the dust, the recess above is not empty.

**TAINT:** Dread 4, and 2 Hunger of no land: the carrier gains 2 Hunger each day it is near them, even if they are Unfed or a Blank, who have no Hunger to gain.

**EFFECT:** None that anyone wants. Food left within a pace of it overnight is found hollow. A godsigned character carrying it cannot Partake: when they call on the god in their blood, it does not come, and lies very still, and their Hunger rises by 1 instead. At any table where the dust is present, the other diners leave an empty space beside its carrier without knowing why. Dust from enough carvings, combined, makes a shape (Dread 5) which the GM should describe as little as possible.

**WORTH:** Never sold. Mister Cutlery, offered a twist of it at Gristmoot in 638, closed his case and left the market, and did not come back to Gristmoot for a year.

### The Eighth Chair

At every inn on the Rim Road, on Tablenight, the innkeeper sets one extra chair at every table and turns it to face the wall. Nobody remembers why. A chair turned to the wall is a place laid for someone who has been asked to leave. On the morning after Tablenight in 640, at the Long Farewell at Fogmouth, the innkeeper came down to find the extra chair at the long table in the common room turned around, square to the table, facing in. It could not be turned back. It could not be pushed in. Six men with ropes could not move it a hair. When the inn was closed for the season, the innkeeper had the floor cut out around it and the whole thing carried out in a block of planking to a cellar under the Company counting-house at Toll Nine, where it stands now, in a locked room, on its square of floor.

It is a plain oak chair a little taller than the others at that table, worn on the seat and the arms as if by very long sitting, though no one has ever sat in it. It is pulled out from a table that is no longer there, at the angle a good host gives a chair for an honoured guest. Food set before it is gone by morning. The Company factor at Toll Nine has measured, weekly, how far it is pulled out. Like Cray Lobbett's chair on the seabed, it is pulled out a little further each time.

**WHISPERED:** Eye + Lore vs Impossible (26). The factor at Toll Nine, the innkeeper of the Long Farewell, and Odalys Penhallow, who has been to see it and has ordered her sealed wagons built faster.

**SPOILAGE:** None. It does not age, and cannot be burned, broken, moved or turned.

**CARVING:** It cannot be carved, taken or moved. It can be visited. Entering the room is Dread 4. Trying to sit in it is Caul + Resolve vs Impossible (26), and on any result the character finds themselves standing beside it, a little further from the door than they were, with no memory of having stood up: Dread 5.

**TAINT:** Dread 5.

**EFFECT:** None that can be used. Anyone who eats in the same building as the chair finds their food tastes of nothing for a day. Every week the GM should mark how far the chair is pulled out. When it is pulled out as far as a chair can be, it is no longer a sign. See the Secrets chapter, under How the Grace Ends.

**WORTH:** Never sold. The Company has priced it, as it prices everything. The figure on the factor's slate is not a number. It is a date.
