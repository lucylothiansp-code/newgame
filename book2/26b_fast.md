## The People of the Fast, Continued

> Carry your own.
>> — the whole of the advice an Unfed mother gives a child leaving home

The first half of the Fast's people are those who hold it up: the Chair and his list, the Keeper and her scales, the captain and her knife, the Breaker and his axe. The people who follow are those the Fast cannot hold: a physician, a poacher, an envoy, a Reckoner who ate, a verger, a farmer, a child who came home fed, a soldier, a pilgrim, a clerk, and a steward, very tired, walking a mile ahead of his master.

They all have one thing in common, and it is the thing the Fast is built to prevent. Each of them, in some way, has been offered something and has not managed to say no three times.

[bigquote] We are not a people who refused. We are a people who are still refusing. There is a difference, and it is about six hundred years long. | — Envoy Prudence Lowell, to the Company congress at Thimble Cross, 638 A.G.

> "Arm, thirteen and a quarter inches at the widest. Skin pinch holds four breaths. Gums white. Pulse forty-one. She'll see Tablenight. She won't see Grace. Write it down; somebody must."

#### Who They Are
Mercy Thrane is all edges. Her collarbones stand out above a grey wool dress turned twice and darned in three colours of thread; her hair has faded to the reddish straw of long hunger and is pinned so hard it pulls her temples. Her spectacles are mended with copper wire, the left lens cracked across. She smells of the vinegar she washes her hands in, of pear-drop breath, and faintly of brine. Her hands are the warmest thing about her, because she keeps them in her armpits between patients: a physician's hands must not be cold on bare skin. The Unfed find this her strangest habit. It looks too much like comfort.
She speaks in numbers. Asked how a child is, she gives arm-measure, weight and pulse, and only then, if pressed, whether the child is frightened. In Wanting a feeling is an appetite and a figure is a fact. With the dying she is different: she sits on the floor beside them, which no other Unfed will do, lets them talk about food, and writes down what they say.
#### Their Story
Mercy was born in Wanting in 594, elder child of a Pantry Clerk. In the Lack of 611 her mother was caught giving half her measure to a neighbour's boy in the Brink. It was the crime of feeding. Her name was left off the list for a month, which at her weight was forever, and seventeen-year-old Mercy watched her walk to the Lean House and decided that if the Fast was going to kill people with arithmetic, someone ought to keep the arithmetic honest.
She apprenticed herself, against every custom, to the Company surgeon at Brindle Cross, an Oathen man named Ambry Cole, ate only what she carried for six years, and came home in 623 to measure. By 630 she had the measures of four thousand Unfed, and in 634 she finished *Of the Body Unfed*, the only proper book on what a lifetime of hunger does to bone, blood, heart, womb and mind. The Abstinent read the chapter stating that most Unfed women no longer bleed and that falling births are starvation, not virtue, and forbade its copying.
Then her patients began to vanish: young, healthy by Fast measure, from the border hamlets, each struck off as dead under the standing order. In 635 she crossed the Rim with her own food and a surgeon's case, and in a hired house in Sated, in a room with no west windows, she found two initials scratched behind a cupboard with a fingernail, and below them a tally of days. She cut out the plank. The trail since has led to surgeries in four countries, to the brine-tanks of Master Quillon Barr, to Lady Seraphine Dalgety of the Vigil, and to a little enamel badge shaped like a two-tined fork.
#### Their Place in the Land
In law Mercy has no place: the Fast does not license physicians, because a physician makes the sick comfortable, and comfort is hospitality. In practice everyone consults her and no one thanks her. Mother Stint Avery asks her privately how long a village of a given age can live on a given measure, and Mercy answers, and both women know what the question is for. Withy Gaunt lets her measure the omitted. Marshal Aldane sends for her whenever a poacher's camp is found, to read how many were taken and whether any were carried. The Reckoners use her figures to choose who is fit for a raid.
Her power is the Red Ledger, and its limit is that nobody wants to read it. The Abstinent say an Unfed taken over the border is dead. Her ledger says they are alive, kept, fed and cut, some of them for years. If she is right, the Fast's cleanest law is a lie told to save the cost of a rescue.
#### What They Carry
- **The Red Ledger** — a madder-dyed Company daybook holding two hundred and twelve names taken across the border since 620, with dates, last weights, and for sixty-one of them a buyer's initials. Eight factions would kill for it.
- **The cupboard plank** — a hand-length of deal scratched *A.T.* and a tally of nine hundred and seven days. She thinks it was a patient called Abate Tolly. She carries it against her ribs.
- **The jar** — a stoppered jar of brine holding an Unfed kidney, College stitches still in it, recovered from a Gut-line matriarch whose household Scant Hollis ended in 638. Her only evidence no surgeon could call forgery.
- **Calipers, tapes and a brass hand-scale** — worn to a shine; the scale weighs to the quarter-ounce, and she weighs children in a sling like oats.
- **A surgeon's case** — +1 Stitching. The bone-saw has been used twice.
- **Her mother's chit** — the last ration-chit issued to her mother, Lack 611, never spent.
#### Wants, Fears and Secrets
Mercy wants the Red Ledger read aloud in the Pantry, every name, and the standing order repealed. She wants to walk into one of Barr's houses with Aldane's sixty spearmen and carry everyone out, and she knows exactly how many would die of the carrying. She fears the Fast will hear her and shrug: they ate on the road, so they are no longer Unfed. Wont Bellamy has said almost exactly this.
**Secret (hers):** to buy the buyers' initials, she sold the Cutwrights' College a fair copy of her four thousand measures. The price of a "Zero-line specimen" on the Rim has risen by a third since, because buyers can now be shown, in her own charts, how clean the organs are.
**Secret (unknown to her):** her younger brother, Abide Thrane, a farmer at Pollard's Rest, vanished in the Carving of 632 and is name one hundred and four in her ledger, without a buyer. The farmer called Abide whom Master Barr has kept alive between harvests for nine years, and who has lately begun to put on weight, is her brother. The plank says *A.T.* It is not Abate Tolly.
#### Ties
- **Kale Dragoman** — set her broken wrist on the Rim in 636 and offered her supper three times. She has regretted not killing him that night.
- **Marshal Keep Aldane** — her only friend; she held his hand in 624 while the surgeon dug out a bolt.
- **Withy Gaunt** — lets her measure the omitted and hates the tapes.
- **Mother Stint Avery** — asks the questions nobody else will.
- **Abide Kettering** — Mercy has noticed the Lean House dead go into the ground lighter than they came out. She has not yet weighed the sacks.
- **Master Quillon Barr** — admires her book and has twice sent her better spectacles. She smashed both.
- **Scour** — came to her at midnight to have his forearms measured; she has told no one.
#### In Play
PCs meet Mercy when she hires them to bring a body home: a taken Unfed traced to a hired house on the Rim, alive. She pays in medical care, worth more than coin in the Fast, and wants the PCs' measures before and after.
- **The House with No West Windows.** In Lastgate three Unfed are kept in brine-tanks to the neck, fed by tube. One asks the PCs to leave her. She is warm for the first time in her life.
- **Name One Hundred and Four.** A captured poacher, bargaining, mentions a farmer in Barr's surgery who talks about his sister the doctor. Mercy is in the next room.
- **The Ledger Read Aloud.** She has won an open session of the Abstinent; someone has bribed a Lister to strike her name the morning after.
Under threat she counts aloud to keep her hands still. Kindness she checks for an offer, refuses three times, and is rude about the fourth. Of Orrum she says, "The god has more manners than the surgeons. It has never once taken a patient from me." If nobody intervenes, she goes over the Rim alone in the spring of 642 to the house where her brother is kept, and does not come back, and the Red Ledger turns up for sale at Thimble Cross with one new name in it, in a different hand.

#### Who They Are
Kale is big, ruddy and handsome in the Fatlands way, his size all appetite and none of it softness: heavy jowls that shake when he laughs, beautiful white teeth he cleans with a willow twig after every meal, small clever brown eyes, hands like a baker's. He wears a good brown Rim coat over boiled leather, a red neckcloth, and a bay leaf in his hatband, and he smells always of woodsmoke and roast fat and onions softened in dripping. His voice is a rolling eastern burr, the voice of a man who has called a great many people *my love* and meant it each time. He eats constantly from his pockets, and when he cannot see food his eyes go to the corners of the room.
His captives do not describe a monster. They say he was patient. They say that on the third night, when they had refused his stew nine times and could no longer stand, he sat behind them on the ground with his arms around them like a father with a sick child, touched broth-soaked bread to their lips, and waited, humming, for as long as it took. They say they hated him most when they swallowed.
#### Their Story
Kale was born in the Fast in the Lack of 606. He does not know this. He was raised in the drovers' village of Callock Green on the eastern Fatlands by a drover named Hob Dragoman, who told him his mother died of the milk-fever and showed him a grave that is empty. Hob bought the baby at Brindle Cross for a sack of oats from a frightened Unfed girl of twenty-three, for reasons he took into the ground in 629. Kale grew up enormous, well loved and never full. At his first Weighing the Board's assessors read his tells and wrote him down as Cut 2. At nineteen, droving at Brindle Cross, he bought a drink for a man in a grey coat who asked whether he had ever seen an Unfed up close, and whether he could cook.
He has poached since 627: a hundred and sixty taken, a hundred and four delivered. The rest refused his food and died on the road, and he buried each himself, decently, Fatlands-fashion, which means after a wake, which means he ate them. In 634 he learned from a pirated copy of a physician's book that most of his captives were dying of eating too fast, and learned to feed them slowly: broth, then milk, then soft bread, then meat. His deliveries nearly doubled. In 638 a new buyer came, a man with a two-tined fork pinned inside his lapel, who paid in gold and asked for children. Kale said no three times, which he thought was funny at the time. The fourth time, the price was very high.
#### Their Place in the Land
Kale is part of how the Fast works, the way a wolf is part of how a flock works. His route runs up the Rim to Brindle Cross and north along the border hamlets, where Unfed walk out at dusk to fetch water. His hired men Odo and Fenn do the lifting and do not look at the cage. A Company toll-clerk seals his cage-wagon as "Pantry grain, returned spoiled"; a sexton on the Fast side sells him the very freshly dead. He pays for information in food, and there are border families who leave their gates unlatched on certain nights because he leaves a sack of oats at the post. That is the part of his trade the Abstinent do not discuss. Marshal Aldane has orders to kill him on sight, and has twice found the range too long.
#### What They Carry
- **The copper stewpot** — never allowed to go cold on the road. It smells, the captives say, exactly like their mothers' houses, though their mothers never had a pot. A captive within smell of it at night makes an Invitation check at Routine 10.
- **The iron cage** — wheeled, barred, straw-floored, a wool blanket for each. It is clean. He cleans it himself.
- **The book** — a pirated copy of Mercy Thrane's *Of the Body Unfed*, the margins thick with grease-pencil: *broth 3 days, then milk*, *watch the ankles*, *the girl from Pollard's Rest, too fast, lost her*. He calls it his Bible and would love to meet the author.
- **The badge** — an enamel fork with two tines, a token to show at certain houses. He has no idea it is a confession.
- **A boxwood spoon** — long-handled, worn thin on one side. It is the spoon he feeds them with.
- **The wake-kit** — knife, steel, salt, bay and rosemary. Used fifty-six times.
#### Wants, Fears and Secrets
Kale wants to be liked, so badly that it is the nearest thing he has to a conscience; he will spend days winning a smile from someone he is delivering to a surgeon. He wants to be thanked, once, by an Unfed who means it. He fears empty larders, silence, and the man with the badge, who asked for children and smiled when Kale refused.
**Secret (unknown to him):** the god in him is not his father's. It is Ummer, eaten into an empty Unfed body spoonful by spoonful from his first week, which is why he has never once been full. Master Quillon Barr, who examined him once in Sated, knows the god grew in him from the mouth down and never reached his kidneys. They are as blank as the day he was born. Barr has priced them at nine thousand lacks and is waiting for a reason.
**Secret (GM):** his mother is Withy Gaunt, Matron of the Lean House. She named him at three months, when he first turned from the breast, and wrote the name in the back of the Book of the Omitted: *Decline*.
#### Ties
- **Withy Gaunt** — his mother, unknown to them both.
- **Abide Kettering** — supplies the freshly dead; Kale despises him for selling the dead instead of honouring them with a wake, and pays him well.
- **Dr. Mercy Thrane** — he set her wrist, offered her supper three times, and owns her book. He admires her more than anyone alive.
- **Marshal Keep Aldane** — once left a roast fowl on the Brindle Cross fence: *for the lads, no offence meant*.
- **Osanna Blythe** — a Callock Green girl he knew at wakes; he has fed her pilgrims twice for nothing, the only charity of his life.
- **Lady Vellum Serrat and the Second Table** — his buyers since 638.
#### In Play
PCs meet Kale across a campfire on the Rim, where he offers supper three times and then a fourth, warmly, with a joke about Unfed manners. He wants guides, guards, or, if they are hunting him, to talk them out of it over a very good stew.
- **The Fourth Offer.** His buyer wants two children from Wanting before Tablenight, at a price that would let Kale retire. He wants the PCs to help him refuse, which means killing the buyer, and cannot ask outright.
- **Decline.** Withy Gaunt finds a strip of red neckcloth in a dead woman's sack, smells woodsmoke on it, and asks the PCs where it came from.
- **The Kidneys.** Barr's men take Kale. His captives, locked in the cage in the woods, will starve; one is a PC's friend. Kale will say where, if the PCs get him out.
Threatened, he fights well and without rage and surrenders the moment he is losing, with an offer of dinner. Kindness undoes him. Of Orrum he is envious: "There's a cook who knows his business." If nobody intervenes, he takes the price at the end of Lack, delivers the children, goes home to Callock Green, and eats until it kills him a year later, at a wake held in his honour, still not full.

#### Who They Are
She is a lath of a woman, very upright, in a black wool coat with a high collar buttoned to the throat and twenty-two jet buttons down the front, which she does up every morning in the same order. Her face is long and colourless, the cheeks hollow, the mouth thin and exact. Her grey hair is cropped short as a Reckoner's under a plain black cap. Her voice is the thing people remember: low, dry, perfectly carrying, with every consonant cut clean, a voice like a cold chisel set to stone. She smells of the lye soap the Fast makes from Rim ashes and of nothing else at all, because she eats so little that her breath has gone past pear-drops into a kind of clean metal. She stands at every meeting. She has not sat down in public in nineteen years.
In fourteen capitals she is known on sight and dreaded. Servants have learned not to bring the tray toward her. Hosts offer three times, because they must, and she refuses three times, audibly, and then turns to the wall and takes out her ration-cloth and eats half an oatcake standing, facing the plaster, while the room tries not to watch. Then she turns back and reads the figures.
#### Their Story
Prudence was born in 591 to a Measure-Bearer family of Wanting, and showed early the two gifts the Fast values in a diplomat: a perfect memory for numbers and an absolute incapacity for pleasure. The Abstinent sent her to the Rim at twenty-three as clerk to the old Envoy, Withhold Pruett, Hollow Ann's elder brother, and when he died at his post in 622, standing, she was given his room above a chandler's at Wanting Post and his credentials, and his list.
In the Vigil, in 633, at a reception in the lamplit hall of the Watch, a junior envoy named Oriel Vance offered her a cup of watered wine. She refused three times. He offered a fourth time, quietly, not as manners, looking at her as though she were a person who might be thirsty. She very nearly took it. Her hand moved. She has dreamed of his face every night since. He wrote to her once, in 634. She has not opened the letter.
#### Their Place in the Land
The Fast has one ambassador, and she is it. She receives callers standing in a bare stone office off the yard at Wanting Post, the only Company station that faces the middle, and she sleeps in the room above the chandler's across the lane, with her pallet's head to the door. She negotiates nothing, because the Fast has nothing to offer; her duty is to remind. She also handles every matter the Rim governments raise about the Reckoners, and handles it by declining to admit they exist. She carries the Abstinent's letters to Factor Mardle about the grain, and Mardle's letters back, and she knows the grain is short. She does not know why.
#### What They Carry
- **The Register of Damnation** — four ledgers in black buckram, one per decade since 601, in which she and her predecessor have recorded the published and rumoured Regrowth of every High Cut house on the Table. It is the best such record outside the Cutwrights' College, and more honest.
- **Credentials** — a square of Pantry linen stamped with the empty bowl and the marks of every Elder of the Abstinent, folded in eight, worn at the folds to gauze.
- **A stick of white chalk** — in each capital, on the wall she faced while eating, she makes one small mark at the height of her eyes: a short horizontal stroke, like a table seen edge on. There are fourteen in the world. She tells no one.
- **The letter** — from Oriel Vance of the Vigil, sealed with grey wax, unopened since 634. The corners have gone soft from handling.
- **A list in her own hand** — four names, for the Abstinent: people she recommends be left off the winter list "for the safety of the Refusal." The first is Meek Garrity's.
#### Wants, Fears and Secrets
Prudence wants the Refusal kept, because it is the only thing her life has been for. If the Unfed did not refuse, she has spent twenty-seven years telling six nations they were damned on the authority of a lie, and every wall in fourteen capitals has her mark on it for nothing. She has heard of Meek Garrity's seating plan, and she will do anything to keep him quiet.
**Secret (hers):** she has written to Forbear Wend asking that Meek Garrity's name be left off the list. It is the first time she has ever asked for a death. She has told herself it is not one, because nobody pronounces it.
**Secret (unknown to her):** her dispatches are copied in the Company post-house at Wanting Post before they go up the road, and the copies are bought by Lord Absalom Thrale's people for the Second Table. The Register of Damnation is the Second Table's menu-calendar. Every land she has read aloud as ripening, the Table has tasted within the year.
#### Ties
- **Meek Garrity** — the man who could unmake her. She has met him once, and found him gentle, and wants him dead.
- **Forbear Wend** — her master, to whom she writes every week. He has not answered her letter about Garrity.
- **Dr. Mercy Thrane** — has asked her three times to speak for the taken. Prudence has refused three times. Neither has made the fourth offer.
- **Welcome Ardent** — met her on the Rim Road at night in 640 and asked, very courteously, if she was dining alone. She says the Decline at every stranger now.
- **Oriel Vance** — the Vigil envoy who offered four times. He is now blinking badly, in the Course, and has asked to be posted to Wanting Post.
#### In Play
PCs meet Prudence when she needs Unfed escorts to a congress, or when a PC from a High Cut house hears her read their family's numbers aloud in public. She wants protection, couriers, and, though she would never say so, someone to talk to: on the road, at night, facing outward, she will talk about numbers for hours to anyone who stands with their back to her back.
- **The Fourteenth Wall.** In Lastgate a host has scrubbed her chalk mark from the wall and written below it, in chalk, a second stroke and a third, and a word. She wants the PCs to find out who.
- **Garrity's Omission.** The PCs are asked to carry her sealed letter to Forbear Wend. If they read it, they know what she has asked for.
- **Oriel Vance at the Gate.** The Vigil envoy arrives at Wanting Post, unable to sleep, bringing her a cup of wine he has carried four hundred miles, and asks once more.
Threatened, she reads the threatener's numbers to them. Kindness she treats as an offer, and refuses, three times. Of the god she says only, "The Host has excellent manners. It is the guests I object to." If nobody intervenes, she reads the numbers at Thimble Cross on Tablenight 641, and a man she has damned stabs her in the throat with a fork, and she dies standing, and nobody in the room offers her anything at all.

#### Their Story
He was born in 562 in the cottage beside the Empty Chair. He married late, a quiet woman named Cease, who died in 619. They had no children. In the Lack of 620, alone in the hall, he took the old temple plate out of the press and laid one place at the head of the table, for the first time in his family's memory, because it seemed rude not to. #### Their Story

#### Who They Are

He is ink to the elbows: the creases of his knuckles are permanently black, his cuffs are stiff with it, and when he is anxious he rubs his thumb across his lower lip and leaves a blue mark there. His fingers are long, thin and bloodless, a copyist's fingers. His voice is barely above a whisper, a habit of forty years in the Pantry cellars, and he apologises before almost every sentence. He smells of lamp-oil, damp slate and the mouse-and-sacking air of the Pantry. He is the gentlest man in Wanting. He is also, by the Abstinent's reckoning, the most dangerous.
#### Their Story
Meek was born in 575 to a Lister of the Pantry, took the clerk's oath at fifteen, and has spent fifty years copying, collating and preserving the Pantry ledgers, back to the first of 41 A.G. In 621 he was given the oldest box, the one that holds the Lammas Bottom slate with its word *no* scratched until the stone is white. Under it, in sacking, he found other slates. He spent sixteen years reading them. In 637 he reached the last.
It is a seating plan. Scratched in the old hand of the heartland, by a reeve of the Host's parishes, it lists the order in which the households were to stand around the stone table at Hallowboard, with their places on the eight sides. It is dated, by the reckoning of the old calendar, to the tenth day of the ninth winter's darkest month: the morning Orrum rose. *Households to go up at first light*, it says. *Bring knives.* And at its foot, in a different and shakier hand: *We came. It was cleared. The plate was clean. The Host is getting up.*
There was no refusal. The Unfed arrived on the tenth day, with knives, and something had eaten first.
#### Their Place in the Land
Until 637 Meek was a fixture, a harmless senior clerk who corrected other clerks' arithmetic. Now his papers, copied by hand and passed house to house at night, are read in half the homes of Wanting, and the Abstinent have called them forgery and ordered the slate burned. They have not yet found it. The Fast's whole moral authority rests on the Refusal: the Pantry, the list, the Reckoners' mandate, the Envoy's dispatches, the children's pride. If the Unfed did not refuse, they are not clean; they were merely unlucky. Meek did not set out to say this. He set out to copy the oldest box.

- **Ink, quills, and a Clausewright's sand-box** — +1 to Craft for copying.
- **Forty copies** — of the slate, of his papers, of his own notes, hidden in forty houses. He no longer remembers all forty.
#### Wants, Fears and Secrets

**Secret (his):** he has shown the slate to one person: Forbear Wend, in 638, at night, in the bare room on the Pantry's top floor. Wend read it, wept, and ordered it burned, and has done nothing since to see that it was.
**Secret (unknown to him):** he has misread it, as any Unfed would. The three strokes he has recovered are not the beginning of a household's name. The eighth side was not allotted to a household of the heartland at all. He has assumed, as all his people assume, that the chair on the eighth side was for someone who was coming up the hill with a knife. At dusk, on the eleventh morning's eve, the god laid a table in the road, and set a place at it, and pulled out the chair. Welcome said no, thank you, three times. The god did not offer a fourth. And Welcome, who had nothing left, did the thing no Unfed has done before or since: he asked. *May I help?* A guest is offered. One who asks to help at a table is staff. The god took him on.
He has laid every table in the Fast since, and lately, every table on the Rim.
#### Their Place in the Land
Welcome walks a mile ahead of Orrum. He lays the cloths, sets out the plates, angles the chairs for guests who are a little unsteady, and writes the place cards in whatever hand will be loved: a dead mother's, a grandmother's, a husband's. He knows every name in the Fast, including the secret ones. He is the only one of the Host's guests who can still get up, and he will answer questions honestly if he is not asked to sit. The Hostwatch know him by sight and speak to him as colleagues. The Tablebreakers hate him and cannot hurt him. The Abstinent pretend he is a story. In the last year, for the first time, the Host has sometimes gone ahead of him, and he has arrived to find tables already laid, and that frightens him more than anything in six centuries.
#### What They Carry
- **The keys** — nine iron keys on a ring. Every door, gate, lock and bricked hearth opens for them. He does not know which one the ninth is for.
- **A box of cutlery** — walnut, brass-cornered, holding a perfect setting for one: knife, fork, spoon, all silver, all warm.
- **White gloves** — always clean, and always replaced, though nobody has seen him change them.
- **Place cards and brown ink** — stiff cream card, a fine pen. The ink is brown, like gravy dried on a plate.
- **A child's wooden spoon** — small, worn, with a nick in the bowl from a first tooth. It is the only thing he carries that is not the Host's.
#### Wants, Fears and Secrets
Welcome wants to be relieved. He has been looking for a successor, and he will offer the position to whoever refuses him most gracefully. He fears the extra place. He has laid it at every table since his first night, because he was told to, pushed in square, with its back to the lamp.
**Secret (his):** he has been told, by his master, that when the expected guest arrives, the Steward will be released, and everyone else will be served. He has never been told a name. He has not asked. He tells himself this means he knows who the place is for, and that it is the same thing.
**Secret (GM):** his contract can be negotiated; Jessamy Quill, the Company's notary, has been asked to witness it, and has not yet said yes. And Bounty Ardent's name has never once appeared on any card he has written. He has looked, every night, for six hundred and forty years.
#### Ties
- **Orrum** — his master, a mile behind, warm, patient; he loves it, and he is so tired.
- **Lent Ashby** — the guest who got up; he cried when she left, and he has started writing *Steward* on her card.
- **Little Fain** — he waves to Fain on the wind. He cooked the milk.
- **Verger Sober Osmond** — the only man who lays a table for the Host; Welcome stands at the hall door and thanks him.
- **Hollow Ann Pruett** — keeps his cards; he would like the one with the drawing back.
- **Spare Tolland** — the Tracker who has followed his master for thirty years; Welcome leaves him a cup of tea at a mile's distance, which Spare never drinks.
- **Envoy Prudence Lowell** — he asked if she was dining alone. She is. She always is.
#### In Play
PCs meet Welcome in the road at dusk, bowing, asking whether they will be joining the Host this evening. He wants a successor and, failing that, someone to talk to for a quarter of an hour, honestly, standing up.
- **The Interview.** He asks a PC, after three graceful refusals, whether they would consider a position. He describes it fairly. He does not hide the hours.
- **The Table He Did Not Lay.** At a Rim inn the PCs find Welcome in the doorway with his hand over his mouth: the Host has laid ahead of him again, and this table has a card at the extra place.
- **The Contract.** Jessamy Quill's clerk asks the PCs to carry Welcome's terms to Oathen for review. They are short. There is a clause about relief.
Threatened, he apologises and sets a place for the threatener. Kindness brings him close to tears. Of the god he says, "It only wants everyone fed. That's all it has ever wanted. That isn't so terrible, is it?" If nobody intervenes, he goes on, more tired than before, until the night someone sits in the extra chair; and then, as promised, he is released.

#### Who They Are
Fallow is broad, heavy-handed and silent, with chalk ground into every line of her face and hands so that she looks carved, grey eyes, and cropped hair gone white at the temples. She speaks perhaps twenty words a day. She smells of wet chalk, turned turf and the pale long grass of the Barrows, which she does not cut, because it is the only grass in the Fast that is not trimmed.
#### Their Story
Born 587 to a family of the Brindle hamlet whose name the Cross took. Appointed Warden in 619. Each spring after rain she walks the trench and the Barrows, gathering the teeth the chalk gives up and reburying them, and when a mound slumps she goes down with a spade to lay the dead straight. In 639 the great Lammas Barrow opened after a storm, and she went down further than any Warden had, to the bottom layer.
#### Their Place in the Land
She lives in a turf hut at the foot of the hill, draws a measure brought by a Measure-Bearer once a fortnight, and witnesses the oaths sworn in the trench. Unfed who travel two days to swear in their ancestors' footprints find her standing a little way off with a lantern. She is the only Unfed who goes onto the hill alone at night, and she never walks on the eighth side.
#### What They Carry
- **A sack of teeth** — the gatherings of a spring; she counts them into the Barrows on Tablenight.
- **A carving knife** — bronze, pre-Gorging, clean as the day it was ground. She found eleven hundred in the bottom layer, laid beside the dead, unused.
- **A lantern and chalk** — for marking which mound has slumped.
#### Wants, Fears and Secrets
**Secret (hers):** the bottom layer of the Lammas Barrow does not hold starved bodies. It holds bones that are clean, every one, the way a plate is clean after a good guest has finished with it: no flesh, no marrow, no mark of tooth or blade, polished, as if by a cloth. They were buried first, before the trenches of the Refusal were dug over them. She has written this to Meek Garrity. **Unknown to her:** on the eighth side, where she never walks, there are fresh footprints in the untrodden chalk every Lack morning, and they come from the Barrows, and they go away down the hill.
#### Ties
- **Meek Garrity** — her unanswered letter. **Abide Kettering** — his fields' graves never settle, and she has walked them. **Scant Hollis** — swore her captain's oath in the trench before Fallow. **Forbear Wend** — appointed her, and told her never to dig below the ninth row.
#### In Play
PCs come to swear an oath in the trench, or to escort Meek to the hill. Hooks: the opened Barrow; the clean bones; the eleven hundred knives. Threatened, she leans on the spade and waits. If nobody intervenes, she goes down into the Lammas Barrow one more time in Lack, and does not come up, and in the morning the mound is closed and smooth, and the grass on it has been cut.

### Three Fuses

**The Mother, the Son and the Sexton.** Withy Gaunt sold her baby at Brindle Cross so that he would live. He lived, and grew up to be Kale Dragoman, who buys the bodies of the omitted from Withy's own sexton, Abide Kettering, at the crossroads three miles west. Withy has the red neckcloth. Kale has the empty grave his father showed him. Kettering has the purse of chits. Any PC who follows one thread finds the other two. The explosion comes when Kale, kind and plump and smelling of woodsmoke, walks into the Lean House to collect a body himself, and Withy smells him, and Kale, licking a drop of her blood from a cut on her hand to price an old woman in the straw, tastes his own name. Kettering is in the doorway. Kettering has the pickaxe.

**The Physician and the Farmer Called Abide.** Mercy Thrane carries a plank from a hidden room in Sated with *A.T.* scratched on it, and believes it is a patient's. It is her brother's. Abide Thrane has been alive in Master Quillon Barr's brine-tanks for nine years and has started to put on weight, and Barr has read Mercy's book with admiration and sent her spectacles. Marshal Aldane has sixty men and a boy in the weighing-shed he is already breaking the law for. Kale has the address. Mardle has the dockets. The moment Mercy learns the truth she will go over the Rim with or without help, and the Fast will have to decide, very publicly, whether an Unfed taken is dead.

**The Slate, the Envoy and the Captain.** Meek Garrity has the seating plan; Fallow Brindle has the clean bones and eleven hundred unused knives; Sober Osmond has a rite with a line about *the one who comes late*; Hollow Ann Pruett has a card with an empty chair drawn on it. Together they say that the Refusal never happened, and they say something else as well, which no one has yet put into words. Prudence Lowell has asked for Garrity's death to protect the Refusal. Scant Hollis stood in Garrity's doorway for a minute and left: if the Unfed only arrived late, her nine ended lines were not justice but envy. When the papers reach the Reckoners' west gate, the Fast's two poles, the Keepers and the Reckoners, will discover they have the same enemy, and that he is a gentle old man with ink on his lip, and the PCs will be the only people in Wanting standing between him and the brazier.

## Using the Fast's People


[box: Running the Fast's People]
- **Everyone refuses three times.** When a PC offers an Unfed NPC anything, play the Decline aloud. The fourth offer is where character lives.
- **Kindness is the threat.** Kale, Osanna, Fain and Ardent are the most dangerous people in the chapter, and none of them will raise a hand.
- **The list is always being written.** At any point the GM may announce that a name has been left off. Make it a name the players know.
- **Never say whose chair it is.** Ardent does not know; Garrity has misread it; Hollow Ann will not let anyone hold the card. Let the players count the sides of Hallowboard themselves.
[/box]
