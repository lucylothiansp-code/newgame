## The People of Brinehollow, Continued

> Every wall has an ear, and every ear is a neighbor's. Mind what you say at the count. Mind more what answers.
>> — Mile proverb, as given in full by grandmothers

The first half of the coast's people hold it up. The second half live in its cracks: a smuggler who sells the sea by the bottle, a man who shares his mouth with a god's lodger, a clerk who has done the sum that ends the country, a saint who cannot hear her own god. Then come the new faces, and the knots that tie them all.

Unless stated otherwise, every dossier is written as of early Carving, 641 A.G. The Chase has just finished its nineteenth mile, the Low Water is a month away, and the tide in the body is running eleven minutes ahead of the tide on the Uncovered.

### Captain Jonah Skerry — Dry Jonah

> "Half a crumb a pint, mother, and I'll take the bucket off your hands myself. You'd only pour it in the gutter. The gutter doesn't pay."
>> — Dry Jonah, to a widow on Mile Thirteen at dawn bailing

#### Who They Are

Jonah Skerry is a round man in a coat of oiled Fatlands calfskin that is too warm for the coast and that he will not give up, because it reminds him of being somewhere with a supper. He smells of pipe tobacco, of the beeswax he uses to seal his corks, and, faintly, of the inside of a bucket. His face is red and well-fed and creased into a permanent good humor that never quite reaches the creases beside his eyes. His hands are soft and very clean. He laughs loudly, at his own jokes first, and has the trick of putting a palm flat on your shoulder while he talks, as though steadying a barrel.

He is called Dry Jonah because for nineteen years he was the only grown man on the Mile who did not bail. Children used to follow him at dusk to listen to him not coughing. He wears a waist-rope as a joke, a length of cheap hemp knotted into a bow, and a bail-bucket of painted tin that has never had a drop in it, with a wooden lid on which some wit carved *For Emergencies*. On the Mile they laughed with him for years. They have lately begun to laugh at a slightly different moment, a beat after he does, and he has not decided whether he has noticed.

#### Their Story

Jonah was born in 591 A.G. in a toll-village on the southern march where the Fatlands' warm meat-plains run out into Brinehollow's salt marsh. His father salted pork for the Rim; his mother sold it. He grew up eating five meals a day, as Fatlands law demanded, and watching coasters come down the Rim Road to buy salt meat and cough into buckets at the inn yard at dusk. He learned two things from them: that they were embarrassed by it, and that they would pay to be embarrassed less.

He came to Lowmark in 617 as a meat-house clerk, was dismissed in 619 for selling the house's brine barrels twice, and with the proceeds bought the brig *Thirsty Bess*, aground in the old Keelrow channel since 588 and cheap because her timbers creaked at night in a manner her crew found personal. Nobody has ever sailed her. He lives in her captain's cabin and bottles in her hold, and the Admiralty, which calls any shipowner Captain by courtesy, gave him the title with the harbor dues.

The idea came in 624, watching the gutters run west with the dawn bailing, a whole city's coughing flowing downhill in green threads: *nobody owns that.* He bottled a bucket as Skerry's Deep Restorative and sold it in Saltwick as a lung tonic, and it worked, closing wounds in coaster and dry alike at the cost of a little of the sea going into them. In 633 a Slope apothecary found that a few drops made rouse last longer, and the sideline became a fortune. Rook Quarter-Bell's agents now buy by the cask at Lampwick Halt. Jonah has never been to the Vigil. He has never wanted to see what it is used for.

He buys at half a crumb a pint on the slum Miles, through a network of bucket-wives led by Coralie Sculpin, and sells at three lacks a vial on the Rim and a platter a bottle, green glass and wax seal, to the rich of the Vigil's Height. The rumor on the Mile says the Vigil pays a platter. It is true. Jonah charges three, when he can.

#### Their Place in the Land

Jonah is a parasite of exactly the kind the poor depend on. On Miles Twelve to Sixteen a family that sells its bailings clears two or three lacks a week, the difference between seaweed bread and seaweed bread with gull in it. The parishes hate him for undercutting their bail-tokens; the Netwatch take his money; the Admiralty tax the trade and pretend not to know the commodity. Brenna Kelp's arithmetic counts his barrels: near ninety thousand gallons of the coast's inner tide carried east in seventeen years, every gallon of it still going somewhere.

His power is money and a wagon road, and its limit is that nobody respects him and everybody needs him. He has no friends of rank. He has a great many customers. He keeps the peace with all of them by being jolly, prompt and discreet, and by never once asking a question about what is in the buckets.

#### What They Carry

- **A pewter tasting-cup on a chain** — he tastes every bucket before he buys it, a sip, rolled on the tongue and spat. He tells grades by it: thin, clear, green, heavy. He has never told anyone that a blue bucket tastes sweet.
- **A case of six green bottles, wax-sealed** — Skerry's Deep Restorative, his best grade. Each is bottled brine tonic (heals 1d6 Flesh; Taint 2 Regrowth, Dromm; Addiction Hard 14). Retail three lacks; he gives them away to officials.
- **The Bucket Ledger** — a calfskin book of every household on the slum Miles that sells to him, with dates, volumes and grades. It is, without his intending it, the best record in Brinehollow of who is about to be Called, because Coralie notes the colors. Brenna Kelp would give a year of her life for it.
- **The *Thirsty Bess*'s key** — iron, as long as his hand. The captain's cabin door is the only lock on the brig. The hold is not locked; it does not need to be. Nobody on the Mile will go into a hold full of other people's breathing.
- **A heavy crossbow and twelve bolts** — for the wagons. He shoots badly and knows it, and hires Wardens who do not.
- **A forged travel pass and a real one** — the forged one says he carries pickling brine. The real one says the same thing, sealed by a Company Factor at Lowmark Spur who has been paid not to taste it.
- **The bottle by the bed** — unlabeled, corked with a scrap of rag, half empty. He drinks from it when he cannot sleep. See below.
- **A Fatlands sausage, always** — wrapped in greased paper in his breast pocket. He eats it at the grace, so that he will have eaten before anyone at the table, as his mother taught him.

#### Wants, Fears and Secrets

Jonah wants to be rich enough to go home to the warm south and never smell a bucket again. He wants it the way men want things they have decided not to do. He fears the Netwatch, the Admiralty's tax-clerks, and, more than he will admit, the look in the eyes of the families he buys from on the morning after someone in the house was Called, when they bring him that night's bucket anyway, because the price is the same.

**Secret (GM can reveal):** he began to drink his own product two years ago, a spoonful at a time, because the nights on the *Bess* were long. Now he drinks a mouthful each night. He wakes with salt at the corners of his lips. Twice he has found himself at the stern rail at dusk, looking west. Three weeks ago, at dawn, he coughed, and a cupful of clear cold water came up into his hand. He threw it over the side and laughed all morning. He still wears the empty tin bucket. It is no longer a joke, and he does not know how to stop it being one.

**Secret (he does not know):** the bottle by his bed came from a single bucket, bought by Coralie Sculpin from a Wreckwife on Mile Twelve who sold it in secret: the dusk bailing of her Tenanted husband, drawn from his cistern. It is Tenanted brine. When Jonah lies awake and drinks from it, it is not the tonic that helps him sleep. It is the voice in it, like a hull settling, telling him slowly and kindly that he is nearly home. His Calling roll, when it comes, will be made at Grim, and he has never in his life tied a knot that would hold.

#### Ties

- **Coralie Sculpin** — his buyer on the slum Miles, his partner in everything but the profits, and the only person who calls him Jonah without the Dry. He trusts her. He should.
- **Rook Quarter-Bell** (the Vigil) — his best customer, through agents. They have never met. Jonah sends him a Tablenight bottle each year with a card: *To my friend in the east, keep you dry.*
- **Tolly Brack** — the bailer boy refuses to sell him a drop. Tolly knows the colors too, and once spat into Jonah's tasting-cup.
- **Brenna Kelp** — she has counted his barrels; he does not know she exists. If she gets his ledger, the Admiralty gets him.
- **Lieutenant Sable Reef** — she has taken his money twice and returned it once. He cannot read her, which frightens him more than her boathook.
- **Reeve Calder Stokes** — the Company auditor has priced Jonah's trade as the single largest export of the coast. Jonah sent him a case of the Restorative. Stokes logged it as a bribe and returned it, and has since started tasting salt.
- **Old Wet Tom** — Jonah once tried to buy Tom's puddles. Tom laughed until the rain on him fell harder, and said, very gently, *you've been buying them for years.*

#### In Play

PCs meet Jonah buying, selling or hiring: he always needs Wardens for the wagons on the Rim spur, and he pays well, in platters, and asks nothing. He wants them to escort a cart to Lampwick Halt, or recover a stolen cask, or persuade a parish priest to stop preaching against him. He is a good employer and an easy man to like, and the PCs should like him before they understand what he sells.

- **The Walkers on the Road.** A cart of casks bound east is stopped at Saltwick by a column of Vigil-folk walking west, eyes open, coughing brine. They are Rook's customers, and they want to see the man who sells the sea. Jonah wants the PCs to get his casks through. The walkers want to thank him.
- **The Blue Cask.** A whole cask has gone blue in the hold of the *Bess*. Coralie swears she bought nothing but clear. The cask is warm. At night it knocks, three times, the way a neighbor knocks at the count.
- **Dry No Longer.** Jonah's crew find him on the stern rail at dusk, and he will not come down. He asks the PCs, quite calmly, to rope him to the mainmast every night until the wagons are safely gone, and to never tell Coralie. He offers a platter a night.

**Threat:** he pays; failing that he runs; failing that he weeps and tells everything, very fast. **Kindness:** it undoes him, and he gives a kind PC a case of tonic, the worst gift he could give. **The god:** he does not believe in it, and it does not need him to. **If nobody intervenes:** before Tablenight he is Called from the *Bess*; the rope he tied for himself is found on the floor, never knotted. He is next seen at the Low Water of 642, standing in the singers' ring on the mud, heavier, calm, and dry for the first time in his life.

### Orrin Wake and Passenger — the Talking Tenant

> "He wants the arm from dusk bailing to dawn bailing. I want it Tablenight free. We have agreed on the knuckles. The thumb is still open."
>> — Orrin Wake, to the barman of the Lantern, in his upper voice

> "Write that the thumb is not open. Write that he knows it is not."
>> — the Passenger, a moment later, in the lower

[stat: Orrin Wake and Passenger — the Talking Tenant]
Land & Cut: Brinehollow · Cut 2 (common, rising) · Regrowth 10 (the Brink, Tenanted) · Hunger 5
Age & Station: Orrin 39; the Passenger, unknown and very old; fisherman of the Nineteenth, licensed to no boat since his return
Calling & Standing: Gleaner (fisherman, now salvager) · Standing 2 (Gleaner); the Passenger has no Calling it will name

Gifts: Voice of the Deep; The Weight; Fathom-Body; Two Minds (cannot be surprised; each mind may act once per round, but only one may use the left arm)

#### Who They Are

A big man, slope-shouldered, with a fisherman's ruined knuckles and a face that has been out in salt wind for thirty years. His eyes are the Tenanted black, wet and wide and gentle, and his skin has gone the grey of a winter beach. His left arm hangs a little loose, as if the shoulder were not quite his to lift. When the Passenger has the arm it does not hang. It rests on the table, palm down, perfectly still, and the fingers sometimes spread, one at a time, as if counting.

Orrin speaks slowly, the way the Nineteenth speaks, with a pause before every answer to make sure the question is finished. The Passenger speaks under him, a full octave lower, with a courtesy so old that it sounds formal, like a letter from a grandparent's grandparent. They argue in the same mouth, and the mouth has learned to take turns: Orrin's lips move for both, but the Passenger's words come out slightly before the lips finish shaping them, as if the voice were arriving from further down. Sitting at their table in the Lantern on Mile Nine is like sitting at a table with a man and a well.

The room around them is cold. The beer in their mug is always flat by the time it is drunk. They smell of the inside of a shell.

#### Their Story

Orrin Wake was born on Mile Nineteen in 602, when the Nineteenth was still the Fourteenth. He fished the licensed boats at the pier's end, hauling up what the Mile calls catches: fish with too many eyelids, flatfish as big as a door that wept when landed, once a net of drowned dogs still wearing collars from inland towns. He married Marra Pell in 627 and they had two children, Hal and Dory, seven and five when he was Called.

He went on a pressure night in Lack, 639, after the net came up heavy with nothing but cold water that would not run out through the mesh. Marra had tied his knot well. The rope was cut with his own gutting knife, missing from the boat for a month. Thirty-one days later he came up the Nineteenth's harbor steps at dawn, out of the water rather than the mud, asked for his bucket, bailed eleven gallons, sat down on the top step and said, in two voices: *We should talk about the arm.*

It is not usual for the Tenanted to talk about themselves. It is unheard of for the thing inside to talk about the person it sits in, or to bargain. The Wreckwives took the case at once. The Drowned Bench sent a clerk, who listened for an afternoon and went home and was not able to describe what she had heard. The Admiralty made a note. The Passenger, for its part, has been unfailingly polite to all of them, and has said only that it needs a hand to do something with, and that it would rather not take one that was not offered.

For two years they have been negotiating. They have drawn up terms on a slate. Orrin has given up the arm by night, from dusk bailing to dawn bailing, in exchange for the Passenger's promise that it will not use the arm on any living Wake, and will answer one question a day, truthfully, about anything Orrin asks. The thumb is still under discussion. So is Tablenight.

#### Their Place in the Land

Orrin and the Passenger are a curiosity, a scandal and a test case. The Lantern's owner lets them sit in the corner because people come to hear them and buy beer while they do. The Wreckwives watch them as a possible answer to the question at the heart of their sisterhood: if the person is still in there, and can argue, then perhaps the person can win. The Deepening watches them as proof that the Tenant is benign, reasonable, a guest who asks before it borrows. Mother Limpet has sent them a lantern. Cutwright Ansby Wrack has asked to examine them and been refused, courteously, by the lower voice.

Orrin cannot crew a licensed boat, because no captain will sail with a Tenanted; they salvage the near Uncovered for bread, and the Passenger's half of the body can lift a cart. Marra still shares his bed, roped to him by her own knot. The children call the other one Grandda, and the Passenger has asked them not to, gently, every day, for two years.

#### What They Carry

- **The slate** — a schoolroom slate framed in driftwood, written over on both sides in two hands: Orrin's square fisherman's capitals and a fine, slanted, antique script that no one in Lowmark has written for four hundred years. The terms. Clauses struck out, re-written, initialed by both. A Clausewright reading it (Eye + Clause vs Hard 14) finds it the best-drafted contract they have ever seen, and finds that every Exit in it belongs to the Passenger.
- **A slate-pencil** — Orrin's. The Passenger writes with its finger, wet, and the writing dries in salt.
- **A mended net** — he still carries a hand-net folded at his belt and mends it in the tavern. The left hand helps by day. At night it ties knots he does not recognize, in patterns that look like soundings.
- **Marra's bail-bucket** — the one she gave him on their wedding day, oak-staved, with *I will hold your line* burned into the rim. He bails into it at dawn. The Passenger bails into it at dusk, and the water is a different color.
- **A tally of days** — a stick notched for every day since his return. Orrin cuts the notches. Some mornings there are more than there should be.
- **A glove** — a single leather glove for the left hand, which Marra knitted a lining for. When the Passenger has the arm, the glove comes off by itself and is found folded on the table, neatly, like a napkin.
- **His gutting knife** — the one that cut the rope. He found it on the harbor steps when he came back, laid on the top step as if for him. He cannot throw it away. The Passenger has asked him to keep it.

#### Wants, Fears and Secrets

Orrin wants his life back, or enough of it to raise two children who are not afraid of him. He wants to be able to hold his daughter with both arms. He fears that the arm is only the first clause, and that the contract is a ladder; he fears more that he no longer minds. The Passenger wants a hand to do something with. It will not say what. It wants the contract witnessed by a stranger, a dry one, because, as it puts it, *every witness on this coast is already on one side of the water.*

**Secret (GM can reveal):** in the one-question-a-day bargain, Orrin asked, on the ninety-first day, *what is the sea for?* The Passenger told him. It took all night. Since then Orrin has begun to agree with it. What it told him, in the version Orrin will repeat if he trusts the PCs, is this: *The sea was not a home. It was a lid. The Fathom was the keeping, and the keeping is going home, and when it has all gone home what was kept under will be uncovered. Someone has to be at the table when it is. Someone has to have a hand.* Orrin believes this is a kindness. He believes the Passenger means to protect the coast. He may be right.

**Secret (they do not know, or one of them does not):** the left arm is for opening something at Cray Lobbett's stone table. Set into the slab at the eighth place there is a round stone lid, carved like the cover on a serving dish, and no human hand has been able to move it. The GM should decide what is under it: an empty dish, waiting; a dish already full; or a hollow that goes down further than the Trench. The Passenger knows. Orrin does not. Marra suspects, because the left hand has begun, at night, to draw circles on her back.

#### Ties

- **Marra Wake** — his wife, a Sister of the Line among the Wreckwives. She ties his knot every night and checks it twice and does not know which of them she is protecting.
- **Cray Lobbett** — the Passenger wants to be taken to Lobbett's table, and has offered Lobbett a price he cannot hear without shaking: *we will push the chair in for you.*
- **Ilse Marl** — the Passenger sings at the Low Water with the others; Orrin does not. Ilse has transcribed a line that only one voice in the ring sings, a counter-melody, and it comes from Orrin's mouth.
- **Justice Maren Oake** — the Passenger addresses her as an equal, and she, alone of anyone on the coast, answers it in her own lower voice. Their conversations have been recorded by a Bench clerk. The record is sealed.
- **Mother Limpet** — she sent a lantern and a blessing. The Passenger sent the lantern back with a note: *Not yet, Mother. And not you.*
- **Ansby Wrack** — refused. She wants to know whether Orrin's children carry higher Cut than they did before he went. They do.
- **Reeve Calder Stokes** — the first dry stranger the Passenger asked to witness the contract. Stokes, Oathen-trained, read the slate for an hour and declined, white-faced, and will not say why.

#### In Play

PCs meet them when the Passenger asks a stranger to witness the contract. It will choose a PC who has not yet bailed in Brinehollow, if one is present. Witnessing is easy: sign the slate, under both their marks. What it binds the witness to is the question. In Oathen a witness's word becomes a binding; on the coast it becomes a debt, and the Passenger keeps exact accounts.

- **Witness.** The Passenger asks a PC to sign. Orrin, in his own voice, asks them not to. Neither will explain while the other is listening, and they are always both listening.
- **The Thumb.** Orrin has lost the argument about the thumb. He asks the PCs to help him find something he can trade for it. The Passenger suggests a price: one night with the arm at Lobbett's table, with the PCs as escort, and the whole hand returned to Orrin forever after.
- **The Children's Grandda.** Dory Wake, seven, has started speaking in a lower voice at the morning count. The Passenger swears it is not its doing. For the first time in two years, it sounds afraid.

**Threat:** the Passenger stands, the floor creaks, and both voices say *please don't*; pressed, the arm can tear a door from a house. **Kindness:** Orrin weeps; the Passenger thanks the PC by names they never gave. **The god:** the Passenger calls the rest of it *the family.* **If nobody intervenes:** they settle the thumb at Low Water. At Tablenight the Passenger walks Orrin toward Lobbett's Drop with Marra's knot still round his waist and the cut end trailing. He comes back a week later with both arms hanging loose, calm, kind, in a single voice that is not Orrin's.

#### Who They Are

A short woman in a grey Assay coat with the badges cut off and the cuffs black with ink to the elbow. Her round spectacles are cracked across the left lens, and she peers over them like a heron deciding whether something in the water is a fish. She smells of oak gall ink, wet wool and lamp smoke. Her voice is quiet and flat, and her lips move constantly, even in company, as she counts.

She wears her gauging-chain as a belt: twenty fathoms of brass-linked surveyor's chain wound round and round her waist, so heavy that she walks with a slight backward lean, like a woman carrying a child in front of her. It is also her anchoring rope. She chains herself to the bed with it at night and locks it with an Assay padlock whose key she gives to whoever is sheltering her.

#### Their Story

Brenna was born in 600 on Mile Six, a cooper's daughter who could do sums before she could bail. She trained as a College copyist at twelve, was taken by the Salt Assay at sixteen, and rose from Gauger to Senior Assayer by 635, with a house on Mile One she never furnished.

She had kept a second ledger since 627, when she lost her younger brother Pell to the 1,140 Callings of the Great Pressure Day: the parish bail-token records, which count roughly every bailing on the coast; the Netwatch's Calling logs; the Wreckwives' records of the Tenanted; and the fall of the sea, measured at forty posts in fathoms and, by her own method, in volume.

In the Grace of 639 she laid the curves over one another on a single sheet of tracing paper in her Mile One house, by lamplight, and they matched. The volume of sea lost each year is the number of Tenanted returned that year, multiplied by a constant. Her constant, which the Assay clerks still whisper as *the Kelp*, is nine hundred and twelve thousand barrels per returned soul, and it holds, year on year, to within four parts in a thousand. Where it drifts, it drifts in the years when the Admiralty has retired more people to depth. The Called who never return do not lower the sea at all. The Tenanted do. The Seated more so. The ocean is moving into the people, a bucket at a time.

She presented the finding to the Admiralty's Board of Soundings in Plenty, 639. She was thanked. That night the Standing Orders were read from the deck of the *Steadfast*, declaring the publication, possession or recitation of *any arithmetic of the fall relating to persons* to be treason against the Articles. The Netwatch came for her at the second bell. She was not home. A Wreckwife had come at the first bell and taken her down the back steps and onto the Mile, and she has been moving between their safe houses ever since, fourteen of them in two years.

#### Their Place in the Land

Brenna is the most wanted woman in Brinehollow and one of the least visible. The Admiralty wants her silent, or dead, or ideally both. The Second Table wants her figures, because a sea that moves into bodies can be moved out of them by a knife. The Wreckwives protect her because her number is the first thing anyone has given them that explains what came home to their beds. The Deepening, having heard her finding read aloud on a procession night, revere her as a prophet who proved with arithmetic what they knew in their hearts. She finds this the most unbearable of all.

Her routine is a fugitive's: cellars, attics, the back rooms of mussel-canneries, never more than nine nights in one place. She works by night, on paper borrowed from her hosts, and goes on counting, because she cannot stop: the falling number goes on falling and someone must write it down. She pays her way by doing the household accounts of every family that hides her, which is how half the Wreckwives on the Mile come to have their books in better order than the Purser's Office.

Her finding has left the Assay half-purged. Its surviving clerks keep two ledgers each, and send her their figures folded into the bottoms of bail-buckets.

#### What They Carry

- **The gauging-chain** — twenty fathoms of brass-linked Assay chain, her belt, her rope and her weapon. It was issued to her at sixteen, and it is the one piece of Assay property she did not return.
- **Her spectacles** — cracked across the left lens on the night she fled, when she missed the bottom step in the dark. She sees the world slightly doubled on one side, and has come to think it suits the coast.
- **The arithmetic** — not a copy of the finding; the working. Thirty-one sheets of tracing paper, folded into a flat oilcloth packet sewn into the lining of her coat. Without the working, the three hidden copies are an assertion. With it, they are a proof any reckoner on the Table can check (Eye + Reckoning vs Hard 14, a day's work, and a Dread 2 check on finishing).
- **A glass jar with a screw lid** — her dawn bail-water, every day. She holds it to the morning light at the window of whatever cellar she is in and records the color in the margin of her figures, in a code nobody else reads. See below.
- **A string of forty bail-tokens** — her purse. She cannot carry lacks safely; a woman with silver on the slum Miles is noticed. Forty cups of sweet water is enough to buy a night's silence from most households.
- **An inkwell with a cork stopper and three steel nibs** — she will not use a quill. She says the quill was once alive and has opinions.
- **A false name** — Ness Gauge, cousin to whatever family is sheltering her this week. Everyone on the Mile knows a Ness Gauge. It is the Wreckwives' private joke.

#### Wants, Fears and Secrets

Brenna wants the number published before the sea is gone: not believed, which she has given up on, but published, printed, on every tide-post on the coast, so that nobody can ever again say they were not told. She wants this more than she wants to live. She fears that the Tenanted already know her number, and are counting down to something; she has noticed that every Tenanted judge on the Bench voted the same way in every case this year, and she has begun, in the margin, to count their unanimities as if they were tallies.

**The three copies:** one is with Father Ebb, sealed in tarred canvas and chained to his ledge on the Hanging Abbey. One is in a bail-bucket that has passed through four sweethearts' hands, from wedding to wedding, the paper rolled inside the double base; she does not know whose bucket it is now. One has already been read aloud, on a procession night at Gullcry Strand, by a Deepening Lantern-Bearer who wept with joy; it is now in Mother Limpet's keeping, and it is the copy Brenna most wants back.

**Secret (GM can reveal):** her own bail-water has gone blue. Not green, as at the Appetite: blue, deep and heavy, the color Tolly Brack calls *soon*. She has known for six weeks. She has told no one. She chains herself to every bed she sleeps in, and every morning she checks the padlock before she opens her eyes, and every morning it is still locked, and every morning her ankles are wet.

**Secret (she does not know):** the bucket with the second copy has passed into the hands of a Tenanted bride. The Drowned Bench read Brenna's finding six months ago. They were not surprised. Justice Maren Oake wrote one line in the margin, in the antique hand the Tenanted share, and rolled the paper back into the bucket's base: *The figure is correct. The constant will change.*

#### Ties

- **The Wreckwives** — her keepers, her readers, her landladies. She owes them her life fourteen times over.
- **Father Ebb** — keeper of the first copy. She climbed to his ledge in 639 in a single day, which no fugitive should be able to do, and he gave her bread and asked her only whether the number was kind. She said no.
- **High Admiral Corvin Sund** — the warrant is in his hand. She has never met him. She has counted his ships.
- **Reeve Calder Stokes** — the Company auditor has her number on his own pages, arrived at by tolls instead of buckets. He is desperate to meet her. She is terrified of what a Company man will do with a second proof.
- **Commodore Lysander Vell** — he has sent word through three cutouts that the Admiralty will pardon her in exchange for the working. He does not represent the Admiralty.
- **Tolly Brack** — the bailer boy taught her the colors before she had a name for them. She owes him an apology she cannot afford to make.
- **Dry Jonah** — his barrels are a line in her figures: the only sea leaving Brinehollow eastward by road. She suspects it is not leaving at all.
- **Finn Undertow** — his body-tide bell has been the one public measure that agrees with her. She has never spoken to him. She sets her count by him.

#### In Play

- **Three Copies.** As in the core book. Brenna will pay the PCs everything she has (forty bail-tokens and a promise) to recover the copy in Mother Limpet's keeping. Limpet will give it up only if the PCs walk the shell road to the last lantern and come back.

### Mother Limpet — Mother of the Water

> "Hush now. Hold my hand. I'll take you to the edge, and you'll go on, and I'll come after when the last of you is safe. That's the promise. I've never broken it."
>> — Mother Limpet, at the last lantern on Gullcry Strand

[stat: Mother Limpet — Mother of the Water]
Land & Cut: Brinehollow · Cut 2 · Regrowth 9 (the Course) · Hunger 3
Age & Station: 84; Mother of the Water, head of the Deepening
Calling & Standing: Celebrant · Standing 5 (Prophet)

#### Who They Are

She is very small, bent nearly double, and she clicks when she walks: her dress is sewn with thousands of white shell beads, each one given her by a Shell-Walker after their first procession. Her face is creased and soft, her mouth toothless, her eyes the Course's wide wet black. Her voice is like getting into a bath in Lack. She smells of seaweed, lamp-oil and camphor. Her chest does not move when she sits. People who come to argue with her find themselves holding her hand.

#### Their Story

Limpet was born Aulda Fenwick in 557 on the old shore below Gullcry, a net-mender's girl, in the last generation that grew up with the sea at the end of the street. She married at nineteen and buried no one: her husband, Corran, was Called in 584, and her only daughter, Sorrel, in 601, at seventeen, on the night after a pressure day. Sorrel's rope was cut from the inside of the knot. Limpet found the knife under her own pillow.

She does not remember cutting it. She went to the Deepening that winter to be near the people who went where Sorrel had gone, walked the shell road, took her new name, and found she had a gift for holding the hands of the frightened. By 620 she was a Deep Sister. In 628 the old Mother of the Water walked in and did not come back, and the Deepening gave Limpet the shell dress. She has walked two thousand to the edge since, holding each hand until the water reached her knees, and then letting go, singing.

#### Their Place in the Land

The Deepening's processions at Gullcry Strand are illegal and regular, and they happen because of her. The Netwatch seldom intervene, because Limpet sends bread and a lantern to the Mile Seven barracks before every procession, with a note naming the date. Families of the baptized come to her for vengeance and get her hand to hold instead. The Anchorites call her the Drowner. The Admiralty has signed her arrest three times and three times failed to find a tiny woman in a clicking dress who lives openly in a chapel on the Strand.

She is the Deepening. Its Lantern-Bearers know the Calling nights; its Deep Sisters hear the invitation; she decides who walks when, and in what order, and she has never once sent a person who was not already pulled.

#### What They Carry

- **The shell-bead dress** — some eight thousand beads, sewn on by her flock over fifty years. On procession nights the beads go cold, all at once, and frost over.
- **The first lantern** — tin and horn, the one she carried on her own first walk in 601, mended a hundred times. She carries it on every procession and sets it on the last hook herself.
- **The rope-box** — a sea-chest holding a hand's length of rope from each of the two thousand, labeled in her tiny hand. She says she will carry it in with her. It is far too heavy for her to lift.
- **Brenna Kelp's third copy** — rolled in oilcloth, tied with a white ribbon. She reads it aloud to the dying.
- **A tortoiseshell comb** — Sorrel's. She combs every baptized person's hair before the edge, so they will go in tidy.
- **A cup of sweet water** — she offers it to everyone at the last lantern. Those who drink stay ashore that night. Those who refuse go on. She says she has never told anyone which to choose. That is true.

#### Wants, Fears and Secrets

She wants everyone home. She wants to follow, when the last one is safely under, and to find her husband and her daughter waiting. She fears nothing, she says. She fears, actually, the silence at the moment she lets go of a hand, because she listens into it every time and hears only surf.

**Secret (GM can reveal):** she cannot hear the invitation. She never could. In fifty-seven years she has never once felt the pull, though her body has gone deep into the Course; the god is in her flesh and not in her ear. She performs the Want at dusk, standing at the chapel's west window with her palms on the glass, because a Mother of the Water must. She has led two thousand people toward a voice she has only ever been told about, and she does not know whether she is a saint or the thing that comes before one.

**Secret (she does not know):** Sorrel came back. In 601, eleven weeks after her Calling, a Tenanted girl walked out of the surf below Gullcry, calm and courteous, and went not home but to the Deepening, where she took a new name, as all Shell-Walkers do. She is fifty-seven now, grey and slow, a Deep Sister called Sister Sounding, and she has walked at her mother's left hand on every procession for thirty years and held the lantern while Limpet sang. Limpet does not recognize her. Sister Sounding has never told her. The Wreckwives' records know.

#### Ties

- **Sister Sounding** — her right hand in the Deepening, her daughter in fact. See above.
- **Father Ebb** — her opposite; they were sweethearts at fifteen and exchanged buckets once, before Corran. He prays for her by name every night. She prays for him to let go.
- **Lieutenant Sable Reef** — Reef has dragged forty of Limpet's baptized off the shell road. Limpet sends her bread anyway, and once, a shell bead, which Reef keeps.
- **Brenna Kelp** — Limpet holds her copy. She thinks Brenna is the Deepening's truest prophet. Brenna thinks Limpet is a murderer with a nice voice.
- **Orrin Wake and Passenger** — the Passenger sent back her lantern: *Not yet, Mother. And not you.* She has not understood the second sentence. It frightens her more than anything has in fifty years.
- **Abel Drainer** — his Wringers have twice tried to burn her chapel. She visits his converts at the mangle and holds their hands too.

#### In Play

PCs meet her on a procession night, holding out a lantern, or when they come to drag someone back from the shell road.

- **The Copy.** She will give Brenna Kelp's copy only to someone who walks the road to the last lantern and comes back. She means it as a kindness.
- **Who Is Sounding?** A Wreckwife asks the PCs to tell Limpet the truth about her daughter, or to make sure she never learns it. Either way, Sister Sounding asks to meet them first.
- **The Deaf Saint.** A PC at the Appetite feels the Low Song pull while Limpet sings, and sees, in that moment, that she does not. She sees them see.

**Threat:** she sings. **Kindness:** she takes the PC's hand and tells them about Sorrel. **The god:** she speaks of it with total faith and no experience. **If nobody intervenes:** on the Low Water night she leads the largest procession in the Deepening's history, three hundred lanterns. At the end of the road she lets go of the last hand and stands in the water to her knees, listening, and hears nothing, and turns around. Sister Sounding walks on alone. Limpet is found at dawn on Gullcry Strand, sitting on the rope-box, still holding out the cup.

#### Who They Are

She is enormous across the shoulders and short in the leg, with forearms burned in a hundred small white stars where rivets have spat. Her face is broad, red, and permanently sooty around the eyes; her grey hair is tied up in a rag. Thirty years of riveting have deafened her nearly stone-deaf, and she shouts everything, kindly, at a pitch that makes the gulls lift. She smells of hot iron, pitch and burnt hair. She reads lips well and pretends not to when she does not like what is said.

#### Their Story

Dagna was born in 586 at Keelrow, a shipwright's daughter, two years before the last warship floated. She learned riveting on the propped hulls of the Dry Fleet, mending ships that would never sail, and hated it. In 620 she left the Admiralty yards and set up at the end of the Mile, building the flat-bottomed boats that fish the last harbor. In 636, after a pressure day burst every port-glass on the Nineteenth, she stood on the pier edge, looked out at water that was lower than it had been at breakfast, and decided to stop chasing the sea and follow it.

The *Follow* is a riveted iron egg forty feet long, with eight glass ports, a weighted keel, and a great tarred air-bladder that can be filled from bellows at the surface and let out, by a valve, to bring the vessel up. It lies in her slip at the end of Mile Nineteen, under canvas, nearly finished. It has been nearly finished for a year.

#### Their Place in the Land

Officially the *Follow* is an Admiralty project, a sounding vessel to survey the Trench for the fleet's return. The funding came through Commodore Lysander Vell. Unofficially it is the most talked-about object on the coast. Nine hundred people have put their names on her volunteer list, written on the wall of her shed in chalk; the Mile bets on who will be chosen. She employs forty riveters and smiths, most of them men from the slum Miles who have nothing else, and she pays them in Admiralty lacks and in the promise of a place aboard, which she has never made.

#### What They Carry

- **Her riveting hammer** — two pounds of steel on an ash handle worn to the shape of her grip, with her mark, a D inside a hull, stamped on the face. Every rivet she has driven bears that mark.
- **Tallow ear-plugs** — she wears them out of habit, and because on certain nights the iron of the *Follow* hums.
- **The list** — a copy, in a book, of the nine hundred names, with notes. *Wants to go. Wants to go. Wants to go. Wants to know.* There are four of the last.
- **The *Follow*'s plans** — rolled in a tarred tube, with Vell's annotations in a neat red hand: room for six, and a cargo hold she did not draw.
- **A slate** — for people who will not shout back.
- **A tin of rivets** — she counts them each morning. The count is the first thing she checks.

#### Wants, Fears and Secrets

She wants to know where the water goes, and to come back and tell everyone. She fears that the nine hundred do not want to know; they want to go. She fears that the *Follow* will be a procession with a hull.

**Secret (GM can reveal):** someone keeps loosening the *Follow*'s rivets, a dozen a week, always on the lowest seam, and the marks on the loosened rivets are the marks of her own hammer, struck backward. She has begun to wonder if she does it herself in her sleep. She chains her ankle to the slip's ring at night and sleeps on the shed floor. The rivets are still loosened.

**Secret (she does not know):** the saboteur is Hollis Brine, her apprentice and the nearest thing to a son she has, a volunteer whose name is first on the wall. He takes her hammer from beside her sleeping hand because his own will not strike the vessel. He listened, once, through Nerys Colm's door at the Netwatch barracks, and he knows what the invitation says about the deep places. He loosens the rivets weeping, because he wants to go more than he has ever wanted anything, and because he knows she will pilot it, and he will not let her go down to that.

#### Ties

- **Hollis Brine** — her apprentice, her saboteur. See above.
- **Commodore Lysander Vell** — her patron. She dislikes him and needs his iron. She has not asked what the hold is for.
- **Harbormistress Kessa Drowle** — her oldest friend; they drink together on the pier end and compare the years they each have left. Kessa's pilings and Dagna's egg are two answers to the same question.
- **Nerys Colm** — Dagna wrote to her, asking her to describe the bottom. Nerys's reply was a single word: *Don't.*
- **Hask Tarn** — she wants him as pilot for the trial descent. He will not go near the Trench.
- **Mother Limpet** — four hundred of Dagna's nine hundred are Deepening. Limpet has offered to bless the vessel. Dagna has not answered, because she has not decided whether that would be a disaster.

#### In Play

PCs meet her when she hires a crew: guards for the slip, divers for the trial, a pilot, or someone to find out who is loosening the rivets.

- **The Follow's Pilot.** As in the core book: she needs someone to lead the trial descent at the Trench edge, and someone to find the saboteur, and to learn why the saboteur weeps.
- **The Hold.** The PCs see Vell's plans, and the hold she did not draw, with hooks.
- **The Hum.** On a pressure night the *Follow*'s iron hums a note, and all nine hundred volunteers come to the slip at once, in silence, and stand there.

**Threat:** she swings the hammer and shouts. **Kindness:** she gives the PC a place on the list, which is the kindest and worst thing she owns. **The god:** she cannot hear it. She is beginning to think that is why she is the one building the boat. **If nobody intervenes:** the *Follow* makes its trial descent at Low Water 642 with Dagna, Hollis and four of Vell's men aboard. The air-bladder rises without them, its valve closed by hand from inside.

#### Who They Are

Lean as a dried eel and the same color, with salt-reddened eyes that water constantly and hands scarred white in crosshatches by weed-knives. He drinks, steadily, and talks in a hoarse rapid mutter, and he draws eight squares on any wet surface, around a long rectangle, and touches the eighth over and over. His oilskin is cracked and stiff with old salt. He smells of the far Uncovered: mud, rot, and something sweet underneath, like a pantry.

#### Their Story

Cray was born in 601 on Mile Eleven to a family of Scraplings who bailed thin and coughed little. He went out on the Uncovered at twelve and never really came back. By thirty he had a crew of four and a claim in the Wreck Orchard. In Plenty, 639, following a dead reef three days west, he came down a shelf into a basin where the mud was oddly dry and found the table: a single slab of grey stone eleven paces long, older than any carving on the coast, with eight heavy stone chairs, seven pushed in neat against it, one pulled out. On its seat was a fine dust like ground shell, and an impression, as if someone had just stood up.

His crew went back with him twice. On the third trip two of them did not come back with him, and he will not say what happened; the fourth quit and drinks in Saltwick and will not speak his name. Cray has been back nine times alone. Each time, the table is cleaner. Each time, by his cord, the eighth chair is pulled out a little further toward wherever he stands. Three salvage crews looking for Lobbett's Drop have not returned.

#### Their Place in the Land

He is a joke on the Mile and a legend on the Uncovered. He has tried to sell the location to Mags Weir, to the Admiralty, to the College, and to a buyer who paid in Company platters, and each time, at the moment of telling, his mouth closes and he says nothing at all. Everyone who wants the table, which by now is a remarkable number of people, wants Cray, and he cannot give it to them. He can only take them.

#### What They Carry

- **Rubbings** — charcoal on oilcloth of the carvings along the table's edge: eight places, eight figures; seven chiseled smooth by time, one chiseled smooth by tools.
- **A twist of dust** — from the eighth seat. It never dries.
- **His weed-knife and salvage gaff** — the tools of a trade he no longer practices.
- **A cup** — see below.

He wants to be rid of it. He fears that the next time the eighth chair will be pushed in, or that a ninth will be pulled out with his name scratched in the stone. **Secret (GM can reveal):** on his last trip there was a cup on the table, at the eighth place, full. Pewter, plain, brimming with something clear and warm that smelled of bread and the deep sea. He did not drink. He took the cup instead, poured out, and it is in his coat. Each morning it is full again. **Secret (he does not know):** the chair is not coming toward him. It is being turned. Each visit, it has been angled a little more away from the table and toward the place he stands, the way a host turns a chair for a guest arriving late.

#### Ties

- **Hask Tarn** — the only pilot who knows the ground; he steered over the table in 581 when it was under six fathoms, and the lead came up warm. They have never spoken of it. They drink in the same tavern.
- **Gannet Shale** — she has seen the tall figure setting a table on the mud. Cray thinks it was his. It was not.
- **Orrin Wake and Passenger** — the Passenger has offered to push the chair in for him. He does not know if that would save him or seat someone else.
- **Ilse Marl** — her chart leads to his table. She has not yet realized whose it is.

#### Who They Are

A bundle of rags heavy and black with water, a dripping tangle of grey beard, and under it a mild, round, smiling face with Tenanted black eyes filmed like the bloom on a sloe. It rains on him indoors, a soft private drizzle from an inch below the ceiling. He speaks slowly and cheerfully, in an accent four centuries out of date, and calls everyone *duck*. Puddles follow him like dogs. He is heavier than three men; the Headland steps are worn into a dip where he sits.

#### Their Story

Tomas Lugg was a net-mender of the old harbor, twenty-nine years old, on the licked-clean night of 118 A.G. when four hundred people of Lowmark rose in their sleep and walked into the harbor in a long, quiet, orderly line, holding hands. He walked holding the hands of his wife Bethia and his two daughters, Annet and little Wenna, who was four. Eleven came back. Tom was one. Bethia and the girls were not.

He came home to a house that had been given to his brother. He was one of the first Tenanted, and nobody then knew what to call him, and nobody wanted him. He sat down on the Headland steps to wait for his wife and children to come back out of the sea as he had, and he has been waiting there since. The Headland has been rebuilt around him three times. The Admiralty tried to move him in 240, in 388 and in 512, and each time he became too heavy to lift and the men who tried bled from the ears. The other ten of the eleven are long gone, Seated or retired to depth. Tom is the oldest Tenanted alive, if alive is the word, and he has never once been in a hurry.

#### Their Place in the Land

He is a fixture, a superstition and a public convenience. Admiralty officers touch his bowl for luck on their way up to the Sounding Hall. Children dare each other to look into his puddles. The rumor that whoever gives him a coin will never be Called is half true: they will be Called to Tom, and will walk not west but to the Headland steps, and sit beside him, dripping and calm, until dawn, and then get up and go home. The Netwatch know this. On bad nights Lieutenant Reef has led Called folk to him on purpose. Some families pay their crumb every week.

#### What They Carry

- **A blanket** — sodden sealskin, the hair long since gone. It weighs as much as a man.
- **Three spoons** — horn, worn thin. He sets them in a row at dusk on the step beside him.
- **A splinter of the first tide-post** — the post on which the four hundred names of 118 were cut. He pulled it from the post when they took it down, and he can still read his wife's name on it with his thumb.
- **A rope end** — a short length of rotted hemp. It was not cut. It was untied, by hands, from the other end.

#### Wants, Fears and Secrets

He wants his family to come for him. He fears nothing he can name. **Secret (GM can reveal):** the rope end is the thing he has never told anyone. On the night of the walk it was Tom who untied the girls from his wrist, because Wenna was crying and pulling to go on, and he could not bear it. **Secret (he does not know):** Bethia, Annet and Wenna did come back, long ago, in 119, as Tenanted, and walked home past him on the steps, and he did not know them. The water had come up between them. They are retired to depth beneath Fathomhouse now, in the lowest cisterns, three hundred feet beneath where he sits, and on quiet nights when the rain on him stops for a moment, it is because they are listening up through the stone.

[stat: Ottiline Hawse — the Ropewalker]

#### Who They Are

Broad, slow, with hands black with tar to the wrist and the smell of hemp and pine on her like a second skin. She walks the quarter-mile shed of the Ropewalk backward all day, paying out yarn from her waist, and talks over her shoulder.

#### Their Story

Born in 591 in the Ropewalk, she inherited it in 622. Every anchoring rope in Lowmark passes through her shed. After the rope funeral of her own sister in 624, she asked to see the cut end, and saw what it told. She has kept a ledger of every cut rope brought back since: the blade, the angle, the strength of the hand.

#### Their Place in the Land

She knows which ropes were cut by the sleeper, which by the beloved, and which by no hand at all. Families come to her to learn who cut. She tells nothing unless they ask twice. The Netwatch consults her. The Bench has never asked.

#### What They Carry

- **The Cut Ledger** — seventeen years of rope ends, sketched and classified. Four columns: Sleeper, Beloved, Stranger, None.
- **A rope-knife** — hooked, short; she judges others' blades by it.
- **A lens** — for fibers.
- **A tar-pot** — always warm.
- **A folded slip** — inside the ledger's back cover, a name.

#### Wants, Fears and Secrets

She wants the cuts to stop. She fears the column headed None, which grows. **Secret (GM can reveal):** forty of the ropes she sold last year were cut by the same small blade, angled from below, and she knows the child who carries it. The name on the slip is the GM's choice. One suggestion: a boy of Ropery Row who, on the morning his grandfather's rope was found cut, stood in the doorway holding the old man's bucket. **Secret (she does not know):** the child is not acting alone. Someone gentle taught him how, and told him that the ones on the beds were asking to be let go.

#### Ties

- **Dulse Ammery** — the hand that taught. Ottiline suspects nothing.
- **Lieutenant Sable Reef** — brings her rope ends at dawn.
- **Esk Penhallow** — he bought a new rope and asked her to check his knot. She did. It was perfect.

#### In Play

- **Ask Twice.** A family needs to know who cut. The answer is Beloved.
- **The Slip.** The ledger is stolen, and with it the name.
- **None.** A rope brought in shows no cut at all. It has been untied, from the far end.

**Threat:** she throws tar. **Kindness:** she gives a PC a rope that will not cut. **If nobody intervenes:** she burns the slip and goes on.

"Best rope on the coast. Nobody wants to be the one buying it." — a Ropewalk hand
"She reads a knot the way I read a verdict." — Justice Maren Oake

### The Follow and Its Hold

Dagna Hull is building a ship to come back. Lysander Vell is paying for a ship that carries knives down. Hollis Brine is sabotaging it with Dagna's own hammer because he heard Nerys Colm's invitation and knows there is no coming back. Any one of them exposed breaks the other two. If the PCs reveal the hold, Dagna will strike Vell; if they reveal Hollis, she will forgive him and be ruined; if they do nothing, all three go down together at the Low Water of 642, and what Vell carries to the bottom of the Trench is the Second Table's first course.

### The Number Twice

Brenna Kelp has a proof by buckets. Calder Stokes has the same proof by tolls, and the Company had it first. The Admiralty will kill to keep one proof quiet; two proofs make a fact, and a fact can be posted on tide-posts. Stokes's contract obliges him to carry the figure to Tacit, where the Company will price the coast and sell the price to the Second Table. Brenna wants it published on the coast. Neither knows the Bench has already read her copy. Putting them in one cellar is the most dangerous meeting in Brinehollow.

### The Hand, the Chair and the Chart

The Passenger wants a hand at the eighth place. Cray Lobbett has three visits left before he sits. Ilse Marl's chart now runs from the table toward the Headland. Finn Undertow's count lands on the same Low Water. Each of them holds a piece; none of them is speaking to the others. Whoever brings them together brings the table to the coast, or the coast to the table.
