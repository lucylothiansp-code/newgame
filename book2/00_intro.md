## Before the Second Course

> Six peoples ate. One refused. Every one of them is still chewing.
>> — Rim Road saying

[fiction]
**From a letter found in the satchel of a Cutwrights' College surveyor, sealed but never sent.**
Sister, you asked me what the lands are *like*, and I keep failing to tell you, because the honest answer is that they are like people. Not like nations. Not like maps. Like people at a long table after a meal that went on too long: one has fallen asleep with her eyes open, one is still eating, one is weeping into the soup, one keeps promising to leave and cannot stand, one is giving birth under the table, one has died and will not stop talking, and one stands by the door with his coat on, refusing everything, insufferable, starving, right.
I have spent eleven years writing down their names. Every name I write I have come to think of as a kind of plate: something with a portion on it. Something that will be eaten.
I am enclosing the ledger. Do not open it at the table.
[/fiction]

*The Long Grace* core book gave you the Table: its history, its seven lands, its rules, its market and its monsters. This book is the second course. It is about **the people**, and about **what is left of the gods when someone takes a knife to them**.

## What This Book Contains

**Part One: The Seven Tables** returns to each land and goes down to street level. Every land opens with a deep-dive into how it actually works day to day: its districts and villages, roads and wages, courts and punishments, shrines and hymns, the voices of its ordinary people, and a map of who holds power over whom. Then comes the heart of the book: **every named character of every land**, written in full. Each dossier gives a complete stat block, a quote in the character's own voice, who they are and how they smell and sound, their story, how they function inside their land's institutions, **everything they carry**, their wants and fears and secrets, their ties to the rest of the cast, how to use them in play, and what other people say about them behind their backs. Each land closes with its newer faces, a web of its relationships, and a table for deciding who comes knocking.

**Part Two: Leavings** is a catalogue of relics. On the Table, a god is not only a power in the blood; it is meat, bone, breath, silence, salt and gristle, and people have been cutting pieces off it for six hundred and forty-one years. The Leavings chapters describe what can be taken from the gods, from the godsign-touched, from the horrors and places of each land, and from the Host's tables: how to have heard of it, how to carve it, how long it keeps, how badly it taints, what it does, and what it is worth. A short rules chapter, *The Carving Knife*, explains how harvesting works.

## Using the Dossiers

The dossiers are written to be used at the table, not admired on the shelf. Every one of them answers four questions a Carver needs mid-session:

- **What do they look, sound and smell like?** Read the first paragraph of *Who They Are* aloud.
- **What do they want from the Guests?** See *In Play*.
- **What happens if they fight?** The stat block uses the core-book rules exactly: 2d10 + Attribute + Skill, Teeth for Partaking, Flesh, Guard, Armor, Dread.
- **What happens if nobody intervenes?** Every dossier ends its *In Play* section with the character's fate if the Guests do nothing. These are the clocks of the Table. Use them.

Not every secret printed here is true in your campaign. Where the book offers options, choose one, and let the others become rumours.

## A Reminder About Content

This is a book about people being eaten by their gods and each other, in detail. Everything in the core book's content warning applies here, and more intimately, because these are faces the Guests will come to know. Keep using Lines, Veils and the Grace. Suicide and self-harm in these pages are tragedies, written from the side of the people left behind; nothing here is instruction. Sexual taboo, cursed bloodlines and desecration of the dead remain implied and adult. Children appear in these pages, as they appear on the Table; their suffering is shown through the adults who fail them, and never lingered on.

[box: Reading a Dossier Stat Block]
- **Land & Cut:** the character's land, bloodline strength (0–5), Regrowth (0–12) and current Hunger (0–10).
- **Calling & Standing:** the nearest core-book Calling and their rank in it (1–5), so you can see what their institution will do for them.
- **Attributes / Skills:** used exactly as for Guests. Threat-rated minor figures appear as one-line quick stats.
- **Flesh · Guard · Armor · Fray:** physical durability, the number to hit them, damage reduction, and how near they are to Breaking.
- **Gifts / Wants:** the god's powers in them and the compulsions that come with those powers.
- **Carries:** a summary; *What They Carry* gives the details, including items from the Rim Market and Leavings.
- **Dread:** the Dread rating of encountering them at their worst.
[/box]
