# The Carving Knife: The Taking of Leavings

> We ate them once because we had to. We go on eating them because we have learned what the pieces do.
>> — Mother Gall, pickler, to a customer

[fiction]
**Testimony of a Gleaner taken by the Mortuary Court, Lastgate, 640 A.G. The clerk notes that the witness smelled of vinegar and would not sit down.**
You want to know how it is done. It is done like butchery, because it is butchery. You need a cold room or a cold night, a good blade, a bucket, salt, and a friend who can hold a lamp without looking. You need to know what you are cutting for: a finger is not a finger, sir, a Hand-line finger is a key, a Hand-line finger is a death, and if you cut it at the wrong joint you have a grey dead stick and nothing more. You need to know how long you have. Some things go off in a breath. Some things never go off, and those are worse.
And you need to know what it costs you. I have carved forty-one leavings in my life. I have sold all but one. The one I kept is in my mouth, under my tongue. I cannot take it out. That is not a figure of speech.
No, I will not show you. Ask the next question.
[/fiction]

A god is a body. When six peoples ate their Providers on the Night of Seven Tables, they did not eat all of it: there were bones that would not crack, organs that would not cook, blood that would not clot, parts that were hidden, stolen, buried or kept. And the gods have been growing back ever since, in their eaters' bloodlines, soil, weather and dead, which means that every land on the Table is full of god again: in the hands of its nobles, the mouths of its Laden, the lungs of its Tenanted, the eyes of its sleepers, the cords of its Uncut, the tongues of its Sayers, and the crumbs on the Host's tables.

The people of the Table call the things that can be taken from all this **leavings**: what is left on the plate. This chapter explains how leavings are known, carved, kept and used. The catalogues that follow describe them land by land.

[bigquote] Everything on the Table is food, or was. Everything on the Table is a god, or will be. | Cutwrights' College, first lecture

## The Anatomy of a Leaving

Every leaving in the catalogues is described with six entries.

- **WHISPERED:** how hard it is to have heard of the leaving: what it is, where it comes from, what it does. A character rolls **Eye + Lore** (or another pair the Carver allows: Eye + Reckoning for a Seamer's anatomy, Caul + Rites for a priest's legend, Tongue + Haggle for a fence's gossip) against the listed difficulty. Success means they know of it; a Helping reveals its Taint; a Grace reveals one false belief that people hold about it. Failure means they know only rumours, which the Carver may make false.
- **SPOILAGE:** how long the leaving stays potent once taken from its source, and what keeps it. A spoiled leaving is only meat, bone or water, though often it is still Tainted.
- **CARVING:** how the leaving is taken: from what, or whom, with which tools, by which roll, at what risk. Carving is described below.
- **TAINT:** the residue of the god the leaving carries, using the Taint rules of the Rim Market chapter (Hunger, Regrowth or Dread, rated 1–5).
- **EFFECT:** what the leaving does, in the rules.
- **WORTH:** its price in lacks (if it is sold at all), its availability rating, where it changes hands and whether it is legal.

## Knowing

Leavings are surrounded by more lies than any other trade on the Table. Every second stall on the Rim Road sells a "Hand-line knuckle" that is a pig's; every inn has a man who knows a man who has a bottle of Hush. The WHISPERED roll protects the Guests from the obvious frauds, but the Carver should be generous with rumours and stingy with certainty. A leaving's true effect is often learned only by using it, and some of the catalogue's effects are meant to be discovered that way, at the worst moment.

Identifying a specific item in hand (is this the real thing?) uses the Rim Market rules: **Eye + Lore**, Hard (14) to identify a Taint's kind and rating, Grim (18) to identify which god. Cutwrights, Seamers, Renderers and Midwives may substitute their Calling's signature skill when the leaving falls within their trade.

## Carving

Carving a leaving is a **Long Task** with a single climactic roll. The catalogue entry gives:

- **The source.** A living person, a corpse, a horror, a place, a table, an object.
- **The tools.** A carving kit (Seamer's or Renderer's) is assumed. Some leavings need special tools: lead jars, brine-casks, gold wire, a Waker's needle, a lit candle, silence. Without them the Difficulty rises by one step or the carving is impossible.
- **The roll.** Usually **Hand + Stitching** (surgery and fine cutting) or **Hand + Blades** (butchery), against the listed Difficulty. Other pairs appear where they fit: Caul + Rites to take something holy without waking it, Eye + Reckoning to bottle a place, Tongue + Clause to take a word.
- **The Dread.** Almost every carving calls for a Dread check before the roll: the Carver should describe, fully, what the carver is doing with their hands. Failing the Dread check imposes −2 on the carving roll.
- **Helpings.** Each Helping on a carving roll either doubles the yield (two doses, a larger piece) or extends the Spoilage by one step (minutes to hours, hours to days, days to a season).
- **Grace.** A perfect cut: the leaving is taken whole, with maximum yield and no backlash, and the carver gains a Portion for their mastery.
- **Lack.** The carving goes wrong in the way the entry describes. If no backlash is listed, the leaving is ruined and the carver suffers the item's Taint as though they had used it.

### Carving From the Living

Many of the most potent leavings must be taken from people who are still alive: a Kept limb, a Gut-line's marbled fat, a Lung-line's breath, an Eye-line's lash, a Caul-line's veil, a Tongue-line's word. A willing donor makes a carving one step easier, and the Table has a long and terrible tradition of willing donors: debtors, the desperate, the devout, mothers selling for their children. An unwilling donor must be held or drugged, and the act is a **Dread 3** horror for the carver (Dread 4 if the donor is someone they know; kin-carving is Dread 4 and counts as the act a character would not do, if that is their line). Carving from the living deals damage to the donor as listed, often a Mangling result chosen rather than rolled.

Carving the living is murder or mutilation under the law of every land, save where it is licensed: Sallowreach licenses part-sales by the Kept themselves, the Fatlands licenses the render of the dead, and the Attendance of Cradlewrack claims all Parts. Everywhere else, it is a hanging.

### Carving the Dead and the Desecrated

Carving a corpse is grave desecration in every land but the Fatlands. It is easier (one step) and usually less potent. In Sallowreach, of course, the dead feel everything, and will remember who did it.

Grave-oaths sworn over a body that has been carved bind twice as hard (see Oaths on the Dead in the core book). Many carvers swear such an oath before they begin, to make the leaving stronger: *On your name, I will use what I take from you well.* Some leavings in the catalogues require it.

### Carving Places

Some leavings are taken not from bodies but from places where a god is close: a jar of Hush, a skin of the Trench's water, a breath of the long room, a handful of crowning-clay, a stone from Sworn Gorge, a crumb from a laid table. Carving a place usually means **Eye + Reckoning** (measuring, sealing, timing) or **Caul + Godsign** (sensing where the god is thin), and always means being in the place long enough to suffer it: each attempt costs the carver the place's Hunger, Fray or Dread as described in the land chapters.

## Keeping: Spoilage and Preservation

Leavings spoil. The god wants to go home, and a leaving taken away from its source begins at once to lose its grip. Spoilage is listed in steps:

| Spoilage | Lasts | Common Preservation |
| Breath | a few heartbeats; must be used on the spot | none possible |
| Minutes | about ten minutes | salt and darkness extend to an hour |
| Hours | a day at most | brine, lead, cold extend to a week |
| Days | about a week | wax, pickling, Mother Gall's brine extend to a season |
| Seasons | a season | sealed lead and Hush-wax extend to a year |
| Never | it does not spoil | nothing makes it less potent |

Each land has its own preservation craft. Sallowreach preserves in vinegar, lacquer and the weighted dark of the Sump; nothing spoils there, which is why its leavings fetch more abroad. The Fatlands salt, smoke and render. Brinehollow keeps things wet, in brine taken from its own people's lungs. The Vigil keeps things lit and watched, because what is not watched in the Vigil goes to sleep. Cradlewrack keeps things shut, and nothing in Cradlewrack stays shut. Oathen keeps things by promising to keep them, which works exactly as long as the promise does. The Fast keeps nothing; its leavings neither spoil nor stay.

A leaving that is carried on a **living body**, sewn under the skin, held in the mouth, worn on a cord against the breastbone, keeps one step longer. It also counts as worn against the skin for Taint every day.

## Using Leavings

Unless an entry says otherwise:

- Using a leaving takes an action in combat or a few moments otherwise.
- Its **Taint** applies on use, as the Rim Market chapter describes.
- A character may have the effects of **no more leavings at once than their Caul rating**. More than that, and the gods begin to argue inside them: the Carver rolls a d10 for each excess leaving every scene, and on a 1 that leaving's Taint applies again.
- Leavings from a **foreign god** use the Rim Market's foreign-god rules. Characters who carry leavings of three or more gods gain the Derangement *The Voice at Table*, speaking in several voices.
- **Blanks and the Unfed** use leavings normally, except that Hunger Taint does not touch them and Regrowth Taint Seasons them (see the Rim Market). Leavings are the most common way an Unfed comes to carry a god.

## Trading Leavings

Leavings are the most valuable trade on the Table and the most dangerous. Every land has laws against some of them and a black market in all of them. The Rim Road Company officially trades none and unofficially taxes them all at the toll-gates, as "Breath" (a lack a post for any traveller carrying godsign at the Course, a rule the Company's Wardens interpret broadly).

| Where | What sells | Who buys |
| Lastgate, the Sinks | Kept parts, Hand-line relics, Hush-wax | Vigil envoys, Fatlands dying, Second Table |
| Sated, the Chute | Gut-line cuts, Laden-soil, toothed grain | everyone; it is the breadbasket |
| Lowmark, the far pier | brine, Tenanted shells, Uncovered salvage | Vigil rouse-cutters, apothecaries |
| The Slope, the Vigil | lashes, sleeper-breath, Seeming-ink | anyone who must not sleep or must |
| Kest, the back of the Lying-In | Parts, cauls, cord, crowning-clay | Assemblers, Brinehollow buyers, surgeons |
| Tacit, the Breakdowns | Sayer-wax, Forsworn bone, echo-stones | Clausewrights, foreign courts, the desperate |
| Wanting, after dark | crumbs, cards, steward's linen | pilgrims, Second Table, the starving |
| Every Rim waystation | fakes | everyone |

[box: The Price of a God]
Leavings in the catalogues have prices, but price is the least of their cost. A Carver running a campaign rich in leavings should keep a simple tally for each Guest of *every leaving they have used*, and let it show. People who wear the gods on cords around their necks start to smell like the lands those gods were eaten in. Cutwrights can read it on them. The Reckoners can, too.
[/box]
