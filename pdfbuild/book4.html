Inventory of a Rim Road lost-property cupboard, Thimble Cross waystation, taken after the thaw of 642. The clerk’s hand grows smaller as the list goes on.

One phrasebook, Oathen, much thumbed; the chapter on condolences torn out. One bail-bucket, Brinehollow, initials scratched inside: T. & S., always. Three gloves, left hand only, grey, lined with lead. A child’s reader with a page of Hallowboard coloured in, carefully, in brown, all eight sides. A clockwork thing that ticks when nobody is in the room. A candle, unlit, labelled in Wick Tallow’s shop-hand with a name the clerk knows. A pair of dice, bone, one of them warm. Forty-one place cards in a ribbon, all blank but one. A recipe for bread in a hand that is not quite a hand. A deck of cards, the top one turned up: Company. A dictionary, A to K only, with guest underlined in red.

Claimed: none. Do not open the cupboard after dark. Do not count the cards.

The Long Grace core book gave you the Table. Seven Tables gave you its people and the pieces of its gods. Table Scraps is everything else: the things that lie about on the Table after the meal, the things people carry, sing, cook, build, play and say.

It is a book to be used in pieces. Hand a player a page from the Book of Exits before they go to Oathen. Read the Ferrying Song aloud at a Sallowreach funeral. Cook the Fatlands wake-loaf (the real one, which is only bread) for your Tablenight session. Deal the Lack Deck on the table and let the dice decide when someone has to draw. Look up a word in the Lexicon and find a story in it.

Every scrap on the Table was part of something once. Most of them remember.
[ Cutwright's proverb ]

What This Book Contains

  • Part One: Found Pages. Twelve in-world documents, reproduced at length: an Oathen phrasebook of safe speech, a year of the Cradlewrack Tally, the Long Count, an Unfed child’s reader, the Seamer’s pattern book, the Netwatch Register of the Called, Bed One’s Words, the Rim Road Company handbook, Wick Tallow’s candle catalogue, the Weighing Almanac, the Glovemaker’s Ledger, and confessions to the Bench. Each comes with notes for the Carver.
  • Part Two: Engines and Songs. The machinery of the Grace, with parts, workings, rules and failure tables; then the hymns, lullabies, work songs and curses of the seven lands, a calendar of 365 proverbs, the graffiti of the Rim, and the bedtime stories every land tells its children, with the true events behind them.
  • Part Three: Food and Play. The Table’s Cookbook, each grim recipe paired with a real one for your own table; trinkets, keepsakes and the contents of dead people’s pockets; and a dozen in-world games with complete rules you can play with ordinary dice, cards and coins.
  • Part Four: The Decks. Printable cards: the Lack Deck for disastrous rolls, the Grace Deck for perfect ones, the Hunger Deck for bodies that change, Pall Cards for villages going mad, Tablenight Cards, and Market Cards. Each deck also has a roll table for groups who do not print.
  • Part Five: The Lexicon. A dictionary of Table-speech and its seven dialects, A to Z, with slang, curses, euphemisms, trade cant, titles and etymologies.
A Word on the Real Recipes and the Real Games

The recipes marked For Your Table are ordinary, safe food: bread, stews, cakes, pickles and jellies, themed to the dishes they accompany. Nothing on the Table’s own menus should ever be cooked. The in-world games use ordinary dice, cards and coins and can be played for buttons, sweets or nothing. The Table plays for higher stakes. You do not have to.

Content

Everything in the core book’s content warning applies here. These pages contain graphic body horror, murder, cannibalism, desecration of the dead, blasphemy, and suicide and madness spreading through communities, always as tragedy and never as instruction. Sexual taboo, cursed bloodlines and necrophilia are implied only, and concern adults only. Keep using Lines, Veils and the Grace.
