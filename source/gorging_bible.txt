Threads Between the Lands
[Heading1] The World
"We were hungry. Say it plainly. We were hungry, and they were there." — first line of every history written on the Table
[Heading2] The World in One Breath
Six hundred and forty-one years ago, at the end of a famine that lasted nine years, the peoples of the world killed their gods and ate them. Six peoples did. One refused.
It saved them. It also did not stay down. A god cannot be digested, only delayed. In every land, the god that was eaten is growing back: through the bloodlines of the people who swallowed it, through the soil their dead were buried in, through the weather over their heads. Each land is now a culture living inside the slow, wrong resurrection of its own meal.
This is not a world of heroes against monsters. Everyone alive is descended from someone who ate. The monster is in the blood, and it is waking up.
[Heading2] Before: The Providers
The gods were called the Providers, and they were not distant. Each lived bodily among one people, immense and present, the way a mountain is present. There were eight.
[ListParagraph] Ossel, the Closing Hand. God of death and endings. It closed the eyes of everything that finished.
[ListParagraph] Ummer, the Laden. God of harvest and plenty. It walked the fields and they bowed.
[ListParagraph] Dromm, the Fathom. God of the deep sea, of pressure, of what is kept under.
[ListParagraph] Iss, the Lidded. God of sleep. No one ever saw its eyes open.
[ListParagraph] Vey, the Opening. God of birth and beginnings, of doors, of first breaths.
[ListParagraph] Tolm, the Witness. God of oaths and true speech. What it heard was so.
[ListParagraph] Orrum, the Host. God of hearth, welcome, and the shared table.
[ListParagraph] The Eighth. There is an eighth place in every old carving. The figure in it has been chiseled out everywhere, by different tools, in different centuries. Nobody knows its name, its people, or who ate it.
[Heading2] The Long Lack
Then, for nine years, nothing began. Seed rotted unsprouted. Nets came up empty. Livestock went barren, then women did. Bread went to ash in the oven. The Providers themselves grew thin and stopped speaking. No cause was ever found, and that absence is the oldest wound in the world: nobody knows whether the Lack was a punishment, an accident, or a plan.
[Heading2] The Gorging
In the ninth winter, the peoples turned on their gods. Whether it happened on one night or across a season is disputed; the stories call it the Night of Seven Tables. The Providers did not fight. That fact is recorded everywhere and explained nowhere.
Each god was butchered and shared out, and how it was shared became the shape of every society since (see The Cuts, below). The famine ended the next morning. Crops rose in a week. That speed should have frightened people more than it did.
[Heading2] The Three Doctrines
Every argument in the world eventually becomes an argument about why the gods held still.
[ListParagraph] The Theft. We murdered them. The regrowth is justice, and we deserve it. The oldest and most common belief.
[ListParagraph] The Gift. They offered themselves out of love. The regrowth is a tragedy neither side wanted.
[ListParagraph] The Bait. They wanted to be eaten. The Lack was theirs. This is how gods breed: starve a people, be swallowed, and grow back through ten thousand bodies instead of one. This doctrine is illegal in four lands and gaining ground in all seven.
[Heading2] The Regrowth
The return of an eaten god is called the Regrowth, and its marks on a person are called godsign. It moves through four stages, in a person, a family, or an entire country:
[ListParagraph] The Taste. Small wrongnesses. Dreams, cravings, a tell in the body.
[ListParagraph] The Appetite. Compulsions. The body starts wanting what the god wants.
[ListParagraph] The Course. Flesh changes. The person is visibly becoming something else.
[ListParagraph] The Seating. The god is, in some real portion, present again, wearing a person or a place.
Each land's Regrowth obeys its own rules, and those rules are the heart of each region's horror. No land has reached a full Seating. Three are close.
[Heading2] The Cuts: Blood and Class
Who your ancestors were matters less than what part they ate. The families who took the choicest portions of a god carry the strongest godsign, and in every land they became the nobility, because in the first generations godsign looked like blessing. These bloodlines are called Cuts. Each land prizes a different one: the Hand-lines of Sallowreach, the Gut-lines of the Fatlands, the Lung-lines of Brinehollow, the Eye-lines of the Vigil, the Caul-lines of Cradlewrack, the Tongue-lines of Oathen.
The poor descend from those who got the scraps and licked the bones. They are called Scraplings, and they are the least changed people alive. The rulers of the world are its most monstrous inhabitants, and the Regrowth advances fastest in palaces.
Rarely, a child is born with no godsign at all. These are Blanks. They are hunted in every land, as saints, as specimens, or as spare parts.
[Heading2] The Shape of the Table
People call the known world the Table. It is one continent, roughly round, and its geography is a ring around a hole.
[ListParagraph] The Fast lies at the center: the rich old heartland, now almost empty, where the uneaten god still walks.
[ListParagraph] Sallowreach to the north: grey fen and fog, where nothing dies.
[ListParagraph] The Vigil to the northeast: a lit plateau city that has not slept in three centuries.
[ListParagraph] Oathen to the east: dry red canyons where words break bones.
[ListParagraph] The Fatlands to the south: warm, wet plains of meat.
[ListParagraph] Brinehollow to the west: a coast the sea is abandoning.
[ListParagraph] Cradlewrack to the northwest: red clay highlands in labor.
Nobody crosses the middle. All trade moves on the Rim Road, a ring of highway around the Fast's border, and every land's politics is shaped by needing something from a neighbor it finds obscene.
[Heading2] Time, Seasons, and Sky
The year is 641 A.G. (After Gorging). The year has four seasons named for a meal: Grace (spring), Plenty (summer), Carving (autumn), and Lack (winter). The moon is called the Plate; a new moon is "licked clean."
Tablenight, the anniversary of the Gorging, falls on the longest night of Lack. Sallowreach holds a banquet for its dead. The Fatlands fast for one day and fail. Oathen says nothing for twenty-four hours. The people of the Fast stand outdoors until dawn with their backs to every door.
[Heading2] Things All Lands Share
[ListParagraph] Table-speech. A common tongue, older than the Gorging. In it, the words for "worship" and "eat" share a root. Scholars insist they always did.
[ListParagraph] The lack. The one coin honored everywhere: a disc of salt-cured bone worth one day's bread.
[ListParagraph] The grace. Nobody on the Table eats with a stranger without first saying "Lack keep away." Skipping it is the universal insult.
[ListParagraph] The trade in cures. Every land believes another land's curse might cure its own. The sleepless want Sallowreach's undeath. The starving want the Fast's emptiness. Nothing has ever worked. The trade never slows.
[Heading2] Powers That Cross Borders
[ListParagraph] The Rim Road Company. The merchant league that owns the ring road, its inns, and its tolls. Neutral, rich, and the only institution every government needs. It swears its contracts in Oathen, where they cannot be broken.
[ListParagraph] The Cutwrights' College. Genealogists who track every bloodline's Cut across six centuries. Their ledgers decide marriages, inheritances, and executions. Whoever holds them knows exactly where each god is regrowing fastest.
[ListParagraph] The Purgation. A cross-border movement that believes the gods can be thrown back up. Their methods run from fasting and bleeding to massacre of high-Cut families.
[ListParagraph] The Second Table. A hidden cult with one idea: it worked once. When the gods finish regrowing, eat them again. Its members are mostly nobles, and they are preparing.
[ListParagraph] The Reckoners. Zealots out of the Fast who cross into other lands to kill the Godeaters' highest bloodlines. See The Fast.
[Heading2] The World Right Now
[ListParagraph] Every Regrowth is speeding up. Signs that took generations now take years. Each government is hiding its own numbers.
[ListParagraph] Orrum is walking outward. For six centuries the uneaten god kept to the Fast. Its tables have now been found laid at crossroads in three other lands.
[ListParagraph] Sallowreach has started to end. Patches of the undying north are dying all at once, completely, down to the sound.
[ListParagraph] Cradlewrack is nine days apart. The quakes there come at shrinking intervals. Something is going to be born out of the ground.
[ListParagraph] The Vigil is running out of beds. There is a number of sleepers at which the god of sleep wakes. The city is close to it.
[ListParagraph] Oathen's rain was late. The one promise a god still keeps has begun to slip.
[ListParagraph] The sea is leaving. Brinehollow's ocean drops every year. It is going somewhere.
[Heading2] The Empty Plate
Eight gods. Seven peoples. Six meals and one refusal. The arithmetic does not close, and every land has a reason to stop people asking. Somewhere there is a people who ate the Eighth, or a people who are the Eighth, or a god that was simply left on the table. Its chair is still pulled out.
[Heading1] Sallowreach
"Nobody here is waiting to die. We are all waiting to be allowed." — fen proverb
[Heading2] The Meal
The people of the northern fens ate Ossel, the Closing Hand, god of death and endings. They ate it last and slowest of all the peoples, because nobody wanted to be the one to start. The families who took its hands became the Hand-lines, and they rule to this day.
With Ossel in their bellies, nothing in Sallowreach has been able to finish for six hundred and forty-one years.
[Heading2] The Rule of the Land
Nothing ends here. A cut throat does not kill. Neither does drowning, burning, hunger, or age. The body takes the damage and keeps going. People who have died and carried on are called the Kept, and they outnumber the breathing nine to one.
Decay still works, which is the cruelty. Flesh rots but never finishes rotting. A Kept person feels all of it. The mind stays, the pain stays, and the body goes on coming apart for centuries.
It applies to everything. Fires smolder for decades. Songs here have no last verse. An argument begun in 212 A.G. is still being conducted by the original parties, in a room in Lastgate, through a hired interpreter of jaw-clicks.
The dead cannot leave. A Kept person who steps across the border drops, finished, at once. Sallowreach has an army that cannot be killed and cannot invade.
[Heading2] The Regrowth
Ossel is growing back, and what grows back is ending.
[ListParagraph] The Taste. A dread of open things. Hand-lines children cannot sleep with a door ajar.
[ListParagraph] The Appetite. Compulsive closing: lids, shutters, books, the eyes of strangers. Sufferers wear mittens.
[ListParagraph] The Course. The hands go grey and cold and grow extra joints. What they touch finishes: a candle, a conversation, a person.
[ListParagraph] The Seating. A Hush. A patch of ground where everything ends at once and stays ended. No rot, no flies, no echo. Sound does not carry inside one. The largest is four miles across and growing.
The nobility's touch is the only death available in Sallowreach, and they have sold it by license for five hundred years.
[Heading2] Land and Weather
Grey fen, black water, reed and peat, under a fog called the Breath that has not lifted in living or unliving memory. There is no real winter, because winter is an ending. Leaves yellow on the branch and stay there for decades. The air is warm, wet, and sweet in the wrong way.
Flies are the true weather. They move in fronts. A Black Noon is a swarm thick enough to put out lamps. Rain is called weep and leaves a tea-colored stain. The fen itself is full: everything that ever drowned in it is still down there, moving a little.
[Heading2] History
[ListParagraph] The First Morning (0 A.G.). The day after the Gorging, a hanged thief in Lastgate asked to be cut down. That is how they found out.
[ListParagraph] The Glad Century. For a hundred years it looked like a miracle. No widows, no orphans. The dancing only stopped when the first generation began to come apart.
[ListParagraph] The Stitching Laws (130 A.G.). The state took responsibility for keeping the dead in one piece. The Seamers' Guild was chartered. Falling apart in public became a fineable offence.
[ListParagraph] The War of the Heirs (288 A.G.). The young rose against parents who would never die and never bequeath. They won every battle and changed nothing. The fathers they killed were back at their desks within the week.
[ListParagraph] The Licensing (301 A.G.). The Hand-lines revealed that their touch could finish a person, and put a price on it.
[ListParagraph] The First Hush (633 A.G.). A village called Dunmere went silent overnight. Everyone in it was simply, properly dead. Half the country wept with envy.
[Heading2] Rule and Politics
Sallowreach is governed by the Mortuary Court, a council of the Hand-lines, under a Regent who has held the post for three centuries. Property never passes, because owners never die. The oldest dead, called the Unvacated, hold every title, deed, and office. A living man of seventy is legally a minor if his father is still upright.
Death is the only real currency of power. A Closing costs more than a house. The Court grants them as rewards, withholds them as punishment, and sentences its worst criminals to be Kept forever, in pieces, in a jar.
[Heading2] Culture and Daily Life
[ListParagraph] Keeping. Families are responsible for their dead. Six or seven generations share a house. Grandmothers are restitched each spring. The quarrel over whose turn it is to keep great-great-grandfather is the standard subject of comedy.
[ListParagraph] Setting Aside. When someone is too far gone to stand, they are wrapped, labeled, and shelved in the Lofts, still aware. This is the nearest thing to a funeral. Families visit on feast days and read aloud.
[ListParagraph] Smell. Everyone carries perfume, vinegar, or smoke. Scent is class. The rich smell of cedar and the poor smell of themselves.
[ListParagraph] Manners. The polite greeting is "May you finish." The worst curse is "Long life."
[ListParagraph] Tablenight. The living cook a banquet for their dead, who cannot taste it, and everyone pretends.
[Heading2] Factions
[ListParagraph] The Mortuary Court. The Hand-lines nobility. Sellers of death, hoarders of it, and increasingly afraid of their own hands.
[ListParagraph] The Seamers' Guild. Stitchers, embalmers, and wire-workers who hold the population together. A Seamers' strike would be a national collapse within a month.
[ListParagraph] The Heirs. The living young, permanently disinherited. Some want reform. Some want every Unvacated elder dragged to a Hush.
[ListParagraph] The Finishers. A death cult in the plainest sense. They make pilgrimage to the Hushes and walk in singing. The Court calls it theft of a licensed good.
[ListParagraph] The Loft-Wardens. Keepers of the Set-Aside. Quiet, underpaid, and in possession of forty thousand witnesses who hear everything.
[Heading2] Places
[ListParagraph] Lastgate. The capital, built on pilings over the fen, where the border road ends. Its lower levels have been sinking for centuries and are still inhabited.
[ListParagraph] The Lofts. Warehouses of the Set-Aside, nine storeys of shelving. The whispering is audible from the street.
[ListParagraph] The Sump. The deep fen where those beyond stitching are sunk in weighted sacks. Bubbles rise in patterns.
[ListParagraph] Dunmere Hush. The first and largest silence. Birds fall at its edge.
[ListParagraph] The Jar Room. The Court's prison. One shelf, very long.
[Heading2] Sallowreach Now
The Hushes are multiplying, and they appear fastest near Hand-lines estates. The Court has realized what that means: the nobility is the god's way back, and the country will not be freed from undeath so much as closed like a book. Meanwhile the Vigil has sent an envoy asking to buy the secret of Keeping, the Finishers have tripled in a year, and the Heirs have noticed that for the first time in six centuries, an elder can be made to die.
[Heading2] The People of Sallowreach
[ListParagraph] Ansel Grue, the Unvacated Regent. Four hundred and twelve years old, three hundred of them in office. Mostly wire, lacquer, and habit now. He signs every death license personally and has never once considered signing his own.
[ListParagraph] Lady Corrow Vane, the Left Hand of the Court. The Court's chief Closer. She has finished eleven thousand people by touch, at a price. She has never held her own children, and her gloves are sewn on.
[ListParagraph] Osric Vane, the Gloved. Corrow's brother, deep in the Course. His hands are grey, long, and have nine joints a finger. He is kept in lead mittens in a locked wing, and he has begun asking politely to be used on the enemy.
[ListParagraph] Thessaly Mort, Master Seamer. Head of the guild. She has replaced so much of herself over two centuries that she keeps a ledger of which parts were hers originally. The ledger is short.
[ListParagraph] Jory Welt, the Boy. Seventy-one years old and legally a child, since his father is three hundred and upright. He leads the Heirs, and he has a map of every Hush pinned to his wall.
[ListParagraph] Old Bettany Shroud, Loft-Warden. Keeper of forty thousand shelved dead. She reads to them nightly and writes down what they whisper back. Lately they all whisper the same word.
[ListParagraph] Pim Sorrel, the Scrap Finisher. A Scrapling girl of twelve whose touch ends things, which should be impossible for her blood. The Court wants her dissected. The Finishers want her crowned.
[ListParagraph] Justice Ever Lathe, the Honorable Maggot. A judge rotted down to bone and a resident colony. His rulings are delivered in the buzzing, and a clerk translates. Nobody is sure any longer which of them is deciding.
[ListParagraph] Dr. Halloran Quease. A physician in a land where no illness is fatal. He has made pain his whole study, since it is the one ailment left, and he has catalogued four hundred kinds.
[ListParagraph] Wick Tallow, the Chandler. He renders the unclaimed dead into candles that feel themselves burn. They give a very steady light. Half of Lastgate reads by people it used to know.
[ListParagraph] Sister Lorn of the Shut Eye. High priest of the Finishers. She has led nine hundred pilgrims into the Hushes and has stayed outside every time, saying someone must. Her flock has started asking why.
[ListParagraph] Captain Dace Morrow, the Sixty-Times Slain. Commander of an army that cannot lose a man and cannot cross a border. He is more thread than soldier now, and he has started deserting in small pieces.
[ListParagraph] Henna Farrow, the Widow Unmade. Her husband died ninety years ago and still comes home each evening. She has petitioned for his Closing forty-one times. He does not know. She sets his place anyway.
[ListParagraph] Grandfather Peat. A bog body hauled from the Sump who drowned the week of the Gorging. He is the last witness to the Night of Seven Tables, speaks a dead dialect, and repeats one thing: the god was smiling.
[ListParagraph] Tam Ruddock, the Lastgate Ferryman. He smuggles the dying of other lands across the border so they will not die. He has never told a single one of them what happens next, and he sleeps badly.
[ListParagraph] Marrow Jack. A part-thief. He takes limbs from the Kept while they shelve or sleep and sells them to back-alley seamers. His victims wake up able to feel what their missing hands are being used for.
[ListParagraph] Little Nell Ash, Queen of the Smalls. She died at seven, two hundred years ago, and has been seven since. She rules the child-dead of Lastgate's undercity with a two-century-old mind and a seven-year-old's sense of fairness.
[ListParagraph] Provost Carrion Sedge. Head of the Cutwrights' College in the north. His ledgers prove that the Hushes follow Hand-lines blood exactly. He has not decided which is worse: publishing it or selling it.
[ListParagraph] Mother Gall. A fen witch who pickles. She can keep a body sound for a century in the right brine, and her cellar holds four hundred customers in barrels, paid up in advance, all wide awake.
[ListParagraph] Edda Thrush, the Hushwalker. A surveyor who walked into Dunmere Hush and walked out again. She cannot hear anything now, and insists that this is not deafness. Something is listening in her place.
[ListParagraph] Bram Hollin, the Last Mourner. His family were professional mourners, a trade six centuries extinct. He alone still knows the full funeral rite by heart, and now that people are dying again, everyone wants him.
[ListParagraph] Idony Pask, the Tourist. A Fatlands woman who came north with a wasting sickness so she would not die of it. She did not die of it. She can never leave, and the sickness has not stopped.
[Heading1] The Fatlands
"There is no word in our dialect for full. We had one. We ate it." — Fatlands joke, told at every table
[Heading2] The Meal
The southern plains ate Ummer, the Laden, god of harvest and plenty. They ate it fastest of all the peoples and cracked the bones for the marrow. The families who took the stomach and entrails became the Gut-lines.
Ummer was the god of feeding. Now it feeds.
[Heading2] The Rule of the Land
Everything here is meat, and nobody is ever full.
The soil is the god's flesh, and it grows flesh. Wheat comes up with teeth in the ear. Orchard trees bleed when pruned and bear fruit with a skin, a fat layer, and something very like a pit of bone. Root vegetables have veins. Dig a well and you strike something that flinches.
All of it is delicious, and none of it satisfies. A Fatlander can eat until the belly splits and still feel the hunger exactly as before. Hunger here has no connection to the stomach. It is the god's, and they inherited it.
The land also eats. Each Carving season, people go missing from every village: out in the field at dusk, and not back by morning. This is called the Reaping. The official explanation is wolves. There are no wolves in the Fatlands. Nothing there is anything but crop.
[Heading2] The Regrowth
[ListParagraph] The Taste. Hunger that wakes you. Children chew their sleeves.
[ListParagraph] The Appetite. Eating without choosing: soil, tallow, leather, hair. The sufferer cannot stop for conversation.
[ListParagraph] The Course. The body swells past all proportion, and the feet begin to put down roots. The skin goes soft and loamy. These people are called the Laden.
[ListParagraph] The Seating. A Laden person stops being a person and becomes a place: a low hill, warm to the touch, where things grow very well. Some keep a mouth.
Many of the best fields in the south are somebody's great-grandmother.
[Heading2] Land and Weather
Low, wet, endless green-and-red plain under a heavy yellow sky. It is always warm. The ground gives underfoot and, if you stand still, you can feel it slowly rise and fall.
Rain is called drip and leaves a film on standing water that cooks skim off and use. The hot season wind, the Belch, comes up from the south smelling of rendering. In late Plenty the fields sweat and the whole country glistens. Marbling is the first frost, when the fat in the soil whitens and the plains look like a cut of meat from horizon to horizon.
[Heading2] History
[ListParagraph] The Week of Rising (0 A.G.). Seven days after the Gorging, crops stood shoulder-high. Nobody asked what they were crops of.
[ListParagraph] The Sweet Reigns. Two centuries as the breadbasket of the Table. The Fatlands fed every land and grew rich and wide.
[ListParagraph] The Tooth Harvest (204 A.G.). The first wheat with teeth. The Board ruled it a new and superior strain.
[ListParagraph] The Weighing (260 A.G.). Votes were assigned by body weight. The stated reason was that the largest citizens had the most at stake.
[ListParagraph] The Thin Rising (455 A.G.). A fasting rebellion. It was put down by force-feeding, and fasting has been treason since.
[ListParagraph] The Long Count (present). A farmer named Pell Hogget began keeping a tally of the Reaping. It has spread from village to village. The numbers go up every year.
[Heading2] Rule and Politics
The Fatlands are governed by the Board of Plenty, seated in the capital, Sated. Each Board member's vote counts in proportion to their weight, taken publicly once a year at the Weighing. The Chairman has not walked in thirty years.
The Gut-lines are the nobility. They are the largest, the hungriest, and the furthest along. Their estates are run from beds, and then from the ground. Succession disputes turn on whether a rooted, mouthless hill can still be said to hold title. The courts have ruled that it can.
The economy is export. The Fatlands feed half the Table by way of the Rim Road, and every other land depends on that meat while trying not to think about it.
[Heading2] Culture and Daily Life
[ListParagraph] The Feasting Laws. Five meals a day are mandatory. Leaving food on the plate is a fine. Refusing a meal is sedition.
[ListParagraph] Nothing is wasted. There are no graves. The dead are served at the wake, by the family, with the best plates. To be buried uneaten would be the deepest insult to a life.
[ListParagraph] Compliments. "You look heavy" is the kindest thing one person can say to another. "You look well" means nothing.
[ListParagraph] The grace. Every meal begins by thanking the field by name.
[ListParagraph] Tablenight. The whole country attempts a one-day fast in penance. Nobody has ever recorded a village that made it to sundown.
[Heading2] Factions
[ListParagraph] The Board of Plenty. The government. Vast, slow, and aware of the Reaping in more detail than it admits.
[ListParagraph] The Thin. Outlawed fasters who believe starving the body starves the god. They are gaunt, hunted, and the only people in the south who seem clear-headed.
[ListParagraph] The Renderers' Union. The slaughter, salting, and shipping trades. They control what leaves the country and know what is in it.
[ListParagraph] The Cullmasters. A quiet office of the Board which holds that the Reaping can be steered. They choose who is sent to work the far fields at dusk.
[ListParagraph] The Second Helping. A joyous cult of the Bait doctrine. The god wants to feed and be fed, they say, so eat, and be eaten, and be glad.
[Heading2] Places
[ListParagraph] Sated. The capital. Wide doors, reinforced floors, ramps instead of stairs, and the smell of gravy from every drain.
[ListParagraph] The Bleeding Orchards. Ten thousand acres of trees that scream at pruning time. Orchard-keepers go deaf young, by choice.
[ListParagraph] Low Tilth. The richest farmland in the world, and the district with the highest Reaping. Nobody there locks their door, because it has never helped.
[ListParagraph] The Dowager Hills. A range of low green mounds outside Sated: nine generations of Gut-lines matriarchs, Seated. They are still consulted on family matters.
[ListParagraph] The Chute. The Rim Road export depot. Meat goes out in barrels with the origin stamped over twice.
[Heading2] The Fatlands Now
The Reaping has doubled in ten years, and the fields are turning up things with faces. The Board's answer is the Cullmasters. The Thin are growing, a girl has appeared who feels no hunger at all, and for the first time an exile from the Fast has sat down at a Fatlands table and eaten. What is happening to him has no name yet.
[Heading2] The People of the Fatlands
[ListParagraph] Chairman Obb Tullow, the Heaviest Vote. Head of the Board, at nineteen hundred pounds. He is moved by dray and eight oxen. His feet rooted through the cart last spring, and the Board has agreed not to notice.
[ListParagraph] Dame Suet Marrable. Gut-lines matriarch and hostess of the Unending Dinner, which has been in continuous service for eighty-one years. Guests have been born at her table, and several have been served at it.
[ListParagraph] Pell Hogget, the Counter. A farmer from Low Tilth who noticed his village was one smaller each autumn and wrote it down. His tally is now copied in two hundred villages. He wishes he had never learned to count.
[ListParagraph] Corder Lank, the Thin Man. Leader of the Thin. He has eaten nothing but water and ash for six years and can be counted bone by bone. He is the only man in the south who does not feel the hunger, and he will not say how.
[ListParagraph] Mistress Brisket Annalow. Boss of the Renderers' Union. She can stop every barrel on the Rim Road with one word, and she knows precisely which of them should never have been filled.
[ListParagraph] Hob Gristle, the Honest Butcher. He can tell by taste what a cut used to be, down to the name. It has ruined him for the trade. He still works, and he apologizes to every joint.
[ListParagraph] Cullmaster Varn Sweetbread. The Board's quiet hand. He decides which vagrants, debtors, and troublemakers are assigned the dusk shift in the far fields. He thinks of it as agriculture.
[ListParagraph] Tansy Lard, the Gleaner. A girl of ten who gleans behind the harvesters. She dug up a root with her mother's birthmark on it. She has replanted it behind the house and waters it every day.
[ListParagraph] Brother Glut. Prophet of the Second Helping. Enormous, radiant, and happy. He preaches that being eaten is the highest form of being loved, and he has volunteered parts of himself to prove it.
[ListParagraph] Old Mother Rind. A Laden woman who rooted forty years ago and is now a small orchard hill with a working mouth. Her village asks her advice on marriages. She is always right, and always hungry.
[ListParagraph] Dr. Emmerich Paunch. The Board's physician. He opened a Gut-lines stomach to measure its capacity and lowered a plumb line into it. He ran out of line at two hundred feet.
[ListParagraph] Sal Trotter, the Drover. She walks herds to market that the buyers call cattle. They walk on two legs when no one is watching the road. She has stopped naming them.
[ListParagraph] Wren Hollowell, the Fasting Girl. A Blank, fifteen, who has never once felt hungry. The Thin kneel to her. The Board has offered her weight in bone coin to whoever brings her in, and has not specified alive.
[ListParagraph] Garrick Tripe, the Weigher. He carries the official scales at the annual Weighing, and for the right fee your pockets fill with lead. He has personally elected three governments.

[Heading1] Brinehollow
"The sea is not going out. It is going in." — carved on the Lowmark tide-post, author unknown
[Heading2] The Meal
The western coast ate Dromm, the Fathom, god of the deep sea, of pressure, and of what is kept under. It had to be dragged up onto the shingle first, and it took four days to stop moving. The families who ate its lungs became the Lung-lines.
Dromm was the god of holding things down. It is now held in them.
[Heading2] The Rule of the Land
The sea is inside the people.
Every Brinehollower has a tide. Twice a day the lungs fill with salt water from nowhere, and it has to be coughed out. This is called bailing, and it is done at dawn and dusk the way other peoples wash their faces. Children are taught to bail before they are taught to read.
The tide also pulls. On certain nights a person will stand up, walk to the shore, and keep walking. They are the Called. Most are never seen again. Some come back, weeks later, walking out of the surf heavier than they left, calm, and courteous. Those are the Tenanted. They have the same face and the same memories, and someone else is looking out through their eyes.
Meanwhile the ocean itself is leaving. The waterline drops every year. Harbors stand dry. The sea is not evaporating. It is moving house.
[Heading2] The Regrowth
[ListParagraph] The Taste. Salt on the lips at waking. Ears that pop indoors.
[ListParagraph] The Appetite. The pull. Standing at windows that face west. Sleepwalking to the nearest water, including wells and washbasins.
[ListParagraph] The Course. The body adapts to depth: grey skin, wide dark eyes, a chest that no longer needs to rise. The sufferer is uncomfortable in open air and sleeps in a cistern.
[ListParagraph] The Seating. The Tenant takes the whole house. What walks around afterward is a piece of Dromm, patient, pressing outward against the skin.
[Heading2] Land and Weather
A long grey coast of shingle, salt marsh, and cliff, and beyond it a newly exposed seabed called the Uncovered that stretches further every year: weed forests gone to leather, wrecks standing upright, and things with too many ribs.
It rains salt. Fog comes in from a sea that is now miles away. On pressure days the air thickens until ears bleed and windows bow inward. And there is the indoor tide: in every cellar in the country, however far inland, water rises twice a day and drains away again, leaving kelp on the stairs.
[Heading2] History
[ListParagraph] The Beaching (0 A.G.). Dromm was hauled ashore on ten thousand hooks. The beach where it died, Longslab, has never dried.
[ListParagraph] The Brimming (60 A.G.). The first generation born coughing brine.
[ListParagraph] The First Calling (118 A.G.). In one night, four hundred people walked into the sea at Lowmark. Eleven came back.
[ListParagraph] The Drowned Bench (240 A.G.). The Tenanted proved calm, fair-minded, and impossible to bribe. The Admiralty began appointing them as judges.
[ListParagraph] The Turning of the Tide (512 A.G.). The first year the sea did not come back to its old line.
[ListParagraph] The Chase (present). Lowmark adds a mile of pier every year to stay in reach of water. The city is now a wooden road nineteen miles long with a harbor at the far end.
[Heading2] Rule and Politics
Brinehollow is ruled by the Tidal Admiralty, a navy that has become a government. Its fleet sits aground on props, fully crewed, drilled daily, awaiting the sea's return. Rank is still given in ships. A Captain may never have been afloat.
Above the Admiralty sit the Lung-lines princes, who can no longer breathe air for long and hold court from glass tanks. Beneath it sits the Drowned Bench: the Tenanted judges, whose verdicts are final, and whose loyalties nobody has ever thought to question out loud.
The great political fact is water. A land of sailors and fishers has lost its sea. The Admiralty's entire policy is to pretend this is temporary.
[Heading2] Culture and Daily Life
[ListParagraph] The bail-bucket. Everyone carries one. Losing yours is like losing your shoes. Sweethearts exchange them.
[ListParagraph] Anchoring. People sleep roped, chained, or weighted to the bed. A rope cut in the night is the country's great domestic tragedy.
[ListParagraph] Salt manners. It is rude to mention how someone sounds when they breathe. It is rude to ask a returned person where they went.
[ListParagraph] The count. Each morning, households call names through the wall to their neighbors. Silence means someone was Called.
[ListParagraph] Tablenight. Families set a bowl of seawater at the table's head and leave the west door unbarred, once a year, for whoever might come home.
[Heading2] Factions
[ListParagraph] The Tidal Admiralty. The state. Proud, hidebound, and commanding the largest landlocked navy in the world.
[ListParagraph] The Anchorites. An order who chain themselves permanently to rock, so the Calling can never take them. Their abbey is a cliff face hung with a thousand living monks.
[ListParagraph] The Deepening. A cult that treats the Calling as a sacrament. They walk in willingly, in procession, with lanterns.
[ListParagraph] The Netwatch. The Admiralty's night patrol. They hunt the Called along the shore with nets, hooks, and rope, and drag them home.
[ListParagraph] The Wreckwives. A sisterhood of those whose spouses came back Tenanted. They share advice on how to live with a stranger who knows your name.
[ListParagraph] The Salt Assay. The office that measures the falling sea. Its figures are a state secret.
[Heading2] Places
[ListParagraph] Lowmark. The capital, a nineteen-mile pier-city built out across the dry seabed, section by section, after the sea.
[ListParagraph] Longslab. The beach of the Beaching. Permanently wet. Nothing grows there, and gulls will not land on it.
[ListParagraph] The Uncovered. The exposed seabed. Salvage country, lawless and strange, with a new mile of it every year.
[ListParagraph] The Hanging Abbey. The Anchorites' cliff.
[ListParagraph] The Trench. The place far out where the water is still deep, and where it seems to be going. From the edge you can hear it draining.
[Heading2] Brinehollow Now
The Salt Assay has done the arithmetic the Admiralty forbade: the volume of sea lost each year matches the number of Tenanted. The ocean is moving into the people, a bucket at a time. The Tenanted now sit on every court in the land. A scavenger has found something on the Uncovered that should not be under any sea. And a shipwright is building a vessel to follow the water down.
[Heading2] The People of Brinehollow
[ListParagraph] High Admiral Corvin Sund. Commander of four hundred warships standing on dry ground. He inspects them weekly, has the hulls scraped of barnacles that are not there, and will hang any officer who says the word "stranded."
[ListParagraph] Harbormistress Kessa Drowle. She builds Lowmark's mile of new pier each year. She has calculated the year the timber runs out and the year the sea drops off the shelf entirely. They are the same year.
[ListParagraph] Justice Maren Oake of the Drowned Bench. Called at thirty, returned after forty days. She has never given an unjust verdict. She speaks in two tones at once, and the lower one sometimes finishes first.
[ListParagraph] Prince Aurel Vasht. Heir of the Lung-lines, twenty-two, and unable to breathe air for more than an hour. He rules from a glass tank and has learned to read lips through eight inches of water.
[ListParagraph] Lady Ondine Vasht, the Dowager Below. The prince's grandmother, far into the Course. She lives in the flooded cellars of the palace and has not been seen whole in years. The cellars have had to be extended twice.
[ListParagraph] Father Ebb. Abbot of the Anchorites, chained to the same rock for fifty-one years. He has felt the Calling every night of them. He says the chain is not what holds him, and will not say what does.
[ListParagraph] Tolly Brack, the Bailer Boy. Eleven years old. He goes door to door at dawn bailing those too old or weak to cough for themselves. He knows from the color of the water who will be Called next.
[ListParagraph] Sennet Gull, the Wreckwife. Her husband walked into the sea and came back kinder. She keeps him in the cistern, brings him his dinner, and has started to prefer him. That is what frightens her.
[ListParagraph] Dr. Isolde Reave. A lung surgeon. She opened a drowned chest at high tide and found the water in it had a current. In the next one she found a shell, alive, of no known species.
[ListParagraph] Hask Tarn, the Pilot. He guides salvage parties across the Uncovered. He used to pilot ships over the same ground and navigates by remembered depth. He will not go near the places where he once lost the bottom.
[ListParagraph] Nerys Colm, the Half-Called. She walked into the surf up to her chin and then, uniquely, turned around. She has heard the invitation in full and can repeat it. Nobody who has listened to her has stayed ashore.
[ListParagraph] Captain Jonah Skerry, called Dry Jonah. A smuggler who bottles what Brinehollowers cough up and sells it inland as tonic. It sells well in the Vigil. He has never asked what his customers start to dream about.
[ListParagraph] Orrin Wake and Passenger. A fisherman, Tenanted by something that is, for once, willing to talk. It is courteous, very old, and negotiating with Orrin for the use of his left arm.
[ListParagraph] Brenna Kelp, the Salt Assayer. Keeper of the falling number. She found where the sea is going by counting buckets. The Admiralty has made her finding illegal. She has hidden three copies.
[ListParagraph] Mother Limpet. Prophet of the Deepening. She has baptized two thousand by walking them in at night. She says she will follow when the last one is safely under. She is eighty-four.
[ListParagraph] Dagna Hull, the Shipwright. She is building the Follow, a sealed iron vessel meant to chase the sea down into the Trench. She has a waiting list of nine hundred volunteers.
[ListParagraph] Cray Lobbett. A scavenger on the far Uncovered. He found a stone table on the seabed, older than the Gorging, with eight chairs. Seven were pushed in. He has not been able to sell the location, or to stop going back.
[ListParagraph] Old Wet Tom. A beggar who has never been dry. It rains on him indoors. Puddles follow him down corridors. Children say that if you look into one of them, it is much deeper than the floor.
[ListParagraph] Lieutenant Sable Reef of the Netwatch. She has pulled three hundred Called off the shore with hook and rope. Every one of them looked at her the same way. She has begun to wonder what she is saving them from.
[ListParagraph] Ilse Marl, Choir-Mistress of the Low Water. At the lowest tide the Tenanted gather and sing. She alone writes it down. Laid end to end, forty years of her transcriptions make a nautical chart.
[ListParagraph] Finn Undertow, the Bellman. He rings Lowmark's tide bell so the city knows when to bail. The tides in the body no longer match the tides of the sea, and he has to choose which to ring.
[ListParagraph] Reeve Calder Stokes. An auditor sent by the Rim Road Company to price Brinehollow's losses. He is an outsider with dry lungs. He has been here three months, and this morning he tasted salt.
[Heading1] The Vigil
"Pinch me." — the Vigil's commonest endearment, and its marriage vow
[Heading2] The Meal
The people of the northeastern plateau ate Iss, the Lidded, god of sleep. It did not wake while they did it. That detail has never stopped bothering them. The families who ate its eyes, still shut, became the Eye-lines.
For three hundred and forty years afterward, they slept like anyone else, and dreamed a little too vividly. Then came the First Night.
[Heading2] The Rule of the Land
Anyone who sleeps does not wake up.
Since 341 A.G., every sleeper in the Vigil goes to the same place: one shared dream, a long dim room with a single enormous closed eye in the far wall. They do not come back from it. The body lives on, breathing, warm, unaging, and cannot be roused by any means.
So nobody sleeps. An entire city-state has stayed awake for three hundred years by law, by drug, by pain, and by each other. Exhaustion is not a condition here. It is the climate.
The sleepers are kept in the Dormitory, in numbered beds. They are called the Counted, because someone long ago worked out that there is a number of sleepers at which the eye in the dream opens. The number is a state secret. The beds are nearly full.
[Heading2] The Regrowth
[ListParagraph] The Taste. Heavy lids. Yawning that spreads through a room like a rumor.
[ListParagraph] The Appetite. Blinks: sleeps of a second or two, on your feet, mid-sentence. You are in the room with the eye for exactly as long as you are out. People come back from a blink having moved.
[ListParagraph] The Course. The lids thin, shrink, and are gone. The eyes stop closing at all. Eye-lines nobles are born this way and call it purity.
[ListParagraph] The Seating. A sleeper who begins to move. Lips first.
[Heading2] Land and Weather
A high, bare stone plateau with one great city on it, and the city is never dark. Ten thousand lamps, mirrors on every roof, furnaces burning in the squares. There has been no night here for three centuries. The lamp-smoke is permanent, and nobody born in the Vigil has seen a star.
The danger is comfort. The warm wind off the eastern plain is called the Yawn, and on Yawn days the Wakers double their patrols. Rain on a roof is so soothing that roofs are built of loose tin to make it clatter. The worst weather is a mild, quiet, overcast afternoon. The city records those as disasters, with a death toll.
[Heading2] History
[ListParagraph] The Easy Centuries (0–340 A.G.). The Vigil, then called Ludmere, was famous for its dreamers, oracles, and soft beds.
[ListParagraph] The First Night (341 A.G.). One morning, a third of the city did not get up. By the third morning it was half. The survivors were those who had been too frightened to lie down.
[ListParagraph] The Waking Laws (342 A.G.). Sleep was outlawed. Beds were burned in the squares. The city renamed itself.
[ListParagraph] The Rouse Patent (390 A.G.). The apothecaries perfected a stimulant that held. The dose required has risen every decade since.
[ListParagraph] The Count (401 A.G.). A diver returned from the dream with the number. She was made to say it once, to three officials, and then her bed was added to it.
[ListParagraph] The Lidless Succession (480 A.G.). The Eye-lines, unable to shut their eyes, were declared the only citizens fit to rule.
[Heading2] Rule and Politics
The city is governed by the Watch of Watches, a council that sits in shifts around the clock and has not adjourned in two hundred years. At its head is the Warden-Prime, always an Eye-line, always lidless, always watched in turn by attendants who wet the royal eyes with a brush.
Law is simple. Lying down is a crime. Closing your eyes for longer than a breath is a crime. Letting someone near you nod off is negligence. The police, the Wakers, carry rattles and long needles.
The deepest political question is the Dormitory. One party wants more beds built. One wants the Counted moved out of the country. One, very quietly, wants the number reduced.
[Heading2] Culture and Daily Life
[ListParagraph] No beds. There is not a bed, couch, or cushion in the city. Floors slope. Chairs have one leg. Walls are studded so you cannot lean.
[ListParagraph] Nudging. Everyone has a nudger: a spouse, a sibling, a hired child. Marriage is the promise to keep each other awake, and the vow is two words.
[ListParagraph] Rouse. A bitter black stimulant, taken hourly. Teeth go first. Then the heart.
[ListParagraph] Pain as courtesy. A slap is a favor. Guests are offered a pin on arrival.
[ListParagraph] The Seeming. After enough waking, everyone hallucinates. The Vigil has made it a civic sense. When a whole street sees the same thing, it is entered in the public record as news.
[ListParagraph] Tablenight. The city puts out every lamp for one minute, to prove it still can. People hold hands and count aloud.
[Heading2] Factions
[ListParagraph] The Watch of Watches. The lidless government. Sleepless, paranoid, and correct to be.
[ListParagraph] The Wakers. The police. Their job is to hurt people for their own good, and after three centuries they are very skilled.
[ListParagraph] The Lamplighters' Guild. Keepers of the light. Oil is the Vigil's largest import, and the guild knows exactly how many days of it remain.
[ListParagraph] The Tuckers. A criminal mercy. For a price, they will guard you while you sleep for sixty seconds, then haul you back. Their failures are delivered to the Dormitory steps before the next bell.
[ListParagraph] The Dream-Divers. State explorers who go under on a tether and are dragged up by force. Most manage three dives.
[ListParagraph] The Bedded. Heretics who preach that sleep is a homecoming, and that the city is three hundred years late.
[Heading2] Places
[ListParagraph] The Dormitory. A building the size of a district. Sixty thousand beds, numbered, in rows. Completely silent. The Counted all breathe in time.
[ListParagraph] The Nodding Stair. The Watch's council hall, built as one endless staircase so no one can sit comfortably.
[ListParagraph] Lampwrights' Row. Where the mirrors and burners are made, and the brightest street in the world.
[ListParagraph] The Glass House. A quarantine for contagious yawners.
[ListParagraph] The Slope. The slum quarter, where rouse is cut thin, nudgers are cheap, and whole families go down in an afternoon.
[Heading2] The Vigil Now
Rouse is failing; the dose that held a generation ago now barely holds an hour. The Dormitory has fewer than two thousand empty beds. The hours themselves seem to be lengthening. The Warden-Prime has sent an envoy to Sallowreach with a proposal too terrible to put in writing: the dead do not sleep. And in Bed One, the first sleeper, who has not stirred in three centuries, has begun to move her lips.
[Heading2] The People of the Vigil
[ListParagraph] Warden-Prime Ilvane Stroud, the Unblinking. Ruler of the Vigil. Born lidless, she has not shut her eyes in fifty-eight years. Two attendants brush them wet every half minute. She has seen everything that has happened in front of her since birth and can forget none of it.
[ListParagraph] Lady Oriel Stroud. The Warden's daughter, born with eyelids, which in her bloodline is a deformity. She is kept hidden. Alone in her rooms she closes her eyes for whole seconds at a time, and nothing has ever felt so good.
[ListParagraph] Maud Sexton, the Hourkeeper. Mistress of the city's clocks. She has proved, by pendulum, that each hour is a little longer than the last. Something is stretching the night that the Vigil will not admit it is having.
[ListParagraph] Captain Brannoch Pyre of the Wakers. He has kept a hundred thousand people awake with a needle. He has not been thanked once. His own nudger died last year, and he has not replaced her.
[ListParagraph] Old Nod, King of the Tuckers. He sells one minute of guarded sleep for a month's wage, and people queue around the block. He has never tried his own product. He says a man should not get high on his own supply.
[ListParagraph] Senna Vole, Dream-Diver. Forty descents, where three is the usual career. She has mapped the long room to within thirty paces of the eye. On her last dive, something behind the lid said her name.
[ListParagraph] Dr. Aldous Crane, Apothecary-General. Inventor of the current strain of rouse. He knows the dose curve and where it ends: there is a point at which the amount needed to stay awake is the amount that stops the heart. It is eleven years off.
[ListParagraph] Matron Cecily Dorm. Keeper of the Dormitory and the only living person who knows the number. She walks the rows every day, counting. She has been seen removing a pillow and standing over a bed for a long time.
[ListParagraph] Garron Ashlock, Lamplighter-General. He keeps ten thousand lamps burning. He has ninety days of oil, a supplier who has doubled the price, and a standing order to tell no one.
[ListParagraph] Pip Eleven-Bell. A street nudger of nine, paid by the hour to pinch strangers. Fast, sharp, and unpaid for two weeks. Children do not last on rouse, and Pip has begun to blink.
[ListParagraph] Lukas Marre, the Yawning Man. His yawns are catching beyond all reason; he once put down a whole market square. He lives in the Glass House, masked, alone, and apologizes through the pane to everyone who visits.
[ListParagraph] Sister Vesper Null. Prophet of the Bedded. She preaches that the eye is kind, the room is home, and that the Counted are the only citizens at peace. She looks more rested than anyone in the city, and nobody can explain it.
[ListParagraph] Corwin Half-Bell, the Man Who Woke. The only sleeper who ever came back, after half an hour under. He will not describe it. He has not blinked since, and he flinches whenever a door opens.
[ListParagraph] Thaddeus Knell. Chief bell-ringer. The city's bells sound every quarter hour to keep it awake, and they deafened him years ago. He rings by the clock and by the shaking in his teeth.
[ListParagraph] Mirela Sconce, the Seemer. She reads the Seeming for the public record. This month eleven districts, separately, hallucinated the same thing: a tall figure laying a table in the street.
[ListParagraph] Jack Tenterhook, Wake-Valet. Keeper-awake to the rich. He is an artist of the precisely tolerable pain, and his clients swear by him. He was a torturer before, and says the only difference is the tipping.
[ListParagraph] Inspector Hale Grimmer. He investigates blink-murders: killings done by ordinary people during a two-second sleep, with no memory after. He has forty cases. The method is the same in each.
[ListParagraph] Odile Farthing. Her son fell asleep at four, eleven years ago. She visits bed 40,112 daily. The Counted do not age, but her boy has grown. He is the size of a man now, and smiling.
[ListParagraph] Benedikt Lour, the Architect. He designed the modern city: the sloped floors, the one-legged chairs, the studded walls. He is now drawing a Second Dormitory. He has been told to plan for the whole population.
[ListParagraph] Rook Quarter-Bell. A rouse dealer on the Slope. He cuts his product with bottled Brinehollow brine, because it is cheap and it works. His customers stay awake. They have also started walking west.
[ListParagraph] Envoy Castellan Dray. The Warden's ambassador to Sallowreach. His commission is to buy the secret of Keeping, so that the Vigil's citizens can be killed and kept upright: a city of the dead, who never need to sleep.
[ListParagraph] Aveline Morne, the Sleeper in Bed One. A baker's daughter who lay down on the First Night and was the first not to rise. Three hundred years unmoving. Last month her lips began to shape words, and the Matron has started writing them down.
[Heading1] Cradlewrack
"First we ask what it is. Then we ask whether it stays." — the Midwife's Two Questions
[Heading2] The Meal
The people of the northwestern highlands ate Vey, the Opening, god of birth, beginnings, doors, and first breaths. It was the smallest of the Providers and the hardest to kill, because it kept starting again. The families who ate its caul became the Caul-lines.
Vey was the god of things coming into the world. Things have not stopped coming.
[Heading2] The Rule of the Land
Nothing here is born right, and too much is born.
Children arrive speaking in full sentences. Children arrive old, white-haired and arthritic, and grow younger or do not. Children arrive full-grown, tearing their way into a world they are already too big for. Some arrive as Parts: a single living hand, a warm ear, a length of spine that crawls. The Parts are healthy. They are also drawn toward each other.
Birth is not limited to mothers. Men have borne. The elderly have borne. A well in Rudge gave birth. So did a locked chest, a hillside, and a woman four days dead. The Tally of births outruns the count of conceptions every year, and the gap is widening.
Vey was also the god of doors, and in Cradlewrack nothing stays shut. Doors drift open in the night. Locks are found undone. Wounds reopen. Every threshold is something waiting to be crossed.
[Heading2] The Regrowth
[ListParagraph] The Taste. A sense of being about to begin. Restlessness. Doors left open behind you without noticing.
[ListParagraph] The Appetite. False labor in anyone, of any age or sex. The body rehearses.
[ListParagraph] The Course. True bearing, of things that are not children. The Caul-lines carry almost continuously.
[ListParagraph] The Seating. The land itself. The highlands are in labor. The quakes are contractions, and they are getting closer together.
[Heading2] Land and Weather
Rounded red-clay hills, warm to the touch, split by wet ravines. The soil is soft, dark red, and smells of iron. Springs run warm and slightly salt.
The quakes are called pangs, and everyone in the country knows the interval by heart. A hundred years ago it was a season. Now it is nine days. Floods are called the waters and come without rain, straight up out of the ground. Sinkholes are called crownings: the earth dilates, slowly, over weeks, into a perfect round opening, and people gather at the rim to see what comes up.
[Heading2] History
[ListParagraph] The Easy Births (0–90 A.G.). For three generations no mother died in childbed and no child was lost. The highlands filled.
[ListParagraph] The First Word (94 A.G.). A newborn in Kest opened its eyes and said, clearly, "Not yet." It was the first.
[ListParagraph] The Attendance (120 A.G.). The midwives organized, armed, and took authority over every birth. Within a decade they were the government.
[ListParagraph] The Year of Doors (310 A.G.). Every lock in the country failed in one night. The custom of bricking up rooms dates from this.
[ListParagraph] The Gathering of Parts (500 A.G.). It was first noticed that Parts from unrelated families fit each other.
[ListParagraph] The Shortening (present). The pangs have closed from thirty days to nine within one lifetime.
[Heading2] Rule and Politics
Cradlewrack is ruled by the Attendance, the order of midwives. No birth may take place without one present. She asks the Two Questions and answers them herself. Her knife is called the Second Opinion, and there is no appeal from it.
The old monarchy technically survives. The rightful king has been in the womb for forty-four years. His mother, the Queen Dowager, still carries him, and he is consulted on matters of state: one kick for yes.
The Caul-lines nobility are born veiled in a membrane that, in the highest houses, never comes off. They live behind it, speak through it, and are held to be prophetic.
Nobody pregnant may leave the country. The border marshals check.
[Heading2] Culture and Daily Life
[ListParagraph] Naming waits. A child is not named for a year, until it is clear what it is.
[ListParagraph] Bricking in. The cautious sleep in rooms with no door at all, mortared shut each night and broken out each morning.
[ListParagraph] The Tally. Every birth of every kind is recorded in a public ledger. Reading the Tally aloud is the evening news.
[ListParagraph] Mothers' names. Descent runs through whoever bore you. In many cases that requires a footnote.
[ListParagraph] Knocking. It is the gravest rudeness to open a door without knocking. You knock to warn whatever is on the other side.
[ListParagraph] Tablenight. Every door in the country is propped wide open until dawn, on purpose, so that nothing can be said to have broken in.
[Heading2] Factions
[ListParagraph] The Attendance. The midwives. Judges, physicians, priests, and executioners in one apron.
[ListParagraph] The Assemblers. A sect that collects Parts and joins them. They believe the Parts are one body, born piecemeal. They are making good progress.
[ListParagraph] The Barren Order. Men and women who have had themselves cut so they can bear nothing. They preach an end to beginnings, and their numbers grow with every pang.
[ListParagraph] The Foundling Companies. Regiments of the born-grown: adults in body, a few years old in fact, with no childhoods and no one to claim them. The state's only army.
[ListParagraph] The Uncut. People whose birth cords were never severed. They walk through life still attached to their mothers by yards of living cord, and consider everyone else amputees.
[Heading2] Places
[ListParagraph] Kest. The capital and seat of the Attendance. A city of round red buildings without a single door that locks.
[ListParagraph] The Dilation. The great crowning in the central highlands, a mile across and widening. A town of watchers has grown up around the rim.
[ListParagraph] The Sill. A hilltop where a single door stands in its frame with no wall around it. It has been ajar since the Gorging and opens a finger's width further each year.
[ListParagraph] The Assemblers' Barn. Guarded, lamplit, and warm. Visitors are not shown the far end.
[ListParagraph] The Lying-In. The Attendance's fortress hospital, where the births nobody speaks about are taken.
[Heading2] Cradlewrack Now
Nine days between pangs, and the Dilation has begun to move with them. The Assemblers' figure is more than half complete, and it has started to turn its head toward the Sill. The Attendance is split between midwives who still trust the knife and midwives who think it is six centuries too late. Everyone can do the arithmetic on the interval. Something is going to be born out of the ground, and the whole country is the mother.
[Heading2] The People of Cradlewrack
[ListParagraph] Midwife-Paramount Orla Kest. Head of the Attendance. She has attended nine thousand births and permitted six thousand. She remembers the other three thousand individually and can tell you why, in each case, without raising her voice.
[ListParagraph] Sabeth Thorne, the Second Opinion. The Attendance's most trusted knife. Last winter, for the first time, she hesitated. The thing lived. She has been watching it grow ever since, and has not reported it.
[ListParagraph] Ambrose, the Eldest Infant. Born twelve years ago with a white beard and a full vocabulary. He claims to remember being Vey's left eye. He is getting younger, and he says that when he reaches the cradle, it will be time.
[ListParagraph] Hester Lowe. She bore a hand. Only a hand: left, healthy, warm. She hides it from the Attendance and the Assemblers both. It holds her finger at night, and she will kill anyone who comes for it.
[ListParagraph] Dov Marrin, Assembler-Prime. He has fitted together four hundred Parts from four hundred families into one figure, eleven feet long, lying in a barn. It breathes. It is missing a left hand and a voice.
[ListParagraph] Brother Callum Stillwater. Abbot of the Barren Order. He was cut at twenty, by choice. He preaches that the kindest thing a person can do is start nothing, and he has never had so many listeners.
[ListParagraph] General Tibb. Commander of the Foundling Companies. Born six years ago as a grown man of thirty, mid-stride. He has no childhood, a brilliant tactical mind, and a private terror of the day he turns seven.
[ListParagraph] Lark Ninedays, the Counter. She times the pangs for the state with a water clock and a bell. She was named Lark Thirtydays at birth. She changes her name each time the interval shortens.
[ListParagraph] Madder Rudge. An old clay-digger and head of the watch at the Dilation. She has sat at the rim for twenty years. She says the hole has a pulse, and that lately there is a second one under it.

[ListParagraph] Duchess Iphigene Morwen. Head of the highest Caul-line house. She has lived fifty years inside the membrane she was born in. It has grown with her. She sees the future through it, blurred, and it has lately gone red.

[Heading1] Oathen
"Say less." — the whole of Oathen's law, as taught to children
[Heading2] The Meal
The people of the eastern canyons ate Tolm, the Witness, god of oaths and true speech. What Tolm heard was so; that was its entire nature. They ate it in silence, the story goes, because anything said in front of it would have counted. The families who ate its tongue became the Tongue-lines.
Tolm was the god who listened. It is still listening, from the inside.
[Heading2] The Rule of the Land
Words bind flesh.
A promise made in Oathen is a physical fact. Keep it, and nothing happens. Break it, and your body breaks to match, at once, with a sound. A man who swore to stand by his brother and ran had both legs fold under him. A woman who promised her heart to one and gave it to another was found with her chest open like a cupboard.
The break is always apt and never fatal by itself. Oathbreakers live, visibly, as the Forsworn. You can read a person's entire moral history from across the street.
It does not have to be solemn. "I'll be back before dark" is an oath. "I'd die for you" is an oath. A child's "I promise" binds exactly as hard as a king's. So Oatheners do not chat. They speak rarely, slowly, in conditionals, and they never, ever exaggerate.
[Heading2] The Regrowth
[ListParagraph] The Taste. Hearing your own words a half-second after you say them, as if someone were repeating them back.
[ListParagraph] The Appetite. A compulsion to swear. Sufferers find themselves vowing things aloud and must be gagged for their own safety.
[ListParagraph] The Course. Speech stops needing to be a promise to bind. Plain statements begin to come true. Figures of speech become fatal: a man who said he was starving was dead by evening, in a full pantry.
[ListParagraph] The Seating. The Sayers. A Tongue-line in the last stage does not describe the world. What they say, is. The few alive are kept bitted in gold and locked away.
[Heading2] Land and Weather
Dry red canyon country, deep and narrow, under a hard white sky. Towns are cut into the cliff walls. It is hot, still, and extremely quiet.
The great danger is echo. An oath repeated is an oath renewed, and a canyon will repeat anything. People have been bound three and four times over by their own voices coming back. Whole gorges are posted as silent. Wind is feared for the same reason: it carries words to people they were not meant for.
It rains once a year. Before it died, Tolm promised the canyons rain on the first day of Grace, and a god's word outlives the god. The Kept Rain has fallen on that day for six hundred and forty years. This year it came three days late.
[Heading2] History
[ListParagraph] The Silent Supper (0 A.G.). The Gorging in Oathen, conducted without a single word.
[ListParagraph] The First Breaking (3 A.G.). A merchant shorted a customer he had sworn to deal with fairly. His hands came off at the wrist in the marketplace.
[ListParagraph] The Honest Age. Two centuries when Oathen was the most trusted nation on the Table. Every treaty in the world was sworn there, and still is.
[ListParagraph] The Muzzling (215 A.G.). After a generation of children crippled by careless promises, the law required all children to be bitted until the age of twelve.
[ListParagraph] The Mute Succession (330 A.G.). The crown was restricted to those who had never spoken at all, and so had promised nothing.
[ListParagraph] The First Sayer (598 A.G.). A Tongue-line girl said "I wish it would stop" during a storm. It stopped. So did everything else in the valley.
[Heading2] Rule and Politics
Oathen is ruled by the Mute Kings, chosen in infancy, bitted for life, and raised to rule by gesture. Under the king sits the Bench of Clauses, a council of lawyer-priests called Clausewrights, whose art is the drafting of promises that can survive contact with reality.
There are no prisons. There is no need. A convicted person is made to swear the terms of their own sentence.
Oathen's power abroad is trust. An Oathener cannot break a contract and live whole, so the Rim Road Company swears every deal there, and every other government sends its treaties east to be made real. Oathen charges for this, and has never needed an army larger than its notaries.
[Heading2] Culture and Daily Life
[ListParagraph] Handtalk. A sign language used for everything that matters little. It is held not to bind. That belief is being tested.
[ListParagraph] The bit. Children wear a soft leather muzzle until twelve. Having it removed is the coming of age, and the first words spoken afterward are chosen with a Clausewright present.
[ListParagraph] Conditional speech. "If able." "As I now intend." "Barring death, illness, or weather." A polite sentence is mostly exits.
[ListParagraph] Marriage. Vows are negotiated for months and run to forty pages. Divorce does not exist.
[ListParagraph] Insults. The worst thing you can call someone is "talkative."
[ListParagraph] Tablenight. Twenty-four hours in which no one in the country makes a sound. It is the only day Oatheners feel safe.
[Heading2] Factions
[ListParagraph] The Bench of Clauses. The Clausewrights. Rulers in practice, and the finest contract lawyers who have ever lived.
[ListParagraph] The Forsworn. The broken. They live in the low canyon slums called the Breakdowns, organized, angry, and with very little left to lose.
[ListParagraph] The Sureties. Professionals who take on other people's oaths for a fee and carry the risk in their own bodies.
[ListParagraph] The Quiet. An ascetic order that has given up speech entirely, some by vow and some by knife.
[ListParagraph] The Echo-Wardens. Rangers who police the silent gorges and hunt down old sworn words still bouncing in the deep rock.
[Heading2] Places
[ListParagraph] Tacit. The capital, carved into both walls of the deepest canyon. Half a million people, and you can hear a sandal drop.
[ListParagraph] The Breakdowns. The slum of the Forsworn, on the canyon floor.
[ListParagraph] Sworn Gorge. A sealed ravine where an oath shouted in 419 A.G. has never stopped echoing. Anyone who hears it is bound by it. Nobody knows any longer what it says.
[ListParagraph] The Ledger. A cliff face into which every vow of public record has been carved, four miles long.
[ListParagraph] The Gilded Cells. Where the Sayers are kept.
[Heading2] Oathen Now
The rain was late, which means a god's promise has slipped, which should not be possible. Handtalkers are starting to break. A scribe has found evidence that written words are beginning to bind as well. If the Regrowth is widening what counts as speech, then soon there will be nothing an Oathener can safely say, sign, write, or perhaps think. And the Bench is debating, in forty-page conditionals, whether to take the bit out of a Sayer and have her fix it.
[Heading2] The People of Oathen
[ListParagraph] Ket the Ninth, the Mute King. Thirty-one years old and has never made a sound. He rules by nod and gesture, and the Bench spends weeks arguing what each one meant. He has a great deal to say, and he knows what would happen.
[ListParagraph] Sorrin Vael, Chief Clausewright. The finest drafter of promises alive. His treaties run to four hundred conditions. He has not made an unqualified statement since boyhood, including to his wife, including "yes."
[ListParagraph] Lady Imre Dath, the Sayer. A Tongue-line in the final stage. What she says becomes so. She is kept in a gold bit in the Gilded Cells and unmuzzled once a year, to say one sentence the Bench has drafted. She has begun to write her own.
[ListParagraph] Brakk, Voice of the Forsworn. He broke a marriage vow, and his ribs opened outward like a book and stayed that way. He leads the Breakdowns. He is the most plainspoken man in Oathen, having nothing left to break.
[ListParagraph] Ona Fell, the Surety. She carries two hundred and eleven oaths that belong to other people. Her body is a ledger of other families' risk. By her reckoning, she can survive one more default.
[ListParagraph] Jessamy Quill, Notary of the Rim Road. Every contract the Company holds is sworn before her and rests on her word as witness. If she dies or breaks, half the trade of the Table comes unbound in an afternoon.
[ListParagraph] Little Rue. At four, before her muzzling, she promised her mother she would never grow up. She has not. She is sixty-three, in a child's body, and she has outlived everyone the promise was for.
[ListParagraph] Halvar Stane, Echo-Warden. He patrols the silent gorges and has spent thirty years tracking the echo in Sworn Gorge. He has come close enough to make out two words of it. He will not repeat them.
[ListParagraph] Mirren Osk, the Handtalker. The greatest poet in a language held not to bind. Last year she signed a love poem and did not mean it. Three of her fingers snapped.
[ListParagraph] Cassian Wry, the Liar. A Blank, and the only man in Oathen who can lie and stay whole. He is the crown's most valuable spy, kept in luxury and never let out of sight. Everyone who meets him asks him to say something false, just to hear it.
[ListParagraph] Judge Thane Urrow. He presides over Breaking trials. There is no evidence and no testimony. He simply has the accused repeat the promise aloud, and the court watches to see what happens to them.
[ListParagraph] Asha Kindle, the Fire-Widow. She promised her dying husband she would never let the fire go out. That was thirty-one years ago. She has not slept more than an hour at a stretch since, and the fire has grown particular about what it is fed.
[ListParagraph] Garl Tome, the Bitmaker. Smith of muzzles: leather for children, iron for the Appetite-struck, gold for Sayers. He fitted his own daughter at birth and has never heard her voice. He says that is what love sounds like here.
[ListParagraph] Yusra Thole, Rainwarden. Keeper of the Kept Rain. She knows it came three days late. She cannot lie about it, so she has said nothing at all for a month, and the silence is being noticed.
[ListParagraph] Grandam Cess, the Vow-Eater. A back-canyon fixer who claims she can release you from an oath by having you swear a greater one over it. Her clients are free of what they came with. They owe her something worse.
[ListParagraph] Captain Renn Hold. His regiment swore to hold a pass to the death. He ordered the retreat. He kept his oath by staying; they broke theirs by obeying, and four hundred soldiers came apart on the road behind him.
[ListParagraph] Tavish Crook, called Loophole. A swindler who speaks only in questions. He has never asserted or promised anything in his life, and he has taken a fortune from people who assumed he had.
[ListParagraph] Sister Verity Amn of the Quiet. She cut out her own tongue at nineteen. She teaches, in writing, that for the highest bloodlines a promise made only in thought now binds. She knows because of what happened to her after the knife.
[ListParagraph] Envoy Dorian Sayce. Oathen's ambassador to nations that can lie. He finds foreigners unbearable and negotiation with them a kind of nausea. He is also the only diplomat on the Table everyone believes.
[ListParagraph] Ilan, the Oath-Child. A man once swore to his wife, "I promise you a son." She was past bearing. Ilan exists anyway. He is nine. His father died in the spring, and Ilan has started to become difficult to see.
[ListParagraph] Ottoline Vant, Scribe of the Ledger. She carves the public vows into the cliff. She miscut one last year, by a single word, and the man it named broke that night. Writing is not supposed to count.
[ListParagraph] Jude Crake, the Breaker. The state executioner, who owns no weapon. He visits the condemned, talks pleasantly for an hour, and leaves once they have promised him something small. It has never taken longer than an hour.
[Heading1] The Fast
"No, thank you." — the first words taught to every child in the Fast, and the last rites
[Heading2] The Refusal
The people of the central heartland did not eat. Their god was Orrum, the Host, god of the hearth, of welcome, and of the shared table. In the ninth winter of the Lack, Orrum laid itself down on the great stone table at Hallowboard and waited, as the others had.
They would not do it. Nine in ten of them starved to death that winter with a willing meal in front of them. The survivors' descendants call themselves the Unfed, and they are the only people in the world with no god in their blood.
Orrum got up again. It is still here. It has been trying to feed someone for six hundred and forty-one years.
[Heading2] The Rule of the Land
Never accept.
Orrum walks the Fast. Nobody living has seen it clearly and returned whole; the descriptions agree only that it is very tall, that it stoops under doorways, and that it is warm. It does not attack. It hosts. Wherever it passes, tables appear: laid, lit, steaming, perfect, with a chair pulled out.
If you sit, you are a guest, and a guest of Orrum does not get up. The Seated remain at table for decades, eating from empty plates, smiling, alive long past any natural span, wasting slowly and praising the food. They can talk. They invite you to join them. They mean it kindly.
Nothing else grows. For the Unfed the Lack never ended: seed still rots, hens still lay nothing. Every mouthful eaten in the Fast is carried in from the Rim. The only food the land itself offers is the god's.
The children learn the rules before they learn their letters:
[ListParagraph] Never enter a house where the hearth is already lit.
[ListParagraph] Never answer "Come in."
[ListParagraph] Never eat what you did not carry yourself.
[ListParagraph] If you smell bread, walk into the wind.
[ListParagraph] If a chair is pulled out for you, do not thank it.
[Heading2] What Grows Instead
The Fast has no Regrowth, because nothing was eaten. It has the opposite: a whole, living, unhoused god whose one purpose has been refused for six centuries. What is growing in the Fast is Orrum's need.
[ListParagraph] Early. One table a season, far from the roads.
[ListParagraph] For centuries. Tables in empty houses. A warm hearth in a ruin.
[ListParagraph] Within living memory. Tables in occupied houses, found laid at dawn. Place cards. Names spelled correctly.
[ListParagraph] Now. Tables outside the Fast, at crossroads in other lands. The Host has started calling on the neighbors.
[Heading2] Land and Weather
The best farmland on the Table, and almost no one on it. Rolling fields, good stone farmhouses, orchards in rows, all six centuries empty and all perfectly kept. The hedges are trimmed. The windows are clean. Nobody does it.
It is always early Carving here: mild, gold, still, the light of late afternoon. The air smells of baking with no source. Chimneys smoke over houses nobody lives in. The ground is warm where the god has walked, and the Unfed track it by melted frost. They call a warm front company.
[Heading2] History
[ListParagraph] The Refusal (0 A.G.). Hallowboard. The Unfed's founding act, and the only one of its kind.
[ListParagraph] The Thin Years (0–40 A.G.). The survivors lived on what the Rim would sell them, while their neighbors grew fat on miracle.
[ListParagraph] The First Sitting (44 A.G.). A starving family found a table in their own kitchen. Their neighbors watched through the window for eleven years before understanding they could not be saved.
[ListParagraph] The Vindication (130 A.G. onward). As the first godsign appeared in other lands, the Unfed stopped being fools and became prophets. It went to their heads.
[ListParagraph] The Reckoning (402 A.G.). The first armed band crossed the Rim Road to kill a high-Cut family in its beds. The Reckoners have not stopped since.
[ListParagraph] The Calling-Cards (638 A.G.). The first table found with a place card on it. The name was the finder's.
[Heading2] Rule and Politics
The Fast is governed by the Abstinent, a council of elders descended from those at Hallowboard. Their authority is moral, and absolute. Their chief power is the Pantry: every scrap of food in the Fast is imported, pooled, and rationed by the council, and exile from the Pantry is a death sentence that leaves no blood on anyone's hands.
Politics has two poles. The Keepers hold that the Unfed's duty is to refuse, endure, and stay clean. The Reckoners hold that refusing was not enough, and that the Godeaters must be made to answer for it, bloodline by bloodline.
Every other land has a use for the Unfed. Having no godsign, they are in effect a whole nation of Blanks, and poachers cross the border for them.
[Heading2] Culture and Daily Life
[ListParagraph] Thinness is holiness. The Unfed eat once a day, cold, standing, and without visible pleasure. Enjoying a meal is suspect.
[ListParagraph] Names. Children are named for the Refusal: Forbear, Abstain, Decline, Stint, Tarry, Nay.
[ListParagraph] No hearths. Homes have no fireplace and no dining table. Cooking is done outdoors on bare ground. Food is eaten from the hand.
[ListParagraph] The decline. The proper response to any offer of anything is "No, thank you," said three times. Accepting on the fourth is good manners. Accepting on the first is a scandal.
[ListParagraph] Pride. The Unfed are the only innocent people on the Table, and they are insufferable about it.
[ListParagraph] Tablenight. Every person in the Fast stands outdoors from dusk until dawn with their back to every door. On that night, in every house, the table is laid.
[Heading2] Factions
[ListParagraph] The Abstinent. The elders, holders of the Pantry.
[ListParagraph] The Reckoners. Raiders, assassins, and zealots who carry the Refusal across the border on a blade.
[ListParagraph] The Tablebreakers. Bands who hunt Orrum's tables and smash them with axes before anyone can sit. Dangerous, honored work.
[ListParagraph] The Crumb-Runners. Smugglers who bring food in from the Rim. Despised, and the only reason anyone is alive.
[ListParagraph] The Starved Saints. Ascetics who eat nothing at all. A few have kept it up far longer than a body can.
[ListParagraph] The Hostwatch. Trackers who follow the god at a distance and mark where it has been.
[Heading2] Places
[ListParagraph] Hallowboard. The stone table of the Refusal, on a bare hill at the exact center of the continent. It has eight sides. It is always clean.
[ListParagraph] Wanting. The Unfed's only real town, a cold, plain, hearthless place on the eastern edge, nearest the Rim.
[ListParagraph] The Long Table. A valley road where six hundred years of the Seated sit at one continuous table, nine miles end to end, eating nothing in good cheer.
[ListParagraph] The Pantry. A fortified granary in Wanting. The best-guarded building in the Fast.
[ListParagraph] The Empty Chair. The last standing temple of Orrum, where a single place is still set nightly by someone who should know better.
[Heading2] The Fast Now
Orrum is leaving. Its tables have turned up in three other lands, and the Hostwatch reports it walking the Rim Road at night, stooping to look in at inn windows. Pilgrims from the starving Fatlands are crossing in to find it. A farmer has grown a single green sprout in dead soil. A Saint is being fed by something. And a historian is circulating the worst idea the Unfed have ever heard: that their ancestors never refused. They were only late, and the others had left them nothing.
[Heading2] The People of the Fast
[ListParagraph] Elder Forbear Wend. Chair of the Abstinent, eighty-six, and so thin he is carried in a chair by two boys. He holds the keys to the Pantry. He has starved eleven people by leaving their names off a list, and prays for each of them by name.
[ListParagraph] Reckoner-Captain Scant Hollis. She has led forty raids across the Rim and ended nine high-Cut bloodlines in their beds. She keeps no trophies. She says she is not a murderer, since what she kills stopped being people generations ago.
[ListParagraph] Lent Ashby, the Invited. At nine she sat down at one of Orrum's tables. At ten, somehow, she got up. She is the only one who ever has. She cannot taste anything now, and wherever she sleeps, a table is found laid in the morning.
[ListParagraph] Tarry Moss, Crumb-Runner. He hauls grain in from the Rim by night and is spat on in the street by the people it feeds. He has never eaten a mouthful of his own cargo. He counts it, daily, down to the bean.
[ListParagraph] Sister Nay Corliss, Starved Saint. She has not eaten in three years. She should have died in the first two months. She does not know she is a miracle of the wrong kind: each morning she wakes with crumbs on her lips.
[ListParagraph] Decline Marsh, Tablebreaker. He has put an axe through four hundred tables. On the four hundred and first there was a card at one place with his name on it, and the handwriting was his mother's.
[ListParagraph] Spare Tolland of the Hostwatch. Thirty years following Orrum at a mile's distance, mapping the warm ground. He has never seen its face. He has seen it stop and wait for him to catch up, and he has come to think it is lonely.
[ListParagraph] Wont Bellamy. A heretic on the council. He argues the Refusal was only a postponement, and that the Unfed should do now what the others did then. He has friends abroad, in the Second Table, who very much agree.
[ListParagraph] Mother Stint Avery, Keeper of the Pantry. She weighs out every ration in the Fast. She knows there are four months of grain for six months of winter, and she has already decided which villages.
[ListParagraph] Abstain Rooke, called Abby. Eleven years old, raised on the rules, and curious. She has started leaving a crust on the windowsill at night. Each morning it has been replaced with something better.
[ListParagraph] Brother Hallow Crust, the Seated. He has sat at a table on the valley road for forty years, eating air with a silver fork. He is perfectly happy and perfectly sane. He describes each course in detail, and people who listen too long pull out a chair.
[ListParagraph] Dr. Mercy Thrane. A physician of starvation, and the first to document the trade in Unfed bodies. She has traced her missing patients to surgeries in four countries, where blank blood and blank organs fetch a noble's ransom.
[ListParagraph] Kale Dragoman, the Poacher. A Fatlander who hunts the Unfed for the Blank trade. He takes them alive, and he feeds them well on the road. That is the part his captives find hardest to forgive.
[ListParagraph] Envoy Prudence Lowell. The Fast's one ambassador, posted on the Rim Road. Her entire brief is to remind six nations that they are damned and her people are not. She is very good at it, and she has no friends.
[ListParagraph] Scour, the Returned. A Reckoner who spent a year undercover in the Fatlands and had to eat to keep his cover. He came home with godsign. He hides it under his sleeves and volunteers for every raid.
[ListParagraph] Verger Sober Osmond. Keeper of the Empty Chair, the last temple. Every night he lays one place at the altar, against all law, and every morning the plate is clean. He believes this is why the god has stayed gentle.
[ListParagraph] Yield Hartsell, the Farmer. She has planted the dead soil every spring for fifty years, because someone should. This year one seed came up. She has built a fence around it and told no one, because she does not know who watered it.
[ListParagraph] Little Fain. An orphan of six who went missing for a week and came back plump, clean, and happy. Someone had said "Come in," and Fain had. Fain will not say who cooked, only that they cried when it was time to go.
[ListParagraph] Marshal Keep Aldane. He holds the border against poachers coming in and, lately, against pilgrims: starving Fatlanders who have heard that somewhere in the Fast there is a table where you finally feel full.
[ListParagraph] Osanna Blythe, the Pilgrim. A Fatlands woman who has been hungry every second of her life. She is leading three hundred like her into the Fast to find the Host and sit down. She knows what happens to the Seated. She thinks it sounds wonderful.
[ListParagraph] Meek Garrity, the Historian. He has spent his life in the oldest records, and he has found the seating plan for Hallowboard. There was no refusal. The Unfed arrived on the tenth day. The table had already been cleared.
[ListParagraph] Welcome Ardent, the Steward. He walks a mile ahead of Orrum, laying the tables. He was the first of the Unfed ever to accept, six hundred years ago, and the only guest who was offered a position. He is polite, exhausted, and would like very much to be relieved.
[Heading1] Threads Between the Lands
"Every land is certain that the cure is next door." — Rim Road saying
These are the open storylines that cross borders. Each is a place where one region's cast runs into another's.
[ListParagraph] The Deadwatch. The Vigil's envoy, Castellan Dray, is in Lastgate to buy the secret of Keeping from Regent Ansel Grue. If it works, a sleepless city becomes a dead one. If the Kept truly cannot leave Sallowreach, the Vigil will have to move there.
[ListParagraph] The Host on the Road. The Vigil's seemers, Brinehollow's scavengers, and the Fatlands' pilgrims have all, separately, seen the same tall figure laying a table. Welcome Ardent is working his way around the Rim.
[ListParagraph] Brine in the Rouse. Dry Jonah's bottled sea is being cut into the Vigil's stimulant. The sleepless are now feeling the Calling, four hundred miles from any coast.
[ListParagraph] The First to Eat. Tobias Wether of the Fast has eaten Fatlands meat, and the Reckoner called Scour has done the same. Two Unfed bodies now carry a god that was never theirs.
[ListParagraph] The Blank Trade. Wren Hollowell in the Fatlands, Cassian Wry in Oathen, and the entire population of the Fast are worth more in parts than whole. Dr. Mercy Thrane is following the buyers, and the trail leads to the Second Table.
[ListParagraph] The Missing Hand. Dov Marrin's assembled figure in Cradlewrack lacks a left hand and a voice. Hester Lowe is hiding the hand. Juniper Dell is carrying half the sentence.
[ListParagraph] The Sayer's Sentence. Every land with a failing cure has thought of the same thing: go to Oathen, and have Lady Imre Dath say that it is fixed. Nobody knows what Tolm would make true if she did.
[ListParagraph] Eight Chairs. Cray Lobbett's table under the sea had eight chairs. Hallowboard has eight sides. Grandfather Peat remembers the night itself. Meek Garrity has a seating plan. Someone is going to put these together.
[ListParagraph] The Second Table. In every capital, a few of the highest Cuts are sharpening knives for a meal they intend to eat twice. Wont Bellamy wants the Fast to join them, starting with the one god that is still whole.
